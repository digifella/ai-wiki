# Health Metrics

Health metrics are quantifiable measurements of physiological and behavioral indicators used to assess and monitor an individual's health status. These metrics provide objective data about bodily functions, activity patterns, and overall wellness, serving as essential tools in both clinical medicine and personal health management. Common examples include heart rate, blood pressure, body temperature, sleep duration, physical activity levels, and various blood chemistry values. By tracking these measurements over time, individuals and healthcare providers can identify patterns, detect changes, and make informed decisions about treatment and lifestyle modifications.

## Collection and Monitoring

Health metrics are collected through various methods, ranging from clinical instruments used in medical settings to consumer wearable devices. Traditional approaches include periodic measurements taken during healthcare visits, such as blood pressure checks and laboratory tests. Wearable devices—electronic sensors worn on the body—have expanded the capacity for continuous or frequent data collection, allowing individuals to monitor metrics like heart rate variability, step count, and sleep patterns in real-time. This shift toward continuous monitoring has made health data more accessible to both patients and providers.

## Clinical and Personal Applications

In clinical settings, health metrics inform diagnosis, treatment planning, and disease management across conditions ranging from cardiovascular disease to diabetes. Healthcare providers use standardized metrics to track patient progress and adjust interventions accordingly. In personal health contexts, individuals use metrics to establish baseline measurements, set wellness goals, and maintain accountability for lifestyle changes. The interpretation of health metrics often requires professional expertise, as individual variation, measurement timing, and overall health context all influence what metrics actually indicate about a person's wellbeing.
