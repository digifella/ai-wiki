# Image Text Correction

Image text correction is the process of identifying and rectifying errors in text that has been automatically extracted from visual sources such as infographics, screenshots, photographs, and scanned documents. As optical character recognition (OCR) and AI-powered extraction tools become more accessible through platforms like Adobe Acrobat and Canva, the volume of text processed by these systems has increased significantly. However, automated extraction often introduces inaccuracies due to complex layouts, non-standard fonts, or low-resolution imagery, necessitating manual review and adjustment to ensure fidelity to the original source.

## Tools and Techniques

Adobe Acrobat Pro utilizes advanced OCR engines to convert scanned PDFs and images into editable text layers. Users can leverage the "Enhance Scans" feature to improve image clarity before extraction, which helps the software distinguish between characters more accurately. Canva’s text extraction capabilities, often integrated with AI assistants, allow users to grab text from design elements or uploaded images. These tools provide a baseline of editable content, but they frequently misinterpret kerning, line breaks, or stylized typography common in creative designs.

## Manual Verification and Editing

The correction phase involves comparing the extracted text against the original visual source to fix character substitutions, spacing issues, and formatting errors. In Adobe Acrobat, this is typically done by toggling between the original image layer and the text layer to verify alignment and content accuracy. In Canva, users may need to manually retype or adjust text boxes where the AI failed to recognize specific glyphs or artistic fonts. This step is critical for maintaining the integrity of the information, particularly when the extracted text is intended for publication or further digital processing.
