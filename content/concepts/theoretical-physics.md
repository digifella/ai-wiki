# Theoretical Physics

Theoretical physics is the branch of [[concepts/physics|physics]] that develops mathematical models and [[concepts/conceptual-frameworks|conceptual frameworks]] to explain natural phenomena and predict their [[concepts/observable-consequences|observable consequences]]. Rather than conducting experiments directly, theoretical physicists use [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]], logic, and existing experimental data to construct theories that describe how the physical world works at various [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]]—from [[concepts/subatomic-particles|subatomic particles]] to cosmic structures. These theories must ultimately be testable against observation and experiment to be considered valid.

## Core Methods and Approach

The fundamental approach of theoretical physics involves three essential steps: formulating a hypothesis or mathematical model, deriving predictions from that model through calculation, and comparing those predictions with empirical observations. This process, sometimes called the hypothetico-deductive method, ensures that theories remain grounded in observable reality rather than becoming purely abstract. When predictions diverge from experimental results, the [[concepts/theory|theory]] must be refined or replaced. Multiple distinct theories can sometimes make equivalent predictions about [[entities/nature|nature]], raising questions about which framework best captures underlying physical truth.

## Major Frameworks

Theoretical physics has produced several overarching frameworks that organize our understanding of nature. Classical mechanics, thermodynamics, [[concepts/electromagnetic-interaction|electromagnetism]], [[concepts/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]], and general relativity each describe distinct domains and scales of physical phenomena. Contemporary theoretical physics also explores unified theories such as [[concepts/string-theory|string theory]], which attempt to reconcile quantum mechanics with [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]] and provide a deeper description of fundamental reality. These frameworks continue to evolve as new [[concepts/empirical-evidence|experimental evidence]] emerges and mathematical techniques advance.
