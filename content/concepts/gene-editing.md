# Gene Editing

Gene editing encompasses techniques for making precise alterations to DNA sequences within living organisms. The field has undergone rapid advancement over the past decade, driven primarily by the development of [[concepts/crispr|CRISPR]]-Cas9 technology, which emerged as a more accessible and efficient alternative to earlier gene-editing methods. CRISPR allows researchers to target specific genetic sequences and [[entities/make|make]] targeted modifications with relative ease and cost-effectiveness, making it the dominant tool in contemporary genetic research and therapeutic development.

## CRISPR and Beyond

CRISPR-Cas9 was adapted from a bacterial defense mechanism and has become foundational to modern gene editing work. However, researchers continue to explore improvements and alternatives to address limitations such as off-target effects and delivery challenges. Recent work [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] institutions like the [[entities/broad-institute|Broad Institute]] has focused on identifying successor technologies that may offer increased precision, efficiency, or applicability to a broader [[concepts/range|range]] of genetic targets.

The evolution of gene editing reflects ongoing refinement in our ability to manipulate genetic material safely and effectively. As new techniques emerge and existing methods are optimized, the potential [[concepts/software|applications]] expand across medicine, [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]], and research, though development of these technologies continues alongside careful consideration of their implications.
