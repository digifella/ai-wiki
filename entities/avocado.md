# Avocado

Avocado is a fruit native to south-central Mexico, belonging to the laurel family (Lauraceae). The plant produces pear-shaped fruits with a large central seed surrounded by creamy, nutrient-dense flesh. Avocados are typically harvested while still firm and ripen after picking, a characteristic that has shaped their commercial distribution and storage practices worldwide. The fruit contains significant quantities of healthy fats, fiber, vitamins, and minerals, including potassium, vitamin K, and folate, making it nutritionally dense compared to most other fruits.

## Culinary and Commercial Use

Avocados are widely used in cuisines around the world, most famously in guacamole and as a topping for toast, salads, and sandwiches. The creamy texture and mild flavor make them versatile in both savory and sweet applications. Commercial cultivation has expanded significantly since the mid-20th century, with major producing regions including Mexico, California, Indonesia, and Colombia. The global avocado market has grown substantially due to increased demand in North America, Europe, and Asia.

## Cultivation and Harvesting

Avocado trees thrive in subtropical and temperate climates with moderate rainfall and well-drained soil. The fruit typically takes 3-4 years after planting to reach commercial productivity. Harvesting is performed by hand, as the fruit does not ripen on the tree. This post-harvest ripening process allows for extended distribution networks, as fruit can be transported while unripe and ripened at its destination to ensure optimal quality and shelf life.
