# Metabolism

**Metabolism** encompasses the sum of biochemical processes that sustain life, including catabolic pathways like Yeasts and Fermentation and anabolic or regulatory responses to substrates such as [[entities/xylitol|Xylitol]]. It dictates energy production (ATP), gas generation for leavening, and flavor compound synthesis in food systems, while also governing species-specific toxicological responses.

## Yeast Fermentation
**Yeast fermentation** is an anaerobic metabolic pathway wherein *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* metabolizes carbohydrates (primarily glucose and fructose) into carbon dioxide and ethanol, generating ATP for cellular survival. In culinary applications, it functions as a biological leavening agent and flavor precursor through the accumulation of organic acids, esters, and alcohols.

### Mechanisms
* **Glycolysis:** Enzymatic degradation of sugars to pyruvate; kinetics modulated by temperature, [[concepts/sugar|sugar]] concentration, and osmotic [[concepts/stress|stress]].
* **Gas Expansion:** CO2 entrapment within gluten matrices drives volume increase; [[concepts/storing|retention]] relies on dough elasticity, hydration, and surface tension.
* **Flavor Biochemistry:** Secondary metabolites yield complexity; extended fermentation elevates acetic-acid and lactic-acid ratios, enhancing sourness and crumb structure development.

### Kinetic Factors
* **Temperature:** Optimal activity 70–80°F (21–27°C); cold retardation inhibits metabolic rate, preserving gas retention and extending fermentation window.
* **Osmotic [[concepts/regulation|Regulation]]:** High sugar/salt concentrations reduce water activity, stressing yeast; salt simultaneously regulates enzyme activity.

## Xylitol Metabolism and Toxicity
Integrate insights from [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Xylitol-Human-Health-Benefits-and-Canine-Toxicity|Xylitol: Human Health Benefits and Canine Toxicity]]:
* **Source & Properties:** Naturally occurring [[concepts/sugar-alcohol|sugar alcohol]] found in plants and mushrooms; sweetness comparable to [[concepts/sucrose|sucrose]] but with ~40% fewer calories.
* **Human Metabolism:** Slowly absorbed in the small intestine and partially metabolized without requiring insulin, resulting in a low glycemic index and minimal impact on blood sugar levels.
* **[[concepts/canine-toxicity|Canine Toxicity]]:** Highly toxic to dogs due to rapid absorption and subsequent stimulation of insulin [[concepts/deployment|release]], causing hypoglycemia; potential for acute liver failure at higher doses.
