# Satellite Launch

Satellite launch is the process of deploying artificial satellites into orbit using specialized launch vehicles. The operation encompasses the entire sequence from pre-flight preparation through orbital insertion, including vehicle assembly, fueling, countdown procedures, and ascent to the target orbital altitude. Launch operations require coordination across multiple systems, including ground infrastructure, flight control centers, and tracking stations. Success depends on precise timing, vehicle performance, and adherence to safety protocols established through decades of aerospace engineering.

## Launch Vehicles and Infrastructure

Modern launch operations rely on heavy-lift rockets designed to overcome Earth's gravity and atmospheric drag. These vehicles utilize complex propulsion systems, often combining liquid and solid propellants to achieve the necessary thrust-to-weight ratios. Ground infrastructure includes launch pads, service towers, and propellant storage facilities that support the final stages of vehicle integration. Tracking stations distributed globally monitor the rocket's trajectory, providing critical telemetry data to flight control centers during the ascent phase.

## Operational Phases

The launch sequence begins with pre-flight checks and vehicle integration, followed by the final countdown. During liftoff, the rocket must navigate through the thickest part of the atmosphere before staging occurs to discard empty fuel tanks and boosters. Once in space, the upper stage performs orbital insertion maneuvers to place the satellite into its designated orbit, whether low Earth orbit, geostationary transfer orbit, or higher trajectories. Post-launch, the satellite deploys its solar panels and antennas, initiating its operational lifecycle while the launch vehicle completes its disposal procedures.

## Industry Context

The landscape of satellite launches has evolved with the introduction of new commercial launch providers aiming to reduce costs and increase frequency. Notable among these developments is the New Glenn launch vehicle, a heavy-lift orbital rocket designed to carry significant payloads to orbit. This vehicle represents a shift toward reusable and highly capable launch systems, aiming to support both commercial communications and government missions. The continued advancement of launch technology is essential for expanding access to space and supporting the growing demand for satellite-based services.
