# Domestic Semiconductor Independence

Domestic semiconductor independence is a national strategic framework designed to reduce reliance on foreign suppliers by establishing comprehensive local chip manufacturing capabilities. This initiative encompasses the construction of domestic fabrication plants (fabs), the support of specialized research institutions, and the development of integrated supply chains for semiconductor production. The primary objective is to achieve technological sovereignty, thereby mitigating vulnerabilities to international trade restrictions and geopolitical pressures.

The strategy involves a multi-layered approach to hardware and software development. On the hardware side, it focuses on scaling local production capacity to meet domestic demand for advanced logic chips and memory devices. On the software side, it promotes the creation of AI models and algorithms that do not depend on proprietary foreign hardware architectures. For instance, the development of Nvidia-free AI models like LongCat 2.0 exemplifies efforts to optimize software performance on domestically produced silicon, ensuring functionality even when access to leading international GPUs is restricted.

This pursuit of independence is driven by the recognition of semiconductors as critical infrastructure for national security and economic stability. By fostering an ecosystem that supports both the physical production of chips and the adaptation of software to local hardware, nations aim to create a resilient supply chain. This reduces the risk of disruption from external geopolitical conflicts and ensures that critical technological advancements can continue regardless of international trade dynamics.
