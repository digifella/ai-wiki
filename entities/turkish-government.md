# Turkish Government

The Republic of Turkey operates as a presidential republic with a unicameral legislature. The President serves as both head of state and head of government, wielding executive authority over the country's administration. The Grand National Assembly (Türkiye Büyük Millet Meclisi) functions as the sole legislative body, comprising 600 members responsible for enacting laws and representing the Turkish population. An independent judiciary forms the third branch, interpreting laws and administering justice according to the Turkish legal system.

## Administrative Structure

The executive branch is headed by the President, who appoints vice presidents and ministers. The President holds significant power in foreign policy, national security, and the appointment of senior civil servants. While the Grand National Assembly retains legislative authority, the 2017 constitutional amendments expanded the executive's role in governance, shifting the system from a parliamentary to a presidential model.

## Legal Framework

The Turkish legal system is based on civil law principles, heavily influenced by European models. The Constitutional Court serves as the highest judicial authority for constitutional matters, while the Court of Cassation and the Council of State act as the highest courts for civil and administrative cases, respectively. The government is responsible for implementing laws passed by the legislature and managing state affairs through various ministries and agencies.
