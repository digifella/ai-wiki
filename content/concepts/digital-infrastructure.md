# Digital Infrastructure

Digital infrastructure refers to the underlying systems and networks that support digital technology, including [[concepts/hardware|hardware]], [[concepts/software|software]], data [[entities/storage|storage]], communication protocols, and network services. It is essential for modern computing and information exchange.

## Key Components
- **Internet**: The backbone of global digital communication.
- **Data Centers**: Facilities used to house computer systems and associated components, such as telecommunications and storage systems.
- **Networks**: Wired and wireless networks that facilitate data transmission between devices.
- **Software**: Programs [[concepts/running|running]] on various platforms to provide services and applications.

### New Information: P vs. NP Problem

The [[concepts/p-vs-np-problem|P vs NP problem]] is a major unsolved question in [[concepts/theoretical-computer-science|theoretical computer science]], with significant implications for digital infrastructure:

- **[[concepts/verification|Verification]] vs Solvability**: It questions whether problems that can be quickly verified by computers can also be quickly solved.
- **[[concepts/million-dollar-prize|Million Dollar Prize]]**: The Clay [[concepts/mathematics|Mathematics]] Institute offers $1 million to the person who solves this problem.
- **Revolutionary Impact**: A [[concepts/solution|solution]] could transform fields like medicine, [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]], and [[concepts/cryptography|cryptography]].

### Historical Context
The problem was first described in a 1956 letter by Kurt Gödel to [[entities/chef-john|John]] von Neumann and has since been formalized as one of the [[concepts/millennium-prize-problems|Millennium Prize Problems]] by the Clay [[concepts/mathematics|Mathematics]] Institute.
