# Lunar Landing

Lunar landing represents a critical [[concepts/purpose|objective]] of [[concepts/nasa|NASA]]'s [[concepts/artemis-program|Artemis program]], which aims to return humans to the Moon. The [[concepts/success|success]] of this goal depends on the readiness of two primary systems: the Space Launch System (SLS) and the [[concepts/human-landing-system|Human Landing System]] (HLS). Artemis 3, scheduled as the first crewed lunar landing mission of the program, [[concepts/faces|faces]] significant technical and developmental challenges that affect its launch timeline and operational feasibility.

## SLS and HLS Development Status

The SLS, [[entities/nasa|NASA]]'s heavy-[[entities/lift|lift]] launch vehicle, and the HLS, which includes the lunar lander and ascent systems, remain under active development. Both systems must demonstrate [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] and readiness before [[concepts/crewed-missions|crewed missions]] can proceed. Technical challenges, manufacturing complexities, and integration issues have delayed progress, echoing the iterative [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] seen in earlier eras of spaceflight.

## Historical Context: Project Gemini

Understanding the current challenges in lunar landing infrastructure benefits from analyzing the historical progression of US spaceflight capabilities. The transition from early orbital flights to complex [[concepts/lunar-return|lunar missions]] required intermediate technological leaps.

*   **Bridging the Gap:** [[concepts/project-gemini|Project Gemini]] served as the crucial intermediary program that bridged the technological gap between the initial US [[concepts/mercury-program|Mercury program]] and the ambitious [[concepts/apollo-program|Apollo moon landing missions]].
*   **Mastering Challenges:** Gemini focused on mastering spaceflight challenges such as long-duration survival, rendezvous, and docking, which are foundational for modern lunar landing operations.
