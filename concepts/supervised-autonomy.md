# Supervised Autonomy

Supervised autonomy is a regulatory classification for autonomous vehicles that permits operation under human oversight rather than requiring continuous manual control. Under this framework, the vehicle autonomously manages routine driving tasks including acceleration, braking, steering, and lane maintenance, while a human operator remains attentive and capable of intervention. This approach represents an intermediate regulatory position between fully manual driving and complete vehicle autonomy, enabling manufacturers to deploy advanced driver-assistance systems in specific contexts where full self-driving certification has not yet been granted.

The concept gained significant attention when Tesla received approval to operate its Full Self-Driving (FSD) software in the Netherlands under this classification. This approval serves as a precedent for how European Union member states might evaluate and permit the use of Level 2+ automation systems. It allows drivers to utilize hands-free capabilities on designated roads while maintaining legal liability and responsibility for the vehicle's actions, bridging the gap between current assisted driving technologies and future regulatory standards for higher levels of autonomy.
