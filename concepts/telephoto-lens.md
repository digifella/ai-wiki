# Telephoto Lens

A telephoto lens is a camera lens with an extended focal length, typically 70mm or greater, that magnifies distant subjects and brings them closer in the photographic frame. By increasing focal length, these lenses allow photographers to capture fine detail from subjects that are far away without physically moving closer to them. The magnification effect is directly proportional to focal length—a 200mm lens provides roughly twice the magnification of a 100mm lens.

## Optical Characteristics

Telephoto lenses are distinguished by their narrow field of view and compressed perspective, which flattens the apparent distance between foreground and background elements. This optical compression is a defining aesthetic trait, often used to isolate subjects from their surroundings. The design typically employs a specific arrangement of positive and negative lens groups to achieve a physical length shorter than the focal length, allowing for more compact construction than simple long-focus lenses.

## Applications and Considerations

These lenses are commonly used in wildlife, sports, and portrait photography where physical proximity to the subject is impractical or undesirable. Due to the narrow angle of view, telephoto lenses are highly susceptible to camera shake, often requiring faster shutter speeds or image stabilization technology to maintain sharpness. Additionally, the depth of field becomes shallower at equivalent apertures compared to wider lenses, facilitating strong subject isolation through background blur.
