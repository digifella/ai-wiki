# Gravitational Waves

Gravitational waves are ripples in spacetime produced by accelerating massive objects, predicted by Einstein's general [[concepts/theory|theory]] of relativity and directly detected for the first time in 2015. These waves travel at the speed of light and can be observed using sensitive interferometric detectors such as LIGO and Virgo, which measure minute distortions in space as the waves pass through Earth.

## Detection of Compact Objects

Gravitational wave observations have enabled the detection and characterization of colliding [[concepts/neutron-stars|neutron stars]] and stellar-mass [[concepts/black-holes|black holes]]. More recently, analysis of gravitational wave signals has revealed evidence for sub-solar mass compact objects—those with masses below that of our Sun. These detections are significant because such low-mass objects were not expected to form through conventional stellar processes, raising questions about their origin and [[entities/nature|nature]].

## Primordial Black Holes

One explanation for sub-solar mass compact objects detected through gravitational waves is that they could be primordial black holes. These hypothetical objects would have formed in the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]] from density fluctuations rather than through stellar collapse, and their detection through gravitational waves would provide direct evidence for a long-standing theoretical prediction. The mass range and frequency of such detections may help constrain models of primordial black hole formation and their contribution to [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]].
