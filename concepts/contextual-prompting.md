# Contextual Prompting

Contextual prompting is a technique for customizing AI language models to produce output tailored to specific professional domains and use cases. By providing detailed custom instructions to models like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini, users establish persistent context that shapes how the AI responds to subsequent queries. Rather than repeating background information with each individual prompt, contextual prompting embeds this information into the model's operating parameters, allowing for more consistent and specialized outputs across multiple interactions.

## Application in Legal Work

Legal professionals use contextual prompting to adapt general-purpose AI models for law-specific tasks. Custom instructions can specify the jurisdiction, practice area, document type, and desired tone for legal analysis. For example, a lawyer might configure a model to assume a particular state's substantive law, to flag ethical considerations relevant to their practice, or to format contract reviews according to their firm's standards. This reduces the need to respecify these parameters in every prompt and minimizes the risk of the model applying inappropriate legal frameworks or conventions.

## Implementation and Limitations

Effective contextual prompting requires clear articulation of domain requirements, preferred methodologies, and output constraints. The technique works best when instructions are specific and unambiguous. However, users should recognize that custom instructions do not fundamentally alter model capabilities or training data—they function as formatting and behavioral guidelines. Contextual prompting cannot guarantee accuracy or replace professional judgment, and legal users must continue to verify AI-generated output independently.
