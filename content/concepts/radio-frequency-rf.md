# Radio Frequency (Rf)

Radio frequency refers to [[concepts/light|electromagnetic radiation]] in the frequency range typically used for wireless communications, spanning from approximately 3 kHz to 300 GHz. In [[concepts/satellite-communication|satellite communication]] systems, RF signals serve as the fundamental mechanism for transmitting data between ground stations and orbiting satellites. The characteristics of RF signals—including frequency band, bandwidth, power, and modulation—determine the capacity, range, and efficiency of wireless networks.

## RF in Satellite Architecture

Satellite communication systems like Starlink rely on RF technology to establish bidirectional links between user terminals and orbital platforms. The choice of frequency band affects signal propagation, atmospheric attenuation, and antenna design. Higher frequency bands offer greater [[concepts/bus-bandwidth|bandwidth capacity]] but require more precise alignment and are more susceptible to weather interference. Starlink's evolution across versions has involved optimizations to RF signal architecture, including improvements to antenna arrays, beamforming capabilities, and spectral efficiency to increase throughput and reduce latency.

## Signal Physics and Performance

The physics governing RF propagation includes path loss, multipath fading, and Doppler effects, all of which present engineering challenges in satellite systems where the transmitter and receiver are in relative motion. Modern satellite constellations employ sophisticated [[concepts/signal-processing|signal processing]] techniques—such as adaptive modulation, error correction, and phased-array beamforming—to maintain reliable communication across varying conditions. Successive generations of satellite systems, including [[concepts/starlink-architecture|Starlink v3]], have incorporated advances in RF component miniaturization and signal processing to enhance link margins and capacity.
