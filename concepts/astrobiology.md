# Astrobiology

Astrobiology is an interdisciplinary scientific field that examines the conditions necessary for life to exist beyond [[entities/earth|Earth]] and investigates where such life might be found in the universe. The discipline combines methods and knowledge from biology, chemistry, [[concepts/geology|geology]], and [[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]] to address fundamental questions about life's origins, distribution, and the environmental requirements for [[concepts/habitability|habitability]]. Rather than engaging in speculation, astrobiology grounds its inquiries in empirical data, focusing on the physical, chemical, and biological processes that enable life to emerge and persist.

## Origins and Evolution of Life

A primary objective of astrobiology is to understand the origin of life on Earth to establish a baseline for detecting life elsewhere. Researchers study abiogenesis, the process by which life arises from non-living matter, by analyzing ancient geological records and conducting laboratory experiments that simulate early Earth conditions. This research aims to identify the universal chemical pathways that lead to self-replicating molecules and cellular structures, providing clues about how life might have begun on other planetary bodies.

## Habitability and Extremophiles

The field investigates the limits of life by studying [[concepts/extremophiles|extremophiles]], organisms that thrive in environments previously thought uninhabitable, such as deep-sea hydrothermal vents, acidic lakes, and frozen tundras. These organisms serve as models for potential extraterrestrial life, suggesting that life could exist in diverse and harsh environments across the solar system and beyond. By mapping the boundaries of habitability, astrobiologists identify key indicators, such as liquid water, energy sources, and essential elements, that define the "habitable zone" around stars where planets might support life.

## Search for Extraterrestrial Life

Astrobiology drives the search for biosignatures, measurable substances or phenomena that provide evidence of past or present life. This involves analyzing the atmospheres of exoplanets for chemical imbalances that suggest biological activity, as well as exploring Mars and icy moons like Europa and Enceladus for signs of microbial life. Missions utilize rovers, orbiters, and future sample-return campaigns to collect data on planetary geology and chemistry, refining our understanding of where life is most likely to be found in the cosmos.
