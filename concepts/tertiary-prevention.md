# Tertiary Prevention

Tertiary prevention represents the final tier of the disease prevention continuum, applied after a condition has been diagnosed. Unlike primary prevention, which aims to stop disease before it occurs, or secondary prevention, which focuses on early detection, tertiary prevention targets individuals who already have established health issues. The primary objective is to manage existing conditions to slow their progression, prevent complications, and mitigate the impact of the disease on the patient's daily life.

This approach is particularly critical for chronic diseases and long-term conditions where a complete cure may not be feasible. Through ongoing medical management, rehabilitation, and supportive care, tertiary prevention seeks to maintain or improve the patient's quality of life. It involves strategies designed to reduce disability, manage symptoms, and prevent further deterioration of health status, thereby enhancing functional capacity and social participation.

Key components of tertiary prevention include comprehensive care plans that address both physical and psychological needs. These plans often involve multidisciplinary teams working to manage comorbidities and prevent secondary health issues. By focusing on long-term management rather than acute intervention, this tier of prevention helps patients achieve the best possible outcomes despite the presence of a persistent condition.
