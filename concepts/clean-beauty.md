# Clean Beauty

Clean beauty refers to cosmetic and personal care products formulated without certain ingredients considered potentially harmful or controversial. The category typically excludes synthetic chemicals, parabens, sulfates, phthalates, and other additives that consumers or manufacturers regard as unsafe or environmentally damaging. While there is no universally agreed-upon definition of "clean," the movement generally emphasizes transparency regarding ingredient sourcing, manufacturing processes, and product composition.

## Definition and Scope

The term "clean beauty" lacks regulatory standardization, meaning manufacturers apply varying criteria when labeling products as clean. Some brands focus exclusively on avoiding synthetic compounds, while others emphasize natural or organic ingredients, cruelty-free testing, or sustainable packaging. This inconsistency has led to criticism that "clean" functions primarily as a marketing category rather than a scientifically defined standard. Consumer definitions also vary widely, reflecting different priorities around health, environmental impact, and ingredient philosophy.

## Industry Growth and Consumer Adoption

The clean beauty market has expanded significantly since the early 2010s, driven by increased consumer awareness of ingredient safety and environmental concerns. Major cosmetic companies have launched clean beauty lines or acquired brands specializing in the category. Marketing emphasizes ingredient transparency, often through detailed labeling and educational content about formulation choices. However, regulatory bodies like the FDA and European Commission have not established formal "clean beauty" standards, leaving enforcement and verification to individual companies and third-party certifications.
