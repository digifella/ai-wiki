# Pan Searing

Pan searing is a cooking technique in which meat, typically steak, is cooked in a hot pan with minimal added liquid to develop a flavorful crust. The method relies on high heat to trigger the Maillard reaction, a chemical process that creates complex flavors and browning on the meat's surface. This approach contrasts with cooking methods that use added moisture or lower temperatures, which can inhibit crust formation and result in a less flavorful exterior.

## Technique and Temperature

Successful pan searing requires a sufficiently hot pan—typically preheated to 400°F (200°C) or higher—and meat that is brought close to room temperature and patted dry before cooking. The steak is placed in the pan with minimal fat (often just a light coating of oil) and left undisturbed for several minutes to allow direct contact with the hot surface. This uninterrupted contact is essential for crust development. The meat is then flipped and cooked on the other side, with the duration depending on desired doneness and thickness.

## Avoiding the Gray Band

One key advantage of pan searing over certain other cooking methods is its ability to minimize the gray band—a band of overcooked meat that forms between the crust and the interior. By using high, concentrated heat for a relatively short time, the temperature gradient from surface to center remains steep, preserving the desired interior doneness while building the crust. This makes pan searing particularly effective for achieving a well-browned exterior with a properly cooked interior.
