# Historic Preservation

Historic preservation is the practice of maintaining, protecting, and restoring buildings and structures of historical significance. This field encompasses the identification of culturally important properties, their documentation, and the application of conservation techniques to slow deterioration and prevent loss. Preservationists work to retain original materials and architectural features while addressing structural concerns and adapting spaces for contemporary use where necessary.

## Scope and Methods

Historic preservation operates across multiple [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]], from individual buildings to entire districts and landscapes. Practitioners employ various approaches depending on the [[concepts/structure|structure]]'s condition, historical value, and intended use. Common methods include stabilization to prevent further decay, restoration to return a building to a previous historical state, and rehabilitation to [[entities/make|make]] a structure suitable for modern purposes while respecting its historical character. Documentation through measured drawings, photographs, and written records forms a critical component of [[concepts/preservation|preservation]] work.

## Significance and Challenges

Preserving historic structures serves multiple purposes: maintaining [[concepts/cultural-heritage|cultural heritage]], supporting community identity, sustaining architectural knowledge, and often reducing environmental waste compared to demolition and new construction. Preservation efforts face ongoing challenges including inadequate funding, competing development pressures, environmental damage, and the technical complexity of working with historic materials and construction methods. Balancing preservation with necessary building [[concepts/code|code]] [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] and [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]] requirements remains a persistent tension in the field.
