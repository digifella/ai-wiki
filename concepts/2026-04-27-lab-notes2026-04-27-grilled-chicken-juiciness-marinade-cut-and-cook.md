## Marinade Composition and Mechanisms

Marinades enhance grilled chicken juiciness through chemical modification of muscle proteins and moisture retention. Acidic components such as citrus juice, vinegar, or wine denature surface proteins, creating a protective barrier that slows evaporative moisture loss during cooking. Salt-based marinades work through osmotic action, allowing the brine solution to penetrate muscle fibers and increase water-holding capacity. Enzymatic ingredients like pineapple, papaya, or ginger break down protein structures, promoting deeper seasoning penetration and improved moisture distribution throughout the meat.

## Cutting Techniques

The size and orientation of chicken cuts significantly affect cooking evenness and moisture retention. Thinner, uniform pieces cook more quickly and retain juiciness better than thick cuts, which risk drying at the surface while remaining undercooked inside. Cutting against the grain shortens muscle fibers, making the finished product more tender and allowing marinades to penetrate more effectively. Bone-in, skin-on cuts naturally retain more moisture than boneless, skinless portions due to the insulating effect of bone and skin during grilling.

## Cooking Methods and Temperature Control

Maintaining optimal internal temperature is critical for preserving juiciness in grilled chicken. Cooking to an internal temperature of 165°F (74°C) at the thickest point prevents overcooking while ensuring food safety. Using indirect heat or lower temperature zones on the grill reduces surface moisture loss and allows for more controlled cooking. Resting the cooked chicken for 3–5 minutes before serving allows residual heat to distribute evenly and permits muscle fibers to reabsorb retained juices.
