# Focal Length

Focal length is an optical measurement, expressed in millimeters, that defines the distance between a camera's lens and its image sensor when the lens is focused at infinity. This value fundamentally determines how a lens captures and frames a scene, influencing both the magnification of subjects and the perspective of the resulting image. Common focal lengths span a wide spectrum, ranging from 14mm or shorter for ultra-wide-angle lenses to 600mm or longer for [[concepts/telephoto-lens|telephoto]] lenses. A 50mm lens is traditionally classified as standard because its field of view closely approximates that of human [[concepts/computer-vision|vision]].

## Field of View and Composition

Focal length directly controls a lens's field of view, which is the angular width of the scene the lens can capture. Shorter focal lengths provide a wider field of view, allowing more of the environment to be included in the frame, while longer focal lengths narrow the field of view to magnify distant subjects. This relationship is critical in [[concepts/long-lens-photography|panoramic photography]], where selecting the appropriate focal length helps manage distortion and ensures seamless stitching of multiple images. The choice of focal length thus dictates not only what is visible but also the spatial [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] between objects within the [[concepts/writing|composition]].
