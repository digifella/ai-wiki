# Submerged Landscapes

**Submerged landscapes** refer to terrestrial environments that have been inundated by water due to rising sea levels, subsidence, or [[concepts/crustal-movement|tectonic activity]]. These areas preserve geological and archaeological records critical for understanding past climates, ecosystems, and human occupation.

## Geological Context
- **Rapid Submergence:** Coastal plains often sink faster than interior lands due to isostatic adjustment and sediment compaction Isostasy.
- **Sediment Traps:** Submerged river valleys and estuaries accumulate fine-grained sediments, preserving organic matter and artifacts Sedimentology.
- **Paleo-coastlines:** Identification of ancient shorelines helps reconstruct interglacial sea-level standstills Quaternary_Geology.

## Archaeological Significance
- **[[concepts/preservation|Preservation]] Bias:** Anaerobic conditions in submerged sediments favor the preservation of wooden structures and organic materials often lost in terrestrial contexts Archaeological_Methods.
- **Lost Settlements:** Many prehistoric coastal settlements are now underwater, requiring remote sensing for detection Marine_Archaeology.
