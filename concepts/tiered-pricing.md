# Tiered Pricing

Tiered pricing is a business model in which a company offers multiple versions of a product or service at different price points, each with varying features, capabilities, or usage limits. This structure allows customers to select a plan that matches their needs and budget, while enabling providers to capture value across different market segments. Tiered pricing is commonly used in software-as-a-service (SaaS) products, cloud services, and digital platforms where marginal costs of serving additional users are minimal.

## Structure and Implementation

Typical tiered pricing models are organized into distinct levels, such as free, basic, premium, and enterprise tiers. Each tier usually introduces incremental value through expanded storage, advanced functionality, or priority support. The implementation requires careful segmentation of the target audience to ensure that the feature gaps between tiers are significant enough to drive upgrades but not so large that lower-tier users feel restricted. Pricing algorithms often consider the cost of goods sold, competitive landscape, and perceived value to determine the optimal price differential between levels.

## User Reactions and Migration

Changes to tiered pricing structures often trigger significant user reaction, particularly when existing plans are altered or discontinued. In the context of Evernote’s pricing model updates, users frequently express concern over reduced functionality in lower tiers or increased costs for essential features. Migration guidance becomes critical during these transitions, requiring clear communication about data portability, feature availability, and timeline for changes. Effective management of these migrations involves providing trial periods, grandfathering existing rates for loyal customers, and offering transparent comparison tools to help users choose appropriate new plans.
