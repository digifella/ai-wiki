# Biosphere 2

Biosphere 2 is a large-scale Earth science research facility located in Oracle, Arizona. Constructed during the 1980s and completed in 1991, the complex spans approximately 3.15 acres and consists of connected domes and enclosed glass structures designed to simulate Earth's ecosystems. The facility contains distinct biomes, including a rainforest, ocean, marsh, savanna, and desert, alongside agricultural zones and human habitat areas. It was originally built as a closed ecological system to investigate the interactions between natural and human-made environments.

The project gained international attention during its first two manned missions, which lasted from 1991 to 1993 and 1994 to 1997. These experiments aimed to test the viability of closed ecological systems for potential space colonization and to study the dynamics of Earth's biosphere. The missions revealed significant challenges in maintaining atmospheric balance and food production, leading to critical scientific insights regarding the complexity of life support systems.

Following the initial missions, the facility underwent extensive renovation and was reopened in 2007. It is now operated by the University of Arizona and serves as a laboratory for global change research. Scientists use the site to study climate change, ecosystem resilience, and the impacts of human activity on the environment, continuing its legacy as a unique platform for understanding planetary systems.
