# Decluttering

Decluttering is the process of removing unnecessary items from a space to create a more organized and functional environment. While initial decluttering projects can significantly improve a space, the primary challenge for most people is maintaining that order over time. Without systematic approaches, spaces naturally tend to accumulate clutter again, undoing previous efforts and creating a cycle of repeated reorganization.

## Preventing Clutter Recurrence

Effective decluttering requires both initial action and ongoing habits. Rather than relying solely on periodic major cleanup efforts, this approach emphasizes the [[concepts/preventive-care|prevention]] of clutter recurrence through immediate behavioral [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]]. A key strategy within this framework is the application of [[entities/one-minute-rule|Gretchen Rubin's One-Minute Rule]], which suggests that if a task takes less than one minute to complete, it should be done immediately.

By integrating this rule into [[concepts/habits|daily routines]], individuals can address small tasks—such as putting away mail, hanging up coats, or washing a single dish—before they accumulate into larger messes. This method shifts the focus from reactive, large-scale organizing to proactive, micro-habits that sustain order. The goal is to reduce the [[concepts/cognitive-load|cognitive load]] of maintenance by preventing items from ever reaching a state where they require significant effort to manage.
