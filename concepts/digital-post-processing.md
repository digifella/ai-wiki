# Digital Post Processing

Digital post processing refers to the manipulation and enhancement of [[concepts/digital-images|digital images]] or media files after their initial capture or creation. Rather than relying solely on in-[[concepts/camera-settings|camera settings]] or real-time parameters, post processing applies technical [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] through software tools to improve, correct, or creatively alter the final output. This workflow has become standard practice across photography, videography, [[concepts/design|graphic design]], and digital art, serving as a bridge between raw source material and finished product.

## Common Applications

Post processing encompasses a wide range of adjustments. Color correction addresses issues like white balance, [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], and [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] to achieve accurate or intentional color reproduction. Retouching removes unwanted elements such as dust, blemishes, or distracting backgrounds. Sharpening and noise reduction enhance [[concepts/clarity-slider|clarity]] and reduce digital artifacts. Advanced techniques include [[concepts/digital-compositing|compositing]], where multiple images are combined, and [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|color grading]], which establishes a consistent aesthetic or mood across a project through deliberate [[concepts/color-palette-control|color palette control]].

## Tools and Workflow

Professional post processing typically uses dedicated software such as [[entities/adobe-lightroom|Adobe Lightroom]], [[concepts/adobe-photoshop|Photoshop]], DaVinci Resolve, or [[concepts/specialized-tools|specialized tools]] depending on the [[entities/medium|medium]]. The workflow generally follows a logical sequence: organizing and reviewing source files, applying non-destructive [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] when possible, performing localized corrections, and finalizing output for the intended [[entities/medium|medium]]. Many workflows employ [[concepts/non-destructive-editing|non-destructive editing]] techniques that preserve the original file while [[concepts/storing|storing]] adjustment [[concepts/instructions|instructions]] separately, allowing flexibility for future revisions.

Post processing has evolved from a specialized practice to an expected stage of digital media production, with industry standards and [[concepts/best-practices|best practices]] varying by discipline and final output requirements.
