# Doneness Control

Doneness [[concepts/power|control]] in steak cooking refers to techniques that manage internal temperature and [[concepts/texture|texture]] while developing a flavorful crust. A common challenge in achieving [[entities/medium|medium]]-rare or medium steak is the formation of a gray band—an overcooked layer of meat between the crust and the desired pink interior. This occurs when heat penetrates too deeply during [[concepts/pan-searing|searing]], particularly with traditional high-heat methods applied for extended periods.

## Avoiding the Gray Band

The gray band forms through prolonged [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] to moderate temperatures (140–150°F) that denature muscle proteins without reaching the [[concepts/maillard-reaction|Maillard reaction]] threshold. To minimize this zone, cooks can use reverse-sear techniques: cooking the steak slowly [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] low oven temperatures until it reaches target internal temperature, then searing briefly in a very hot pan. This approach allows precise control over the interior while concentrating the crust-building step into a short, intense finish.

## Enhancing Crust Development

A quality crust (the flavorful browned exterior) requires both heat and dry meat surface. Pat steaks thoroughly dry before cooking and avoid moving them excessively during searing, which prevents proper browning. Adequate resting time after cooking—at least five minutes—allows carryover cooking to stabilize and lets juices redistribute, further improving texture and flavor throughout the finished steak.
