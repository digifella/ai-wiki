# Rail Travel

Rail travel in Australia offers an alternative to road and air transport across the continent's vast distances. Passenger train services operate on both regional and long-distance routes, connecting major cities and remote areas. The rail network includes urban commuter systems in capital cities, regional services linking towns and regional centers, and iconic long-distance journeys traversing multiple states.

## Long-Distance Routes

Australia's long-distance rail services include the Indian Pacific connecting Sydney to Perth, the Ghan [[concepts/running|running]] from Adelaide to Darwin through central Australia, and the Queensland Rail's Kuranda Scenic Railway. These services typically operate on a scheduled basis, ranging from overnight journeys between major cities to multi-day expeditions. Train travel on these routes appeals to tourists and travelers seeking leisurely alternatives to flying, with many services offering accommodation and dining facilities onboard.

## Commuter and Regional Services

Major Australian cities operate commuter rail networks providing daily transport for residents. Regional rail services connect smaller towns and rural areas to larger population centers, though coverage and frequency vary considerably by state. Some regional lines have been reduced or withdrawn over recent decades, while others continue to serve essential transport functions.
