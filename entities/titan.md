# Titan

**[[concepts/oceangate-titan|Titan]]** was a deep-sea submersible operated by OceanGate that imploded during a dive to the wreck of the RMS Titanic on June 18, 2023, resulting in the deaths of all five occupants. The vessel utilized a novel hull design combining carbon fiber and titanium, which became the subject of intense scrutiny regarding its safety and [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] validity.

## Incident and Investigation

The implosion occurred at a depth of approximately 3,800 meters. Post-incident investigations and analyses have shifted focus from the inherent properties of the materials to the procedural failures in the vessel's development.

- **[[concepts/engineering-process|Engineering Process]] Failures**: Recent conclusions indicate the primary cause was an **inadequate engineering process** rather than a failure of the carbon fiber material itself. The design lacked rigorous [[concepts/document-review|peer review]] and standard certification protocols typical for deep-sea vessels.
- **Manufacturing Deficiencies**: Specific manufacturing flaws were identified in the hull construction, contributing to structural instability under extreme pressure.
- **Source Analysis**: Detailed breakdowns of these failures are documented in [[lab-notes/2026-07-09-OceanGate-Titan-Implosion-Inadequate-Engineering-and-Man|OceanGate Titan Implosion: Inadequate Engineering and Manufacturing Failures]].

## Technical Specifications

- **Hull**: Cylindrical carbon fiber composite with titanium end caps.
- **Propulsion**: Electric thrusters.
- **Depth Rating**: Claimed operational depth exceeded 4,000 meters, though safety margins were questioned.
