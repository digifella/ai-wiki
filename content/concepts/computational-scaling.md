# Computational Scaling

Computational [[concepts/scaling|scaling]] refers to the relationship between [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]], [[concepts/code-size|model size]], and [[concepts/training|training]] outcomes in [[concepts/machine-learning-systems|machine learning systems]]. The concept examines how the amount of processing [[concepts/power|power]], [[concepts/memory|memory]], and data available affects the ability to train and deploy increasingly complex [[concepts/models|models]] like [[concepts/transformers|transformers]]. Understanding these [[concepts/scaling-laws|scaling laws]] is fundamental to predicting performance improvements and estimating resource requirements for [[concepts/ai-development|AI development]].

## Historical Context and Constraints

The thought experiment of [[concepts/running|running]] [[concepts/transformer-training|transformer training]] on historical [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] like a [[entities/pdp-1144|1979 PDP-11]] computer illustrates the dramatic evolution of computational capacity in the decades since. The PDP-11, a minicomputer from the late 1970s with kilobytes of memory and processing speeds measured in kilohertz, provides a striking [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] to modern [[concepts/gpu-clusters|GPU clusters]] with terabytes of memory and petaflop performance. This comparison [[concepts/highlights|highlights]] how contemporary achievements in deep [[concepts/learning|learning]] depend fundamentally on access to vastly greater [[concepts/compute|computational resources]].

## Implications for AI Development

Computational scaling has practical significance for estimating training timelines, [[concepts/hardware-requirements|hardware requirements]], and the feasibility of different approaches to AI development. As models grow larger and datasets expand, the computational demands scale in ways that may be sublinear, linear, or superlinear depending on the specific [[concepts/architecture|architecture]] and training methodology. These scaling [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] inform decisions about resource allocation and help establish realistic expectations for what can be achieved with available infrastructure.
