# Long-range strike campaign

A strategic military doctrine involving the systematic targeting of deep-backend [[concepts/infrastructure|infrastructure]], logistics nodes, and economic assets within adversary territory to degrade operational capacity and war-sustaining capabilities.

## Strategic Objectives
- Disruption of supply chains and troop movements.
- Degradation of industrial and economic output.
- Psychological impact on civilian and military morale.
- Forced diversion of air defense resources from the front lines.

## Key Domains of Engagement
- **Energy Infrastructure:** Power plants, refineries, and grid substations.
- **Transport Networks:** Rail hubs, bridges, and fuel depots.
- **Logistics & E-commerce:** Warehousing facilities and distribution centers supporting military procurement.

## Recent Developments: The Warehouse War
In August 2026, the campaign expanded significantly into the realm of civilian logistics, specifically targeting the supply chains of major Russian e-commerce platforms.

- **Targeting of Wildberries Logistics:** [[entities/ukraine|Ukraine]] initiated a focused strategy against Wildberries, [[entities/russia|Russia]]'s largest online retailer, which serves as a critical logistical conduit for military supplies and civilian goods.
- **Operational Shift:** Strikes moved beyond traditional military depots to hit "warehouse" nodes, aiming to disrupt the "last mile" and bulk distribution networks that sustain both the Russian economy and military logistics.
- **Economic Impact:** The campaign aims to increase the cost of logistics for Russian forces by destabilizing the commercial supply chains they increasingly rely upon.
- **Russian Response:** Analysis indicates a coordinated Russian response aimed at hardening these civilian nodes and diversifying supply routes to mitigate the impact of Ukrainian long-range strikes.
