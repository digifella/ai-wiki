# 3D Editing

3D editing is the digital practice of creating, modifying, and refining three-dimensional models and assets for use in various media and applications. This process involves using specialized software to construct geometry, apply materials and textures, and position objects in virtual three-dimensional space. 3D editors serve as fundamental tools in industries ranging from visual effects and animation to product design, architecture, and game development.

## Core Functions

The primary capabilities of 3D editing software include modeling (building geometric forms), texturing (applying surface properties and colors), rigging (creating digital skeletons for animation), and rendering (converting 3D data into final images or video). Artists and designers use these tools to build everything from architectural visualizations and character models to environmental assets and product prototypes. The workflow typically involves iterative refinement, where models are continuously adjusted for accuracy, aesthetics, or technical requirements.

## Applications and Context

3D editing has become integral to modern creative production. In film and television, it enables visual effects and computer-generated imagery. In gaming, it produces player characters, environments, and interactive objects. Beyond entertainment, 3D editing supports industrial design, medical visualization, scientific research, and virtual reality experiences. The accessibility of both professional-grade and free or open-source 3D software has democratized the field, allowing individuals and small teams to produce work previously requiring large studio resources.
