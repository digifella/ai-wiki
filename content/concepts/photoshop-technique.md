# Photoshop Technique

[[concepts/blur-correction|Focus stacking]] is a photographic technique used primarily in [[concepts/landscape-photography|landscape photography]] to achieve greater depth of field and overall [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]] across an image. Rather than relying on a single [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], the photographer takes multiple [[concepts/images|images]] with different focus points—typically focusing on the foreground, middle ground, and background separately. These images are then combined in post-processing [[concepts/software|software]] like [[concepts/photoshop|Photoshop]] to create a final composite where all areas appear acceptably sharp.

## The Process

The technique begins in-camera by capturing a series of photographs without moving the camera or changing any settings except the focus point. Each image focuses on a different plane within the scene. In post-processing, these images are loaded as layers in Photoshop, where they are aligned and blended together. [[concepts/layer-masks|Layer masks]] and blending modes are used to selectively combine the sharpest portions of each image, resulting in a single photograph with enhanced sharpness throughout the composition.

## Practical Applications

[[concepts/focus-stacking|Focus stacking]] addresses a fundamental limitation of traditional landscape photography: the inability to maintain sharp focus from foreground to background using standard aperture and [[concepts/focal-length|focal length]] settings alone. This is particularly valuable when photographing scenes with prominent foreground elements, such as rocks or flowers, while also keeping distant mountains or horizons in focus. The technique has become increasingly common among landscape photographers seeking to maximize technical image quality.
