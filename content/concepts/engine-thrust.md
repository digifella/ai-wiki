# Engine Thrust

Engine thrust is the force produced by an engine through the expulsion of combusted gases or other propellant, measured typically in newtons or pounds-force. In rocketry, thrust is generated by the rapid combustion of fuel and oxidizer in the engine's combustion chamber, with the resulting high-pressure gases expelled through a nozzle to produce directional force. The magnitude and duration of thrust generation are critical [[concepts/parameters|parameters]] determining a vehicle's acceleration, trajectory, and payload capacity.

## First-Stage Operation

During the first stage of rocket flight, engines operate [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] or near maximum thrust to overcome the vehicle's weight and atmospheric drag, accelerating the rocket from the launch pad. First-stage engines are typically designed for high thrust [[concepts/output|output]] and must function reliably across a [[concepts/range|range]] of atmospheric conditions and vehicle configurations. The thrust profile—how thrust varies over the burn duration—affects the vehicle's acceleration envelope and structural loads on the airframe and payload.

## Staging and Fairing Events

As a rocket progresses through flight phases, thrust levels and engine configurations change during stage separation events. Payload fairings, which protect the cargo during atmospheric ascent, are jettisoned once dynamic pressure becomes negligible, typically after first-stage burnout or during early second-stage flight. The timing of fairing separation is coordinated with engine thrust profiles to ensure structural [[concepts/integrity|integrity]] and proper staging sequencing.
