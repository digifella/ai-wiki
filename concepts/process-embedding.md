# Process Embedding

Process Embedding refers to the integration of formal [[concepts/governance|governance]] procedures and oversight [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] into [[concepts/business-workflows|organizational workflows]] to manage the [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] and use of emerging technologies. In the context of AI in [[concepts/health|healthcare]], it addresses a significant gap between the rapid deployment of new [[entities/ai-tools|AI tools]] and the availability of established processes to ensure their safe and responsible implementation.

## The Healthcare Governance Gap

[[concepts/health-sector|Healthcare organizations]] are increasingly adopting [[concepts/ai-technologies|AI technologies]], yet many lack adequate procedural frameworks to oversee their deployment. Existing organizational governance structures often predate these technologies and were not designed to address the specific risks and [[concepts/ethical-considerations|ethical considerations]] they present. This creates a situation where clinical and administrative [[entities/employees|staff]] may be using [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] without corresponding institutional processes for monitoring effectiveness, managing risks, or ensuring [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] with [[concepts/product-safety|safety standards]].

## Relationship to Governance Frameworks

While high-level [[concepts/ai-ethics-principles|AI ethics principles]] and frameworks have been developed to guide [[concepts/responsible-ai-use|responsible AI use]], these remain largely abstract. Process Embedding bridges the gap between principle and practice by translating ethical guidelines into concrete operational procedures—such as validation protocols, audit mechanisms, incident reporting systems, and regular review cycles—that can be systematically implemented and maintained within healthcare organizations.

The challenge of Process Embedding is essentially one of [[concepts/organizational-structure|organizational design]]: determining what specific processes need to exist, who should be responsible for them, and how they should integrate with existing clinical and administrative workflows to effectively oversee AI use without creating excessive [[concepts/friction|friction]] or delays in care delivery.
