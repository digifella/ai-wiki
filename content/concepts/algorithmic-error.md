# Algorithmic Error

Algorithmic error refers to failures in automated decision-making systems that produce harmful, unjust, or unintended outcomes. These errors arise from various sources: flawed logic in system [[concepts/design|design]], poor [[concepts/data-conceptsintegrityintegrity|data quality]], inadequate [[concepts/testing|testing]] before [[concepts/deployment|deployment]], or the inappropriate application of algorithms in contexts requiring human judgment and discretion. When algorithmic systems operate [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] scale—particularly in government, [[concepts/health|healthcare]], or financial services—errors can affect thousands of people simultaneously, amplifying the consequences of mistakes that might otherwise affect individuals.

## The Robodebt Case

The Australian [[concepts/government-automation-failure|Robodebt scheme]] exemplifies the serious consequences of algorithmic error in government administration. Between 2015 and 2019, the Department of Human Services used an automated system to identify and recover welfare overpayments from citizens. The algorithm compared income data from the Australian Taxation Office with welfare records, but it operated on flawed assumptions about how income was distributed across financial years. This technical flaw, combined with inadequate human review and notification processes, resulted in incorrect debt notices sent to over 400,000 people. The resulting financial and psychological harm contributed to multiple suicides among affected recipients. The scheme was eventually ruled unlawful, leading to compensation payments and a public inquiry.

## Broader Implications

The Robodebt case demonstrates how algorithmic errors at scale require [[concepts/accountability|accountability]] mechanisms beyond technical fixes. It highlighted the importance of human oversight, transparent decision-making processes, and adequate appeal mechanisms when algorithms determine outcomes that significantly affect people's lives. The incident prompted broader scrutiny of [[concepts/automated-government-systems|automated government systems]] and reinforced the principle that [[concepts/algorithmic-decision-making|algorithmic decision-making]] should not entirely replace human judgment in matters with serious consequences for individual welfare.
