# Chemical Fertilizer Ban

A **Chemical [[concepts/fertilizer-ban|Fertilizer Ban]]** is a regulatory prohibition on the production, import, or use of synthetic nitrogen-phosphorus-potassium (NPK) fertilizers. While often proposed under frameworks of Sustainable [[concepts/agriculture|Agriculture]] and [[concepts/organic-farming]], comprehensive bans are rare due to potential risks to Food [[concepts/security|Security]] and Economic Stability.

## Key Characteristics
- **Policy Scope:** Total prohibition vs. phased reduction or subsidy shifts.
- **Motivations:** Environmental [[concepts/secure|protection]] (reducing eutrophication), [[concepts/population-health|public health]] concerns, reducing trade deficits on imports, and ideological shifts toward traditional/agroecological methods.
- **Risks:** Immediate drops in crop yields, increased food prices, potential famine in agrarian economies, and disruption of global [[concepts/supply-chains|supply chains]].

## Case Study: Sri Lanka (2021–2022)
The most prominent instance of a nationwide chemical fertilizer ban occurred in [[entities/sri-lanka]] in April 2021. This policy serves as a primary case study for the socio-economic impacts of abrupt agricultural restrictions.

- **Policy Implementation:** The government mandated an immediate shift from hybrid seeds and chemical fertilizers to organic alternatives, citing [[concepts/health|health]] benefits and sustainability goals.
- **[[concepts/economic-impact|Economic Impact]]:** The ban contributed significantly to the 2022 Sri Lankan economic [[concepts/crisis|crisis]], exacerbating inflation and currency devaluation by reducing agricultural exports (particularly tea and rice) and increasing import costs for food staples.
