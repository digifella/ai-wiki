# Zippers

A zipper is a fastening device consisting of two parallel strips of material fitted with [[concepts/interlocking-teeth|interlocking teeth]] or coils that are joined or separated by a slider mechanism. The slider pulls the teeth together to close the zipper or pulls them apart to open it. This mechanical design allows for quick, reliable fastening of garments, bags, and other items.

## History and Development

The zipper evolved from earlier fastening concepts, including the "[[entities/clasp-locker|Clasp Locker]]" design. Early versions were unreliable and prone to jamming, limiting their [[concepts/adoption|adoption]]. Over time, improvements to the tooth design and slider mechanism made [[concepts/buttons|zippers]] more practical. By the mid-20th century, zippers had become a standard fastening method across clothing and accessories.

## Manufacturing and Market Dominance

YKK, a Japanese company founded in 1934, became the world's largest zipper manufacturer and currently produces the majority of zippers used globally. The company's dominance stems from innovations in manufacturing efficiency and [[concepts/quality-control|quality control]]. YKK's name or initials appear on many zippers as a mark of manufacture, which is why the letters "YKK" are commonly visible on everyday items like jackets and bags.
