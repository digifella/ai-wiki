# High Oxygen Period

The High Oxygen Period refers to geological intervals characterized by significantly elevated atmospheric oxygen concentrations above modern levels of approximately 21 percent. The most notable example occurred during the Late Paleozoic Era, particularly the Carboniferous period (approximately 359 to 299 million years ago), when atmospheric oxygen levels reached some of the highest concentrations in Earth's history, potentially exceeding 30 percent. These elevated oxygen levels were sustained by extensive coal forest ecosystems and marine productivity, which sequestered vast quantities of carbon and released oxygen through photosynthesis.

## Geological Significance

The elevated oxygen availability during these periods fundamentally altered Earth's geochemical cycles and influenced the formation of significant geological deposits. The abundant oxygen facilitated the oxidation of exposed rock surfaces, contributing to the formation of iron oxide minerals that characterize red-colored geological formations. The Red Centre of Australia, with its distinctive red rocks and soils, reflects in part the oxidizing conditions that prevailed during and after these high oxygen intervals. Additionally, the High Oxygen Period coincided with the formation of extensive coal deposits, as the oxygen-rich atmosphere supported the growth of dense vegetation that was subsequently buried and transformed into coal through geological processes.

## Biological and Atmospheric Effects

The elevated oxygen concentrations enabled the evolution of larger terrestrial and aquatic organisms, as the increased oxygen availability supported higher metabolic rates. This period witnessed the diversification of large insects and amphibians, some reaching sizes substantially larger than their modern descendants. The transition out of High Oxygen Periods occurred gradually as oxygen-consuming weathering processes and shifts in biological productivity altered the atmospheric composition toward levels more similar to those of the present day.
