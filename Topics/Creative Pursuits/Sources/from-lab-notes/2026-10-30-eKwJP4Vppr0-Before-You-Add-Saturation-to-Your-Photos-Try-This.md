## Before You Add Saturation to Your Photos, Try This
**Clip title:** Before You Add Saturation to Your Photos, Try This
**Author / channel:** Mark Denney
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eKwJP4Vppr0

### Summary
The video features the host sharing how to make autumn photography photos pop and look more natural by utilizing three specific Lightroom tools that avoid global saturation sliders: **Luminance**, **Hue**, and **Calibration**.

The speaker starts by demonstrating the **Luminance** tool, which involves increasing the brightness of yellow and decreasing the brightness of orange and green. This is meant to provide color separation, adding depth and making the photos feel a bit more three-dimensional. The host mentions that increasing the saturation for all colors in a photo can make it look busy, so he suggests desaturating the blue and green colors in the sky and trees to simplify the color palette without increasing saturation. Next, the speaker demonstrates the **Hue** tool, which allows you to shift the colors of yellows, oranges, and greens either towards each other or away from each other to make them stand out more. Finally, the speaker demonstrates the **Calibration** tool, which he claims is his favorite and does the most heavy lifting. The speaker demonstrates how to use the blue, green, and red primary sliders to adjust the color and saturation of the photo's pixels.

In conclusion, the speaker claims these three Lightroom tools—Luminance, Hue, and Calibration—help make fall photographs look more natural and vibrant without relying on the global saturation slider.
