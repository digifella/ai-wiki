# Limited Insight

Limited Insight describes a fundamental constraint in the [[concepts/training|training]] and [[concepts/deployment|deployment]] of [[concepts/artificial-intelligence-models|machine learning models]] and [[concepts/expert-systems|expert systems]]. The concept posits that the methods used to train these systems inherently establish specific boundaries on what they can understand and accomplish, regardless of subsequent optimization efforts. This framework builds on theoretical work by Hubert and Stuart [[entities/dreyfus|Dreyfus]], who argued that expertise cannot be fully formalized through rule-based systems.

The Dreyfus perspective suggests that certain forms of [[concepts/human-understanding|human understanding]]—particularly intuition and contextual judgment developed through experience—resist [[concepts/encoding|encoding]] into the structured training processes that AI systems rely upon. Consequently, [[concepts/models|models]] trained through conventional methods may achieve strong performance on well-defined tasks while remaining systematically limited in areas requiring genuine insight or novel application of knowledge.

This thesis has faced significant criticism from researchers including [[entities/gobel|Gobel]] and [[entities/chassy|Chassy]], who argue that the Dreyfus framework overstates the limitations of artificial systems and underestimates the capacity of training approaches to develop more flexible [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]]. The debate remains unresolved within the field, with practical advances in machine [[concepts/learning|learning]] sometimes appearing to vindicate empirical [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]] approaches while other problem domains continue to expose gaps that training alone cannot bridge.
