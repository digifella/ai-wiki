# Autofocus Mode

Autofocus mode refers to the camera's ability to automatically adjust lens focus to achieve sharp images of a subject. In Fujifilm cameras, autofocus functionality is a core feature that determines how the camera locks focus on subjects and maintains [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]] during shooting. The autofocus system works by detecting [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] in the scene and adjusting the lens elements accordingly to bring the subject into focus. This automatic adjustment eliminates the need for [[concepts/manual-focus|manual focus]] adjustment in most shooting situations.

## Autofocus Lock

Autofocus lock is a feature that allows photographers to lock focus on a specific subject before recomposing the frame. In Fujifilm cameras, this is typically achieved by half-pressing the shutter button or using a dedicated autofocus lock button. Once focus is locked, the camera maintains that focus distance even if the subject moves or the composition changes, provided the photographer continues to hold the lock. This technique is useful for precise framing and ensuring critical focus on off-center subjects.

## Operating Modes

Fujifilm cameras offer various autofocus modes to suit different shooting scenarios. Single autofocus mode locks focus once and holds it until the shutter is released, making it suitable for stationary subjects. Continuous autofocus mode tracks moving subjects by continuously adjusting focus as long as the shutter button remains half-pressed. Some models also offer zone-based autofocus selection, allowing photographers to choose specific areas of the frame where focus should be prioritized, rather than relying solely on center-point focusing.
