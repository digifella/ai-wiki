# Haloing

Haloing is a post-processing technique used in Adobe Lightroom to refine sky masks when foreground elements, such as tree branches, obstruct the horizon line. It addresses a specific limitation of Lightroom’s automated masking tools, which often struggle to distinguish between thin, complex foreground shapes and the sky behind them. Without manual intervention, these automated selections can produce unnatural halos or hard edges around branches, or fail to include necessary sky pixels.

The process involves manually adjusting the mask edges using the brush or range selector tools to ensure precise separation between the sky and the foreground. By carefully painting over the affected areas or refining the selection range, editors can eliminate the visible artifacts that result from the software's initial automated guess. This manual correction restores natural transitions and prevents the sky adjustments from bleeding into or missing parts of the foreground objects.

This technique is particularly relevant for landscape photography where high-contrast edges between dark foliage and bright skies challenge the algorithmic detection capabilities of the mask. It serves as a necessary step in the workflow to achieve professional-grade results, ensuring that edits to the sky do not compromise the integrity of the foreground composition.
