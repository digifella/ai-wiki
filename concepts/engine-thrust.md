# Engine Thrust

[[concepts/engine|Engine]] thrust is the force produced by an engine through the expulsion of combusted gases or other propellant, measured in newtons (N) or pounds-force (lbf). In rocketry, thrust arises from the rapid combustion of fuel and oxidizer within the engine's combustion chamber. The resulting high-[[concepts/pressure|pressure]] gases are expelled through a converging-diverging nozzle, which accelerates the exhaust and produces the reaction force that propels the vehicle, following Newton's third law of motion.

## Thrust in Rocket Operations

During first-stage operation, engines operate at maximum capacity to overcome [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]] and atmospheric drag, providing the necessary acceleration to [[entities/lift|lift]] the vehicle off the launch pad and reach orbital [[concepts/velocity|velocity]]. The magnitude of thrust is critical during this [[concepts/phase|phase]], as it must exceed the total weight of the rocket to achieve positive acceleration. As the vehicle ascends and atmospheric [[concepts/density|density]] decreases, the efficiency of the thrust vectoring systems adjusts to maintain stability and control the flight path.

Thrust management is also pivotal during separation events, such as [[concepts/payload-fairing-separation|payload fairing separation]]. While the primary engines continue to provide propulsion, the sudden [[concepts/deployment|release]] of the fairing reduces the vehicle's mass and aerodynamic drag. This change in mass-to-thrust ratio can cause a transient increase in acceleration, requiring precise monitoring to ensure [[concepts/payload-structure|structural integrity]] and optimal trajectory correction for the upper stages.
