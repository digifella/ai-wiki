# Chinese Medicine

Chinese Medicine is a traditional [[concepts/health|health]] practice system that developed over more than two thousand years in East Asia, primarily in China. It represents a comprehensive approach to understanding health, disease, and treatment that differs fundamentally from Western biomedical models. Rather than focusing exclusively on isolated symptoms or pathogens, Chinese Medicine views the body as an integrated system in dynamic balance with its environment.

## Core Theoretical Frameworks

The theoretical foundation of Chinese Medicine rests on several interconnected concepts. [[concepts/five-elements-theory|Five Elements Theory]]—wood, fire, earth, metal, and water—provides a framework for categorizing organs, emotions, flavors, seasons, and other aspects of health and [[entities/nature|nature]]. Another central concept is Qi (pronounced "chee"), understood as vital life force or functional energy that circulates through the body via pathways called meridians. Yin and Yang represent complementary forces whose balance is essential to wellbeing.

## Clinical Practices

Chinese Medicine employs multiple treatment modalities to restore health. Acupuncture involves inserting thin needles at specific points to influence Qi flow. Herbal medicine uses plant-based compounds, often in complex formulas tailored to individual patients. Other common practices include moxibustion (heat therapy), cupping, massage (tui na), and dietary therapy. Practitioners conduct diagnosis through methods including pulse-taking and tongue examination.

## Contemporary Status

Chinese Medicine remains widely practiced throughout Asia and has gained increasing use in other regions. It is recognized by the World Health [[concepts/organization|Organization]] and integrated into some national healthcare systems. While some Chinese Medicine treatments have been studied through modern clinical research methods with varying results, the system as a whole operates from epistemological premises distinct from evidence-based Western medicine, creating ongoing discussions about integration and validation.
