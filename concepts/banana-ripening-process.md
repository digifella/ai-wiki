# Banana Ripening Process

Bananas ripen through a natural biochemical process driven by ethylene, a plant hormone produced by the fruit itself. As ripening progresses, the starch contained within the banana gradually converts to [[concepts/sugar|sugar]], increasing sweetness and improving digestibility. Simultaneously, chlorophyll in the skin breaks down, revealing the yellow pigments beneath. This transformation typically takes 5–7 days at room temperature, though the exact timeline depends on environmental conditions and the banana's maturity level at harvest.

## Environmental Factors

Temperature plays the most significant role in the rate of ripening. Higher temperatures accelerate the production of ethylene and the subsequent chemical reactions, while cooler environments slow the process down. Refrigeration can halt ripening temporarily but may cause the peel to turn brown due to cold damage, even if the fruit inside remains firm. [[concepts/exposure|Exposure]] to direct sunlight can also unevenly affect the ripening [[concepts/speed|speed]] and final coloration.

The ripening stage is visually distinct, moving from green to yellow with potential brown spots indicating peak sweetness or over-ripeness.
