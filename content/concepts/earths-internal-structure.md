# Earth's Internal Structure

Earth's internal [[concepts/structure|structure]] consists of distinct layers defined by their composition and [[concepts/material-properties|physical properties]]: the crust, mantle, outer core, and [[concepts/inner-core|inner core]]. The inner core, located [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] Earth's center, is a solid sphere composed primarily of iron and nickel, subjected to extreme pressure and temperature conditions. Understanding the properties and behavior of the inner core is crucial for comprehending Earth's magnetic field generation, heat dynamics, and overall planetary evolution.

## Inner Core Seismic Anomalies

Seismic waves generated by [[concepts/earthquakes|earthquakes]] provide our primary method for studying Earth's interior. Observations of these seismic waves passing through the inner core have revealed unexpected anomalies that deviate from predictions based on standard [[concepts/models|models]] of iron-nickel behavior under extreme conditions. These anomalies include unusual wave velocity variations and attenuation patterns that cannot be fully explained by conventional [[concepts/physics|physics]] of solid materials at high pressure and temperature.

## New State of Matter Hypothesis

Recent research suggests that the extreme conditions within the inner core—pressures exceeding 360 gigapascals and temperatures near 5,200 Kelvin—may create a previously unknown state of matter distinct from solid, liquid, or gas phases. This hypothetical state could explain the [[concepts/seismic-data|seismic anomalies]] observed in the inner core and may involve exotic behavior of iron nuclei under such extreme conditions. Further investigation through advanced seismology and high-pressure physics experiments continues to refine our understanding of this phenomenon.
