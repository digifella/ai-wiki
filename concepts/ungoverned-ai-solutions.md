# Ungoverned AI Solutions

[[concepts/co-pilot-use|Ungoverned AI solutions]] refer to [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems and frameworks deployed within organizations without adequate oversight, control [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]], or [[concepts/governance|governance]] structures. These systems often emerge from departmental initiatives, experimental projects, or the [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of powerful [[concepts/agentic-frameworks|agentic frameworks]] that operate outside formal IT and [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] processes. The lack of centralized management means these solutions develop without [[concepts/security|security]] reviews, data handling protocols, or alignment with organizational [[concepts/policies|policies]].

## Scope and Manifestations

Ungoverned AI can take multiple forms, from individual use of commercial AI assistants and co-pilots to departmentally-developed [[concepts/ai-bots|chatbots]] and agent-based workflows. [[concepts/shadow-ai|Shadow AI]]—systems deployed and used without IT department knowledge—represents a particular concern. Agentic frameworks, which enable [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] to take autonomous actions across organizational systems and data, present elevated risks when deployed without governance due to their ability to operate at scale and access sensitive resources.

## Risks and Implications

The absence of governance creates several interconnected risks. Data security vulnerabilities emerge when ungoverned systems process or store sensitive information without proper safeguards. Compliance [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] increases when AI systems operate in regulated industries without audit trails or control measures. Operational risks include uncontrolled propagation of [[concepts/ai-tools|AI tools]], inconsistent quality and [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]], and potential conflicts with existing systems. Additionally, ungoverned AI can circumvent established security policies and create dependencies on external vendors or services without organizational knowledge or contractual protections.

## Organizational Response

Effective management of AI adoption requires balancing [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] with governance through formal frameworks that cover system [[concepts/authorization|authorization]], data handling, security standards, and compliance requirements. This approach enables organizations to capture the benefits of AI while maintaining visibility and control over systems operating within their infrastructure and data environments.
