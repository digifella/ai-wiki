# Clip Title Five Interview Questions GenAI Can't Answer

Generative [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] demonstrate impressive capabilities in [[concepts/thematic-analysis|pattern recognition]], [[concepts/information-synthesis|information synthesis]], and language generation, yet struggle with interview questions that require genuine [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]], contextual judgment, or authentic [[concepts/personal-experience|personal experience]]. Interview questions designed to expose these limitations serve as diagnostic tools for hiring managers who need to distinguish between AI-generated responses and evidence of distinctly [[concepts/human-performance|human capabilities]]. Such questions typically require candidates to navigate [[concepts/ambiguity|ambiguity]], demonstrate learned judgment from experience, or articulate values and [[concepts/decision-making-frameworks|decision-making frameworks]] that reflect genuine professional growth.

## Categories of Revealing Questions

Questions about specific failures and how candidates responded to them expose a critical limitation in generative AI: the inability to authentically describe personal experience or demonstrate genuine reflection on concrete professional setbacks. While AI can generate plausible-sounding failure narratives, it cannot ground responses in actual lived experience or show the subtle markers of authentic [[concepts/learning|learning]] that hiring managers recognize in human candidates.

Questions requiring judgment calls in ambiguous situations—where multiple defensible answers exist and the interviewer seeks to understand the candidate's decision-making process—reveal AI's tendency toward cautious hedging or generic frameworks rather than committed judgment. Human candidates typically articulate specific reasoning rooted in their values and context, whereas generative AI tends to present balanced arguments for competing positions without taking a clear stance.

[[concepts/open-ended-questioning|Open-ended questions]] about long-term career [[concepts/computer-vision|vision]], [[concepts/motivation|motivation]], or what candidates want to learn from a specific role often elicit responses from AI that lack coherence across time or genuine self-[[concepts/conscious-thought|awareness]] about interests. [[concepts/generative-ai|Generative models]] produce statistically likely answers rather than responses that reflect an integrated sense of professional identity and trajectory that experienced interviewers recognize as characteristic of engaged human candidates.
