# Adaptive Reuse

Adaptive reuse is the practice of converting existing buildings or structures to serve new purposes while retaining their original architectural and historical character. Rather than demolishing outdated or underutilized structures, adaptive reuse preserves built heritage and directs resources toward maintaining culturally significant spaces. This approach has become an important strategy in urban conservation, allowing cities to retain their architectural identity while meeting contemporary functional needs.

## Environmental and Economic Benefits

Adaptive reuse projects typically consume fewer resources than new construction, as they retain existing structural systems and building envelopes. This reduces the embodied energy and material waste associated with demolition and rebuilding. Economically, adaptive reuse can make underperforming properties viable again, revitalizing neighborhoods and generating tax revenue. Projects often qualify for heritage preservation incentives and tax credits that offset rehabilitation costs.

## Application in Urban Contexts

Cities worldwide have implemented adaptive reuse to address housing shortages, revitalize commercial districts, and preserve architectural heritage. Warehouses have been converted to residential lofts, churches to performance venues, and industrial facilities to cultural spaces. Successful projects require balancing modern building code requirements and accessibility standards with the need to preserve significant architectural features and historical integrity.
