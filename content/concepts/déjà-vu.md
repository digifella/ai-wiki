# Déjà Vu

Déjà vu is the psychological phenomenon of feeling that one has already experienced a present situation, despite having no actual prior experience of it. The term, French for "already seen," describes a brief but striking sense of familiarity with circumstances that are objectively new. It occurs across cultures and demographics, affecting an estimated 60-80% of people at least once in their lifetime.

## Psychological Explanations

The mechanisms underlying déjà vu remain incompletely understood, though several theories have been proposed. The "hologram [[concepts/theory|theory]]" suggests that [[concepts/memory|memory]] retrieval can activate general [[concepts/contextual-information|contextual information]] without activating specific episodic details, creating a false sense of familiarity. Alternative explanations include brief neurological misfirings where present perceptions are momentarily processed through memory systems, or divided [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] where certain aspects of an experience are registered subconsciously before conscious awareness occurs. Some researchers propose that déjà vu may result from similarities between current and past situations processed too rapidly for conscious comparison.

## Related Phenomena

Déjà vu exists within a spectrum of similar experiences. Déjà vécu ("already lived") refers to a false sense of recognition of an entire experience, while jamais vu ("never seen") describes the opposite—a moment when something familiar suddenly feels entirely strange. Presque vu ("almost seen") involves the sensation of having something on the tip of one's tongue without being able to [[concepts/recall|recall]] it. These phenomena suggest common underlying mechanisms in human memory and perception.
