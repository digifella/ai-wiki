# Anomalous Magnetic Moment

The anomalous magnetic moment is the difference between the measured magnetic moment of a particle and the value predicted by the Dirac equation. For [[concepts/elementary-particles|fundamental particles]] like the electron and [[concepts/muon|muon]], this deviation arises from quantum corrections to their electromagnetic interactions. In [[concepts/quantum-field-theory|quantum field theory]], virtual particle-antiparticle pairs briefly exist and interact with the particle, causing its magnetic moment to differ slightly from classical predictions. The anomalous magnetic moment is conventionally expressed as the dimensionless quantity *a*, defined such that the total magnetic moment equals the Dirac prediction multiplied by (1 + *a*).

## Theoretical Prediction and Measurement

The value of *a* can be calculated from quantum electrodynamics (QED) and the [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|Standard Model]] with increasing precision by including higher-order [[concepts/feynman|Feynman]] [[concepts/diagrams|diagrams]]. For the muon, the theoretical prediction includes contributions from electromagnetic interactions, the weak force, and hadronic effects. Experimental measurements of the muon's anomalous magnetic moment have been performed [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] Fermilab's [[concepts/muon-g-2-experiment|Muon g-2 experiment]] and previously at Brookhaven National Laboratory, achieving remarkable precision. A persistent discrepancy between experimental values and Standard Model predictions has motivated these precision measurements and continues to [[concepts/ambition|drive]] theoretical refinements.

## Significance

The anomalous magnetic moment provides a sensitive test of quantum field theory and the Standard Model. Small deviations between theory and experiment could indicate physics beyond the Standard Model, such as contributions from undiscovered particles or interactions. This makes the muon g-2 experiment one of the most stringent tests of fundamental physics currently available.
