# Foot Strength

Foot strength and the way footwear is secured significantly impact runner [[concepts/health|health]] and injury [[concepts/preventive-care|prevention]]. The lacing method used on [[concepts/running|running]] shoes affects how forces are distributed across the foot during activity, influencing stability and comfort. Two primary lacing techniques—[[concepts/bar-lacing|bar lacing]] and cross-lacing—produce different biomechanical outcomes for the runner.

## Bar Lacing vs Cross-Lacing

Cross-lacing, the most common lacing method, creates diagonal tensions across the foot's midfoot region. Bar lacing, by [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], runs the laces horizontally across the shoe, creating more direct lateral support. Research on runner foot health suggests that bar lacing provides better arch support and more even [[concepts/pressure-distribution|pressure distribution]] across the foot, potentially reducing strain on specific pressure points that cross-lacing concentrates.

The choice of lacing technique can influence proprioception and stability during running. Bar lacing may help maintain better foot alignment by distributing tension more evenly, whereas cross-lacing can create pressure points that may contribute to discomfort or fatigue during extended activity. Runners seeking to optimize foot strength and reduce injury risk may benefit from experimenting with bar lacing as an alternative to traditional cross-lacing methods.
