# Wreck Discovery

The wreck of the RMS Titanic was discovered on September 1, 1985, by a team led by oceanographer Robert Ballard. The expedition took place in the North Atlantic Ocean, approximately 370 miles south-southeast of Newfoundland, at a depth of roughly 12,500 feet. The ship was found lying largely intact on the ocean floor, split into two main sections separated by a debris field spanning several miles.

The mission was officially authorized by the United States Navy under the cover of locating two lost nuclear submarines, the USS Thresher and the USS Scorpion. Ballard’s team utilized advanced deep-sea imaging technology, including the remote operated vehicle Argo and the camera sled Pandora, to survey the seabed. This dual-purpose operation allowed the team to conduct the Titanic search while fulfilling the Navy's classified objective of finding the sunken vessels.

The discovery confirmed the location of the Titanic's final resting place and provided critical data on the ship's condition. The debris field and the separation of the hull sections offered insights into the mechanics of the sinking. The expedition marked a significant milestone in deep-sea exploration and maritime archaeology, shifting the focus from mere location to detailed documentation and preservation of the site.
