# Greenhouse Gases

Greenhouse gases are atmospheric compounds that absorb and re-emit infrared radiation, effectively trapping heat within [[concepts/earths-atmosphere|Earth's atmosphere]]. This natural phenomenon, known as the greenhouse effect, is critical for sustaining life by maintaining surface temperatures that are habitable for biological processes. Without this mechanism, Earth's average temperature would be approximately 60 degrees Fahrenheit colder than current conditions. The primary constituents of this category include water vapor, carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), nitrous oxide (N₂O), and various fluorinated gases.

These gases originate from both natural processes and human activities. Natural sources include [[concepts/volcanic-eruptions|volcanic eruptions]], decomposition in wetlands, and respiration by living organisms. Anthropogenic sources have significantly increased the concentration of these gases in recent centuries, primarily through the [[concepts/burning|burning]] of fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial manufacturing. The accumulation of carbon dioxide and methane from these human activities enhances the natural greenhouse effect, leading to global warming and associated [[concepts/climate-change|climate change]].

The rise in greenhouse gas concentrations has profound implications for air quality and [[concepts/population-health|public health]]. Beyond their role in climate dynamics, many greenhouse gases and their precursors contribute to the formation of ground-level ozone and [[concepts/granular-matter|particulate matter]], which are harmful to human respiratory and cardiovascular systems. Consequently, the management of greenhouse gas emissions is often integrated with broader strategies aimed at improving air quality and mitigating the adverse health impacts associated with polluted environments.
