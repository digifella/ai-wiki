# Tertiary Prevention

Tertiary prevention constitutes the final stage of the disease prevention continuum, applied after a medical condition has been diagnosed and established. While primary prevention aims to stop disease before it occurs and secondary prevention focuses on early detection, tertiary prevention targets individuals who already have a confirmed health issue. The core objective is to manage existing conditions to slow their progression, reduce the severity of symptoms, and prevent further complications or disability. This approach is particularly critical in the management of chronic diseases, where long-term care is required to maintain quality of life and functional independence.

## Clinical Management and Rehabilitation

The practice of tertiary prevention involves a multidisciplinary approach to care, often combining medical treatment with rehabilitative therapies. For chronic conditions such as diabetes, hypertension, or heart disease, this includes strict monitoring of biomarkers, medication adherence, and lifestyle modifications to prevent acute exacerbations. In cases of disability resulting from injury or illness, rehabilitation services focus on restoring physical or cognitive function, enabling patients to adapt to their limitations and participate in daily activities.

## Public Health and Social Support

Beyond individual clinical care, tertiary prevention encompasses broader public health strategies aimed at reducing the societal burden of disease. This includes providing social support networks, vocational training, and accessible healthcare infrastructure to help patients reintegrate into their communities. By addressing the psychosocial aspects of chronic illness, tertiary prevention seeks to minimize the long-term economic and personal impact of disease, ensuring that patients receive comprehensive support throughout the course of their condition.
