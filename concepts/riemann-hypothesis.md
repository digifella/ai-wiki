The **Riemann Hypothesis** is one of the most significant [[concepts/unsolved-problems|unsolved problems]] in [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]], carrying a $1 million prize from the Clay Mathematics Institute for its [[concepts/solution|solution]]. Proposed by German mathematician [[entities/bernhard-riemann|Bernhard Riemann]] in 1859, it revolves around the distribution of [[concepts/prime-numbers|prime numbers]], which are crucial in [[concepts/number-theory|number theory]] as they form the basis of all natural numbers.

### Key Points
- **[[concepts/prime-numbers|Prime Numbers]]:** The fundamental units of arithmetic, seemingly distributed randomly among integers.
- **[[concepts/riemann-zeta-function|Zeta Function]]:** Central to the hypothesis, relates to the [[concepts/distribution-of-primes|distribution of primes]].
- **Riemann Hypothesis:** Suggests that all non-trivial zeros of the [[concepts/zeta-function|zeta function]] have real part 1/2.
