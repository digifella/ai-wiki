# Nigerian Prince Scam

The **Nigerian Prince Scam**, also known as **419 [[concepts/fraud|fraud]]** or **Advance-fee fraud**, is a type of confidence scam where victims are promised a large sum of money in exchange for a small [[concepts/upfront-payment|upfront payment]]. It is the modern [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] of the [[concepts/spanish-prisoner-swindle|Spanish Prisoner swindle]].

## History & Evolution
- **Origins**: The scam traces its [[concepts/evolutionary-lineage|lineage]] to the "Spanish Prisoner" fraud, dating back to the late 18th century.
- **Modern Adaptation**: The [[concepts/storytelling|narrative]] shifted from imprisoned aristocrats to Nigerian officials or princes seeking to move funds out of the country, leveraging geopolitical stereotypes and the rise of [[entities/email|email]] communication.
- **Source Analysis**: Detailed [[concepts/historical-context|historical context]] is provided in [[lab-notes/2026-07-13-Evolution-of-Advance-Fee-Scams-From-Spanish-Prisoner-to|Evolution of Advance Fee Scams: From Spanish Prisoner to Nigerian Prince]].

## Mechanism
1. **Initial [[entities/contact|Contact]]**: Unsolicited message (email, letter, or social media) claiming the sender has access to a large sum of money.
2. **The Hook**: The sender claims they need the victim's help to transfer the funds, often citing legal or bureaucratic obstacles.
3. **The Ask**: The victim is asked to provide personal information and/or pay "fees" (legal, banking, or bribes) to [[concepts/deployment|release]] the funds.
4. **Escalation**: Once the victim pays, new fees are invented. The promised money never exists.

## Key Characteristics
- **Urgency**: Pressure to act quickly to avoid losing the opportunity.
- **Secrecy**: Requests for confidentiality to avoid "interference."
- **Greed Appeal**: Promises of disproportionate returns for minimal effort.
