# Ashfall

**Ashfall** refers to the deposition of volcanic ash and tephra from an eruption plume onto the ground or [[concepts/earths-atmosphere|atmosphere]]. It serves as a critical indicator of eruption intensity, dispersal direction, and potential environmental impact.

## Characteristics
*   **[[concepts/writing|Composition]]:** [[concepts/volcanic-ash|Pyroclastic material]] ranging from fine dust to coarse fragments.
*   **Dispersion:** Driven by wind patterns and eruption column height; can cover vast geographic areas.
*   **Impact:**
    *   **Environmental:** Soil acidification, water contamination, and [[concepts/opacity|light-blocking]] aerosols leading to [[concepts/global-cooling|global cooling]] Volcanic Winter.
    *   **Human/Infrastructure:** Roof collapse, respiratory hazards, aviation disruption, and agricultural loss.

## Historical & Climatic Significance
*   **Climate Forcing:** Large eruptions inject sulfur dioxide into the stratosphere, forming sulfate aerosols that reflect solar radiation and lower global temperatures.
*   **Historical Correlation:**
    *   Recent research suggests a link between specific ashfall events and historical socio-climate crises.
    *   See: [[lab-notes/2026-05-19-2022-Study-Volcanic-Eruptions-Triggering-the-Black-Death|2022 Study: Volcanic Eruptions Triggering the Black Death]] for analysis on how volcanic cooling may have influenced rodent migration and [[concepts/plague|plague]] transmission in medieval [[entities/europe|Europe]].
