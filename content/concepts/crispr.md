# CRISPR

CRISPR is a gene-editing technology that has revolutionized molecular biology since its development in the early 2010s. The system works by using a guide [[concepts/rna|RNA]] to direct the Cas9 protein to specific DNA sequences, where it cuts the genetic material. This allows scientists to edit genes with unprecedented precision and ease, making genetic modifications accessible to laboratories worldwide.

## Evolution and Development

The CRISPR system was adapted from a naturally occurring defense mechanism found in bacteria. [[entities/feng-zhang|Dr. Feng Zhang]] and his colleagues [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] the [[entities/broad-institute|Broad Institute]] played a significant role in advancing CRISPR technology and demonstrating its [[concepts/software|applications]] in mammalian cells. Over time, the technology has been refined and optimized for use in research, diagnostics, and therapeutic applications.

## Beyond CRISPR

Researchers continue to develop successor technologies and improvements to [[concepts/gene-editing|gene editing]]. These next-generation approaches aim to address limitations of CRISPR, such as off-target effects and delivery challenges. Dr. Zhang's work includes exploring new gene-editing systems that may eventually complement or surpass CRISPR's [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]], expanding the toolkit available for genetic medicine and biological research.
