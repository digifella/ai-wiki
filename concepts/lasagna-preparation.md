# Lasagna Preparation

Lasagna preparation is a multi-layered cooking technique that combines three essential components: ragout (a slow-cooked meat sauce), pasta sheets, and béchamel (a creamy white sauce). The method requires sequential preparation of each element followed by careful layering and baking. Andy Cooks' approach provides a systematic methodology for coordinating these components into a cohesive finished dish.

## Component Preparation

The ragout forms the foundational flavor layer, developed through slow cooking of ground meat with tomatoes, aromatics, and seasonings over several hours. This process allows flavors to concentrate and meld. The pasta sheets—typically egg pasta—are either fresh or dried and prepared according to package instructions. The béchamel is prepared separately as a roux-based sauce combining butter, flour, and milk, which serves as a binding agent and contributes richness to the finished dish.

## Assembly and Cooking

Once all components are ready, lasagna is assembled in a baking dish through alternating layers: a thin base of ragout, followed by pasta sheets, then béchamel, repeating until the dish is full and finishing with a béchamel layer on top. The assembled lasagna is baked in a preheated oven until the top develops a golden crust and the layers beneath heat through completely. Proper timing and temperature coordination ensures the pasta softens while the sauces set into a unified structure.
