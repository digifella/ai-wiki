# Post-quantum cryptography

[[concepts/post-quantum-security|Post-quantum cryptography]] (PQC) refers to [[concepts/cryptographic-algorithms|cryptographic algorithms]]—specifically public-key primitives—designed to remain [[concepts/secure|secure]] against the computational [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] of a [[entities/quantum-computing]]-enabled adversary.

### Key Concepts
- Critical area of research and development aimed at mitigating Quantum Threats to existing Digital [[concepts/security|Security]] infrastructures.
- Focuses on protecting data even amidst the current [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] and [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]] limitations of [[entities/quantum-computing]].
- Addresses the [[concepts/vulnerability|vulnerability]] of current Classical [[concepts/cryptography|Cryptography]] [[concepts/open-standards|standards]] to future quantum-scale attacks.
