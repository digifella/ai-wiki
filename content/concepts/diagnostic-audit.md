# Diagnostic Audit

A diagnostic audit is a systematic evaluation process used to identify deficiencies, inconsistencies, and areas for improvement in a given output, decision, or strategy. Rather than accepting work at face value, a diagnostic audit applies structured scrutiny to uncover hidden problems—whether factual errors, logical gaps, unsupported claims, or practical limitations. The process is particularly valuable in business contexts where the cost of flawed analysis or poor recommendations can be substantial.

## Core Process

The diagnostic audit typically involves three stages. First, the auditor conducts a rigorous critique of the subject matter, examining [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]], internal [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]], and alignment with underlying assumptions. Second, the auditor identifies specific weaknesses, such as biases in [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]], incomplete information, or solutions that lack feasibility in real-world conditions. Third, the auditor proposes corrected or improved alternatives supported by clearer reasoning or additional evidence. This structured approach transforms reactive [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] into proactive [[concepts/quality-assurance|quality assurance]].

## Business Application

In business strategy, diagnostic audits commonly review strategic plans, market analyses, financial forecasts, and implementation recommendations. They serve as a quality gate before major decisions or resource allocation, helping organizations avoid costly mistakes rooted in incomplete analysis or unexamined assumptions. Diagnostic audits are most effective when conducted by someone with relevant expertise but sufficient distance from the original work to maintain objectivity.
