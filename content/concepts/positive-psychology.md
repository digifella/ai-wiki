# Positive Psychology

Positive [[concepts/psychology|psychology]] is a branch of psychology that emphasizes the study of human strengths, virtues, and optimal functioning rather than focusing primarily on mental illness and psychological disorders. Emerging as a distinct field in the late 1990s, it represents a significant shift in psychological research and practice toward understanding what enables people to flourish and live meaningful lives. Rather than asking "what is wrong?", positive psychology asks "what is right?" and "how can we build on existing strengths?"

## Core Focus Areas

The field examines constructs such as [[concepts/happiness|happiness]], [[concepts/resilience|resilience]], character strengths, meaningful [[concepts/relationships|relationships]], and [[concepts/quality-of-life|life satisfaction]]. Practitioners and researchers investigate factors that contribute to psychological [[concepts/health|wellbeing]], including [[concepts/motivation|purpose]], engagement, positive emotions, and personal growth. This approach complements rather than replaces clinical psychology and the treatment of mental disorders, recognizing that the absence of illness does not automatically constitute [[concepts/wellbeing|wellbeing]].

## Applications and Practice

Positive psychology has practical [[concepts/software|applications]] across multiple domains including education, organizational development, therapy, and [[concepts/personal-development|personal development]]. Interventions derived from positive psychology research—such as gratitude practices, strength-based coaching, and resilience [[concepts/training|training]]—have been integrated into various settings. The field also addresses challenges such as [[concepts/happiness-baseline|hedonic adaptation]], where individuals return to baseline levels of happiness despite positive life changes, recognizing that sustained wellbeing requires ongoing [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] and practice.
