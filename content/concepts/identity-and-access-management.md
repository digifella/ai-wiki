# Identity And Access Management

Identity and Access Management (IAM) is a [[concepts/security|security]] framework that controls who can access what resources within an [[concepts/organization|organization]]'s infrastructure. It encompasses the processes, [[concepts/policies|policies]], and technologies used to authenticate users, verify their identities, and enforce permissions across systems and networks. Effective IAM [[concepts/adoption|implementation]] ensures that only authorized individuals can access specific resources, reducing the risk of unauthorized access, data breaches, and security incidents.

## Core Components

IAM systems typically include three primary functions: [[concepts/authentication|authentication]] (verifying that a user is who they claim to be), [[concepts/authorization|authorization]] (determining what authenticated users are permitted to do), and accounting (tracking and logging user actions). These components work together to create a [[concepts/secure|secure]] perimeter around sensitive systems and data. Modern IAM solutions often integrate with directory services, credential management systems, and multi-factor authentication mechanisms to strengthen security posture.

## Critical Infrastructure Applications

IAM principles are particularly important in protecting critical infrastructure environments, such as military installations. Air force [[concepts/number-systems|bases]], for example, require stringent identity [[concepts/verification|verification]] and access controls to protect sensitive assets, classified information, and operational systems. Hardening these facilities involves implementing layered IAM controls that restrict physical and logical access to authorized personnel only, verifying credentials [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] multiple checkpoints, and continuously monitoring for unauthorized access attempts or anomalous behavior patterns.
