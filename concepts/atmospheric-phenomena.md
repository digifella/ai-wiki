# Atmospheric Phenomena

Atmospheric phenomena encompass the physical and chemical processes occurring within a planet's atmosphere that generate observable effects. These include weather patterns, optical displays, and electrical discharges such as lightning. The fundamental drivers of these processes involve the interaction of solar radiation, planetary rotation, atmospheric composition, and pressure differentials. The underlying physics governing these interactions varies significantly based on the specific environmental conditions of the celestial body in question.

## Martian Atmospheric Dynamics

On Mars, the thin atmosphere composed primarily of carbon dioxide supports distinct meteorological behaviors compared to Earth. Dust storms are a dominant feature, capable of growing to planetary scale and significantly altering global temperature profiles and atmospheric circulation. These events are driven by strong thermal gradients between the equator and poles, as well as the planet's high obliquity.

## Electrical Discharges and Lightning

Observations of electrical discharges on Mars have been a subject of ongoing geological mission analysis. While Earth's lightning is fueled by vigorous convection and ice crystal collisions in thick atmospheres, Martian lightning is hypothesized to arise from dust particle charging during intense dust storms. Data from orbiters and landers continue to refine models regarding the frequency and intensity of these events, which remain less frequent and less energetic than terrestrial counterparts due to lower atmospheric density.
