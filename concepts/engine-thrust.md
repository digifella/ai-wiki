# Engine Thrust

Engine thrust is the force produced by an engine through the expulsion of combusted gases or other propellant, measured in newtons (N) or pounds-force (lbf). In rocketry, thrust arises from the rapid combustion of fuel and oxidizer within the engine's combustion chamber. The resulting high-pressure gases are expelled through a converging-diverging nozzle, which accelerates the exhaust and produces the reaction force that propels the vehicle, following Newton's third law of motion.

## Thrust in Rocket Operations

During first-stage operation, thrust magnitude is critical for overcoming gravity and atmospheric drag to achieve liftoff and initial ascent. The specific thrust profile must be carefully managed to ensure structural integrity while providing sufficient acceleration to reach orbital velocity. Variations in thrust are often adjusted during flight to optimize fuel efficiency and manage aerodynamic loads.

The timing of thrust events is closely linked to major flight milestones, such as payload fairing separation. As the rocket ascends into thinner atmosphere, the aerodynamic pressure on the payload fairing decreases, allowing it to be jettisoned to reduce mass. This separation event is often coordinated with thrust vectoring or engine cutoff sequences to ensure a stable trajectory for the upper stages and the eventual deployment of the payload.
