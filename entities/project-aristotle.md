# Project Aristotle

**[[concepts/project-aristotle|Project Aristotle]]** was an internal research initiative at **[[entities/google]]** aimed at identifying the key dynamics that make a team effective. Conducted the People Operations team, the study analyzed over 180 teams and thousands of data points to move beyond individual [[concepts/performance-data-gathering|performance metrics]] toward understanding collective [[concepts/success|success]].

## Key Findings

The primary finding was that **[[concepts/psychological-safety|psychological safety]]** is the most critical factor for [[concepts/team-effectiveness|team effectiveness]], ranking higher than [[concepts/clear-goals|clear goals]], [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]], or structure.

- **[[concepts/vulnerability|Psychological Safety]]**: The belief that one [[entities/will|will]] not be punished or humiliated for speaking up with [[concepts/ideas|ideas]], questions, concerns, or mistakes.
- **Structured Autonomy**: Clear goals and defined roles allow teams to operate efficiently.
- **Meaning & Impact**: Team members care about their work and its outcomes.
