# Theatrical Marketing

**Theatrical marketing** refers to the strategic use of dramatic, performative, or spectacle-driven events to generate public interest, media coverage, and brand [[concepts/conscious-thought|awareness]]. It leverages [[concepts/conflict|narrative tension]], visual symbolism, and real-[[entities/earth|world]] execution to create shareable moments that transcend traditional advertising.

## Core Principles
- **Spectacle over Information**: Prioritizes visual impact and emotional reaction over detailed product specs.
- **Real-World Execution**: Relies on tangible, physical events rather than digital-only campaigns to enhance credibility and virality.
- **[[concepts/storytelling|Narrative]] Framing**: Uses established cultural tropes or pop culture references to frame the event for immediate recognition.
- **[[concepts/decommissioning|Decommissioning]] as Drama**: Turning the end of a product lifecycle or a technical milestone into a ceremonial event.

## Case Studies

### Figure Robotics F.02 Decommissioning
In October 2026, Figure [[concepts/robotics|Robotics]] executed a high-profile theatrical marketing stunt involving the decommissioning of its [[entities/f02|F.02]] [[concepts/humanoid-robots|humanoid robots]]. The event deliberately mimicked the iconic molten [[concepts/metal|metal]] destruction scene from *Terminator 2: Judgment Day*, creating a viral moment that emphasized the power and finality of the technology's lifecycle.

- **Event Details**: The robots were physically destroyed in a controlled, cinematic manner, debunking CGI rumors and confirming the [[concepts/physical-reality|physical reality]] of the hardware.
- **Media Coverage**: The event was highlighted in videos such as [[lab-notes/2026-10-03-Figure-Robotics-F.02-Humanoid-Robot-Terminator-Style-Dec|Figure Robotics F.02 Humanoid Robot Terminator-Style Decommissioning Event]], where presenter [[concepts/dr-know-it-all-knows-it-all|Dr. Know-it-all Knows it all]] emphasized the authenticity of the stunt.
- **Strategic Impact**: By referencing a major pop culture icon, the company generated significant organic discussion around the capabilities and future of humanoid robotics.
