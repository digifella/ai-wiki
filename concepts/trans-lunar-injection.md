# Trans-lunar Injection (TLI)

Trans-lunar injection (TLI) is a maneuver performed by spacecraft to escape low [[entities/earth|Earth]] orbit and begin traveling toward the Moon. TLI consists of firing the spacecraft's engines at just the right moment and for an extended duration, altering its trajectory from circular or elliptical Earth orbits into a lunar transfer orbit.

- **Definition**: The burn that propels a spacecraft out of [[concepts/low-earth-orbit-leo|low-Earth orbit (LEO)]] and onto a course toward the Moon.
- **Purpose**: To set up an intercept with the Moon's gravitational field for subsequent maneuvers leading to lunar orbit insertion, flyby, or landing.

### Historical Significance
- TLI was first performed by [[concepts/nasa|NASA]] on 20th September 1968 during [[concepts/apollo-8|Apollo 8 mission]], marking a pivotal moment in [[concepts/space-exploration|space exploration]].
- The recent **[[entities/artemis-ii|Artemis II]]** mission successfully executed its TLI burn on Day 2 of the mission, setting a new benchmark for [[concepts/accuracy|precision]] and [[concepts/efficient-operation|operational efficiency]].

### Key Points from Artemis II Mission
- **TLI Burn**: Performed with remarkable accuracy by [[concepts/orion-systems|Orion spacecraft]].
- **Mission Goals**: To conduct a [[concepts/human-lunar-flyby|human lunar flyby]] after nearly five decades since Apollo 17's last crewed Moon landing.
- **Systems Overview**: Demonstrated the capabilities of advanced propulsion and navigation systems critical for future [[concepts/deep-space-exploration|deep space]] missions.
