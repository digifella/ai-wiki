# Arid Climate

An arid climate is a climate classification characterized by extremely low annual precipitation and minimal moisture availability. Regions with arid climates typically receive less than 250 millimeters (approximately 10 inches) of rainfall per year, though some classification systems use 200 millimeters as the threshold. This scarcity of precipitation results in limited water resources and creates distinctive environmental conditions that shape both natural ecosystems and human settlement patterns.

## Distribution and Characteristics

Arid climates are found across approximately 30 percent of Earth's land surface, concentrated primarily between latitudes 15 and 35 degrees north and south of the equator. Major arid regions include the Sahara and Arabian deserts in Africa and Asia, the Australian interior, and parts of the southwestern United States. These areas are characterized by high evaporation rates, sparse vegetation, extreme temperature fluctuations between day and night, and intense solar radiation. Low humidity and minimal cloud cover further contribute to rapid moisture loss and limited water cycling.

## Climate Classification

Arid climates fall within broader climate classification systems, most notably the Köppen-Geiger classification, where they are designated as "B" climates with further subdivision into hot deserts (BWh) and cold deserts (BWk). The distinction between arid and semi-arid climates remains somewhat arbitrary, with semi-arid regions receiving between 250 and 500 millimeters of annual precipitation. Local factors such as elevation, proximity to water bodies, and ocean currents influence the specific characteristics of arid regions and their placement within classification frameworks.

## Ecological and Human Implications

Arid climates support specialized plant and animal communities adapted to water scarcity, including drought-resistant vegetation and nocturnal fauna. Human populations in arid regions have historically relied on irrigation, oasis settlements, and pastoral nomadism. Modern settlement in arid areas depends heavily on groundwater extraction and water management infrastructure, which can contribute to resource depletion over time.
