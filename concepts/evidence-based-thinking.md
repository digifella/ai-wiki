# Evidence-Based Thinking

**Evidence-Based [[concepts/human-cognition|Thinking]]** is the disciplined practice of grounding decisions and beliefs in [[concepts/purpose|objective]] data, empirical research, and rigorous analysis rather than intuition, ideology, or anecdote. It requires minimizing cognitive bias and maximizing the [[concepts/camera-raw|signal-to-noise ratio]] in information processing.

## Core Principles

*   **Empirical Grounding**: Prioritize observable, measurable [[concepts/factual-knowledge|facts]] over assumptions.
*   **Bias Mitigation**: Actively identify and correct for [[concepts/cognitive-biases|Cognitive Biases]] (e.g., confirmation bias, availability heuristic).
*   **Iterative Updating**: Treat beliefs as hypotheses to be updated as new evidence emerges (Bayesian updating).
*   **Source Criticism**: Evaluate the credibility, methodology, and potential conflicts of interest of information sources.

## Integration with Judgment

While evidence provides the raw material for [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]], it does not always yield a single clear path. **Judgment** serves as the critical bridge between data and action, particularly in contexts of [[concepts/ambiguity|ambiguity]] or incomplete information.

*   **Complementary Role**: Evidence reduces uncertainty; judgment resolves residual ambiguity.
*   **[[concepts/leadership|Leadership]] Context**: As noted by [[entities/sir-andrew-likierman|Sir Andrew Likierman]], judgment is "the core of [[concepts/exemplary-leadership|exemplary leadership]]," defined as the ability to combine [[concepts/personal-qualities|personal qualities]] with [[concepts/relevant-knowledge|relevant knowledge]] and [[concepts/experience|experience]] to form [[concepts/opinions|opinions]] when facts alone are insufficient.
*   **Decision Thresholds**: [[concepts/sound-choice|Good judgment]] determines when to act despite imperfect evidence and how to interpret conflicting signals.
