# Oil Types

Oils are liquid fats derived from plants, seeds, nuts, or animal sources, used extensively in cooking and food [[concepts/preparation|preparation]]. They serve fundamental culinary functions including [[concepts/skillet-frying|sautéing]], frying, roasting, and as components in dressings, marinades, and baked goods. The selection of oil influences both the flavor profile and nutritional [[concepts/writing|composition]] of finished dishes.

## Cooking Applications and Smoke Points

Different oils are suited to different [[concepts/cooking-methods|cooking methods]] based on their smoke points—the temperature at which an oil begins to break down and smoke. Oils with high smoke points, such as refined vegetable, canola, and peanut oils, are appropriate for deep frying and high-heat cooking. Oils with lower smoke points, including extra virgin olive oil and unrefined coconut oil, are better suited to low-heat cooking, drizzling, and dressing applications.

## Common Oil Types

Vegetable oils including canola, soybean, and sunflower oil provide neutral flavor and are widely used for general cooking purposes. Olive oil, ranging from robust extra virgin varieties to lighter refined versions, offers distinctive flavor and is a staple in Mediterranean cuisines. Coconut oil, palm oil, and nut-based oils like walnut and almond oil each contribute specific flavor characteristics and nutritional profiles. Seed oils such as flax, sesame, and hemp oil are often used in smaller quantities for their concentrated flavors and potential nutritional benefits.

## Nutritional Considerations

Oils differ significantly in their fatty acid composition, affecting their nutritional properties. Some oils contain higher levels of monounsaturated or polyunsaturated fats, while others are more saturated. These compositional differences influence how oils affect cholesterol levels and overall dietary impact, making oil selection relevant to nutritional [[concepts/health|health]] considerations.
