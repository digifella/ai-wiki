# Geological Hazards

Geological hazards are naturally occurring Earth processes that create direct risks to human life, property, and infrastructure. These phenomena result from the dynamic [[entities/nature|nature]] of the planet's crust, internal heat, and surface processes, operating across timescales ranging from seconds to centuries. The impacts of geological hazards extend beyond immediate physical damage to include long-term environmental disruption, economic loss, and displacement of populations.

## Common Types of Hazards

Major categories of geological hazards include volcanic activity, [[concepts/earthquakes|earthquakes]], landslides, and tsunamis. [[concepts/volcanic-eruptions|Volcanic eruptions]] can produce [[concepts/lava-flows|lava flows]], pyroclastic flows, lahars, and ash fall across wide areas. Earthquakes result from tectonic [[concepts/stress|stress]] release along plate boundaries and faults, causing ground shaking and surface rupture. Mass movements such as landslides and debris flows occur in mountainous or unstable terrain, particularly following heavy rainfall or seismic events. Secondary hazards, such as volcanic [[concepts/landmass-collapse|flank collapse]]—where the unstable side of a volcano fails catastrophically—can trigger tsunamis and pose hazards far from their source.

## Monitoring and Mitigation

Understanding geological hazards requires [[concepts/integration|integration]] of geological mapping, instrumental monitoring, and historical records to assess frequency and magnitude of events. Seismic networks, volcano observatories, and satellite systems provide real-time data for hazard assessment. Risk reduction strategies include [[concepts/land-use|land-use]] planning to avoid high-hazard zones, [[concepts/building-codes-and-standards|construction standards]] designed to withstand expected ground motion or impacts, and early warning systems where feasible. In regions of high geological activity, these approaches form essential components of public safety and disaster preparedness.
