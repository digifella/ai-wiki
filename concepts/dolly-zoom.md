# Dolly Zoom

A cinematic technique where the camera moves forward or backward while simultaneously zooming the lens in the opposite direction, keeping the subject's size constant while altering the background perspective.

## Overview
The dolly zoom creates a disorienting effect, often used to convey psychological [[concepts/anxiety|unease]], realization, or shock. It manipulates the viewer's perception of depth and spatial [[concepts/relationships|relationships]].

## Related Concepts
- Vertigo Effect: Synonymous term often associated with Alfred Hitchcock's film *Vertigo*.
- Perspective Distortion: The geometric change in background elements relative to the foreground subject.
- Camera [[concepts/exercise|Movement]]: Technical classification of the dolly track motion.
- [[concepts/focal-length|Lens Focal Length]]: The optical parameter adjusted during the zoom component.
