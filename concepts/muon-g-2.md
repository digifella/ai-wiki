# Muon G 2

The Muon g-2 experiment is a precision physics measurement conducted at Fermilab that determines the anomalous magnetic moment of the muon. The muon is a fundamental particle similar to an electron but approximately 200 times more massive. The "g-2" refers to a dimensionless parameter related to the gyromagnetic ratio, which characterizes how the muon's magnetic moment relates to its spin angular momentum.

## Measurement and Significance

The experiment involves storing a beam of muons in a highly uniform magnetic field and measuring the frequency at which their spins precess relative to their momentum. This precession frequency allows physicists to calculate the anomalous magnetic moment with extreme precision. The value is sensitive to virtual particles that briefly pop in and out of existence, meaning deviations from theoretical predictions could indicate the presence of new, undiscovered particles or forces beyond the Standard Model of particle physics.

## Experimental Setup

The current iteration of the experiment utilizes a 15-meter-diameter superconducting storage ring equipped with precision magnets and detectors. Muons are produced from a proton beam striking a target and are injected into the ring where they circulate at nearly the speed of light. Detectors surrounding the ring record the decay electrons, which provide data on the muon spin orientation over time. The collaboration compares these experimental results with theoretical calculations derived from quantum electrodynamics and hadronic vacuum polarization to test the consistency of current physical theories.
