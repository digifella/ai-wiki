# Community Health Nursing

[[concepts/community-health|Community health nursing]] is a specialized nursing practice that delivers [[concepts/health|healthcare]] services and promotes health within community settings rather than institutional environments. Unlike hospital-based nursing, [[concepts/public-health|community health]] nurses work with populations and groups to address the collective [[concepts/patient-needs|health needs]] of communities. This population-centered approach enables nurses to identify health patterns, assess community needs, and develop interventions that serve entire neighborhoods or specific populations.

## Practice Settings and Activities

Community health nurses operate across diverse settings including public health departments, schools, workplaces, clinics, and patients' homes. Their work spans health promotion, [[concepts/health-promotion|disease prevention]], health education, and direct care services. Common activities include [[concepts/conducting-health-screenings|conducting health screenings]], managing chronic disease [[concepts/software|programs]], providing prenatal and maternal [[concepts/health-care|health services]], addressing communicable diseases, and advocating for [[concepts/vulnerable-populations|vulnerable populations]]. Community health nurses often serve as liaisons between individuals and broader health systems.

## Focus Areas

A primary emphasis of [[concepts/fundamentals-of-nursing|community health nursing]] is [[concepts/population-health|population health]] assessment and intervention at the community level rather than individual treatment. Nurses identify [[concepts/determinants-of-health|social determinants of health]] affecting communities—such as poverty, housing, education, and [[concepts/healthcare-access|access to healthcare]]—and work to address these systemic factors. They also engage in health education and [[concepts/preventive-healthcare|disease prevention]] activities tailored to community needs and cultural contexts.

## Role and Impact

Community health nurses function as advocates, educators, and coordinators of care. Their work contributes to reducing health disparities, improving public health outcomes, and increasing health equity by reaching underserved populations. The profession requires strong assessment [[concepts/skills|skills]], cultural competency, and understanding of [[concepts/epidemiology|epidemiology]] alongside traditional nursing knowledge.
