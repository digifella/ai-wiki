# Local Health Service Levels

Local health service levels describe the organizational [[concepts/hierarchy|hierarchy]] and scope of [[concepts/health|healthcare]] delivery within communities and regions. These levels represent different [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] of [[concepts/service-delivery|service provision]], from individual clinics and primary care centers to district and regional hospital systems. The [[concepts/structure|structure]] of local health services typically reflects population size, geographic [[concepts/distribution|distribution]], and healthcare infrastructure capacity.

## Primary Care Level

Primary care services form the foundation of local health systems, providing first-[[entities/contact|contact]] care for most health needs. These services are delivered through [[concepts/population-health|community health]] centers, clinics, and general practitioner offices that serve defined local populations. Primary care focuses on [[concepts/preventive-care|prevention]], [[concepts/health-promotion|health education]], and management of common conditions, with referral pathways to more specialized services when needed.

## Secondary and Tertiary Care Levels

Secondary care services include district and regional hospitals that provide more specialized treatment and diagnostic [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]]. [[concepts/tertiary-prevention|Tertiary care]] represents the highest level of local health service provision, typically located in major urban centers and offering highly specialized services, complex procedures, and teaching facilities. The relationship between these levels ensures appropriate case distribution and efficient use of healthcare resources.
