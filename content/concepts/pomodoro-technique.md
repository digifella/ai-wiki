# Pomodoro Technique

The Pomodoro Technique is a time-management method that divides work into fixed intervals of focused activity, traditionally 25 minutes in length, separated by short breaks. Each interval is called a "pomodoro," from the Italian word for tomato, named after the tomato-shaped kitchen timer that creator Francesco Cirillo used as a university student in the late 1980s. The technique aims to improve focus and productivity by leveraging the psychological principle that structured constraints encourage sustained [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] while reducing mental fatigue.

## Structure and Practice

A standard Pomodoro cycle consists of working for 25 minutes with full concentration on a single task, followed by a 5-minute break. After completing four consecutive pomodoros, a longer break of 15–30 minutes is taken. Workers track completed pomodoros and adjust the interval length based on their needs and the task at hand. The rigid timing creates a rhythm that helps practitioners establish clear boundaries between work and rest periods.

## Applications and Variations

The technique has been widely adopted in academic, professional, and personal productivity contexts. Users often combine it with task lists, breaking larger projects into pomodoro-sized units to make progress visible and manageable. While the 25-minute interval remains standard, practitioners frequently adapt durations to suit different work styles, task complexity, and individual attention spans. The method's simplicity and minimal equipment requirements have made it accessible for remote work, studying, and creative projects.
