# Vermicelli Cooking

Vermicelli cooking encompasses techniques for preparing thin, delicate noodles used throughout Asian cuisines. These noodles are typically made from wheat or rice flour mixed with water, then extruded into strands considerably thinner than spaghetti. The delicate structure of vermicelli requires specific cooking approaches to prevent overcooking or breaking the noodles while achieving the desired texture. Basic preparation involves boiling the noodles for a short duration, usually between four and eight minutes, due to their small diameter and high surface-area-to-volume ratio.

## Preparation and Handling

Proper hydration is critical for vermicelli, as the noodles can become mushy if left in boiling water too long. Many varieties, particularly rice vermicelli, are often soaked in warm water to soften them before being briefly blanched or added directly to stir-fries. This method preserves the structural integrity of the strands, allowing them to absorb sauces without disintegrating. Drying the noodles thoroughly after washing removes excess moisture, which is essential for achieving a non-sticky texture in subsequent cooking steps.

## Singapore Fried Vermicelli

A prominent application of these techniques is Singapore fried vermicelli, a stir-fried noodle dish with origins clarified by Flo Lum. The dish typically involves sautéing aromatics such as garlic, shallots, and curry powder before adding the pre-soaked vermicelli. Ingredients like shrimp, char siu pork, and vegetables are incorporated to provide flavor and texture contrast. The high heat of the wok allows the noodles to absorb the curry-infused oil without becoming soggy, resulting in a distinct yellow hue and a firm yet tender bite.
