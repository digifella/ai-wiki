# Autofocus Optimization

Autofocus optimization on [[entities/fujifilm|Fujifilm]] cameras involves understanding and configuring the autofocus modes, zones, and tracking [[concepts/parameters|parameters]] to match specific shooting situations. Fujifilm cameras offer several autofocus modes including Single Point AF, Zone AF, and Wide/Tracking AF, each suited to different subject types and compositional needs. Proper configuration of these modes significantly improves the likelihood of achieving [[concepts/sharpness|sharp focus]] on intended subjects, particularly in challenging lighting or fast-moving [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]].

## Mode Selection and Zone Configuration

Selecting the appropriate [[concepts/autofocus-mode|AF mode]] depends on subject predictability and composition. Single Point AF provides maximum precision for [[concepts/stationary-subjects|stationary subjects]], while Zone AF offers a balance between [[concepts/power|control]] and flexibility for subjects within a defined area. Wide/Tracking AF automatically follows [[concepts/moving-subjects|moving subjects]], making it valuable for wildlife and sports photography. The size and sensitivity of the AF zone can be adjusted within these modes to optimize performance for the specific scene being photographed.

## Advanced Features and Refinement

Fujifilm cameras include advanced features such as AF tracking sensitivity, which controls how quickly the autofocus responds to subject [[concepts/exercise|movement]], and AF point visibility, which helps in confirming focus placement. The AF-C ([[concepts/continuous-autofocus|continuous autofocus]]) mode with adjustable tracking algorithms allows [[concepts/customization|customization]] of how the camera predicts and maintains focus on moving subjects. Regular [[concepts/testing|testing]] of these settings in real shooting conditions helps identify optimal configurations for personal shooting [[concepts/style|style]] and frequently encountered scenarios.
