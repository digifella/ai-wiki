# Gut Brain Axis

The gut-brain axis is a bidirectional communication network that links the central nervous system with the enteric nervous system of the gastrointestinal tract. This complex interaction relies on neural, endocrine, and immune pathways to facilitate continuous signaling between the gut and the brain. The vagus nerve acts as a primary conduit for this exchange, transmitting information that allows physiological states in the digestive system to influence mood and cognitive function, while psychological states such as [[concepts/stress|stress]] can alter digestive processes and gut permeability.

## Mechanisms of Communication

Communication along the gut-brain axis occurs through multiple distinct pathways. Neural signaling involves direct connections via the vagus nerve and the enteric nervous system, which operates independently but communicates with the brain. Endocrine pathways involve the [[concepts/deployment|release]] of hormones and neurotransmitters, such as serotonin and dopamine, which are produced in significant quantities within the gastrointestinal tract. Immune pathways involve the release of cytokines and other immune mediators that can cross the blood-brain barrier or signal via the vagus nerve, influencing neuroinflammation and brain [[concepts/health|health]].
