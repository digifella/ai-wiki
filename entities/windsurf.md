# Windsurf

Windsurfing is a water sport that combines elements of surfing and sailing, requiring practitioners to balance on a specialized board while manipulating an attached sail to harness wind power for propulsion. The sport emerged in the late 1960s as an evolution of traditional surfing, incorporating sailing mechanics to enable movement across various water conditions. Modern windsurfing boards typically measure between 2.4 and 3.5 meters in length, with the sail attached to a flexible mast and boom that allows the rider to adjust angle and trim.

## History and Development

The sport was invented in 1967 by Jim Drake and Hoyle Schweitzer, who patented the first functional windsurfing rig. Their design featured a universal joint connecting the sail to the board, allowing the rider to control both direction and propulsion independently. This innovation distinguished windsurfing from earlier attempts at combining sailing and surfing, which often lacked the necessary maneuverability. The sport gained significant popularity in the 1970s and 1980s, leading to its inclusion in the Olympic Games as a medal event starting in 1984.

## Equipment and Technique

Windsurfing equipment consists of a board, a mast, a boom, and a sail. The board is designed to float and provide stability, while the sail captures wind energy to drive the craft forward. Riders stand on the board and hold the boom to control the sail's angle relative to the wind. By shifting their weight and adjusting the sail's position, windsurfers can accelerate, turn, and perform jumps. The sport accommodates various disciplines, including freestyle, slalom, wave riding, and windsurfing slalom racing, each requiring specific equipment configurations and techniques.
