# Olbers' Paradox

**Olbers' Paradox** is the cosmological argument that the darkness of the [[concepts/night-sky|night sky]] contradicts the hypothesis of an infinite and eternal universe uniformly filled with stars. If such a universe existed, every [[concepts/line-of-sight|line of sight]] should eventually end on the surface of a star, making the night sky as bright as the surface of the Sun.

## Core Concepts
- **The Paradox**: In an infinite, static, and eternal universe, the sky should be uniformly bright.
- **Resolution**: The darkness of the night sky implies the universe is not infinite in age or static. Key factors include:
    - The [[concepts/finite-age-of-the-universe|finite age of the universe]] (light from distant stars has not yet reached us).
    - The [[concepts/expansion-of-the-universe|expansion of the universe]] (redshift reduces the energy of light from distant sources).
    - The finite lifespan of stars.
