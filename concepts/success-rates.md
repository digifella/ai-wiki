# Success Rates

Success rates in the context of AI agents refer to the empirical measurement of how effectively repository-level context files improve agent performance on software development tasks. These specialized files, such as `AGENTS.md` and `CLAUDE.md`, are designed to provide AI systems with structured information about a codebase, including its architecture, conventions, dependencies, and operational requirements. By quantifying the performance improvements these files enable, researchers and practitioners can assess their practical value in agentic workflows.

## Measurement Approach

The evaluation of success rates typically involves comparing agent outcomes on standardized software engineering benchmarks with and without the inclusion of context files. Metrics often include task completion rates, code compilation success, and the accuracy of generated solutions relative to ground truth. This comparative analysis isolates the contribution of explicit contextual guidance from the underlying model's inherent capabilities.

## Impact on Agent Performance

Empirical studies indicate that repository-level context files can significantly reduce hallucination rates and improve the relevance of generated code by grounding the agent's responses in specific project constraints. The effectiveness varies based on the complexity of the repository and the specificity of the instructions provided. Consequently, the inclusion of such files is increasingly viewed as a critical component in optimizing the reliability of autonomous coding agents.
