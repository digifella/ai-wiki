# Homing Pigeons

**Homing pigeons** (*Columba livia domestica*) are domesticated birds renowned for their ability to navigate back to their home loft from unfamiliar locations, often over hundreds of miles. This capability relies on a multimodal sensory system integrating visual landmarks, olfactory cues, and geomagnetic perception.

## Navigation Mechanisms

Homing pigeons employ a hierarchical navigation strategy:
- **Map and Compass**: A "map" sense allows them to determine their position relative home, while a "compass" sense provides directional orientation.
- **Visual Landmarks**: At close range or familiar routes, birds rely on visual topography.
- **Olfactory Map**: Evidence suggests pigeons construct an olfactory map using wind-borne scents to identify location.
- **Infrasound Detection**: Some theories propose detection of low-frequency sound waves generated by landscapes and weather systems.
- **Geomagnetic Sensing**: A critical component for long-distance orientation, especially under overcast conditions or when displaced to novel areas.

## Geomagnetic Perception: The Magnetic Map

Recent research has advanced the understanding of how pigeons detect [[concepts/earths-magnetic-field|Earth's magnetic field]] (**[magnetoreception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetoreception)**).

- **[[concepts/superparamagnetic-macrophages|Superparamagnetic Macrophages]]**: A key discovery identifies superparamagnetism within iron-loaded macrophages in the liver and spleen as the likely mechanism for the "magnetic map" sense.
- **Organ-Specific Sensing**: These macrophages act as mechanosensors; changes in the magnetic field alter the torque on intracellular iron crystals, which are detected via neural connections to the vagus nerve.
- **Distinct from Cryptochromes**: This mechanism is distinct from the **cryptochrome**-based radical pair mechanism found in the eye, which likely serves as a "compass" rather than a map.

> **Key Insight**: The presence of superparamagnetic iron in liver and spleen macrophages provides a physiological basis for how pigeons sense inclination and intensity of the [[concepts/protective-magnetic-field|geomagnetic field]] to determine geographic position.
