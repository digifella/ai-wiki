# Hubble Flow

**Hubble [[concepts/flow|Flow]]** describes the recession of galaxies from one another due to the expansion of space itself, rather than motion through space. This phenomenon is governed by **Hubble's Law**, which states that the recession velocity ($v$) of a galaxy is proportional to its distance ($d$) from the observer: $v = H_0 d$, where $H_0$ is the **Hubble Constant**.

## Key Characteristics

- **Metric Expansion**: The increase in [[concepts/proper-distance|proper distance]] between comoving observers is due to the stretching of the metric of space, not kinematic motion.
- **Isotropy and Homogeneity**: On large [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]], the flow appears uniform in all directions, supporting the **Cosmological Principle**.
- **Redshift**: [[concepts/light|Light]] from receding sources is stretched, resulting in cosmological **redshift** ($z$).

## The Age-Size Paradox

A common misconception arises from the apparent contradiction between the [[concepts/universe-age|age of the universe]] (~13.8 billion years) and the radius of the **[[concepts/observable-universe|Observable Universe]]** (~46.5 billion light-years). If light travels at $c$, the maximum distance should be ~13.8 billion light-years.

- **[[concepts/solution|Resolution]]**: Space has expanded during the time light has been traveling. The proper distance to the source of the oldest light is now much greater than the light-travel distance.
- **Integration**: See [[lab-notes/2026-07-17-Observable-Universe-Expansion-Age-vs.-Current-Size-Expla|Observable Universe Expansion: Age vs. Current Size Explained]] for a detailed breakdown of how the current diameter (~92 billion light-years) reconciles with the universe's age.
