# Computing Architecture

Computing architecture refers to the foundational design and [[concepts/structure|structure]] of computational systems, encompassing both [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] and [[concepts/software|software]] layers that enable information processing. In contemporary contexts, [[concepts/architecture|architecture]] discussions extend beyond traditional machine specifications to include the organizational frameworks through which different types of computational [[concepts/agents|agents]]—human and artificial—interact and exchange information.

## The Speed Asymmetry Problem

Modern AI systems operate at processing speeds substantially faster than human cognitive and decision-making capacities. This speed differential creates [[concepts/friction|friction]] in systems designed to integrate both human and [[concepts/machine-intelligence|machine intelligence]]. Current [[concepts/web-infrastructure|web infrastructure]], originally built around human-paced interactions and manual workflows, becomes a bottleneck when [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] attempt to operate at their native speeds. The architecture itself introduces delays that prevent AI systems from achieving their theoretical performance benefits in practical applications.

## Redesigning for Human-AI Integration

Rebuilding web infrastructure to accommodate both [[concepts/ai-agent|AI agent]] speed and human involvement requires rethinking architectural assumptions. Rather than forcing humans to match machine speeds or conversely throttling AI systems to human pace, human-centric computing architecture would create intermediate layers and [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] mechanisms that allow meaningful human oversight while minimizing artificial latency. This involves reconsidering data structures, API design, and interface patterns to support asynchronous human participation without degrading AI system efficiency.
