# Mixed Reality

Mixed Reality (MR) is a technology that blends digital content with the physical world, enabling interactive experiences where virtual and real elements coexist and interact in real time. Unlike Virtual Reality, which creates entirely digital environments, or Augmented Reality, which overlays digital elements on a camera feed, Mixed Reality anchors digital objects to physical spaces with spatial awareness and persistent interaction. This distinction allows virtual objects to appear as if they are part of the real environment, reacting to physical surfaces and lighting conditions.

## Technical Foundations

The core capability of Mixed Reality relies on advanced spatial mapping and computer vision. Devices utilize sensors to understand the geometry of the surrounding environment, allowing digital assets to be occluded by real-world objects and to remain stationary relative to physical anchors. This persistent anchoring ensures that virtual content maintains its position and scale as the user moves through the space, creating a seamless integration between the two domains.

## Ecosystem and Implementation

In the context of Microsoft Studio 2, Mixed Reality serves as a foundational layer for collaborative and immersive applications. The platform leverages MR capabilities to facilitate shared experiences where multiple users can interact with the same virtual objects within their respective physical environments. This infrastructure supports the development of tools that require precise spatial alignment, enabling use cases ranging from remote assistance to complex data visualization within a physical workspace.
