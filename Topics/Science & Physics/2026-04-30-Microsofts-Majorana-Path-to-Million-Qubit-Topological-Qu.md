### Summary
This video introduces Microsoft's significant strides in [[entities/quantum-computing|quantum computing]], highlighting its potential to accurately model the complex laws of nature and tackle problems currently beyond the scope of even the world's most powerful supercomputers. The central theme revolves around a major breakthrough: the successful observation and control of the elusive [[concepts/majorana-particle|Majorana particle]], which has long been a theoretical concept. This achievement has enabled Microsoft to develop a novel material and a groundbreaking architecture for [[concepts/quantum-computing|quantum computing]], promising to revolutionize various scientific and technological fields.

The core of Microsoft's [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] lies in leveraging the unique properties of the Majorana particle. Previously only theorized, this subatomic particle, which acts as its own antiparticle, has now been observed and precisely controlled. This control allows for the creation of a "[topoconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topoconductor)," a new type of [semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor) that also functions as a [superconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductor). This material forms the foundation for "topological [[concepts/qubits|qubits]]," which are inherently more reliable, smaller, and more controllable than previous qubit designs. Crucially, these [topological qubits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_qubits) are resistant to noise, a critical challenge that has historically hindered the progress of [[concepts/quantum-computing|quantum computing]] and limited the stability and scalability of quantum [[concepts/central-processing-units|processors]].

This new "topological core" architecture addresses the fundamental limitations of existing quantum systems, paving the way for unprecedented scalability. Microsoft's goal is to develop chips capable of hosting millions of qubits, efficiently solving problems involving a seemingly small number of electrons (30-50) that would take classical computers many lifetimes to process. The implications of such a leap are vast and transformative, potentially revolutionizing areas such as medicine (designing new drugs), material science (creating advanced materials with desired properties), and food production. This capability could enable "first-time right" simulations, eliminating the need for costly and time-consuming physical experiments, and could significantly augment [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]], driving discovery on a scale previously unimaginable. After 17 years of dedicated research, Microsoft presents its "[[entities/majorana-1|Majorana 1]]" processor, marking not just an incredible scientific achievement, but the dawn of a new "[Quantum Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_Age)" that will fundamentally redefine human progress.
