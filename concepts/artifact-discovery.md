# Artifact Discovery

Artifact discovery refers to the systematic identification and recovery of historical objects and archaeological materials from the ground. The process combines field [[concepts/survey|survey]] techniques, contextual analysis, and [[concepts/preservation|preservation]] methods to locate and document items of historical significance. These artifacts range from prehistoric tools and pottery to medieval coins and structures, providing material evidence of past human activity and cultural development.

## The Danish Detectorist Program

Denmark developed a distinctive approach to artifact discovery by formally involving private [[concepts/metal|metal]] detectorists in archaeological work. Rather than restricting detection to professional archaeologists, the [[entities/danish-government|Danish government]] [[concepts/artifact-excavation|deputized detectorists]] to search farm fields and other accessible land. This program aimed to capitalize on the enthusiasm and distributed effort of hobbyists while ensuring finds were properly documented and preserved.

The program yielded substantial results, with detectorists uncovering significant quantities of [[concepts/major-gold-deposit|gold]] artifacts and other objects of historical [[concepts/value|importance]]. These discoveries contributed materially to understanding of Danish prehistory and medieval history. The systematic recording of finds, even from non-professionals, added to the archaeological record and enabled researchers to map patterns of settlement and [[concepts/strategic-resource-allocation|resource distribution]] across the landscape.

This model demonstrated that structured collaboration between amateur detectorists and archaeological institutions could efficiently identify and recover artifacts while maintaining scientific standards for documentation and preservation. The approach influenced archaeological practice in other regions considering how to balance public participation with professional archaeological oversight.
