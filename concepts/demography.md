# Demography

Demography is the scientific study of human populations, examining their size, composition, and the processes that change them over time. Demographers analyze quantitative data on births, deaths, migration, and other factors that affect population structure. The field provides statistical frameworks for understanding how societies evolve and for projecting future population trends.

## Core Measurements

The discipline rests on several key measurements. Birth rates and fertility patterns describe reproductive behavior across populations, while mortality rates and life expectancy indicate population health. Migration patterns—both internal movement within countries and international movement—significantly alter population distribution. Age structure, the distribution of people across age groups, reveals whether a population is growing, declining, or stable, and has major implications for economics, healthcare, and social services.

## Applications and Relevance

Demographic analysis informs policy decisions across health, education, urban planning, and economic development. Governments use population projections to plan infrastructure and allocate resources, while public health officials track disease patterns within specific demographic groups. Demography also helps identify inequalities in health outcomes, mortality, and access to services across different populations, making it essential for evidence-based planning in health and social sectors.
