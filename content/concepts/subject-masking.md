# Subject Masking

Subject [[concepts/masking|masking]] is a digital [[concepts/photo-editing|photo editing]] technique used to isolate a portrait subject from its background, allowing selective [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] to be applied to one or the other independently. By creating a precise mask around the subject—typically the person's face and body—editors can modify [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], color, [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], and [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]] without affecting the surrounding areas. This selective approach enables more refined [[concepts/power|control]] over the final image and is particularly useful when the subject and background require different treatment.

## Implementation

Modern [[concepts/photo-enhancement|photo editing]] [[concepts/software|software]], particularly [[entities/adobe-lightroom|Adobe Lightroom]] and [[concepts/photoshop|Photoshop]], offers both manual and [[concepts/automated-masking|automated masking]] tools. Manual methods involve drawing selections or using brush-based masks, while automated approaches leverage [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] to detect and isolate subjects with a single click. These [[concepts/ai-powered-tools|AI-powered tools]] analyze image content to distinguish between subject and background, significantly reducing the time required compared to traditional manual [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] techniques.

## Common Applications

Subject masking is frequently employed to sharpen a portrait subject while keeping the background soft, to brighten or darken a subject relative to its surroundings, or to adjust skin [[concepts/tone|tone]] and color separately from background elements. The technique is also useful for applying localized adjustments such as increasing clarity or reducing noise on a subject's face while preserving the aesthetic qualities of the background. This level of control allows photographers to enhance their portraits with precision and [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]].
