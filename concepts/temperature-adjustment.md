# Temperature Adjustment

Temperature adjustment refers to the process of correcting or modifying the color temperature of digital images, typically to compensate for lighting conditions or achieve a desired aesthetic. In photography and image editing, temperature is measured on the Kelvin scale, with warmer (more orange/red) tones at lower values and cooler (bluer) tones at higher values. This adjustment is one of the fundamental color correction tasks in post-processing workflows, allowing editors to neutralize unwanted color casts or intentionally shift the mood of an image.

## Methods and Tools

Temperature adjustments can be made through various tools depending on the software used. Many applications offer dedicated temperature sliders that simulate warming or cooling filters, while others provide color balance controls that achieve similar results. Adobe Photoshop's Color and Vibrance tool, introduced in the 2025 version, includes temperature adjustment capabilities as part of its expanded color correction suite. These tools allow photographers and designers to make precise adjustments to specific tonal ranges or globally across an entire image.

## Practical Applications

Temperature adjustments serve both corrective and creative purposes. Photographers often use them to correct for tungsten lighting (which produces warm casts) or fluorescent lighting (which can appear cool and green). Beyond correction, intentional temperature shifts are commonly used to establish mood—warming an image for nostalgia or intimacy, or cooling it for a contemporary or melancholic feel. The adjustment remains a cornerstone technique in digital post-processing across photography, web design, and visual media production.
