# Quantum Use Cases

[[concepts/quantum-computing|Quantum computing]] has long been positioned as a transformative technology with [[concepts/software|applications]] across industries ranging from drug discovery to [[concepts/financial-modeling|financial modeling]]. However, the practical distinction between theoretical potential and [[concepts/commercial-viability|commercial viability]] remains significant. While quantum computers [[entities/excel|excel]] [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] specific problem classes—particularly optimization, [[concepts/simulation|simulation]], and certain cryptographic tasks—the [[concepts/range|range]] of immediately deployable [[concepts/scenarios|use cases]] is considerably narrower than early enthusiasm suggested.

## Current Practical Applications

Organizations are finding realistic quantum applications in specialized domains where [[concepts/classical-computing|classical computing]] approaches demonstrably struggle. These include molecular simulation for pharmaceutical and materials [[concepts/science|science]], portfolio optimization for financial services, and certain [[concepts/machine-learning|machine learning]] problems. However, these implementations typically require quantum computers with substantially more [[concepts/qubits|qubits]] and lower error rates than currently available systems. Most present-day quantum devices function best in research and proof-of-concept contexts rather than production environments.

## The Hype-Reality Gap

The diminishing advantage narrative reflects several converging factors: rapid improvements in classical computing efficiency, the slow progress in [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]] quantum [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]], and the discovery that many initially-promising use cases can be addressed adequately through conventional methods. Additionally, the significant infrastructure, expertise, and capital requirements for quantum [[concepts/adoption|implementation]] limit [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]] to well-resourced organizations. This has shifted market expectations from imminent disruption toward a longer timeline for mainstream adoption, typically projected beyond 2030 for most industrial applications.
