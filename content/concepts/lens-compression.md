# Lens Compression

Lens compression refers to the optical effect where longer focal lengths appear to compress the distance between objects in a scene, making elements that are far apart seem closer together in the final image. This characteristic of [[concepts/telephoto-lens|telephoto]] lenses can be leveraged creatively in photography through panoramic stitching techniques.

## Panorama Stitching for Wide-Angle Composition

Shooting and stitching multiple [[concepts/images|images]] taken with longer lenses creates panoramic compositions that combine the compressed perspective of telephoto [[concepts/optics|optics]] with an expansive field of view. This technique allows photographers to achieve wide-angle compositions while retaining the spatial compression qualities associated with longer focal lengths. Unlike traditional wide-angle lenses, which can introduce distortion and alter spatial [[concepts/relationships|relationships]], panoramic stitching with telephoto lenses maintains the compressed perspective throughout the wider frame.

## Practical Applications

This approach offers photographers an alternative method for composing wide vistas without relying on smartphone panorama [[concepts/software|applications]] or ultrawide lenses. By capturing multiple overlapping frames with a telephoto lens and stitching them together, photographers gain greater [[concepts/power|control]] over both the final composition and the visual characteristics of their images, achieving a unique aesthetic that combines compression with breadth.
