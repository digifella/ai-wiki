# Meat Emulsion

**Meat emulsion** refers to a stable mixture of meat proteins, fat, and water, primarily achieved through the extraction and [[concepts/denaturation|denaturation]] of **myosin**. This process is critical for creating cohesive textures in processed meats such as Sausage, Hot Dogs, and Bologna, as well as influencing the binding properties of Burger patties and Meatballs.

## Mechanism: Myosin Extraction
The structural [[concepts/honesty|integrity]] of a meat emulsion relies on the solubilization of myosin, a contractile protein found in [[concepts/muscle-fibers|muscle fibers]].
- **Salt Activation**: Sodium chloride (NaCl) is required to dissolve myosin from the myofibrils.
- **[[concepts/mechanical-agitation|Mechanical Agitation]]**: Mixing or grinding provides the energy necessary to unfold myosin molecules, exposing hydrophobic regions that bind to fat and water.
- **Denaturation**: Heat or further mechanical [[concepts/stress|stress]] [[concepts/causes|causes]] the solubilized myosin to coagulate, forming a gel network that traps fat and water, preventing separation during cooking.

## Applications and Texture Control
The degree of [[concepts/myosin-development|myosin development]] dictates the final [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]] of the product:
- **High Emulsion (Snappy [[concepts/texture|Texture]])**: Extensive mixing and high salt content create a strong protein network, resulting in the firm, elastic bite characteristic of frankfurters and bologna.
- **Low Emulsion (Tender Texture)**: Minimal mixing preserves the [[concepts/integrity|integrity]] of individual meat particles, yielding a softer, more crumbly texture preferred in [[concepts/excellence|high-quality]] burgers.
