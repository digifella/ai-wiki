# Astronomy

Astronomy is the natural [[concepts/science|science]] concerned with the study of celestial objects and phenomena beyond Earth's atmosphere. It encompasses the observation and analysis of [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]], planets, galaxies, [[concepts/black-holes|black holes]], and other cosmic structures, as well as the physical processes that govern their behavior. As one of the oldest scientific disciplines, astronomy combines [[concepts/empirical-evidence|observational data]] with [[concepts/theoretical-physics|theoretical physics]] to develop [[concepts/models|models]] explaining the origin, evolution, and [[concepts/structure|structure]] of the universe.

## Observational and Theoretical Approaches

Modern astronomy relies on both direct observation and mathematical modeling. Astronomers use telescopes and other instruments across the electromagnetic spectrum—from radio waves to [[concepts/gamma-rays|gamma rays]]—to collect data about distant objects. This observational evidence is then interpreted through the lens of established physical laws, allowing researchers to understand phenomena ranging from stellar formation to the expansion of the universe itself.

## Major Fields of Study

The discipline encompasses several interconnected areas of inquiry. [[concepts/astrophysics|Astrophysics]] applies principles of physics to understand stellar processes and cosmic phenomena. [[concepts/cosmology|Cosmology]] addresses large-scale questions about the universe's structure, age, and ultimate fate. [[concepts/planetary-science|Planetary science]] examines worlds within and beyond our solar system, while observational astronomy focuses on the practical techniques and technologies used to gather celestial data. These fields are increasingly interdisciplinary, drawing on chemistry, [[concepts/geology|geology]], and other sciences to address complex questions about the cosmos.
