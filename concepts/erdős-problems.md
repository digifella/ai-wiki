# Erdős problems

**[[concepts/erdős|Erdős]] problems** refer to open mathematical conjectures and theorems proposed by the prolific Hungarian mathematician [[entities/paul|Paul]] Erdős. Characterized by their breadth across combinatorics, [[concepts/number-theory]], graph [[concepts/theory|theory]], and analysis, these problems often featured monetary prizes for solutions, ranging from $5 for easy problems to $1,000+ for significant conjectures. The collection serves as a benchmark for mathematical difficulty and creativity.

## Characteristics

- **Diversity**: Span multiple mathematical disciplines; no single unified theory governs them.
- **Prize Structure**: Erdős assigned cash rewards to incentivize solutions, creating a unique gamification of research.
- **Collaborative Nature**: Many problems were posed during collaborations, reflecting Erdős's nomadic, network-based approach to [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]].

## Notable Categories

- Graph Theory: e.g., the Erdős–Stone theorem.
- [[concepts/number-theory]]: e.g., distribution of [[concepts/number-theory|prime numbers]], [[concepts/prime-gaps]].
- Combinatorics: e.g., Erdős–Szekeres theorem.
