# Iceberg Collision

An iceberg collision occurs when a vessel strikes an iceberg, a hazard specific to polar and sub-polar waters where icebergs calve from glaciers and ice sheets. The most historically significant example is the sinking of the RMS Titanic on April 15, 1912. During its maiden voyage across the North Atlantic, the Titanic struck an iceberg in the early morning hours, resulting in the deaths of over 1,500 passengers and crew. The ship sank within hours of the collision, becoming one of the deadliest peacetime maritime disasters in history.

## Discovery of the Wreck

The location of the Titanic wreck remained unknown for decades until a [[entities/us-navy|US Navy]] mission searching for lost submarines led to its accidental discovery. This expedition identified the debris field on the ocean floor, confirming the site of the disaster. The discovery provided critical data for maritime safety reforms and initiated extensive archaeological studies of the wreck.
