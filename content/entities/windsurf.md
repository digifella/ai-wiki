# Windsurf

Windsurf is a water sport that combines elements of surfing and sailing. Practitioners use a specialized board equipped with a sail attached via a flexible boom and mast system. The rider stands on the board and manipulates the sail to catch wind for propulsion and control, while using body weight and foot positioning to direct [[concepts/exercise|movement]] across the water. The sport emerged in the late 1960s and became an Olympic discipline in 1984.

## Equipment and Technique

Modern windsurfing boards range from 2.4 to 3.5 meters in length and vary in volume depending on skill level and intended use. Sails typically range from 3.5 to 6.5 square meters. Riders must develop balance and coordination to maintain stability while simultaneously managing the sail, which involves tilting, rotating, and adjusting it relative to wind direction and intensity. Harnesses worn around the torso help distribute the sail's weight across the body.

## Disciplines and Conditions

Windsurfing encompasses several disciplines including wave riding in coastal areas, freestyle trick performance, and speed racing. Different conditions suit different styles—sheltered flat waters are ideal for [[concepts/learning|learning]] and freestyle, while coastal breaks attract wave riders. The sport is practiced in diverse environments from lakes and bays to ocean conditions, making it accessible across various climates and water bodies.
