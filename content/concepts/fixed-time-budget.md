# Fixed Time Budget

A fixed time budget is a resource allocation strategy where a defined period—rather than a specific [[concepts/output|output]] quota or completion milestone—serves as the constraint for work completion. In this model, teams or systems operate within a predetermined timeframe to accomplish objectives, after which work concludes regardless of perfection or remaining tasks. This approach contrasts with scope-based or quality-based constraints that may expand timelines unpredictably.

## Strategic Benefits

Fixed time budgets provide predictability in planning and resource allocation, as stakeholders know in advance when deliverables [[entities/will|will]] be available. This constraint encourages prioritization of high-impact work and can reduce scope creep, since teams must [[entities/make|make]] deliberate choices about which tasks to complete within the available window. The approach also accommodates uncertainty by accepting that perfect solutions may not emerge within the timeframe; instead, it emphasizes delivering the best possible result given realistic constraints.

## Applications in Autonomous Systems

In autonomous [[concepts/agent-development|agent development]], fixed time budgets enable rapid [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] cycles. Rather than waiting for a single comprehensive improvement, systems can run repeated experiments within defined intervals—each producing data and refinements that feed into the next cycle. This [[concepts/structure|structure]] is particularly useful where continuous [[concepts/learning|learning]] is valuable and where perfect solutions are either impossible to achieve or economically impractical to pursue. The method acknowledges that iteration [[concepts/speed|speed]] often matters more than individual iteration perfection.

## Relationship to Other Constraints

Fixed time budgets exist alongside other constraint [[concepts/models|models]] in project management. While scope-based approaches fix what must be delivered and let time vary, and quality-based approaches define acceptable [[concepts/open-standards|standards]] with variable scope, fixed time budgets prioritize schedule certainty. Organizations often combine these approaches, using time budgets for iterative phases while maintaining [[concepts/quality-gates|quality gates]] and scope expectations across a broader timeline.
