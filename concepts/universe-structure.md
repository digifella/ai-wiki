# Universe Structure

Universe structure refers to the large-scale arrangement and composition of matter and energy throughout the cosmos. The observable universe exhibits a hierarchical organization, progressing from individual galaxies to galaxy groups and clusters, then to superclusters connected by vast filaments of matter. These structures are separated by cosmic voids—relatively empty regions of space. This large-scale structure formed through gravitational processes acting on primordial density fluctuations established during the early universe.

## Dark Matter and Cosmic Composition

The large-scale structure of the universe is shaped significantly by dark matter, which comprises approximately 85% of the matter content in the cosmos. Dark matter's gravitational influence forms the scaffolding upon which visible matter accumulates, creating the observed distribution of galaxies and galaxy clusters. Observations of gamma-ray emissions from the Milky Way have provided potential evidence for dark matter annihilation signals, where weakly interacting massive particles (WIMPs) interact and produce detectable radiation. These signals, concentrated toward the galactic center, offer insights into dark matter distribution and properties, though definitive confirmation remains an active area of astronomical research.

## Evolution and Large-Scale Features

The universe's structure continues to evolve under the influence of gravity and cosmic expansion. Filamentary structures connect galaxy clusters across hundreds of millions of light-years, while voids between these filaments contain far fewer galaxies. This "cosmic web" pattern has been mapped extensively through large-scale galaxy surveys, revealing a non-uniform but organized distribution of structure throughout the observable universe.
