# Verifiable Facts

Verifiable facts are claims or pieces of information that can be objectively confirmed through evidence, observation, testing, or logical reasoning. They form the foundation of reliable communication and decision-making by establishing a clear distinction between what can be reliably established and what remains uncertain, speculative, or opinion-based. A verifiable fact is typically supported by multiple independent sources, reproducible methods, or logical demonstration that others can examine and validate.

## Role in AI Systems

In the context of AI agents and advisory systems, verifiable facts serve as the primary constraint for generating accurate and trustworthy outputs. AI models are instructed to function as expert advisors by prioritizing step-by-step reasoning that explicitly links conclusions to these verifiable data points. This approach mitigates the risk of hallucination by requiring the system to ground its responses in observable reality rather than probabilistic guesswork.

The operational framework for such agents often includes mechanisms for prompt clarification and iterative verification. When an AI encounters ambiguous inputs, it is designed to seek clarification rather than assume intent, ensuring that the subsequent reasoning process addresses the correct problem space. By adhering to strict protocols for evidence validation, these systems maintain a clear boundary between factual assertion and speculative analysis, thereby enhancing their utility in high-stakes advisory roles.
