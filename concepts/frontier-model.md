# Frontier Model

## Definition
A [[concepts/state-of-the-art-offering|Frontier Model]] refers to the most advanced, large-scale [[entities/ai|artificial intelligence]] models that represent the state-of-the-art in [[concepts/machine-learning|machine learning]]. These models are characterized by extreme computational scale, high-level [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] capabilities, and the [[concepts/emergent-behavior|emergence]] of complex, unprecedented abilities.

## Core Characteristics
- **[[concepts/advanced-reasoning|Advanced Reasoning]]:** Capable of complex [[concepts/open-source-philosophy|logic]], multi-step [[concepts/problem-solving|problem solving]], and high-order cognitive tasks.
- **Scale & Complexity:** Driven by massive parameter counts and unprecedented training [[concepts/training-data|data]] volumes.
- **Dynamic [[concepts/instruction-following|Instruction Following]]:** Ability to adapt to changing constraints and [[concepts/instructions|instructions]] in real-time.
- **[[concepts/structured-output|Structured Output]]:** Generation of precise, machine-readable, or logically structured results for downstream processing.

## Notable Variants: Jev
Recent developments highlight specialized [[concepts/frontier-intelligence|frontier models]] optimized for specific high-value tasks.

- **Jev:** A frontier model designed for rapid, structured [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] in software applications. It refines existing classification and decision models rather than introducing a new category.
- **Key Capabilities:**
    - Fast [[concepts/ai-inference|inference]] for time-sensitive decisions.
    - High adherence to dynamic instructions.
    - [[concepts/structured-data-conversion|Structured output generation]] for integration into software pipelines.
