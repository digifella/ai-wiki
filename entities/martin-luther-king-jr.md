# Martin Luther King Jr

Martin Luther King Jr. (1929–1968) was an American Baptist minister and civil rights activist who became the most prominent leader of the Civil Rights Movement during the 1950s and 1960s. Born in Atlanta, Georgia, he emerged as a central figure in the struggle against racial segregation and discrimination in the United States, particularly in the South. King's approach was grounded in nonviolent resistance and civil disobedience, principles he adopted from Mahatma Gandhi's philosophy.

## Major Contributions

King played a pivotal role in organizing and leading key events that reshaped American society. He was instrumental in the Montgomery Bus Boycott, which challenged segregation in public transportation, and later organized the 1963 March on Washington, where he delivered his historic "I Have a Dream" speech. His advocacy was crucial in the passage of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965, landmark legislation that dismantled legal segregation and protected voting rights for African Americans.

## Legacy

King was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1964 for his nonviolent struggle for civil rights for the marginalized. His assassination in Memphis, Tennessee, in 1968 sparked national mourning and further accelerated the civil rights agenda. Today, he is remembered not only for his political achievements but also for his enduring influence on purpose-driven leadership and the power of inspiring collective action toward social justice.
