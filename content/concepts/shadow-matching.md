# Shadow Matching

Shadow matching is the process of ensuring [[concepts/shadows|shadows]] in a compositing [[concepts/workflow|workflow]] align with the environmental [[concepts/light|light]] source, color temperature, and intensity. When combining elements from different sources—such as placing a photographed object into a new background—the shadows must correspond to the same lighting conditions to maintain visual [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]]. Mismatched shadows are among the most noticeable inconsistencies in composite work, as viewers intuitively understand how light behaves in physical space.

## Key Considerations

Effective shadow matching requires [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to several factors. The direction and angle of shadows must align with the apparent light source in the scene. Color temperature matters significantly; shadows in warm lighting environments appear differently than those in cool light, and this shift must be reflected in the composited elements. Shadow intensity and softness should also match the environmental conditions—hard shadows suggest direct, bright light, while soft shadows indicate diffuse or distant light sources.

## Technical Approaches

Shadow matching traditionally involves manual adjustment using painting tools, [[concepts/layer-styles|layer blending]] modes, and careful observation. Compositors analyze the existing shadows in their background plate and adjust composited elements accordingly, sometimes creating shadow passes separately. Recent developments include automated tools like [[concepts/photoshop|Adobe Photoshop]]'s [[concepts/beta-version|Harmonize feature]], an AI-powered function designed to automatically align color, lighting, and shadow properties between layers, reducing the manual work required for achieving consistency.
