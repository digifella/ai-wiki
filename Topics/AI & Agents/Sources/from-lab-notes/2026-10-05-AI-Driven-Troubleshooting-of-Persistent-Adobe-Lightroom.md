## AI-Driven Troubleshooting of Persistent Adobe Lightroom Classic Crashes
**Clip title:** I gave AI access to my [[concepts/catalog|Lightroom Catalog]]. Here's what happened...
**[[entities/tasia-custode|Author]] / channel:** Anthony Gugliotta
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BYHwZtcv1j8

### Summary
This video documents a photographer's journey to solve a persistent and debilitating problem with [[entities/adobe-lightroom|Adobe Lightroom Classic]] crashing unexpectedly. For two years, the [[concepts/creator|creator]] experienced inexplicable freezes and crashes when performing routine tasks like copying [[concepts/develop-settings|develop settings]], rating photos, or changing modules. This issue became particularly critical after an extensive photography trip to Iceland, where he captured 8,000-9,000 photos, [[concepts/fat-rendering|rendering]] the [[concepts/digital-imaging-workflow|post-production process]] nearly impossible due to the software's instability. Traditional troubleshooting and online searches yielded no solutions, leaving him to accept it as an unavoidable inconvenience.

Drawing on his previous successes using AI to manage mundane administrative tasks and even troubleshoot complex technical issues like a NAS [[concepts/connection|connection]] problem, the [[concepts/creator|creator]] decided to [[concepts/leverage-ai|leverage AI]], specifically an agent named [[concepts/claude-ai|Claude]], to diagnose his Lightroom woes. He granted Claude full access to his computer, framing the challenge as a complex technical puzzle that an AI might be uniquely positioned to solve, unlike human experts who would struggle to understand his customized networking setup or spend prohibitively expensive hours investigating. The AI's role, he emphasized, was not to replace his creative photography but to automate and resolve the difficult, administrative, and technical hurdles.

Claude successfully identified and proposed several actionable solutions to optimize Lightroom's performance and prevent crashes. Key [[concepts/recommendations|recommendations]] included managing Lightroom's [[concepts/catalog|catalog]] backups, which were accumulating 4-5GB weekly without deletion; the AI suggested deleting old backups and isolating the active catalog file to prevent backup software from attempting to save partially written, corrupted versions. It also pinpointed a third-party plugin for his Asus monitor's dial as a significant culprit, causing delays in flagging, rating, and labeling photos, leading to its recommended removal or disabling.

Further optimizations involved adjusting Lightroom's preview settings: changing the "Automatically discard 1:1 previews" option to "Never" to prevent constant regeneration, increasing the [[concepts/camera-raw|Camera Raw]] cache size from 5GB to 100GB for better handling of large raw files, and setting the "Standard Preview Size" to "Auto (3840px)" to match his 4K monitor. Finally, Claude uncovered a specific bug within Lightroom's new "Assisted Calling" auto-analysis tool. The "Automatically analyze all photos in catalog" feature, when enabled, was identified as a cause for crashes during large photo imports, leading to the recommendation to turn it off. Ultimately, the AI provided precise, implementable steps that resolved the long-standing crashing issue and significantly improved his photo [[concepts/creative-workflow|editing workflow]].
