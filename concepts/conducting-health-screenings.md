# Conducting Health Screenings

Health screenings are systematic procedures designed to identify disease, health risks, or abnormal conditions in individuals or populations who may have no symptoms. Operating as a key preventive tool in [[concepts/population-health|public health]] and [[entities/clinical-practice|clinical practice]], screenings enable [[concepts/health|healthcare]] providers to detect conditions at earlier stages when treatment is often more effective. Screenings differ fundamentally from diagnostic testing in that they are applied to asymptomatic populations to identify those at risk, rather than confirming suspected illness in symptomatic patients.

## Types and Applications

Screenings take various forms depending on the population and condition. Universal screenings are offered to all individuals in a given age or demographic group, such as mammography for breast cancer or cholesterol testing. Targeted screenings focus on high-risk populations based on factors like family history, occupation, or lifestyle. Common screenings include blood [[concepts/pressure|pressure]] measurement, blood glucose testing, cervical cancer screening, and colorectal cancer screening. Prenatal screening identifies risks during pregnancy, while newborn screening detects metabolic and [[concepts/genetic-disorders|genetic disorders]] early in life.

## Effectiveness and Considerations

The effectiveness of a screening program depends on several factors, including the prevalence of the condition in the population, the sensitivity and specificity of the test, and the availability of effective treatment for detected cases. A screening test should ideally have high sensitivity to minimize false negatives and adequate specificity to avoid unnecessary follow-up testing. Healthcare providers must balance the benefits of [[concepts/secondary-prevention|early detection]] against potential harms, including false positives that may cause [[concepts/anxiety|anxiety]] or lead to unnecessary treatment. Cost, [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]], and patient [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] also influence the [[concepts/success|success]] and reach of screening initiatives.
