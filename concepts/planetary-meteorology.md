# Planetary Meteorology

The study of atmospheric processes, [[concepts/weather-patterns|weather patterns]], and climate systems on planetary bodies. This field encompasses the dynamics of [[concepts/earths-atmosphere|atmosphere]], fluid dynamics in rotating frames, and radiative transfer [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] unique to specific planetary compositions and gravitational environments.

## Key Phenomena

### Saturn's Hexagonal North Pole
A persistent, massive, geometric cloud pattern located at Saturn's northern polar region. This feature represents a unique case study in jet stream dynamics and [[concepts/fluid-dynamics]] within gas giants.

*   **Discovery & Observation**: First identified by the [[concepts/voyager-2]] probe in 1981; later extensively characterized by the [[concepts/cassini-mission|Cassini mission]].
*   **Structure**: A stable, six-sided hexagonal cloud band surrounding the polar vortex.
*   **Theoretical Explanations**: Recent analyses suggest the shape may result from specific atmospheric wave interactions and shear instabilities.
*   **Related Research**: For detailed breakdowns of the geometric stability and recent findings, see [[lab-notes/2026-07-18-Saturns-Hexagonal-North-Pole-Mystery-and-Explanations|Saturn's Hexagonal North Pole: Mystery and Explanations]].

## Core Concepts

*   **Atmospheric Circulation**: Global-scale [[concepts/exercise|movement]] of air driven by thermal gradients and planetary rotation (Coriolis effect).
*   **[[entities/storm|Storm]] Systems**: From terrestrial hurricanes to the Great Red Spot on Jupiter, understanding storm longevity and intensity is critical.
*   **Climate Modeling**: Using computational models to simulate long-term atmospheric behavior and predict weather patterns on exoplanets and solar system bodies.
