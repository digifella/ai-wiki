# Semiconductor Supply Chain

The semiconductor supply chain represents the complex network of design, manufacturing, and [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] processes required to produce integrated circuits and chips used across industries. Traditionally, this chain has been fragmented globally, with specialized companies handling different stages—from design and fabrication to packaging and distribution. This [[concepts/specialization|specialization]] has created dependencies and vulnerabilities, particularly as demand for advanced semiconductors continues to grow across computing, automotive, aerospace, and [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] sectors.

## Vertical Integration Strategies

Some major technology companies have pursued vertical integration within the semiconductor supply chain, seeking to consolidate multiple stages of production under single corporate entities. This approach aims to reduce supply chain disruptions, improve cost efficiency, and maintain proprietary control over critical chip production. The strategy involves companies developing or acquiring capabilities in chip design, manufacturing facilities, and related technologies, rather than relying entirely on external suppliers.

## Advanced Semiconductor Manufacturing

The production of cutting-edge semiconductors, particularly those at advanced process nodes (such as 2-nanometer technology), requires enormous capital investment and specialized expertise. Manufacturing at these [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] involves complex photolithography processes, [[concepts/quality-control|quality control]] systems, and fabrication facilities (fabs) that represent some of the most technologically sophisticated industrial operations. Companies pursuing advanced chip development must balance the technical challenges of miniaturization with the economic feasibility of production at scale.
