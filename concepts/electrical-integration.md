# Electrical Integration

**Electrical Integration** refers to the process of connecting disparate electronic systems, components, or devices to function as a unified whole. In automotive contexts, this often involves merging modern electronics with legacy vehicle architectures, requiring careful management of voltage, grounding, signal compatibility, and physical concealment.

## Key Principles
- **Voltage [[concepts/regulation|Regulation]]:** Ensuring modern low-voltage components (e.g., LEDs, microcontrollers) are protected from legacy 12V/6V systems.
- **Signal Translation:** Converting analog signals to digital or vice versa for compatibility between old and new systems.
- **Physical Integration:** Concealing wiring and components to maintain aesthetic [[concepts/honesty|integrity]], particularly in Classic Car [[concepts/preservation|Restoration]] projects.
- **Safety & [[concepts/software-reliability|Reliability]]:** Ensuring that added electronics do not interfere with critical safety systems (e.g., Brake Systems, airbags).
