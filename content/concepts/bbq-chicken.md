# BBQ Chicken

BBQ chicken refers to chicken prepared using [[concepts/bbq|barbecue]] [[concepts/cooking-methods|cooking methods]], most commonly grilling or smoking, with the application of barbecue sauce, dry rubs, or other seasonings. The cooking technique imparts characteristic smoky flavors and develops a caramelized exterior while the meat remains moist inside. BBQ chicken is a staple across many regional barbecue traditions, particularly in North American cuisine, though variations exist globally in terms of sauce styles, wood choices, and cooking temperatures.

## Cooking Methods

The primary cooking approaches for BBQ chicken involve grilling over direct or indirect heat and smoking at lower temperatures over several hours. Grilling typically occurs at higher temperatures and produces faster results, while smoking uses wood smoke at temperatures between 225-275°F to develop deeper flavors. Both methods benefit from marinating the chicken beforehand or applying dry rubs containing spices such as paprika, garlic, cumin, and black pepper.

## Flavoring and Sauces

BBQ chicken is characterized by its sauce and seasoning profiles, which vary significantly by region. Common barbecue sauces are tomato-based, mustard-based, or vinegar-based, each associated with different American regional traditions. The sauce may be applied during cooking, at the end, or on the side. Dry rubs offer an alternative seasoning method that creates a flavorful crust without liquid sauce.
