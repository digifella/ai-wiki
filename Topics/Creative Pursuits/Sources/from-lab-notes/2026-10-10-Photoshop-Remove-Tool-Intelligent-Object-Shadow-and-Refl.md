## Photoshop Remove Tool: Intelligent Object, Shadow, and Reflection Removal
**Clip title:** [[concepts/adobe-photoshop|Photoshop]]’s Amazing New Remove Tool (Trace Erase)

### Summary
The video introduces an enhanced "[[concepts/photoshop-beta|Remove Tool]]" in [[concepts/beta-version|Photoshop Beta]], specifically highlighting its "[[concepts/related-elements|Related elements]]" feature, which functions similarly to [[concepts/adobe|Adobe]]'s "Project [[concepts/object-removal|Trace Erase]]" previously showcased at [[concepts/adobe|Adobe]] Max. This advanced tool intelligently removes objects from an image while also eliminating their associated [[concepts/shadows|shadows]], reflections, and other subtle traces, creating a much more seamless and realistic result than previous removal methods.

The presenter demonstrates this capability with several impressive examples. He first removes a car and its shadow from a winding road, then effectively eliminates a woman with an umbrella, her reflection, and even the surrounding water splashes from a wet path. Further demonstrations show the tool removing a kayaker and paddle from the water, flawlessly filling in the disturbed ripples, and even vapor-like chalk dust from around dancers, leaving a clean background. These examples illustrate the tool's ability to handle complex and diffuse elements with remarkable [[concepts/accuracy|precision]].

However, the video also addresses two important caveats. Firstly, while the initial removal is effective, the [[concepts/solution|resolution]] of the replaced area can sometimes be low. A new "Enhance" option resolves this by regenerating the area in higher detail, but using it, along with the "Related elements" option, consumes [[concepts/generative-credits|generative credits]] (10 credits per operation). Secondly, the "Enhance" feature, if not managed carefully, can subtly alter the entire image rather than just the specific removed area. To counter this, the presenter recommends a workaround: creating an inverted (black) mask on the "Remove tool edits" layer and selectively painting with white only where the high-[[concepts/solution|resolution]] generated area is desired, thus localizing the enhancement.

In conclusion, the enhanced "Remove Tool" with "Related elements" is a powerful and valuable addition to [[concepts/adobe-photoshop|Photoshop]] for tackling complex object removals, particularly those involving [[concepts/shadows|shadows]], reflections, or intricate details that leave subtle traces. The presenter advises users to be selective: for simpler removals, the standard "Remove Tool" (which doesn't use credits) is often sufficient. However, for challenging [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]] where manual cleanup would be time-consuming or difficult, the credit-costing "Related elements" and "Enhance" features offer a highly efficient and impressive solution, especially when combined with the [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] workaround for precise control.
