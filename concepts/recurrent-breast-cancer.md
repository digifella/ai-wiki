# Recurrent Breast Cancer

**Recurrent breast cancer** refers to breast cancer that has returned after a period of remission. It may recur in the same area (local recurrence), nearby tissues (regional recurrence), or distant organs (metastatic recurrence).

## Clinical Overview
- **Definition**: Return of cancer cells after treatment and a disease-free interval.
- **Prognosis**: Varies significantly based on receptor status (ER/PR/HER2), time to recurrence, and extent of spread.
- **Management**: Typically involves systemic therapy, surgery, or radiation, depending on prior treatments and disease biology.

## Emerging & Experimental Therapies
- **[[concepts/oncolytic-virotherapy|Oncolytic Virotherapy]]**: Use of genetically modified viruses to selectively infect and kill cancer cells while stimulating an immune response.
- **Self-Administered Protocols**: Investigational approaches allowing patients to manage certain aspects of therapy at home, reducing hospital visits.

### Notable Case Study: Dr. Beata Halassy
- **Profile**: Croatian [[concepts/virologist|virologist]] diagnosed with aggressive, recurrent breast cancer.
- **History**: Experienced multiple recurrences despite standard interventions including surgery and four rounds of chemotherapy.
- **Intervention**: Pursued self-administered oncolytic virotherapy.
- **Outcome**: Reported significant clinical improvement and long-term remission, challenging conventional prognoses for her specific case.
