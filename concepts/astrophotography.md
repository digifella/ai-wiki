# Astrophotography

**Astrophotography** is the specialized practice of photographing celestial objects such as [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]], constellations, planets, and nebulae. It requires specific techniques to capture [[concepts/light|light]] from faint sources against a dark background, often involving long exposures, high [[concepts/iso-settings|ISO sensitivity]], and precise tracking.

## Mobile Astrophotography

The advent of computational photography has democratized astrophotography, allowing smartphones to capture details previously requiring dedicated [[concepts/dslr-photography|DSLR]] or mirrorless cameras. Key advancements include:

*   **[[concepts/night-mode|Night Mode]] [[concepts/algorithms|Algorithms]]:** Multi-frame stacking and noise reduction to enhance [[concepts/low-light-performance|low-light performance]].
*   **[[concepts/sensor-size|Sensor Size]] & [[concepts/aperture|Aperture]]:** Larger sensors and variable apertures allow for greater light intake.
*   **Computational HDR:** Balancing [[concepts/dynamic-range|dynamic range]] between bright stars and dark landscapes.

## Hardware Evolution: iPhone Series

[[entities/apple|Apple]] has progressively improved its mobile astrophotography capabilities through iterative hardware and [[concepts/app-updates|software updates]]:

*   **[[entities/iphone|iPhone]] 11 Pro:** Introduced initial **Night Mode** capabilities, marking a significant step for mobile [[concepts/night-sky|night sky]] photography.
*   **iPhone 12–14 Series:** Enhanced sensor sizes and improved computational photography algorithms refined star detail and reduced noise.
*   **[[concepts/iphone-18-pro|iPhone 18 Pro]] Max:** Features a **[[concepts/variable-aperture|variable aperture]]** system, allowing for manual control over light intake and depth of field, potentially offering superior performance in extreme low-light conditions.
