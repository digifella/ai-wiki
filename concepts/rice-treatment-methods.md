# Rice Treatment Methods

Rice treatment is a fundamental step in preparing fried rice, as the texture and moisture content of the rice directly affect the final dish. The primary goal of rice treatment is to achieve separate, non-clumpy grains that can absorb flavors and ingredients evenly. Raw rice contains surface starches that cause grains to stick together, so proper preparation begins with rinsing the rice to remove excess starch before cooking.

## Cooking and Cooling

After rinsing, the rice should be cooked using a method that minimizes water retention, such as the absorption method with a slightly reduced water ratio or the pasta method where rice is boiled in excess water and then drained. Regardless of the cooking technique, the critical phase occurs after cooking. The rice must be cooled rapidly to halt the cooking process and allow moisture to evaporate from the surface. Spreading the rice on a baking sheet or large tray facilitates this evaporation.

## Refrigeration and Ingredient Integration

For optimal results, the cooked rice is typically refrigerated for several hours or overnight. This chilling process causes the starch molecules to retrograde, firming the grains and further reducing surface moisture. When ready to cook, the cold rice is added to a hot wok or pan. The temperature differential helps prevent sticking and allows the grains to fry individually. Common ingredient mix-ins include aromatics like garlic and ginger, proteins such as eggs, shrimp, or char siu, and vegetables like peas, carrots, and scallions, which are incorporated after the rice has begun to separate.
