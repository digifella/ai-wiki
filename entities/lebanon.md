# Lebanon

Lebanon is a Middle Eastern country located on the eastern Mediterranean coast, bordered by [[entities/syria|Syria]] to the north and east and [[entities/israel|Israel]] to the south. The nation occupies a strategic position within the Levant region, serving as a crossroads between Mediterranean and Middle Eastern spheres of influence. Its capital and largest city is Beirut, historically established as a major commercial and cultural center in the region.

## Geography and Demographics

The country's terrain is defined by the Mediterranean coastline to the west, the fertile Bekaa Valley in the interior, and the Anti-Lebanon Mountains to the east. Lebanon covers approximately 10,400 square kilometers and has a population of around 5-6 million people, though this figure has been significantly affected by internal displacement and refugee populations, particularly from Syria. The country's population is ethnically and religiously diverse, with Maronite Christians, Shia and Sunni Muslims, and smaller communities including Druze and other groups.

## Political Structure and Governance

Lebanon operates under a confessional political system in which key government positions are allocated among religious communities. The president is traditionally Maronite Christian, the [[concepts/prime-lens|prime]] minister Sunni Muslim, and the [[entities/speaker|speaker]] of parliament Shia Muslim. This power-sharing arrangement, formalized in the National Pact of 1943 and the Taif Accord of 1989, reflects the country's complex sectarian [[concepts/writing|composition]] but has also contributed to institutional fragmentation and [[concepts/governance|governance]] challenges.

## Regional Security Context

Lebanon's position has made it a venue for regional and international competition involving multiple state and [[concepts/civic-society|non-state actors]]. The country experienced a devastating civil war from 1975 to 1990, and continues to host Palestinian refugees and more recently Syrian refugees. The presence of Hezbollah, a significant political party and armed group designated as a terrorist organization by several Western nations, further complicates Lebanon's [[concepts/security|security]] environment and international relations.
