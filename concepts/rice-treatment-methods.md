# Rice Treatment Methods

Rice treatment is a fundamental step in preparing fried rice, as the texture and moisture content of the rice directly affect the final dish. The primary goal of rice treatment is to achieve separate, non-clumpy grains that can absorb flavors and ingredients evenly. Raw rice contains surface starches that cause grains to stick together, so proper preparation begins with rinsing the rice to remove excess starch before cooking.

## Cooking and Cooling

After rinsing, rice should be cooked using a standard water-to-rice ratio, typically 1.5 to 2 parts water per part rice, depending on the specific variety. Once cooked, the rice must be cooled rapidly to reduce surface moisture and prevent further starch gelatinization. Spreading the rice on a baking sheet or placing it in the refrigerator for several hours allows the grains to firm up and dry out, which is essential for achieving the desired distinct texture in the final fried rice.

## Ingredient Integration

Properly treated rice serves as a neutral base that readily accepts mix-ins such as proteins, vegetables, and seasonings. The dry, firm grains prevent the dish from becoming mushy during the high-heat stir-frying process. This method ensures that each grain remains distinct while coating evenly with oil and sauces, resulting in a balanced mouthfeel and consistent flavor distribution throughout the meal.
