# Logographic System

A writing-system in which characters represent words or morphemes rather than phonemes. Systems prioritize [[concepts/semantic-representation|semantic representation]], often requiring large character sets and significant memorization effort compared to phonetic scripts.

## Core Properties
- **[[concepts/relationship-mapping|Semantic Mapping]]:** Signs correspond to meaning units (e.g., ideograms, determinatives) rather than sound sequences.
- **High Information [[concepts/density|Density]]:** Single characters convey complex concepts, reducing space requirements but increasing [[concepts/cognitive-load|cognitive load]].
- **Lack of Phonetic Uniformity:** Pronunciation is often not deducible from the sign alone, limiting [[concepts/resilience|adaptability]] to dialectal variation.
- **Structural Complexity:** Distinction relies on intricate visual differentiation rather than combinatorial rules of smaller units.

## Historical & Contemporary Instances
- **[[concepts/chinese-characters|Chinese Characters]] (Hanzi):** Dominant logographic system; utilizes phono-semantic compounds where logograms combine semantic radicals with phonetic [[concepts/indicators|indicators]].
- **Egyptian Hieroglyphs:** Logo-syllabic mix; employed logograms alongside phonograms and determinatives for disambiguation.
- **Sumerian Cuneiform:** Began as logographic accounting [[concepts/tokens|tokens]]; evolved to incorporate syllabic and alphabetic elements over millennia.
- **Maya Script:** Highly logographic with syllabic supplements; used for ritual and dynastic records.

## Evolutionary Dynamics
- **Phoneticization Pressure:** Logographic systems frequently generate auxiliary phonetic scripts to manage character set size and improve literacy acquisition.
- **Reduction to Alphabetic:** Long-term evolution often trends toward alphabetic-system via fenianization, where signs reduce to represent minimal phonological units.
