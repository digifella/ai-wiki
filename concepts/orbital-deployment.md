# Orbital Deployment

**Orbital Deployment** refers to the process of placing payloads, satellites, or spacecraft into a stable orbit around a celestial body, typically [[entities/earth|Earth]]. This involves achieving sufficient velocity to counteract [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravitational pull]] while maintaining altitude, often utilizing Rocket Launch vehicles and precise trajectory calculations.

## Key Components
- **Launch Vehicles**: Systems designed to overcome atmospheric drag and gravity. Recent developments emphasize [[concepts/reusable-rocket-technology|Reusable Rocket technology]] to reduce costs and increase launch frequency.
- **Orbital Mechanics**: The application of [[concepts/physics|physics]] to predict and control the motion of objects in space, including insertion burns, orbital [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]], and station-keeping.
- **Payload Integration**: The [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] and securing of satellites, probes, or crew modules for the stresses of launch and the [[concepts/vacuum-of-space|vacuum of space]].
