# Carl Jung

Carl Jung (1875–1961) was a Swiss psychiatrist and psychoanalyst who founded analytical [[concepts/psychology|psychology]], one of the major schools of depth psychology. He initially trained under Sigmund Freud and collaborated closely with him during the early 1900s, but later developed his own distinctive [[concepts/theory|theoretical framework]] that diverged significantly from Freudian psychoanalysis.

## Key Contributions

Jung's most influential concepts include the collective unconscious, a deeper layer of the [[concepts/unconscious-mind|unconscious mind]] shared by all humanity containing universal archetypes such as the Shadow, Anima, and Self. He also developed the theory of psychological types, which categorized personality along dimensions of introversion-extraversion and four mental functions ([[concepts/human-cognition|thinking]], feeling, sensation, and intuition), a framework that influenced modern personality assessment tools. His therapeutic approach emphasized individuation—the process of integrating different aspects of the psyche to achieve psychological wholeness and self-realization.

## Legacy

Jung's [[concepts/ideas|ideas]] extended beyond psychology into culture, spirituality, and symbolism. He conducted extensive research on dreams, mythology, and religion, viewing these as [[entities/windows|windows]] into the unconscious mind. Though some of his theories remain controversial or have been superseded by modern research, his [[concepts/power|influence]] on psychology, literature, anthropology, and popular culture remains substantial. He spent much of his later career in Switzerland, continuing to develop and refine his theories until his death in 1961.
