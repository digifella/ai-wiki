# Gulf Cooperation Council

The Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) is a regional intergovernmental [[concepts/organization|organization]] established in 1981 to promote political and economic cooperation among six Gulf states: [[entities/saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], the United Arab Emirates, [[entities/kuwait|Kuwait]], Qatar, Bahrain, and Oman. The organization was formed during a period of regional instability and has served as a forum for [[concepts/dialogue|dialogue]] on shared security concerns, economic integration, and coordinated development [[concepts/policies|policies]].

## Structure and Governance

The GCC operates through a Supreme Council composed of the heads of state of member nations, which meets annually to set policy direction. A Ministerial Council handles ongoing diplomatic and administrative functions, while a Secretariat manages day-to-day operations. Decision-making typically requires consensus among member states, which can slow the pace of reform but ensures broad agreement on major initiatives.

## Economic and Political Focus

The organization has facilitated trade agreements and coordinated economic planning among member states, though economic integration has progressed unevenly. Beyond economics, the GCC has addressed regional security matters, border disputes, and diplomatic coordination on international issues. Member states maintain independent foreign policies, and the organization does not function as a political union. In recent decades, internal divisions have occasionally limited the organization's unified action, particularly regarding relations with Iran and Qatar's regional disputes.
