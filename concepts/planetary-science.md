# Planetary Science

Planetary science is the scientific study of planets, moons, and planetary systems throughout the universe. The discipline integrates knowledge from geology, atmospheric physics, chemistry, physics, and biology to investigate the composition, structure, evolution, and potential habitability of planetary bodies. Researchers employ multiple methodologies including direct observation through telescopes, analysis of data collected by spacecraft and rovers, and laboratory simulations of extraterrestrial environments.

## Methodologies and Scope

The field relies on a combination of remote sensing, in-situ exploration, and theoretical modeling. Remote sensing allows scientists to analyze planetary atmospheres and surfaces from a distance using spectroscopy and imaging. In-situ exploration involves sending probes, landers, and rovers to collect physical samples and measure local conditions directly. Laboratory simulations recreate extraterrestrial environments to test hypotheses about geological processes and chemical reactions under specific pressure and temperature regimes.

## Interdisciplinary Connections

Planetary science is inherently interdisciplinary, drawing heavily from astrophysics to understand the formation of planetary systems and from earth sciences to compare terrestrial and extraterrestrial geological processes. A significant area of focus is the search for biosignatures, such as the investigation of Mars soil composition and its implications for potential microbial life. By analyzing mineralogical and chemical data from missions to Mars and other celestial bodies, scientists aim to determine past or present habitability and the distribution of water and organic compounds.
