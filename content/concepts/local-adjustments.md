# Local Adjustments

Local adjustments refer to edits applied to specific regions of a photograph rather than to the entire image. This technique allows photographers to selectively enhance or modify particular areas—such as brightening a subject's face, adjusting color in a specific region, or correcting [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] in isolated zones—while leaving the rest of the image unchanged. Local adjustments are fundamental to non-destructive digital editing workflows.

## Implementation in Lightroom

[[entities/adobe-lightroom|Adobe Lightroom]] provides several tools for making local adjustments, including the brush tool, graduated filter, and radial filter. More advanced [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]], such as [[concepts/intersect-masking|intersect masking]], enable photographers to create precise selections based on specific criteria like [[concepts/color-ranges|color ranges]], luminance values, or subject detection. These masks can be refined and combined to target [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] with considerable [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]], reducing the need for manual brushwork and improving editing efficiency.

## Workflow Advantages

Local adjustments support [[concepts/non-destructive-editing|non-destructive editing]], meaning the original image data remains intact and edits can be modified or removed [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] any time. This approach is particularly useful in landscape and [[concepts/portrait-photography|portrait photography]], where different areas of an image often require different treatment. By isolating adjustments to specific regions, photographers can achieve more polished results while maintaining flexibility in their editing process.
