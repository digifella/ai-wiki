# Opinions

Opinions are subjective assessments or beliefs held by an individual, often formed through the synthesis of [[concepts/personal-qualities|personal qualities]], [[concepts/relevant-knowledge|relevant knowledge]], and [[concepts/experience|experience]]. Unlike [[concepts/purpose|objective]] [[concepts/factual-knowledge|facts]], opinions serve as the bridge between raw data and actionable decisions.

## Characteristics
- **Subjectivity**: Rooted in individual perspective rather than universal truth.
- **Fluidity**: Capable of evolution as new evidence or experiences accumulate.
- **Instrumental**: Serve as the foundation for Judgment and subsequent [[concepts/decision-making]].

## Formation and Judgment
According to [[entities/sir-andrew-likierman|Sir Andrew Likierman]]'s framework in [[lab-notes/2026-05-28-The-Elements-of-Good-Judgment|The Elements of Good Judgment]], opinions are not merely passive beliefs but active components of leadership capability. Key aspects include:

- **Synthesis**: Judgment is the ability to combine personal qualities with knowledge and experience to form opinions.
- **Interpretation**: In the absence of clear evidence, leaders are expected to provide an interpretation of available data that points toward the right choice.
- **Core Competency**: [[concepts/sound-choice|Good judgment]], manifested through the formation of robust opinions, is described as the core of exemplary [[concepts/leadership]].
- **Contextual Application**: Opinions become critical when facts are assembled but arguments remain ambiguous, requiring a decisive interpretive stance.
