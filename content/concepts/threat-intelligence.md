# Threat Intelligence

Threat intelligence encompasses the collection, analysis, and application of information about current and potential security threats to an [[concepts/organization|organization]]'s systems and data. It serves as a foundational component of security infrastructure, enabling organizations to understand adversarial capabilities, intentions, and methods. Threat intelligence sources range from internal security logs and network data to external feeds from government agencies, commercial vendors, academic institutions, and security research communities.

## Integration with RAG Systems

Threat intelligence has become increasingly relevant to retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems, where accurate, timely threat data must be rapidly synthesized and presented to decision-makers. Building effective RAG systems for security contexts requires integrating diverse threat intelligence sources—including [[concepts/indicators|indicators]] of compromise, [[concepts/vulnerability|vulnerability]] disclosures, attack patterns, and threat actor profiles—into searchable knowledge bases that can provide contextual [[concepts/responses|responses]] to security queries.

## Data Quality and Sourcing

The [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] of threat intelligence depends heavily on source quality and verification. Organizations typically combine multiple intelligence sources to validate findings and reduce false positives. Factors such as timeliness, [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]], and relevance determine whether threat intelligence can be effectively operationalized within security workflows and [[concepts/automations|automated systems]].
