# Techno Economic Modeling

Techno-economic modeling is an analytical framework that integrates technical and economic analysis to evaluate the feasibility and viability of large-scale infrastructure projects. Rather than treating engineering design and financial assessment as separate disciplines, this approach produces unified evaluations that demonstrate how technical decisions directly influence capital costs, operational expenses, and revenue generation. The methodology is particularly valuable for projects requiring substantial upfront investment where technical choices have cascading financial implications, such as space-based infrastructure and AI data centers.

## Core Methodology

The process begins with the development of detailed technical models that define system architecture, performance metrics, and resource requirements. These technical parameters are then mapped to economic variables, including material costs, labor, energy consumption, and maintenance schedules. By linking these domains, the model calculates key financial indicators such as the levelized cost of energy, return on investment, and payback periods. This integration allows stakeholders to identify cost drivers and optimize designs for economic efficiency without compromising technical integrity.

## Application in Space-Based Infrastructure

In the context of space-based infrastructure, techno-economic modeling addresses the unique challenges of high launch costs, extreme environmental conditions, and complex supply chains. For projects like orbital AI data centers, the model evaluates trade-offs between satellite mass, power generation efficiency, and thermal management systems. It assesses how technical innovations, such as modular construction or in-orbit servicing, impact the overall economic viability of the project. This analysis helps determine whether the long-term operational benefits justify the significant initial capital expenditure required for deployment.

## Relevance to AI Data Centers

For AI data centers, whether terrestrial or orbital, the model focuses on the relationship between computational density, energy efficiency, and cooling requirements. It analyzes how hardware specifications and software optimization affect operational expenditures, particularly electricity costs. By simulating various scenarios, techno-economic modeling provides insights into the optimal balance between performance and cost. This enables developers to make informed decisions about technology selection and site planning, ensuring that the infrastructure remains economically sustainable over its lifecycle.
