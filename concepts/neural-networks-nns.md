# Neural Networks Nns

Neural networks are computational systems inspired by biological neural structures found in animal brains. They consist of interconnected nodes, known as artificial neurons, organized in layers that process information through weighted connections. Each node receives inputs, applies a mathematical function, and passes outputs to the next layer. This layered architecture enables neural networks to learn complex patterns from data through a training process in which the network adjusts its internal weights and biases to minimize prediction errors.

The core architecture typically includes an input layer, one or more hidden layers, and an output layer. Information flows forward through these layers in feedforward networks, while recurrent networks allow connections to loop back, enabling the processing of sequential data. The efficiency of these systems relies heavily on matrix multiplication operations during the forward and backward propagation phases, which are computationally intensive and require significant processing power.

In the context of AI agents, recent developments have focused on optimizing these computational bottlenecks. A notable Tesla AI patent proposes a breakthrough involving the conversion of multiplication operations into addition operations. This approach aims to reduce the energy consumption and latency associated with traditional matrix multiplications, potentially allowing for more efficient deployment of neural networks in resource-constrained environments. Such optimizations are critical for scaling AI agents that require real-time processing and low power usage.
