# Color Theory

[[concepts/color-conceptstheorytheory|Color theory]] is the practical application of color [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] and corrections in photography and visual media. In the context of concert photography, understanding color theory becomes essential for managing the extreme [[concepts/color-casts|color casts]] produced by stage lighting. Concert venues typically use colored gels, LED systems, and specialized lighting equipment that create dominant color tints—such as deep blues, magentas, or warm ambers—that can overwhelm natural [[concepts/skin-tones|skin tones]] and scene details.

## Correcting Concert Lighting Color Casts

Adobe Lightroom provides several tools for addressing these challenging color conditions. The White Balance sliders, Hue [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]], and HSL (Hue, Saturation, Luminance) panel allow photographers to selectively target and neutralize specific color tints without affecting the entire image uniformly. [[concepts/exposure|Exposure]] and [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] adjustments often accompany color correction to restore detail in areas lost to extreme lighting ratios common in live performances.

The process typically involves identifying the dominant color cast, then using Lightroom's correction tools to either neutralize it or shift it toward more pleasing tones. Rather than forcing concert photos into artificially neutral colors, photographers often use color theory to balance competing color casts while preserving the atmosphere and energy of the lighting design itself. This requires understanding how colors interact and influence viewer perception of the final image.
