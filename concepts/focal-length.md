# Focal Length

Focal length is the distance, measured in millimeters, between a camera's lens and its sensor when the lens is focused at infinity. This optical measurement fundamentally determines how a lens captures and frames a scene. Common focal lengths range from 14mm or shorter for ultra-wide angles to 600mm or longer for telephoto lenses. A 50mm lens is traditionally considered standard, as it approximates the field of view of human vision most closely.

## Field of View and Composition

Focal length directly controls a lens's field of view—the angular width of the scene it can capture. Shorter focal lengths (14-35mm) produce wider fields of view, while longer focal lengths (85mm and above) produce narrower, more magnified views. This relationship affects composition significantly: wide-angle lenses emphasize foreground and spatial depth, whereas telephoto lenses compress perspective and isolate subjects. Understanding focal length helps photographers and cinematographers select the appropriate tool for their intended visual effect.

## Application in Panoramic Photography

In panoramic photography, focal length plays a key role in image stitching and perspective control. Wide-angle focal lengths are commonly used because they capture broad scenes efficiently, though any focal length can be used depending on the desired final composition. Consistent focal length across multiple shots is essential for successful panorama creation, as it ensures uniform perspective and reduces distortion artifacts during the stitching process.
