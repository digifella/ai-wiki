Energy capture refers to the process of collecting and converting energy from various sources into forms suitable for [[entities/storage|storage]], use, or redistribution. It represents a foundational step in energy systems, preceding both storage and consumption. The specific methods and technologies employed depend significantly on the [[concepts/energy-source|energy source]] being exploited, the scale of operation, and the intended application—ranging from small-scale personal devices to large industrial installations.

## Common Capture Methods

Energy capture techniques vary widely depending on the source. Solar capture uses photovoltaic cells or thermal collectors to convert sunlight into electricity or heat. Wind energy is captured through turbines that convert kinetic energy into electrical power. Hydroelectric systems capture the potential energy of flowing or falling water. Fossil fuel-based systems capture chemical energy through combustion, while geothermal systems extract thermal energy from beneath the [[entities/earth|Earth]]'s surface. Emerging methods include capturing energy from ocean waves, tidal movements, and temperature differentials.

## Applications and Scale

Capture systems operate at multiple [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] and serve different purposes. Utility-scale installations feed power into electrical [[concepts/grids|grids]], while distributed systems serve individual buildings or communities. Portable capture devices—such as hand-crank generators or solar-powered chargers—enable energy collection in remote or mobile contexts. Industrial processes often capture waste heat or byproduct energy that would otherwise be lost, improving overall system efficiency.

The effectiveness of energy capture depends on environmental conditions, technology efficiency, and system design. As energy systems transition toward renewable sources, capture technologies continue to develop, with improvements in efficiency, cost-effectiveness, and integration with storage and distribution networks.
