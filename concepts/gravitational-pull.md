# Gravitational Pull

Gravitational pull is the fundamental attractive force [[concepts/acting|acting]] between all objects possessing mass. Described mathematically by Newton's law of universal gravitation and refined by Einstein's general relativity, this force operates across all distances without shielding or elimination. The strength of gravitational attraction between two masses depends on their magnitudes and decreases with the square of the distance between them, following an inverse-square relationship.

## Role in Cosmic Structure

Gravitational pull is the dominant force governing large-scale cosmic structure, driving the formation of [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]], planets, and galaxies. Unlike electromagnetic forces, which can cancel out due to positive and negative charges, gravity is strictly attractive and cumulative. This property allows it to dominate on astronomical [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]], pulling vast amounts of matter together over time to create dense celestial bodies.

## Interaction with Dark Matter

The behavior of [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] provides a critical context for understanding gravitational limits. Dark matter interacts primarily through gravity and lacks significant [[concepts/electromagnetic-interaction|electromagnetic interaction]], meaning it does not emit, absorb, or reflect [[concepts/light|light]]. While its gravitational pull [[concepts/causes|causes]] it to clump into [[concepts/halos|halos]] around galaxies, it does not collapse into [[concepts/black-holes|black holes]] in the same manner as [[concepts/baryonic-matter|baryonic matter]]. This is because dark matter particles lack the mechanism for dissipating [[concepts/kinetic-energy|kinetic energy]] through radiation or [[concepts/friction|friction]], preventing the dense compression required for black hole formation under standard [[concepts/gravitational-collapse|gravitational collapse]] models.
