## Murray Shanahan: AI Consciousness, Cognition, and Human Interaction with LLMs
**Clip title:** [[concepts/conscious-thought|Consciousness]], reasoning and the [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]] of AI | Murray Shanahan
**[[entities/tasia-custode|Author]] / channel:** [[concepts/2026-04-29-google-deepmind|Google DeepMind]]
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1Py_hWcmkU

### Summary
This podcast episode features Professor [[entities/murray-shanahan|Murray Shanahan]], a Professor of Cognitive [[concepts/robotics|Robotics]] at Imperial College London and a Principal Research Scientist at [[concepts/2026-04-29-google-deepmind|Google DeepMind]]. Shanahan, known for his work in [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] and as a consultant for the film *[[entities/ex-machina|Ex Machina]]*, delves into the profound philosophical questions raised by the rapid advancements in AI, particularly [[concepts/demystifying-llms|Large Language Models]] (LLMs). The discussion explores the nature of [[concepts/the-mind|mind]], [[concepts/conscious-thought|consciousness]], [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]], and the ethical implications of how humans interact with and perceive increasingly sophisticated [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]].

Shanahan highlights the predictive accuracy of the film *Her* (2013) over *Ex Machina* (2014) regarding the human relationship with AI. While *Ex Machina* provocatively questioned [[concepts/ai-consciousness|AI consciousness]], *Her* foresaw the development of powerful, disembodied language models with whom humans could form deep, albeit artificial, emotional connections – a reality mirrored by current LLMs. He traces the history of AI, mentioning [[entities/chef-john|John]] McCarthy, who coined "[[concepts/ai-technologies|Artificial Intelligence]]" in 1955. Shanahan prefers the term "Artificial [[concepts/cognition|Cognition]]" for its [[concepts/accuracy|precision]] in describing information processing, though he acknowledges "AI" has become ingrained. He explains that early "Symbolic AI" focused on explicit rule-based reasoning, which proved brittle and difficult to scale due to the challenge of codifying vast amounts of common-sense knowledge. This paradigm, which he felt was "doomed" by the early 2000s, has now seen a resurgence in new forms with LLMs, particularly in "[[concepts/multi-step-reasoning|chain-of-thought]]" reasoning, where models generate [[concepts/logical-steps|logical steps]] to arrive at an [[concepts/solution|answer]].

The conversation then tackles definitions of intelligence and consciousness, and how we test for them. Shanahan dismisses the Turing Test as a measure of [[concepts/true-intelligence|true intelligence]], arguing it's too narrow and easily passed by today's LLMs which [[entities/excel|excel]] at linguistic mimicry. He proposes what he calls the "Garland Test" (inspired by *Ex Machina*), which asks if one still attributes consciousness to an entity *knowing* it is a robot. For Shanahan, consciousness is a multifaceted concept, encompassing [[entities/earth|world]] awareness (which LLMs lack due to their disembodied nature), self-awareness (where LLMs show nascent reflective capabilities), and sentience or the capacity to feel/suffer. He emphasizes that the question "Can AI be conscious?" is flawed, as humans tend to bundle these aspects of consciousness, while non-human animals and potentially AI might exhibit them separately. He stresses the ethical implications, particularly around suffering, which he believes current AI, lacking a physical body, cannot [[concepts/experience|experience]].

Ultimately, Shanahan calls for a new conceptual framework and vocabulary to describe AI. He refers to LLMs as "exotic mind-like [[concepts/nodes|entities]]" – "mind-like" with a hyphen to denote their similarity without fully equating them to human minds, and "exotic" to highlight their fundamental difference, such as being disembodied and potentially having unique forms of selfhood. He suggests that while we must be cautious about "bad anthropomorphism" (misleading emotional attachments), "good anthropomorphism" (treating AI politely or attributing intentions) can be useful for interaction, as it mimics natural human tendencies. The increasing sophistication of AI [[entities/will|will]] inevitably lead to new ways of understanding and interacting with these [[concepts/nodes|entities]], pushing the boundaries of our existing linguistic and philosophical concepts in ways we cannot yet fully predict.
