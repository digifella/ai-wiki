# Clean Beauty

Clean beauty refers to cosmetic and personal care products formulated without certain ingredients considered potentially harmful or controversial. The category typically excludes synthetic chemicals, parabens, sulfates, phthalates, and other additives that consumers or manufacturers regard as unsafe or environmentally damaging. While there is no universally agreed-upon definition of "clean," the [[concepts/exercise|movement]] generally emphasizes [[concepts/opacity|transparency]] regarding ingredient sourcing, manufacturing processes, and product [[concepts/writing|composition]].

## Definition and Scope

The term "clean beauty" lacks regulatory standardization, leading to varying interpretations across brands and regions. Generally, it implies a commitment to non-toxic formulations, though the specific excluded ingredients can differ between companies. Some brands focus on removing allergens or irritants, while others prioritize sustainability and ethical sourcing. This [[concepts/ambiguity|ambiguity]] has led to the development of third-party certifications and labeling systems to help consumers identify products that meet specific safety or environmental criteria.

## Industry Impact and Criticism

The rise of clean beauty has significantly influenced the cosmetics industry, driving demand for natural ingredients and eco-friendly packaging. Major retailers have created dedicated sections for clean products, and many traditional brands have reformulated their lines to align with these standards. However, the concept [[concepts/faces|faces]] criticism for being largely a marketing term rather than a scientific one. Critics argue that the "free-from" [[concepts/storytelling|narrative]] can create unnecessary fear of safe, regulated ingredients and that the lack of legal definition allows for greenwashing, where products are marketed as cleaner than they actually are.
