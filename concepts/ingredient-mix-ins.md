# Ingredient Mix Ins

Ingredient mix-ins are components added to fried rice during the cooking process to enhance flavor, [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]], and nutritional content. These additions are incorporated according to specific timing and techniques that ensure even distribution and proper texture in the finished dish. Mix-ins form a fundamental element of the [[concepts/fried-rice-formula|fried rice formula]], working alongside properly treated rice and cooking fat to create a balanced, cohesive dish.

## Categories by Cooking Time

Mix-ins are organized by when they are added during the cooking process. Proteins such as eggs, meat, and seafood are typically cooked first or separately to ensure they are fully prepared before being combined with the rice. Eggs are often scrambled in the wok before the rice is introduced, while denser meats may be stir-fried initially to develop a sear. Seafood requires careful timing to avoid overcooking, often being added midway through the process.

Vegetables and aromatics are generally added based on their required [[concepts/cooking-time|cooking time]] and desired texture. Hard vegetables like carrots or peas may be added earlier to soften, while delicate greens or scallions are often added at the end to retain their color and crunch. Aromatics such as garlic and ginger are usually sautéed at the beginning to infuse the cooking fat with flavor, providing a foundational taste profile for the entire dish.
