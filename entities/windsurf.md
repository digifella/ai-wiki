# Windsurf

Windsurfing is a water sport that combines elements of surfing and sailing, requiring practitioners to balance on a specialized board while manipulating an attached sail to harness wind power for propulsion. The sport emerged in the late 1960s as an evolution of traditional surfing, incorporating sailing mechanics to enable movement across various water conditions. Modern windsurfing boards typically measure between 2.4 and 3.5 meters in length, with the sail attached to a flexible mast and boom that allows the rider to adjust angle and trim.

## History and Development

The sport was invented in the late 1960s by Jim Drake and Hoyle Schweitzer, who sought to create a sailing craft that was easier to transport and handle than traditional dinghies. Their design featured a universal joint connecting the sail rig to the board, allowing the sail to rotate freely. This innovation led to the commercial production of the first windsurfing boards in the early 1970s, sparking a global craze. Over subsequent decades, the sport diversified into various disciplines, including slalom, freestyle, wave riding, and windsurfing racing, each with specialized equipment and techniques.

## Equipment and Technique

Windsurfing equipment consists of a board, a mast, a boom, and a sail. The rider stands on the board and holds the boom to control the sail's angle relative to the wind. Propulsion is achieved by adjusting the sail to catch the wind, while steering is managed by shifting body weight and tilting the board. The universal joint allows the sail to pivot independently of the board, enabling the rider to tack and gybe without lifting the rig out of the water. Mastery of the sport requires a balance of physical strength, coordination, and understanding of wind dynamics.
