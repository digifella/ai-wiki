# Agricultural field archaeology

A branch of archaeology focused on [[concepts/systematic-study|systematic study]] of artifacts and landscapes within active or historically agricultural fields, emphasizing non-invasive [[concepts/survey|survey]] techniques and [[concepts/land-use|land-use]] history.

Key developments:
- Denmark's government deputized private [[concepts/metal|metal]] detector users to survey farm fields, yielding unprecedented detail on historical land use and settlement patterns.
- The program demonstrates successful integration of amateur [[concepts/expertise|expertise]] into professional archaeological practice, with findings published in [[entities/scientific-american|Scientific American]].
- Notable field practitioners include [[entities/kristen-nedergaard-dreiøe|Kristen Nedergaard Dreiøe]] and [[entities/marie-aagaard-larsen|Marie Aagaard Larsen]], who conduct systematic surveys in southern Denmark's grain-producing landscapes.
