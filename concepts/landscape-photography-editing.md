# Landscape Photography Editing

Landscape photography editing encompasses a range of techniques designed to enhance natural scenes while preserving their authentic appearance. The editing process typically begins with fundamental adjustments to exposure, white balance, and contrast to establish a proper tonal foundation. Photographers work to recover detail in shadows and highlights, adjust color saturation and vibrance, and refine local contrast to bring out the dimensional qualities of terrain, sky, and vegetation.

## Core Editing Workflow

A standard landscape editing workflow progresses from global adjustments—which affect the entire image—to localized refinements. After addressing overall exposure and color balance, editors often use graduated filters or luminosity masks to selectively adjust skies, foregrounds, or specific tonal ranges. Clarity and dehaze sliders help define atmospheric depth, while vibrance and saturation controls are applied carefully to maintain natural color relationships.

## Automation and Masking Tools

Modern editing software, particularly Lightroom Classic, offers automated masking capabilities that streamline selective adjustments. AI-powered masking presets can isolate skies, landscapes, or specific color ranges, allowing photographers to apply targeted corrections without manual selection work. These tools accelerate workflow while enabling adjustments like sky darkening, foreground brightening, or subject isolation that would traditionally require manual mask creation. Such automation has become a standard component of contemporary landscape editing practice.
