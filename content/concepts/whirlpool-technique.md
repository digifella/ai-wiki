# Whirlpool Technique

The whirlpool technique is a method used in egg poaching that involves creating a circular current in simmering water before adding the egg. By [[concepts/stirring-technique|stirring]] the water with a spoon or whisk, the cook generates a vortex that helps the egg white wrap around the yolk more uniformly, resulting in a neater, more cohesive poached egg with better shape retention.

## Application

This technique is typically performed immediately before adding the egg to the pot. The water should be at a gentle simmer—not a rolling boil—and the whirlpool motion should be steady but not vigorous. Once the whirlpool is established, the egg is carefully slipped into the center of the vortex, where the spinning motion encourages the white to envelop the yolk rather than dispersing into the water.

## Related Methods

While the whirlpool technique is effective on its own, it is sometimes combined with other approaches to poaching, such as using vinegar in the cooking water to help set the egg white more quickly. The choice between techniques often depends on personal preference and the desired final [[concepts/texture|texture]] of the poached egg.
