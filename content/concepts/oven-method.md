# Oven Method

The oven method is a cooking technique for preparing bacon that uses controlled heat in an oven to achieve consistently crispy results across all pieces. Unlike stovetop pan-frying, which can produce uneven browning due to hotspots and manual turning, the oven method distributes heat uniformly throughout the cooking chamber, cooking all bacon strips [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] the same rate.

## Technique

To use the oven method, bacon strips are arranged in a single layer on a baking sheet, typically lined with foil or parchment paper for easier cleanup. The sheet is placed in a preheated oven at around 400°F (200°C) and cooked for 15-20 minutes, depending on the thickness of the bacon and desired crispness level. Some variations involve adding water to the baking sheet before cooking, which creates steam that can help render fat more evenly and prevent excessive spattering.

## Advantages

The oven method is particularly useful when preparing large quantities of bacon, as it eliminates the need for batch cooking or constant monitoring. The uniform heat [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] produces consistent results, and the method generates less grease splatter than stovetop cooking. Additionally, cooking bacon in the oven frees up stovetop space for other dishes, making it practical for meal [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] and entertaining.
