# Guessing Laws

Guessing Laws refers to the initial phase of Richard Feynman’s three-step scientific method, a framework designed to structure scientific inquiry. In this stage, a researcher formulates a hypothesis or educated conjecture regarding the operation of a natural phenomenon. This step prioritizes the active proposal of a tentative explanation over passive data collection, requiring the scientist to synthesize existing theoretical frameworks, prior observations, and established knowledge into a coherent prediction.

The process emphasizes the creative and intuitive aspects of scientific inquiry, distinguishing it from the subsequent steps of computation and empirical verification. By establishing a specific prediction early in the workflow, the method provides a clear target for mathematical modeling and experimental testing. This approach underscores the role of imagination in science, suggesting that progress often begins with a bold, albeit unproven, assertion about how the universe behaves.
