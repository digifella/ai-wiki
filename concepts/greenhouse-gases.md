# Greenhouse Gases

Greenhouse gases are atmospheric compounds that absorb and re-emit infrared radiation, thereby trapping heat within Earth's atmosphere. This natural process, known as the greenhouse effect, is essential for life as it maintains habitable surface temperatures. The primary greenhouse gases are water vapor, carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), nitrous oxide (N₂O), and fluorinated gases. Without these gases, Earth's average temperature would be approximately 60 degrees Fahrenheit colder than current conditions.

## Natural and Anthropogenic Sources

While greenhouse gases occur naturally in the atmosphere, human activities have significantly increased their concentrations since the Industrial Revolution. Fossil fuel combustion for energy and transportation releases CO₂, while agriculture and waste management generate methane emissions. Industrial processes produce nitrous oxide and fluorinated gases. These anthropogenic emissions have elevated atmospheric greenhouse gas levels beyond historical variations, intensifying the greenhouse effect and driving contemporary climate change.

## Climate and Air Quality Impacts

The accumulation of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere raises global temperatures, altering weather patterns, sea levels, and ecosystems. Beyond their role in climate change, elevated concentrations of certain greenhouse gases and their related pollutants contribute to unhealthy air quality. For example, methane and CO₂ often accompany other air pollutants from combustion sources that directly harm respiratory and cardiovascular health. Understanding greenhouse gases in relation to both climate systems and air quality is essential for addressing interconnected environmental health challenges.
