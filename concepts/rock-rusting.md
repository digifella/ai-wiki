# Rock Rusting

Rock rusting is a geological process in which iron-bearing minerals within rocks oxidize upon exposure to atmospheric oxygen. This chemical weathering occurs at or near the Earth's surface, transforming primary minerals such as iron silicates and iron sulfides into secondary iron oxide minerals, primarily hematite and goethite. The resulting rust-colored rocks serve as a visible indicator of long-term interaction between the lithosphere and an oxidizing atmosphere.

Extensive rock rusting took place during the high oxygen period, a geological era characterized by significantly elevated atmospheric oxygen levels. This widespread oxidation event contributed to the distinctive red coloration of the Australian continent's interior. The process remains a key example of how global atmospheric changes can drive large-scale surface geochemical transformations over geological timescales.
