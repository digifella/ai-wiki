From the Smarter Every Day channel, host Destin Sandlin embarks on a [[concepts/four-year-experiment|four-year experiment]] to [[concepts/solution|answer]] the question: Is it possible to manufacture a product entirely in America and be competitive in the marketplace?
The video documents the entire process of creating the "Smarter Scrubber," a grill brush designed to be a safer and more durable alternative to traditional wire bristle brushes. Destin's [[concepts/motivation|motivation]] stems from his personal history with American manufacturing—his parents were union autoworkers—and a realization during the 2020 pandemic about the country's dependence on foreign supply chains for critical goods.
The video is structured in three parts:

1. **Why:** Destin explains that while global market forces push manufacturing overseas, local production ensures a community's self-reliance, stability, and freedom. The pandemic highlighted this [[concepts/vulnerability|vulnerability]], inspiring him to try and build something locally.
2. **How:** This section details the challenging journey of designing and manufacturing each component in the United States.
	* **[[concepts/design|Design]]:** The scrubber uses a chain mail head on a heat-resistant silicone pad, which is safer than wire bristles that can break off and be ingested.
	* **Manufacturing:** The process involved extensive research and collaboration. The plastic components were made using injection molding, with Destin [[concepts/learning|learning]] CNC machining to create the initial molds. The metal handle was created through sheet metal stamping, a process that revealed a critical shortage of skilled tool and die makers in the US.
	* **Sourcing:** Sourcing American-made parts proved extremely difficult and costly. A simple stainless steel bolt cost nearly four times as much when sourced domestically. He also uncovered a "supply chain shell game," where chain mail ordered from an Indian supplier was discovered to have been drop-shipped from China.
3. **The [[entities/pitch|Pitch]]:** Destin pitches the final product, the Smarter Scrubber. He argues that while it has a higher upfront cost, its durability makes it more economical over time, referencing the "Boots [[concepts/theory|Theory]] of Socioeconomic Unfairness." The product is also safer and more versatile, with a detachable, dishwasher-safe head for cleaning cast iron pans.

Ultimately, the video is a compelling case study on the complexities, costs, and profound importance of reviving local manufacturing. Destin encourages viewers to support the experiment by purchasing the Smarter Scrubber, thereby investing in American jobs and the crucial knowledge of how to make things.
