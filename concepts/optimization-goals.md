# Optimization Goals

Optimization goals in the context of large language models refer to the key objectives that guide model development, training, and deployment. These goals represent the priorities that organizations and researchers pursue when building and refining LLMs, though these priorities often create competing demands that require careful trade-offs.

## Primary Objectives

The main optimization goals for LLMs typically include maximizing performance on standardized benchmarks, reducing computational costs during inference, and improving alignment with human values. Achieving high accuracy on tasks such as reasoning, coding, and natural language understanding remains a central focus for developers seeking to demonstrate capability. Simultaneously, there is a strong industry drive to lower the latency and energy consumption required for running these models, making them more accessible for real-time applications.

## Alignment and Safety

A critical component of modern optimization involves ensuring that model outputs are safe, truthful, and helpful. This process, often referred to as alignment, utilizes techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) to steer models away from generating harmful or biased content. Researchers must balance the model's raw capability with its reliability, ensuring that improvements in one area do not degrade safety standards or introduce new vulnerabilities.

## Efficiency and Scalability

As models grow in size and complexity, optimizing for efficiency becomes increasingly important. This includes reducing the parameter count through techniques like quantization and pruning, as well as improving training data quality to maximize learning per token. The goal is to maintain or improve performance while decreasing the financial and environmental costs associated with training and deploying large-scale systems, thereby enabling broader adoption across various sectors.
