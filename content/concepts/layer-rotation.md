# Layer Rotation

Layer rotation is the process of rotating a specific layer within a multi-layered [[concepts/structure|structure]]. The concept is most commonly associated with mechanical puzzles like the Rubik's Cube, where individual layers of the cube can be turned independently while keeping the rest of the structure fixed. This fundamental mechanic enables the puzzle's [[concepts/solution|solution]] and has become a core principle in puzzle [[concepts/design|design]] and manipulation.

## Digital Implementation

Layer rotation has been adapted into digital environments and interactive [[concepts/software|applications]]. Notable implementations include Rubik's Cube simulators built with [[concepts/htmljavascript|HTML/JavaScript]] and [[concepts/threejs|Three.js]], which allow users to perform layer rotations through software interfaces. These digital versions have extended the traditional concept by supporting [[concepts/dynamic-sizing|dynamic sizing]], enabling [[concepts/simulation|simulation]] of cube variants ranging from standard 3x3x3 configurations to larger formats such as 20x20x20 cubes.

## Applications Beyond Puzzles

The principles of layer rotation extend beyond puzzle-solving into broader contexts of computer graphics and [[concepts/image-editing|image editing]], where layered systems require rotational manipulation of individual elements. The concept remains relevant across creative and technical domains where multi-layered structures need precise [[concepts/power|control]] of individual component positioning.
