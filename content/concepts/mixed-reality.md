# Mixed Reality

Mixed Reality (MR) is a technology that blends digital content with the physical world, enabling [[concepts/interactive-experiences|interactive experiences]] where virtual and real elements coexist and interact in real time. Unlike Virtual Reality, which creates entirely digital environments, or Augmented Reality, which overlays digital elements on a camera feed, Mixed Reality anchors digital objects to physical spaces with spatial awareness and persistent interaction [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]].

## Technical Implementation

Mixed Reality [[concepts/software|applications]] typically require specialized [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] such as spatial computing devices or AR headsets that can map physical environments and track user position and [[concepts/exercise|movement]]. The technology uses [[concepts/computer-vision|computer vision]], depth sensing, and real-time rendering to create convincing [[concepts/integration|integration]] between digital and physical layers. [[entities/microsoft|Microsoft]]'s Mixed Reality platform, including tools within Studio 2, provides development frameworks and spatial mapping capabilities for creating MR experiences.

## Applications and Use Cases

Mixed Reality finds applications across [[concepts/design|design]], engineering, education, [[concepts/health|healthcare]], and manufacturing sectors. In these fields, MR enables visualization of complex data in physical space, remote collaboration with holographic representations, [[concepts/training|training]] simulations, and interactive [[concepts/product-design|product design]]. The ability to anchor digital content to real-world locations makes MR particularly useful for spatial [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] and complex technical work.
