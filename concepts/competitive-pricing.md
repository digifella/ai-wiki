# Competitive pricing

A strategy where businesses set prices relative to competitors to attract customers while maintaining profitability. Requires balancing cost structures, [[concepts/value-perception|value perception]], and market position to sustain viability.

- **Case study**: Destin - manufacturing in [[entities/america|America]]: [[entities/destin-sandlin|Destin Sandlin]]'s four-year [[entities/smarter-every-day|Smarter Every Day]] [[concepts/scientific-experiment|experiment]] manufacturing the "[[entities/smarter-scrubber|Smarter Scrubber]]" ([[concepts/space-jetpacks|safer]], durable grill brush) entirely in [[entities/united-states|America]]. Aimed to prove [[concepts/domestic-production|domestic production]] can achieve competitive pricing despite higher labor costs, motivated by parents' union autoworker background. Documents challenges of cost [[concepts/market-competitiveness|competitiveness]] in manufacturing without offshore outsourcing.
- **Key insight**: Domestic manufacturing requires optimizing supply chain efficiency and value proposition to offset higher production costs while maintaining price parity with imported alternatives.
- **Market implication**: Competitive pricing in domestic manufacturing hinges on [[concepts/opacity|transparency]] about quality/safety benefits (e.g., eliminating wire bristles) to justify premium [[concepts/pricing|pricing]].
