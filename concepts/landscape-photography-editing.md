# Landscape Photography Editing

Landscape photography editing involves a series of technical adjustments aimed at enhancing natural scenes while maintaining their authentic appearance. The process typically begins with global corrections to exposure, white balance, and contrast to establish a solid tonal foundation. Photographers then focus on recovering detail in shadows and highlights, adjusting color saturation and vibrance, and refining local contrast to emphasize the dimensional qualities of terrain, sky, and vegetation.

A standard workflow progresses from broad adjustments to specific local enhancements. Initial steps often include lens profile corrections and geometric straightening to ensure structural integrity. Subsequent stages involve using graduated filters or radial gradients to balance light between the sky and foreground, ensuring that neither area is overexposed or underexposed. This phase is critical for managing dynamic range, which often exceeds the capabilities of a single exposure.

Modern editing practices increasingly incorporate automated tools to streamline repetitive tasks. In Lightroom Classic, for instance, AI-driven masking presets allow for precise selection of elements such as skies, foregrounds, and specific textures. These tools enable photographers to apply localized adjustments with greater speed and accuracy, reducing manual effort while maintaining control over the final image's aesthetic. The goal remains consistent: to produce a visually compelling image that respects the natural lighting and composition of the original capture.
