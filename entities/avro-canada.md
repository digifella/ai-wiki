# Avro Canada

**[[concepts/avro-canada|Avro Canada]]** (A.V. Roe [[entities/canada|Canada]] Limited) was a Canadian aircraft manufacturer based in Malton, Ontario, known for significant innovations in jet propulsion and swept-wing design before its abrupt closure in 1962.

## Key Projects & Entities

*   [[entities/avro-arrow]]: The company's most famous project, a supersonic interceptor cancelled by the Diefenbaker government in 1959, leading to the company's dissolution.
*   [[entities/avro-c102-jetliner]]: A proposed transatlantic [[concepts/jet-transport|jet airliner]] designed to compete with the Boeing 707 and Douglas DC-8.
*   CF-100 Canuck: A successful all-weather interceptor that served as the backbone of the Royal Canadian Air Force's [[concepts/air-defence|air defence]].
*   Avro Canada V-100: A proposed supersonic bomber that influenced the design of the [[entities/avro-arrow]].

## The Avro C102 Jetliner

The [[entities/avro-c102-jetliner]] represents a critical but often overlooked chapter in Canadian aerospace history, overshadowed by the political controversy surrounding the [[entities/avro-arrow]].

*   **Design & [[concepts/ambition|Ambition]]**: The C102 was designed as a long-range, high-altitude jet airliner capable of non-stop transatlantic flights. It featured a swept-wing design and was intended to carry up to 100 passengers.
*   **[[concepts/historical-context|Historical Context]]**: As detailed in [[lab-notes/2026-07-15-Avro-C102-Jetliner-Canadas-Unfulfilled-Promise-in-Commer|Avro C102 Jetliner: Canada's Unfulfilled Promise in Commercial Jet Aviation History]], the project highlighted Avro's capability in commercial aviation, not just military defense.
*   **Cancellation**: The project was cancelled in 1959, largely due to the same political and economic pressures that led to the cancellation of the [[entities/avro-arrow]]. The Canadian government withdrew support, and Avro lacked the capital to proceed independently.
*   **Legacy**: The C102 is viewed as a "what if" scenario for Canadian commercial aviation. Its cancellation contributed to the decline of Canada's domestic aerospace industry, shifting focus toward military contracts and eventual reliance on foreign manufacturers.
