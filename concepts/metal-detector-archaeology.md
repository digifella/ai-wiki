# Metal detector archaeology

The systematic use of [[concepts/metal|metal]] detector technology in archaeological contexts to locate and recover metallic artifacts, balancing public engagement with scholarly rigor.

## Key aspects
- Legal frameworks vary: Some countries restrict detector use to prevent looting (e.g., [[entities/uk|United Kingdom]]'s Portable Antiquities Scheme), while others like Denmark have formalized [[concepts/software|programs]].
- Requires training to ensure proper documentation, context recording, and artifact [[concepts/preservation|preservation]].
- Reveals often-overlooked aspects of daily life (e.g., coins, tools, jewelry) that complement traditional excavation.

## Case study: Denmark's program
- Government deputized private detectorists to search farmland, creating a structured archaeological initiative.
- Findings reveal Denmark's historical landscape in unprecedented detail (e.g., settlement patterns, trade networks).
- Involves trained amateurs like [[entities/kristen-nedergaard-dreiøe|Kristen Nedergaard Dreiøe]] and [[entities/marie-aagaard-larsen|Marie Aagaard Larsen]] conducting systematic surveys in agricultural zones.
