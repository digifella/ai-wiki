## Photoshop Camera Raw Filter: Color Grading for Photo Tonal Adjustments

### Summary
This video [[concepts/tutorial|tutorial]], presented by [[entities/aaron-nace|Aaron Nace]] of [[entities/phlearn|PHLEARN]], focuses on a powerful, often "hidden" tool within [[entities/adobe-photoshop|Adobe Photoshop]] for advanced color grading: the [[concepts/camera-raw-filter|Camera Raw Filter]]'s Color Grading tab. The main topic is how to use this feature to add a professional artistic touch to photographs.

The tutorial begins by emphasizing the importance of converting your image layer into a Smart Object before applying any filters. This crucial step ensures a non-destructive [[concepts/workflow|workflow]], allowing for [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] and changes to be made at any time without permanently altering the original pixels. Once the layer is a Smart Object, the user navigates to Filter > [[concepts/camera-raw-filter|Camera Raw Filter]], which opens a comprehensive editing interface described as a "carbon copy of [[concepts/lightroom|Lightroom]] built into [[concepts/photoshop|Photoshop]]."

Within the Camera Raw Filter, the core of the tutorial lies in the "Color Grading" tab. This tab allows for precise control over the color and [[concepts/tone|tone]] of [[concepts/shadows|Shadows]], [Midtones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtones), and [Highlights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highlights), either individually or using a "3-Way" combined adjustment. The instructor demonstrates using a color wheel to select hues and saturation, with inner movements controlling saturation and outer movements affecting [[concepts/hue|hue]]. For more [[concepts/granular-control|granular control]], individual sliders are also available for [[concepts/hue|Hue]], Saturation, and [Luminance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminance) for each tonal [[concepts/range|range]]. The video highlights the utility of a before-and-after view to [[concepts/feynmans-three-step-scientific-method|compare]] changes as they are made.

Further [[concepts/fine-tuning|fine-tuning]] options include "Blending" and "Balance" sliders, which control the [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] of the color grading effect across different tonal areas. The instructor suggests starting with [[concepts/shadows|shadows]], increasing saturation to clearly see the effect, choosing a desired hue, and then subtly reducing saturation for a more natural look. A key takeaway is that color grading is an artistic process with no right or wrong approach, encouraging experimentation. The non-destructive nature of [[concepts/smart-filters|Smart Filters]] also allows users to re-enter the Camera Raw Filter to [[entities/make|make]] further adjustments, or even change the overall [[concepts/opacity|opacity]] and blend mode of the applied filter directly within Photoshop's layers panel, providing maximum flexibility and creative control over the final image.
