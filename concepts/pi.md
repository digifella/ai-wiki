# Pi

Pi (π) is a mathematical constant representing the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter. Its value is approximately 3.14159, though it cannot be expressed exactly as a finite decimal or fraction. Pi is a [[concepts/transcendental-number|transcendental number]], meaning it is not the root of any polynomial equation with rational coefficients. As a result, its decimal expansion continues infinitely without repeating.

## Mathematical and Physical Applications

Pi appears throughout [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] and [[concepts/physics|physics]] in contexts far beyond basic geometry. It is fundamental to trigonometry, calculus, and number theory, frequently appearing in formulas involving circles, spheres, and waves. In physics, it is essential for describing periodic phenomena, quantum mechanics, and general relativity.

## Cultural and Computational Significance

The constant has attracted significant attention in popular culture and computational research. In Carl Sagan's novel *Contact*, pi is featured as a potential source of hidden messages when analyzed in base-11 representation, highlighting its perceived depth and mystery. Computationally, calculating pi to billions of digits serves as a benchmark for testing supercomputer performance and verifying algorithms, with modern records exceeding tens of trillions of digits.
