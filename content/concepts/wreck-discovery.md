# Wreck Discovery

The wreck of the RMS Titanic was discovered on September 1, 1985, by a team led by oceanographer [[entities/dr-robert-ballard|Robert Ballard]]. The discovery occurred during a mission authorized by the [[entities/us-navy|United States Navy]] ostensibly to inspect the wrecks of two lost nuclear submarines, the USS Thresher and USS Scorpion. This official cover story provided funding and operational legitimacy for what was primarily an expedition to locate the Titanic, one of history's most famous shipwrecks.

## The Mission Context

The dual-[[concepts/motivation|purpose]] [[entities/nature|nature]] of the expedition reflected Cold War priorities and scientific interests. While the Navy's primary concern was examining the submarine wrecks to assess how deep-sea conditions affected nuclear vessels, Ballard and his team used the remaining time and resources to search for the Titanic. The ship had sunk in 1912 after striking an iceberg in the [[concepts/north-atlantic|North Atlantic]], and its precise location had remained unknown for over seventy years.

## The Discovery

Using deep-sea exploration technology including remotely operated vehicles (ROVs), Ballard's team located the Titanic [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] a depth of approximately 3,800 meters. The wreck was found roughly 400 miles off the coast of Newfoundland. The successful discovery demonstrated advances in underwater exploration technology and provided the first detailed visual documentation of the ship's condition on the ocean floor, revealing that the vessel had broken into two main sections.
