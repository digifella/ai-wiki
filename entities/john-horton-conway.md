# John Horton Conway

**[[concepts/john-horton-conway|John Horton Conway]]** (1937–2020) was a renowned English mathematician known for his work in group [[concepts/theory|theory]], [[concepts/number-theory]], and combinatorial [[concepts/game-theory|game theory]]. He is perhaps best known for inventing the Game of Life, a cellular automaton that sparked widespread interest in complex systems.

## Key Contributions

- **The Game of Life:** A zero-player game demonstrating [[concepts/emergent-behavior|emergence]] and complexity from simple rules.
- **Conway's Constant:** A mathematical constant related to the Look-and-say sequence.
- **Surreal Numbers:** Co-developed the class of numbers that includes real numbers, infinitesimals, and transfinite ordinals.
- **Monstrous Moonshine:** Significant contributions to the theory of finite simple groups and their [[concepts/connection|connection]] to modular functions.

## Mathematical Curiosities

Conway was known for creating functions and objects that challenged standard mathematical intuition.

- **[[concepts/conways-base-13-function|Conway's Base 13 Function]]:** A pathological function that takes on every real value in every interval. It is constructed using [[concepts/base-13-representation|base-13 representation]] to encode decimal digits, allowing it to exhibit the Darboux property while being discontinuous everywhere.
