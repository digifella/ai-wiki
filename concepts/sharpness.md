# Sharpness

Sharpness in photography refers to the clarity and definition of details in an image. It is determined by several factors including focus accuracy, aperture settings, shutter speed, and lens quality. A sharp image preserves fine details and produces crisp edges, while an unsharp image appears soft or blurred. Achieving sharpness requires careful attention to both camera settings and technique during capture.

## Challenges in Landscape Photography

Landscape photographers face particular difficulties in maintaining sharpness across an entire scene, since foreground and background elements may lie at different distances from the camera. Wide apertures provide more light but reduce depth of field, making it difficult to keep all planes in focus simultaneously. This technical constraint has led photographers to develop alternative approaches for capturing images with uniform sharpness throughout the composition.

## Focus Stacking

Focus stacking is a technique that addresses the depth-of-field limitation by combining multiple images taken at different focus distances. The photographer captures a series of photographs, adjusting the focus point slightly between each exposure while keeping other camera settings constant. These images are then merged during post-processing, with specialized software selecting the sharpest portions from each frame to create a final composite image. This method allows landscape photographers to achieve sharp detail from the nearest foreground elements through distant background features, even when a single exposure would require an impractically small aperture.
