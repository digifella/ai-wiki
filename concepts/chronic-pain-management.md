# Chronic Pain Management

[[concepts/chronic-pain|Chronic pain]] management refers to the medical and therapeutic approaches used to treat pain that persists for extended periods, typically lasting more than three to six months. Unlike acute pain, which serves as a protective warning signal for injury or illness, chronic pain often continues beyond the initial healing [[concepts/phase|phase]] and can significantly impact [[concepts/quality-of-life|quality of life]], mobility, and [[concepts/mental-health|mental health]]. Common conditions requiring chronic pain management include arthritis, neuropathic pain, fibromyalgia, lower back pain, and post-surgical pain syndromes.

## Treatment Approaches

Management strategies typically combine multiple modalities tailored to individual [[concepts/patient-needs|patient needs]]. Pharmaceutical interventions may include analgesics, anti-inflammatory medications, anticonvulsants, and antidepressants. Physical and occupational therapy help restore function and prevent deconditioning. Psychological interventions such as cognitive-behavioral therapy address the emotional and behavioral components of chronic pain. Other approaches include interventional procedures, acupuncture, and lifestyle modifications including [[concepts/exercise|exercise]] and [[concepts/sleep|sleep]] optimization.

## Neurobiological Mechanisms

Chronic pain involves complex neurobiological changes distinct from acute pain processing. Central sensitization, whereby the nervous system becomes increasingly responsive to pain signals, plays a key role in many chronic pain conditions. [[concepts/bdnf|Brain-derived neurotrophic factor]] (BDNF) is implicated in pain plasticity and neural adaptation, with altered BDNF signaling associated with both pain [[concepts/data-persistence|persistence]] and recovery potential. Understanding these [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] has led to the development of targeted interventions aimed at modulating neural pathways involved in pain processing.

## Integrated Care

Effective chronic pain management typically requires coordinated, multidisciplinary care involving physicians, physical therapists, psychologists, and other specialists. A biopsychosocial model recognizing the interaction of biological, psychological, and [[concepts/social-psychological-factors|social factors]] has become standard practice, moving away from purely biomedical approaches. Patient education and self-management strategies are central to improving outcomes and reducing reliance on opioid medications.
