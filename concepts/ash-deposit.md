# Ash Deposit

Ash deposit is a layer or accumulation of [[concepts/volcanic-ash|volcanic ash]], tephra, and other ejecta that results from the eruption of a volcano. This material can be transported through pyroclastic flows, ballistic trajectories, or by wind. It forms significant stratigraphic layers in geological contexts and plays a crucial role in dating eruptions. Ash deposits are critical for understanding volcanic histories and their environmental impacts.

- Located northeast of [[concepts/melbourne-region|Melbourne]], Australia, the Cerberean [[concepts/supervolcano|Supervolcano]] represents one of the largest known [[concepts/volcanic-eruptions|volcanic eruptions]] in Australian history.
- The volcano features a massive collapsed [[concepts/caldera|caldera]] spanning approximately 27 kilometers.
- Researchers have identified extensive ash deposits from this [[concepts/supervolcano|supervolcano]], providing insights into its [[concepts/explosive-history|explosive history]] and geological evolution.
