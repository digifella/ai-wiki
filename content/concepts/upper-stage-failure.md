# Upper Stage Failure

Upper stage failure refers to the malfunction or loss of the upper stage component of a launch vehicle during flight operations. The upper stage is responsible for final orbital insertion and payload delivery after the lower stages have completed their burn sequence and separated. Failures at this point in a mission typically result in loss of the payload and the vehicle itself.

## New Glenn Context

New Glenn is Blue Origin's heavy-lift launch vehicle currently in development. The upper stage of New Glenn has been a focus of engineering and [[concepts/testing|testing]] efforts as the vehicle progresses toward its initial operational flights scheduled for the mid-2020s. Like all large launch systems, upper stage [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] is critical to mission success and represents a key engineering challenge during the vehicle's development and qualification phase.

Upper stage failures can result from various causes including propulsion system malfunctions, structural issues, guidance and control problems, or failures in the avionics and flight [[concepts/software|software]]. The consequences extend beyond the immediate mission loss to potentially impact the schedule and confidence in the launch provider. Thorough ground testing, [[concepts/simulation|simulation]], and incremental flight validation are standard approaches to mitigating upper stage failure risks before operational use.
