# Flags

In Adobe Lightroom, flags function as a binary marking system that categorizes images into two distinct states: flagged and unflagged. This simple two-option structure allows photographers to efficiently manage large volumes of data during the initial review phase. By avoiding the complexity of multi-level rating systems or color labels, flags provide a rapid method for separating images worth retaining from those that should be discarded.

The primary utility of flags lies in their role as a first-pass filter during the culling process. Photographers typically use the "Flag" command, often accessed via keyboard shortcuts like 'P' for flag and 'X' for reject, to quickly sort through large batches of photos. This method streamlines the workflow by enabling users to isolate the best candidates for further editing while isolating or removing inferior shots without permanently deleting them from the catalog.

This binary approach supports a non-destructive editing philosophy, ensuring that the original files remain intact while the user’s selection evolves. Once the initial culling is complete, the flagged images serve as the definitive set for subsequent stages of post-processing, such as color correction and retouching. This separation of concerns between selection and enhancement helps maintain organization and reduces decision fatigue during the editing phase.
