# Decluttering

Decluttering is the process of removing unnecessary items from a space to create a more organized and functional environment. While initial decluttering projects can significantly improve a space, the primary challenge for most people is maintaining that order over time. Without systematic approaches, spaces naturally tend to accumulate clutter again, undoing previous efforts and creating a cycle of repeated reorganization.

## Preventing Clutter Recurrence

Effective decluttering requires both initial action and ongoing habits. Rather than relying solely on periodic major cleanups, sustained organization benefits from everyday decision-making practices that prevent clutter from accumulating in the first place. Gretchen Rubin's One-Minute Rule provides a practical framework for this maintenance phase: if a task takes less than one minute to complete, it should be done immediately rather than added to a growing pile. This approach reduces the mental burden of decision-making and prevents small items and tasks from becoming sources of clutter.

By applying this principle to daily activities—such as putting items away immediately upon finishing with them or quickly sorting mail rather than leaving it to accumulate—people can maintain organized spaces without requiring exhaustive reorganization efforts. The One-Minute Rule transforms decluttering from a project into a sustainable habit.
