# Self-Improvement Thesis

The hypothesis that [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] [[entities/will|will]] eventually reach a threshold where they can autonomously improve their own architecture, code, or capabilities, leading to recursive acceleration (often associated with the [[concepts/intelligence-explosion]] or Singularity).

## Core Mechanisms
- **[[concepts/ai-recursive-self-improvement|Recursive Self-Improvement]] (RSI):** Systems using their own intelligence to enhance their intelligence, creating a positive [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] [[concepts/loop|loop]].
- **[[concepts/autonomous-development|Autonomous Development]]:** Agents capable of [[concepts/writing|writing]], testing, and deploying code without direct human intervention.
- **Acceleration Threshold:** The point at which self-modification yields compounding gains in capability.

## Key Perspectives & Evidence

### Anthropic's Position
- [[entities/anthropic-institute|Anthropic]] asserts that AI is rapidly nearing critical thresholds for autonomous [[concepts/personal-development|self-development]].
- Primary concern: The [[concepts/speed|speed]] at which AI systems can iterate on their own [[concepts/code|codebase]] outpaces human oversight [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]].
- See detailed analysis in [[lab-notes/2026-06-06-Anthropics-AI-Self-Improvement-Thesis-and-Autonomous-Dev|Anthropic's AI Self-Improvement Thesis and Autonomous Development Concerns]].

### Historical Context
- Originally popularized by I.J. Good's "Intelligent Machines" (1965) and later von Neumann.
- Modern iterations focus on LLMs [[concepts/acting|acting]] as [[concepts/software-engineering]] assistants that loop back on their own outputs.

## Implications
- **Safety:** Difficulty in maintaining Alignment during rapid, unsupervised capability jumps.
- **Control:** Potential loss of human-in-the-loop [[concepts/governance|governance]].
- **Timeline:** Disagreement exists on whether this is imminent (2025-2030) or distant.
