# Electricity Supply And Demand

Electricity supply and demand refers to the continuous equilibrium between electrical power generated and distributed through a grid and the amount consumed by end users. Unlike physical commodities such as oil or grain, electricity cannot be stored efficiently at scale, necessitating that generation matches consumption in real time. This immediate balancing act is a fundamental operational requirement for maintaining grid stability, as any deviation can lead to frequency fluctuations that threaten the integrity of the entire network.

## Operational Mechanics

Grid operators must constantly monitor load levels and adjust generation output to maintain a stable frequency, typically 60 Hz in North America or 50 Hz in Europe. This process involves dispatching power plants based on predicted demand curves, which vary by time of day, season, and weather conditions. When demand exceeds supply, frequency drops, potentially triggering automatic load-shedding protocols to prevent cascading failures. Conversely, excess supply can cause frequency spikes, damaging sensitive equipment and requiring generators to reduce output or curtail renewable energy sources.

## Case Study: 2021 Texas Power Grid Outage

The February 2021 Texas power grid outage illustrates the critical consequences of supply-demand imbalance during extreme weather events. A severe winter storm caused widespread freezing of natural gas infrastructure and wind turbines, drastically reducing available generation capacity. Simultaneously, heating demand surged, creating a massive gap between supply and demand. The Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT), which operates an isolated grid not fully interconnected with neighboring regions, lacked sufficient reserve margins to cover the shortfall. This resulted in mandatory rolling blackouts to protect the grid from total collapse, highlighting the vulnerabilities of isolated systems and the importance of adequate reserve capacity during peak stress periods.
