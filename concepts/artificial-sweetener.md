# Artificial Sweetener

Artificial sweeteners and sugar substitutes are substances used to sweeten foods and beverages without the caloric load of [[concepts/sugar]]. They vary widely in chemical structure, origin, and metabolic impact.

## Types & Properties

*   **Sugar Alcohols (Polyols):**
	*   **[[entities/xylitol|Xylitol]]**: A naturally occurring [[concepts/sugar-alcohol|sugar alcohol]] found in plants and mushrooms.
	*   **Properties:** Sweetness comparable to [[concepts/sucrose|table sugar]]; ~40% fewer calories.
	*   **[[concepts/health|Health]] Impact:** Generally beneficial for human dental health (reduces cavities).
	*   **Toxicity Warning:** Highly toxic to dogs; [[concepts/causes|causes]] rapid insulin [[concepts/deployment|release]] leading to hypoglycemia and potential liver failure.
	*   *Source:* [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Xylitol-Human-Health-Benefits-and-Canine-Toxicity|Xylitol: Human Health Benefits and Canine Toxicity]]

*   **High-Intensity Sweeteners:**
	*   Aspartame, Sucralose, Stevia, Erythritol.
	*   Often zero-calorie or negligible-calorie.
	*   Metabolic effects vary by compound and individual [[concepts/microbiome-health|gut microbiome]].

## Key Considerations

*   **Caloric Content:** Varies from [[concepts/concept-of-nothingness|zero]] (high-intensity) to ~2 kcal/g (sugar alcohols).
*   **Glycemic Index:** Most have low GI, making them suitable for diabetes management.
*   **Safety Profiles:** Generally recognized as safe (GRAS) by FDA/EFSA for humans in moderate amounts, though specific contraindications exist (e.g., Phenylketonuria and aspartame).
*   **Dental Health:** Some sweeteners (like xylitol) are non-cariogenic or anti-cariogenic; others are neutral.
