# Karaage Recipe

Karaage (唐揚げ) is a Japanese culinary technique involving bite-sized, boneless pieces of meat, most commonly chicken, that are marinated and deep-fried. The defining characteristic of this dish is its preparation method, which distinguishes it from Western fried chicken traditions. Instead of relying on a heavily seasoned outer crust for flavor, the seasoning penetrates the meat during the marination process, resulting in tender chicken with well-developed flavor throughout.

The traditional marinade typically consists of soy sauce, sake, ginger, and garlic, which impart a savory and aromatic profile to the meat. After marinating, the chicken is coated in starch, usually potato starch or cornstarch, which creates a light, crispy exterior when fried. This coating absorbs less oil than traditional flour batters, contributing to a texture that is crisp on the outside and juicy on the inside.

Karaage is widely consumed in Japan as a popular snack, bar food, or component of bento boxes. It is often served with a wedge of lemon or sudachi to cut through the richness of the fried coating. Variations may include different marinade ingredients or the use of other meats such as pork or fish, though chicken remains the standard. The dish has gained international popularity for its balance of umami flavor and accessible preparation method.
