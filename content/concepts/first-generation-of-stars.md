# First Generation Of Stars

[[concepts/population-iii-stars|Population III stars]] represent the universe's first generation of stars, formed from primordial hydrogen and helium in the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]] without any heavier elements. These stars are [[entities/theoretically-media|theoretically]] predicted to be extremely massive and short-lived, burning out quickly compared to later stellar generations. Their existence has long been sought by astronomers as they would provide crucial insights into cosmic evolution and star formation in the early universe.

In April 2026, the [[concepts/jwst|James Webb Space Telescope]] ([[entities/jwst|JWST]]) detected spectroscopic evidence consistent with Population III stars within the distant galaxy [[entities/gn-z11|GN-z11]]. The observations suggested the presence of extremely young, massive stars with minimal metal content—a signature matching theoretical predictions for primordial stellar populations. This finding represents significant progress toward confirming the existence of these first-generation stars, though further analysis and observations are ongoing to confirm the interpretation.

The discovery in GN-z11 is notable because the galaxy itself is observed [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] a very early epoch in cosmic history, making it an ideal candidate for hosting Population III stars. Confirming the presence of such stars would advance understanding of how the earliest galaxies formed and evolved, and how the first heavy elements entered the universe through [[concepts/solar-nucleosynthesis|stellar nucleosynthesis]].
