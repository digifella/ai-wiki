## Prefabricated Homes Outperform 3D Printing in Addressing Housing Crises
**Clip title:** Prefab Homes Solved What 3D Printing Couldn't
**Author / channel:** Megaprojects
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gWq36bjRKgI

### Summary
The video provides a comprehensive comparison between prefabricated homes (modular and mobile) and 3D-printed homes, assessing their effectiveness in addressing global housing crises. While 3D printing initially promised solutions for housing shortages, reducing CO2 emissions, and boosting construction, the video asserts that the technology is not yet mature enough due to numerous logistical issues and insufficient construction quality. Instead, prefabricated homes are highlighted as the leading solution, poised to reach a $114 billion market by 2028 due to their attractiveness to homeowners.

Prefabricated homes are categorized into two main types: modular and manufactured (mobile) homes. Modular homes are built in factory modules, delivered to a site, and then assembled onto a permanent foundation. This "Lego method" allows for custom designs, efficient material use (up to 50% less waste than traditional construction), and energy-efficient manufacturing processes. They come equipped with all necessary utilities and can be used for residential purposes or temporary facilities like schools and medical offices. Mobile homes, while also factory-built, are constructed on a chassis, allowing them to be moved, and are completely finished off-site before being transported. Both modular and mobile homes are lauded for their speed of construction and ecological viability, often completed much faster and with less environmental strain than traditional builds.

In terms of safety and longevity, prefabricated homes are shown to meet or exceed the standards of traditionally built houses. They often utilize non-combustible materials like steel and calcium silicate boards for fire safety and are required to pass the same fire safety tests as conventional buildings. Corrosion is mitigated through galvanized steel and raised foundations for ventilation. For regions prone to natural disasters, prefabs can be reinforced with seismic foundations for earthquakes or steel frames and hurricane straps for storms, having demonstrated resilience against powerful hurricanes. The quality of prefabricated homes, particularly in the United States, adheres to the same stringent codes as site-built homes.

When directly comparing prefabricated homes to 3D-printed homes, the video concludes that prefabs are currently the superior option. While 3D printing can construct the "hull" of a house in days, it is not a complete product; significant manual work, including installing overhangs, electrical, plumbing, insulation, and roofing, is still required, along with a multi-week curing period for the concrete. Economically, 3D printing proves to be substantially more expensive overall (up to five times that of traditional construction, largely due to high project preparation and logistical costs for moving massive, precise equipment), despite potential savings on actual printing labor and material waste. In contrast, prefabricated homes are 10-50% cheaper than traditional builds, are completed faster, offer full utility integration, and are environmentally conscious, making them a more practical, complete, and cost-effective solution for current housing needs.
