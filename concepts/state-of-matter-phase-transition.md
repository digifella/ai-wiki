# State Of Matter Phase Transition

A state of matter [[concepts/phase|phase]] transition is a transformation between different physical states of matter—such as solid, liquid, gas, or plasma—that occurs when conditions like temperature or pressure change sufficiently. During these transitions, the properties of matter undergo discontinuous changes, including alterations to [[concepts/density|density]], crystal structure, and atomic arrangement. Common examples include the melting of ice into water and the boiling of water into steam.

## Types and Mechanisms

Phase transitions are classified as either first-order or higher-order based on the nature of the change. First-order transitions, such as melting and boiling, involve latent heat and abrupt changes in [[concepts/material-properties|physical properties]]. Higher-order transitions, such as the transition to superconductivity, show continuous changes in some properties but discontinuous changes in derivatives of thermodynamic quantities. The transitions occur because different states of matter represent energetically favorable configurations under specific thermodynamic conditions.
