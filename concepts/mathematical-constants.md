# Mathematical Constants

Mathematical constants are fixed numerical values that remain unchanged within mathematical systems and physical laws. Unlike variables, which take different values in equations, constants are invariant quantities that characterize fundamental relationships. They appear across mathematics, physics, engineering, and other scientific disciplines. Pi (π), Euler's number (e), the golden ratio (φ), and the speed of light in vacuum are familiar examples. These constants often represent measurable properties of the universe or describe essential mathematical structures that underpin natural phenomena.

## Patterns in Irrational Numbers

The concept of hidden patterns within the digits of irrational numbers, such as pi, has been a subject of both mathematical inquiry and popular speculation. In Carl Sagan's novel *Contact*, the protagonist discovers a binary message encoded in the digits of pi, suggesting that the universe may contain intentional design. This narrative device explores the idea that mathematical constants might not be purely random but could contain structured information. However, this remains a fictional construct rather than a scientific theory.

## Scientific Perspective on Pi

From a rigorous mathematical standpoint, pi is defined as the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter. It is an irrational and transcendental number, meaning its decimal expansion is infinite and non-repeating. While the digits of pi exhibit statistical properties consistent with a normal number, no definitive proof exists that every possible finite sequence of digits appears within its expansion. Consequently, the notion of finding specific, meaningful patterns like binary code in pi is considered mathematically unproven and physically unsupported by current scientific consensus.
