# Thinking Processes

## Overview
Thinking processes refer to the cognitive activities involved in [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]], [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]], and [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]]. These processes are studied in both [[concepts/human-cognition|human cognition]] and [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems. Recent advancements highlight the role of internal simulations and environmental representations, specifically [[concepts/world-models]], in enhancing AI [[concepts/reasoning-capabilities|reasoning capabilities]].

## Key Aspects
- **Human [[concepts/human-cognition|Thinking]]**: Involves conscious and unconscious mental activities, including [[concepts/memory|memory]], [[concepts/attention|attention]], and logical [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]].
- **[[concepts/ai-technologies|Artificial Intelligence]]**: [[concepts/models|Models]] like [[concepts/large-language-models|large language models (LLMs)]] simulate aspects of human thinking through pattern recognition and statistical analysis.
- **[[concepts/world-models]]**: Internal representations of the environment that allow AI systems to predict outcomes, simulate [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]], and plan actions before execution. This concept bridges the gap between passive data processing and active cognitive [[concepts/simulation|simulation]]. See [[lab-notes/2026-05-24-World-Models-in-AI-Concept-Implementations-and-Applicati|World Models in AI: Concept, Implementations, and Applications]] for detailed analysis of implementations and applications.
- **Clinical [[concepts/software|Applications]]**: [[concepts/integration|Integration]] of AI thinking processes in [[concepts/social-robotics|social robotics]] and elderly care support systems.
