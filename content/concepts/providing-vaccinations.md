# Providing Vaccinations

Providing vaccinations is a core clinical and [[concepts/population-health|public health]] function in which qualified [[concepts/health|healthcare]] professionals administer vaccines to individuals for [[concepts/health-promotion|disease prevention]]. This practice is fundamental to both individual patient care and population-level disease [[concepts/power|control]] efforts. Vaccinations work by introducing a weakened or inactive form of a disease [[entities/agent|agent]] into the body, stimulating the immune system to develop protective antibodies without causing the full illness. This preventive approach has proven effective in controlling or eliminating many infectious diseases that once caused significant morbidity and mortality.

## Clinical Administration

Healthcare providers administering vaccines must follow evidence-based protocols regarding vaccine selection, [[entities/storage|storage]], dosing, timing, and route of administration. Different vaccines require different schedules—some as single doses, others as series spread over time to build adequate [[concepts/immune-system-function|immunity]]. Providers must also assess patient eligibility by reviewing medical history, current health status, allergies, and medication use. Proper documentation of administered vaccines, including lot numbers and administration dates, is essential for maintaining individual health records and population [[concepts/vaccination|immunization]] registries.

## Community Health Role

In [[concepts/community-health-nursing|community health nursing]], vaccination provision extends beyond clinical settings to schools, workplaces, [[concepts/public-health|public health]] clinics, and outreach [[concepts/software|programs]]. [[concepts/community-health|Community health]] nurses play a key role in identifying underimmunized populations, addressing vaccine hesitancy through education, and ensuring equitable access to vaccination services. They work to increase vaccination coverage rates within communities, which is critical for achieving herd immunity thresholds that protect [[concepts/vulnerable-populations|vulnerable populations]] unable to receive vaccines.
