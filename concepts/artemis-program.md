# Artemis Program

The Artemis Program is a NASA initiative designed to return humans to the Moon and establish a sustainable presence for long-term scientific exploration. Named after the twin sister of Apollo in Greek mythology, the program serves as a continuation of the lunar science and exploration efforts initiated during the Apollo missions of the 1960s and 1970s. A primary objective of the program is to land astronauts on the lunar surface, with a specific focus on the south polar region where water ice deposits have been detected.

The program relies on two major hardware systems: the Space Launch System (SLS), a heavy-lift rocket designed to propel the Orion spacecraft beyond low Earth orbit, and the Orion spacecraft itself, which serves as the crew module for deep space missions. To facilitate lunar surface operations, NASA is developing the Human Landing System (HLS), which will transport astronauts from Orion to the lunar surface and back. This system is being developed through partnerships with commercial aerospace companies, marking a shift toward a mixed public-private approach to deep space exploration.

Artemis aims to land the first woman and the next man on the Moon, specifically targeting the lunar south pole. This region is of significant scientific interest due to the presence of water ice in permanently shadowed craters, which could be utilized for life support and fuel production. The program also intends to build the Lunar Gateway, a small space station in orbit around the Moon, which will serve as a staging point for both robotic and human exploration of the lunar surface and as a waypoint for future missions to Mars.

The timeline for the program has experienced several adjustments due to technical challenges and budgetary considerations. The Artemis I mission, an uncrewed test flight of the SLS and Orion spacecraft, was successfully completed in 2022. Subsequent missions, including Artemis II which will carry astronauts around the Moon, and Artemis III which aims for the first crewed lunar landing in over 50 years, have faced delays related to the development and testing of the Human Landing System and other critical technologies.
