# Diagnostic Criteria

**Diagnostic criteria** are standardized sets of rules used by [[concepts/health-care|healthcare]] professionals to identify specific medical conditions, psychiatric disorders, or syndromes. They serve as the [[concepts/framework-for-action|operational bridge]] between theoretical pathology and [[entities/clinical-practice|clinical practice]], ensuring [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] in identification, prevalence estimation, and treatment eligibility.

## Core Functions
- **Standardization**: Reduces inter-rater variability and ensures [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] across different clinicians and regions.
- **Classification**: Facilitates categorization within systems like DSM-5, ICD-11, or DSM-IV.
- **Research Utility**: Enables homogeneous cohort selection for longitudinal studies and intervention trials.
- **Access & Eligibility**: Determines qualification for services, accommodations, or medical interventions.

## Key Historical Shifts & Controversies
The evolution of diagnostic criteria reflects changing cultural, scientific, and political contexts. Significant debates often surround "broadening" vs. "narrowing" criteria.

- **[[concepts/autism-spectrum|Autism Spectrum]] Expansion**: The shift from Kanner-autism (early childhood, severe) to the broader [[concepts/autism|Autism-Spectrum-Disorder]] (ASD) umbrella in DSM-5 controversially merged Asperger’s and PDD-NOS.
	- [[lab-notes/2026-05-28-Uta-Friths-Critical-Look-at-Autism-Spectrums-Widening-Di|Uta Frith's Critical Look at Autism Spectrum's Widening Diagnostic Criteria]]: [[concepts/uta-frith|Uta Frith]] critiques this widening, suggesting the current spectrum model may be conceptually flawed and risks diluting the specific neurodevelopmental profile originally identified.

- **Trauma Models**: The introduction of PTSD and C-PTSD criteria marked a shift from purely endogenous models to trauma-informed diagnostics.
- **Neurodiversity Debate**: Critics argue that current criteria pathologize natural human variation rather than identifying distinct medical etiologies.

## Common Frameworks
- **Categorical**: Presence or absence of a disorder (e.g., binary yes/no).
- **Dimensional**: Severity [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] along specific traits (e.g., social communication deficits, restricted behaviors).
- **Hierarchical**: Exclusionary rules to prevent double-diagnosis (e.g., excluding mood disorders when psychosis is primary).

## Challenges
- **Overdiagnosis/Underdiagnosis**: Broad criteria may lead to false positives; narrow criteria may miss mild or atypical presentations.
- **Cultural Bias**: Many criteria sets are Western-centric, potentially misdiagnosing culturally normative behaviors as pathological.
- **Comorbidity Issues**: High overlap between disorders (e.g., ADHD and Autism) questions the distinctiveness of separate diagnostic [[concepts/nodes|entities]].
