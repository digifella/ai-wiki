# Salting Eggs

Salting eggs is a fundamental cooking technique that affects the texture and tenderness of scrambled eggs and other egg dishes. When salt is added to raw eggs, the sodium chloride dissolves and begins to denature the proteins in the egg white and yolk. This chemical process alters how the proteins coagulate during cooking, directly influencing the final dish's texture—from tender and custard-like to firm and separated.

## Timing of Salt Addition

The timing of salt addition significantly impacts the results. Adding salt immediately after cracking eggs initiates early protein denaturation, which allows the proteins to hold onto more water during the cooking process. This method, championed by America's Test Kitchen, results in scrambled eggs that are uniformly tender, moist, and creamy, preventing the common issue of rubbery or watery curds.

Conversely, adding salt only at the end of cooking or not at all leads to different structural outcomes. Without early salting, the proteins coagulate more rapidly and tightly, often squeezing out moisture and creating a firmer, sometimes grainy texture. While some chefs prefer this approach for distinct curd definition, the pre-salting method is generally recommended for achieving a consistent, delicate mouthfeel in standard scrambled eggs.
