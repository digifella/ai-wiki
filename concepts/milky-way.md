# Milky Way

The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy with a diameter of approximately 100,000 light-years, housing an estimated 100 to 400 billion [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]]. Our solar system resides within the [[entities/orion|Orion]] Arm, one of the galaxy's major spiral structures, located roughly 26,000 light-years from the [[concepts/galactic-center|galactic center]]. The visible architecture of the galaxy comprises a central bar-shaped core, distinct spiral arms extending outward, and a flattened disk composed of stars and interstellar gas.

## Structure and Composition

The galaxy's mass is dominated by a massive halo of [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]], which extends far beyond the visible disk and influences the rotational dynamics of the outer regions. At the heart of the galaxy lies a supermassive [[concepts/black-holes|black hole]] known as [[concepts/sagittarius-a|Sagittarius A*]], which anchors the dense central bulge. The disk contains the majority of the galaxy's visible matter, including young stars, nebulae, and interstellar dust, arranged in a pattern of spiral arms.

## Observational Challenges and Rotation Dynamics

Recent analyses of the Milky Way's rotation curve present significant tensions with established [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|theoretical frameworks]].

- **Data Anomalies**: New [[concepts/empirical-evidence|observational data]] regarding the galaxy's rotation curve defies standard explanations, challenging both the prevailing dark matter paradigm and Modified Newtonian Dynamics ([[concepts/modified-newtonian-dynamics-mond|MOND]]) [[lab-notes/2026-10-12-Milky-Way-Rotation-Data-Challenges-Dark-Matter-and-Modif|Milky Way Rotation Data Challenges Dark Matter and Modified Gravity]].
- **Dark Matter Distribution**: Traditionally, astronomers estimated that the galaxy contains approximately ten times more dark matter than normal matter, with its distribution inferred primarily from the flatness of the rotation curve.
- **Theoretical [[concepts/conflict|Conflict]]**: The new data suggests that neither standard dark matter halo models nor MOND can fully account for the observed kinematic behavior, indicating a potential gap in our understanding of galactic dynamics or the nature of [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]] itself.
