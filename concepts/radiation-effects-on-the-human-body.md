# Radiation Effects on the Human Body

## Overview
Ionizing radiation [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] in [[concepts/deep-space-exploration|deep space]] poses significant risks to astronaut [[concepts/health|health]], distinct from the **[[concepts/low-earth-orbit-leo|Low Earth Orbit]]** (LEO) environment. Key concerns include acute radiation syndrome, increased cancer risk, and central nervous system effects. Current [[concepts/mitigation-strategies|mitigation strategies]] rely on shielding, pharmacological countermeasures, and operational limits.

## Key Findings & Gaps
Research highlights critical deficiencies in current medical capabilities for missions beyond LEO, specifically regarding radiation [[concepts/secure|protection]]:

- **Medical [[concepts/skill-gaps|Capability Gaps]]**: Significant gaps exist in diagnostic and therapeutic protocols for radiation-induced pathologies during long-duration flight. See [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Identifying-and-Closing-Medical-Capability-Gaps-for-Huma|Identifying and Closing Medical Capability Gaps for Human Spaceflight Missions Beyond Low Earth Orbit]].
- **Authoritative Analysis**: **[[entities/moriah-thompson|Thompson]], Lehnhardt, Easter, Lemery, and Suresh** ([[concepts/nasa|NASA]]) emphasized the need for advanced medical autonomy and specialized radiation countermeasures at the AsMA 2023 Annual Scientific Meeting.
- **Risk Profile**: Deep space radiation (GCR and SEP) penetrates standard shielding, requiring biological and chemical mitigation strategies not currently fully operationalized for crew health management.
