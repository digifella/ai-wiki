# Continuous Monitoring

Continuous monitoring refers to the ongoing collection of [[concepts/health|health]] and physiological data using [[concepts/wearable-devices|wearable devices]] equipped with [[concepts/embedded-sensors|embedded sensors]]. These devices, worn directly on the body, gather real-time information about vital signs, activity levels, [[concepts/sleep|sleep]] patterns, heart rate, and other biological metrics without requiring the user to visit a healthcare facility.

## Technology and Data Collection

Wearable monitoring devices use a variety of sensors to detect physiological changes and transmit data to connected [[concepts/software|applications]] or healthcare systems. The embedded sensors can measure [[concepts/parameters|parameters]] such as electrocardiographic activity, body temperature, [[concepts/exercise|movement]], and blood oxygen levels. This continuous stream of data provides a longitudinal record of a person's health status throughout their daily life, rather than snapshots captured during clinical visits.

## Applications in Healthcare

Continuous monitoring has applications across multiple health contexts, from [[concepts/chronic-disease-management|chronic disease management]] to fitness and wellness tracking. Healthcare providers can use the collected data to identify trends, detect anomalies, and [[entities/make|make]] more informed clinical decisions. For patients, continuous monitoring can support self-management of health conditions and provide early warning signs of potential health changes that warrant medical [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]].
