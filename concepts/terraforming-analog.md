# Terraforming Analog

[[entities/biosphere-2|Biosphere 2]] was a terrestrial research facility located in [[entities/oracle|Oracle]], Arizona, completed in 1991 to serve as a [[concepts/closed-ecosystem|closed-ecosystem]] analog for [[concepts/planetary-science|planetary science]] and [[concepts/deep-space-exploration|space exploration]]. The structure enclosed approximately 1.3 million cubic feet of space, containing distinct biomes such as a rainforest, desert, ocean, mangrove wetland, and agricultural zone. It was designed to function as a scaled laboratory for studying the complex interactions required to sustain human life in [[concepts/disconnection|isolation]] from the external environment.

The primary [[concepts/purpose|objective]] of the project was to develop and test the technologies and ecological principles necessary for long-term human habitation on other planets. By creating a self-contained environment, researchers aimed to understand how biological and physical systems interact to maintain atmospheric balance, water cycles, and food production without external inputs. This data was intended to inform the design of future space habitats and provide insights into [[entities/earth|Earth]]'s own biosphere dynamics.

The [[concepts/scientific-experiment|experiment]] involved two distinct phases of human occupancy. The first crew, known as the "Biospherians," lived inside the structure for two years from 1991 to 1993. During this period, the team encountered significant challenges, including declining oxygen levels, the collapse of most pollinator insect species, and the proliferation of cockroaches and rats. These issues highlighted the extreme difficulty of maintaining a stable, closed ecological system and provided critical lessons for future life-support system designs.

Subsequent research and management changes occurred in the 1990s and 2000s, with the facility eventually coming under the management of Columbia University and later the University of Arizona. Today, Biosphere 2 serves as a field laboratory for global change research, focusing on [[concepts/climate-change|climate change]], carbon cycling, and ecosystem responses to environmental stressors. Its legacy remains a foundational case study in the fields of [[concepts/astrobiology|astrobiology]], ecological [[entities/national-academies|engineering]], and sustainable life support systems.
