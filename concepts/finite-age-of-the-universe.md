# Finite Age of the Universe

The **finite [[concepts/universe-age|age of the universe]]** is the cosmological conclusion that the universe had a beginning (the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]]) and has existed for a finite duration (~13.8 billion years). This concept resolves several observational paradoxes that arise from assuming an infinite, static universe.

## Key Implications

*   **[[concepts/solution|Resolution]] of Olbers' Paradox**: The darkness of the [[concepts/night-sky|night sky]] is explained by the finite age of the universe combined with the [[concepts/finite-speed-of-light|finite speed of light]]. We can only observe light from [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]] within our [[concepts/observable-universe|observable universe]], as light from more distant sources has not had sufficient time to reach us.
*   **Cosmological Horizon**: There is a limit to how far we can see, defined by the particle horizon.
*   **Evolution of the Universe**: The universe changes over time (expansion, cooling, structure formation), contradicting the Steady State model.

## Related Concepts

*   [[concepts/olbers-paradox]]: The apparent contradiction that an infinite, static universe should have a bright [[concepts/night-sky|night sky]].
*   [[concepts/observable-universe|Observable Universe]]: The spherical region of the universe comprising all matter that can be observed from [[entities/earth|Earth]] at the present time.
*   [[concepts/finite-speed-of-light|Speed of Light]]: The finite speed ($c$) that limits the propagation of information and light.
*   [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]]: The prevailing cosmological model for the observable universe's early development.
