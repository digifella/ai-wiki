# Ultra Wide Lens

An ultra wide lens is a camera lens with an exceptionally broad field of view, typically 100 degrees or greater. These lenses feature short focal lengths, generally ranging from 14mm to 24mm on full-frame cameras, with proportionally shorter focal lengths on crop-sensor formats. The expanded field of view allows photographers to capture substantially more of a scene than standard lenses, making them particularly valuable for landscape, architectural, and environmental photography.

## Visual Characteristics

Ultra wide lenses produce distinctive visual properties that define their aesthetic. They exhibit pronounced perspective distortion, particularly near the edges of the frame, which can cause straight lines to appear curved or converge toward vanishing points. This distortion becomes more pronounced as focal length decreases. The lenses also tend to render foreground elements as disproportionately large compared to background subjects, creating a heightened sense of depth and spatial separation within the image.

## Applications

Beyond single-frame photography, ultra wide lenses are instrumental in panoramic workflows. Photographers commonly use them as the foundation for image stitching techniques, where multiple overlapping exposures are digitally combined to create an even wider or higher-resolution composite image. This approach leverages the lens's broad coverage while allowing greater flexibility in final composition and resolution. Ultra wide lenses are also practical for confined spaces such as interiors, where stepping back to fit content into frame is impossible.
