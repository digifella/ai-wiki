# Gantt Chart

A Gantt chart is a horizontal bar chart utilized in project management to illustrate a project schedule. Each task or activity is represented by a horizontal bar positioned along a timeline, where the length of the bar corresponds to the task's duration. The horizontal axis displays time in increments such as days, weeks, or months, while the vertical axis lists individual tasks or work packages. This visual format allows project managers and team members to quickly assess project scope, task dependencies, and the overall project duration.

## Structure and Components

The chart typically consists of a vertical list of tasks on the left and a horizontal time scale on the top. Bars are placed on the chart to represent the start and end dates of each task. The position and length of the bars indicate the start date, duration, and end date of each task. Dependencies between tasks are often shown with arrows or lines connecting the bars, indicating which tasks must be completed before others can begin.

## History and Evolution

The concept was developed by Henry Gantt in the 1910s as a method for visualizing production schedules. It gained widespread popularity during World War II for its ability to manage complex military logistics and construction projects. While originally created for manual plotting, modern project management software has automated the creation and updating of Gantt charts, allowing for dynamic adjustments to schedules as project parameters change.

## Applications and Limitations

Gantt charts are widely used in construction, software development, and engineering projects to track progress against a baseline schedule. They are particularly effective for communicating timelines to stakeholders and identifying critical paths. However, they can become difficult to read in highly complex projects with thousands of tasks, and they may not adequately represent resource constraints or non-linear project workflows without additional data layers.
