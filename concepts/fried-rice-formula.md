# Fried Rice Formula

Fried rice is a versatile dish whose success depends primarily on two critical factors: proper rice preparation and thoughtful ingredient selection. The foundation of quality fried rice relies on using the correct type of cooked rice and preparing it correctly before frying. This formula-based approach allows cooks to produce successful fried rice consistently, regardless of which specific ingredients are available or preferred.

## Rice Treatment

The most important step in making fried rice is proper rice preparation. Day-old cooked rice that has been refrigerated overnight works best, as the cooling process allows the rice grains to firm up and separate more easily during cooking. Fresh, warm rice contains too much moisture and will clump together or become mushy when fried. Before cooking, the refrigerated rice should be broken up gently to separate any clumped grains. Some cooks spread the rice on a tray to allow it to come closer to room temperature, which promotes more even heat distribution during frying. Short-grain or medium-grain white rice is generally preferred over long-grain varieties, as it absorbs flavoring more effectively and achieves better texture.

## Ingredient Selection

Beyond rice, the fried rice formula accommodates three main categories of mix-ins: proteins, vegetables, and aromatics. Proteins such as eggs, chicken, shrimp, or tofu should be cooked separately before adding to the rice to ensure proper texture and flavor development. Vegetables contribute both nutrition and flavor, with common choices including peas, carrots, onions, and bell peppers, which are typically diced small for even cooking. Aromatics like garlic, ginger, and green onions provide foundational flavor and are usually added early in the cooking process. The specific ingredients chosen can vary widely based on availability, preference, and cultural tradition, while the underlying technique remains consistent.
