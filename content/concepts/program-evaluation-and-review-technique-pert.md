# Program Evaluation And Review Technique (PERT)

Program Evaluation and Review Technique (PERT) is a statistical project management methodology developed in the 1950s by the [[entities/us-navy|U.S. Navy]] for the Polaris missile program. It provides a framework for planning and managing projects with high uncertainty, particularly those involving novel or complex work where task durations cannot be predicted with precision. PERT is commonly used alongside the [[concepts/critical-path-method-cpm|Critical Path Method (CPM)]] to estimate project timelines and manage risk.

## Key Characteristics

PERT uses a three-point estimation approach to account for uncertainty in task duration: optimistic time (best-case scenario), pessimistic time (worst-case scenario), and most likely time (expected scenario). These estimates are combined using a weighted formula to calculate expected duration. This probabilistic approach allows project managers to identify risks early and build realistic schedules that account for variability, rather than assuming fixed task lengths. PERT also emphasizes identifying the critical path—the longest sequence of dependent tasks that determines overall project completion.

## Application and Limitations

PERT is particularly valuable in research and development, aerospace, defense, and other domains where task uncertainty is inherent. However, the methodology requires significant effort to gather and analyze three estimates per task, making it less practical for smaller projects or those with well-defined, repeatable work. Many organizations use a hybrid approach, applying PERT principles selectively to high-risk or uncertain project phases while using simpler estimation methods elsewhere.
