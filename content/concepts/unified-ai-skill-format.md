# Unified AI Skill Format

The [[concepts/agent-first-organizational-infrastructure|Unified AI Skill Format]] is a standardized file format developed collaboratively by [[entities/anthropic-institute|Anthropic]], [[entities/openai|OpenAI]], and [[entities/microsoft|Microsoft]] to enable interoperability between [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] and [[concepts/organization|organizational systems]]. Announced in April 2026, the format establishes a common specification for defining, packaging, and deploying [[concepts/ai-proficiency|AI skills]]—discrete, reusable units of AI capability—across different platforms and ecosystems. This agreement represents a significant step toward creating a shared infrastructure layer that allows [[concepts/ai-agents|AI agents]] built by different organizations to recognize and utilize [[concepts/skills|skills]] in a consistent manner.

## Purpose and Scope

The format was designed to address fragmentation in how AI skills are defined and integrated into [[concepts/agentic-systems|agent systems]]. By establishing a single file format standard, the three organizations aimed to reduce technical barriers to [[concepts/skill|skill]] sharing and reuse, enabling organizations to build AI systems that are not locked into proprietary implementations. The standardization supports [[concepts/ai-agent-skill-calls|agent-first organizational infrastructure]], prioritizing how autonomous systems [[entities/will|will]] interact with and extend their [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] rather than organizing systems primarily around human-facing interfaces.

## Industry Impact

The collaborative agreement among three major AI organizations signals industry [[concepts/exercise|movement]] toward [[concepts/open-standards|open standards]] for [[concepts/agentic-frameworks|agentic systems]]. By creating agent-compatible organizational infrastructure, the format potentially accelerates AI [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] in enterprise environments where interoperability and portability are critical concerns. The standard allows organizations to mix and match skills and [[concepts/agents|agents]] from different sources, reducing vendor lock-in and fostering a more modular approach to building AI systems.
