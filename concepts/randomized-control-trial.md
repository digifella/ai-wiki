# Randomized Control Trial

A randomized controlled trial (RCT) is a research design in which participants are randomly assigned to either receive an intervention (treatment group) or standard care or placebo (control group). Random assignment is the defining feature of an RCT and helps ensure that any observed differences in outcomes between groups are likely due to the intervention itself rather than to pre-existing differences between participants. This approach reduces selection bias and creates groups that are comparable at baseline across known and unknown variables.

## Key methodological features

The random allocation process distinguishes RCTs from other research designs. By distributing participants unpredictably between groups, researchers minimize the risk that systematic differences in participant characteristics will confound results. Both known factors (age, severity of condition) and unknown factors that might influence outcomes are theoretically distributed evenly across groups. This makes RCTs particularly valuable for establishing causal relationships between interventions and health outcomes.

## Applications in mental health and digital health

RCTs are widely used to evaluate mental health interventions, including digital health approaches. These trials help determine whether new therapeutic tools, apps, or treatment protocols produce meaningful improvements compared to existing standard care. The structured comparison between intervention and control conditions provides evidence about effectiveness that informs clinical practice and policy decisions.
