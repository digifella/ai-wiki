# Composite Imaging

Composite imaging is a photographic technique in which elements from multiple similar photographs are combined to create a single, improved final image. This approach is particularly valuable in situations where it is difficult or impossible to capture a perfect shot in a single [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], such as group photographs where different individuals may blink at different moments, or scenes where lighting or focus conditions vary slightly between frames.

## Common Applications

The most frequent use of composite imaging in portrait and group photography is correcting closed eyes. When photographing groups, it is nearly inevitable that at least one person will have their eyes closed in the best overall shot. Rather than discarding an otherwise excellent image, photographers can selectively blend in the open eyes from an alternative take. The technique is also used to improve composition by combining the sharpest focus point from one image with better lighting or positioning from another, or to remove unwanted elements that appeared in some frames but not others.

## Technical Approach

Composite imaging typically requires image editing software such as [[concepts/photoshop|Photoshop]], where layers and [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] tools allow photographers to precisely blend portions of different [[concepts/images|images]]. The process involves careful alignment of source photographs, selective copying of desired elements, and seamless blending to ensure the final result appears natural rather than obviously manipulated. Success depends on the source images being sufficiently similar in composition, lighting, and angle to allow convincing integration.
