# pH Modification

pH modification is a cooking technique that involves adjusting the alkalinity of [[concepts/ground-meat|ground meat]] to improve its culinary properties. The most common method uses [[concepts/baking-soda|baking soda]] (sodium bicarbonate), a mild alkaline agent, which raises the pH of the meat's surface and interior. This chemical change affects how the meat's proteins behave during cooking, producing noticeable improvements in the final dish.

## Effects on Texture and Moisture

Raising the pH of ground meat causes its proteins to denature differently than they would at the meat's natural pH. This altered protein [[concepts/structure|structure]] allows the meat to retain more moisture during cooking, resulting in a juicier final product. The texture becomes noticeably more tender, even in lean ground meat varieties that might otherwise cook dry. This effect is particularly valuable for ground meats used in dishes where moisture retention is important.

## Browning and Flavor Development

At higher [[concepts/ph-levels|pH levels]], ground meat browns more effectively and develops deeper color during cooking. This enhanced browning—known as the [[concepts/maillard-reaction|Maillard reaction]]—is accelerated by alkaline conditions, allowing the meat to achieve a richer, more developed flavor profile with more extensive browning in less time. This makes pH modification useful for [[concepts/software|applications]] where browning is both aesthetically and flavor-wise important, such as in burgers, meatballs, or ground meat stir-fries.

## Application Method

Baking soda is typically mixed directly into ground meat before cooking, using small amounts (usually less than 1% of the meat's weight). The modification works quickly, often requiring only brief [[entities/contact|contact]] time before cooking begins. This simplicity makes it an accessible technique for home cooks seeking to improve ground meat preparations.
