# Non Collapse

[[concepts/dark-matter|Dark matter]] particles do not collapse under their own gravitational attraction into [[concepts/black-holes|black holes]] or other compact objects, unlike [[concepts/baryonic-matter|ordinary matter]]. This fundamental difference in behavior stems from the absence of electromagnetic interactions in dark matter. While ordinary matter—composed of atoms and molecules—can dissipate energy through [[concepts/light|electromagnetic radiation]], allowing [[concepts/gravitational-collapse|gravitational collapse]] to proceed, dark matter lacks this energy loss mechanism.

## Energy Dissipation and Collapse

For ordinary matter to collapse gravitationally into a compact object, particles must shed kinetic energy. Electromagnetic interactions enable this energy dissipation through radiation and particle collisions. Dark matter particles, which do not participate in electromagnetic forces, cannot radiate energy away. Without this ability to lose energy, gravitational collapse cannot proceed efficiently, and dark matter remains distributed rather than concentrated.

## Implications for Dark Matter Structure

This property means that dark matter forms extended structures like halos around galaxies rather than collapsing into discrete, dense objects. The absence of electromagnetic interactions prevents dark matter from forming the equivalent of stellar-scale structures or [[concepts/compact-stellar-objects|compact remnants]] that ordinary matter readily produces. This distinction is crucial for understanding why dark matter's large-scale [[concepts/gravitational-effects|gravitational effects]] in galaxies and [[concepts/galaxy-clusters|galaxy clusters]] appear so different from the concentrated matter we observe in stars and planets.
