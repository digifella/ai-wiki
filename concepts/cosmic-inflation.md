# Cosmic Inflation

Cosmic inflation is a [[concepts/theory|theoretical framework]] in [[concepts/cosmology|cosmology]] describing the universe's rapid exponential expansion during an extremely brief period immediately following the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]], occurring within the first fraction of a second. Proposed in the 1980s by Alan Guth and developed further by Andrei Linde and others, inflation theory addresses several observational puzzles about the universe's structure and properties. Most notably, it explains the remarkable uniformity of the [[concepts/cosmic-background-radiation|cosmic microwave background]] radiation and the near-flatness of [[concepts/spacetime-geometry|spacetime geometry]] observed today.

The theory posits that a scalar field, often referred to as the inflaton, drove this accelerated expansion, smoothing out irregularities and setting the initial conditions for the subsequent hot Big Bang. This mechanism resolves the horizon problem, which questions how distant regions of the universe have the same temperature despite never having been in causal [[entities/contact|contact]], and the flatness problem, which concerns the precise [[concepts/density|density]] required for a [[concepts/flatness-of-the-universe|flat universe]]. Recent research continues to refine these models, particularly in addressing tensions regarding the curvature of the universe and its implications for standard cosmological parameters.

Current investigations at institutions such as the [[entities/perimeter-institute|Perimeter Institute for Theoretical Physics]] extend beyond the mechanics of inflation to explore the [[concepts/elementary-particles|fundamental particles]] and forces present in the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]]. This includes the search for [[concepts/wimp-particles|Weakly Interacting Massive Particles]] ([[concepts/wimp|WIMPs]]), which are leading candidates for [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] and may have been produced during or shortly after the inflationary epoch. By correlating data from cosmic microwave background observations with [[concepts/particle-physics-experiments|particle physics experiments]], researchers aim to constrain the energy scale of inflation and test the validity of various inflationary models against emerging [[concepts/empirical-evidence|observational data]].
