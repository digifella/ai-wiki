# Ligo

LIGO (Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory) is a large-scale physics experiment designed to detect gravitational waves—ripples in spacetime caused by accelerating massive objects. The observatory consists of two facilities located in the United States, in Livingston, Louisiana and Hanford, Washington, which operate in tandem to identify and characterize gravitational wave signals through laser interferometry. By comparing measurements from both sites, LIGO can triangulate the location of gravitational wave sources and distinguish genuine signals from local noise.

The primary function of LIGO involves using laser interferometry to measure minute changes in distance between mirrors suspended at the ends of two perpendicular arms. When a gravitational wave passes through the Earth, it stretches space in one direction and compresses it in the other, altering the length of the arms by a fraction of the width of a proton. This change is detected as an interference pattern in the recombined laser beams, allowing scientists to infer the properties of the astrophysical events that generated the waves.

Recent analyses of LIGO data have provided evidence for the existence of primordial black holes, specifically through the detection of gravitational waves from sub-solar mass objects. These findings are significant because standard stellar evolution models do not typically produce black holes with masses below that of the Sun. The detection suggests that some black holes may have formed in the early universe, offering new insights into the conditions of the cosmos shortly after the Big Bang.
