# Children And Family Violence

Children and family violence encompasses the range of harmful experiences children encounter within family settings, including physical abuse, emotional abuse, neglect, and [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] to domestic violence between caregivers. Research in this domain examines the prevalence, causes, and consequences of such violence, as well as protective factors and intervention strategies. The field draws on evidence from psychology, sociology, [[concepts/population-health|public health]], and social work to understand how family violence affects child development, [[concepts/health|wellbeing]], and long-term outcomes.

## Current Research Directions

Contemporary research on children and family violence increasingly explores the intersection with emerging technologies and [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]]. This includes examining how AI systems can support early identification of at-risk families, improve crisis response coordination, and enhance therapeutic interventions. Researchers are also investigating potential risks, such as algorithmic bias in child protection decision-making and [[concepts/privacy|privacy]] concerns in digital monitoring systems. This emerging research space seeks to ensure that technological [[concepts/software|applications]] in this sensitive domain are evidence-based, ethical, and genuinely improve outcomes for vulnerable children and families.

## Policy and Practice Framework

In jurisdictions like New South Wales, government agencies including the Department of Justice work to coordinate [[concepts/responses|responses]] to children and family violence through evidence clearinghouses and collaborative research initiatives. [[concepts/group-model-building|Group model building]] and participatory research approaches are increasingly used to engage stakeholders—including practitioners, affected families, and researchers—in developing solutions that reflect real-world complexity and local context.
