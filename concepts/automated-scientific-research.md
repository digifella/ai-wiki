# Automated Scientific Research

Automated Scientific Research refers to the use of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems to autonomously conduct, design, and optimize scientific experiments and analyses. Unlike traditional computational tools that process data generated by human researchers, these systems can propose hypotheses, design experiments, and iteratively refine their approaches based on observed results. This represents a shift toward [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] that actively participate in the scientific process rather than serving as passive analytical aids.

A prominent example of this paradigm is [[concepts/2026-04-29-google-deepmind|DeepMind]]'s [[entities/aletheia|Aletheia]], a [[concepts/enhanced-reasoning|self-correcting AI]] designed specifically for [[concepts/scientific-discovery|scientific discovery]]. Such systems operate by closing the [[concepts/loop|loop]] between hypothesis generation and [[concepts/experimental-validation|experimental validation]], allowing them to learn from outcomes and adjust their strategies without continuous human intervention. This capability enables the exploration of complex parameter spaces and the identification of patterns that may be inaccessible to manual methods.

The [[concepts/infrastructure|infrastructure]] supporting automated research typically involves integrated platforms that manage experimental workflows, data collection, and [[concepts/training-process|model training]] simultaneously. These tools facilitate the transition from [[concepts/dead-files|static data]] analysis to dynamic, iterative discovery, aiming to accelerate the pace of scientific [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] across various domains.
