# Chicken Cooking Techniques

## Marinating

[[concepts/chicken-marination|Marinating chicken]] before cooking is one of the most effective methods for retaining moisture and enhancing flavor. Acidic marinades containing ingredients like citrus juice, vinegar, or yogurt help break down muscle fibers slightly, allowing the chicken to absorb liquids and seasonings more effectively. Marinating for at least 30 minutes—and up to several hours—creates a protective layer that reduces moisture loss during the cooking process. The salt content in marinades also helps the meat retain water through osmosis.

## Cutting and Preparation

The way chicken is cut significantly impacts how evenly it cooks and how much moisture remains. Thinner, uniform pieces cook faster and more evenly than thick cuts, reducing the risk of overcooking the exterior while the interior remains undercooked. Pounding chicken breasts to an even thickness ensures consistent cooking throughout. Keeping skin on bone-in cuts provides additional insulation and helps prevent moisture loss during [[concepts/bbq|grilling]].

## Cooking Method and Temperature

Proper temperature control is critical to maintaining juiciness. Grilling at moderate heat rather than high heat allows the interior to cook through without the exterior drying out excessively. Using indirect heat or a two-zone fire reduces the chance of charring before the meat is fully cooked. Removing chicken from heat when it reaches an internal temperature of 165°F (74°C)—rather than cooking it further—prevents the muscle fibers from squeezing out accumulated moisture. Allowing cooked chicken to rest for several minutes before serving helps redistribute juices throughout the meat.
