# Regulatory Framework

The [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of [[concepts/action-oriented-ai|agentic AI]] by regulatory bodies represents a significant milestone in the integration of [[concepts/agentic-ai|autonomous AI systems]] into institutional [[concepts/governance|governance]]. In December 2025, the FDA began deploying agentic AI technologies for in-house operations, marking a notable shift in how regulatory agencies leverage [[concepts/automation|automation]] for their core functions.

This development reflects broader trends in digital [[concepts/health|health]] and regulatory modernization, where organizations are exploring how autonomous [[concepts/agents|agents]] can enhance [[concepts/cost|operational efficiency]] and decision-making processes. The FDA's move demonstrates confidence in agentic AI capabilities for handling structured regulatory tasks, though the specific [[concepts/software|applications]] and scope of this implementation remain areas of ongoing interest within the digital health sector.

The regulatory framework governing agentic AI continues to evolve as agencies themselves become both implementers and overseers of the technology. This dual role—using agentic AI internally while simultaneously regulating its use across the broader healthcare and pharmaceutical industries—creates a complex landscape where regulatory bodies must balance operational benefits against the need for oversight and public [[concepts/accountability|accountability]].
