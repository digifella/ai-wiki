# Data Center Water Footprint

[[concepts/techno-economics|Data centers]] consume substantial quantities of water primarily for cooling [[concepts/infrastructure|infrastructure]]. Modern server hardware generates significant heat that must be dissipated to maintain [[concepts/efficient-operation|operational efficiency]] and prevent equipment failure. Water serves as an effective heat transfer [[entities/medium|medium]] in [[concepts/cooling-systems|cooling systems]], making it a critical operational resource alongside electricity for [[concepts/large-scale-computing|large-scale computing]] facilities.

## Cooling Technologies and Efficiency

The primary mechanism for water usage involves evaporative cooling towers and direct-to-chip liquid cooling systems. Evaporative cooling relies on the [[concepts/state-of-matter-phase-transition|phase change]] of water to absorb heat, requiring continuous makeup water to compensate for evaporation, drift, and blowdown losses. As computational [[concepts/density|density]] increases, particularly in [[entities/high-performance|high-performance]] computing clusters, the efficiency of these systems becomes a limiting factor. Facilities are increasingly adopting closed-[[concepts/loop|loop]] liquid cooling to reduce direct water consumption, though this often shifts the water burden to the external power generation required to run the cooling pumps.

## Impact of AI and Big Data Expansion

The cooling demand increases with [[concepts/it-capacity|data center capacity]] and computational intensity, particularly as [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] and [[concepts/machine-learning|machine learning]] applications expand. Training [[concepts/demystifying-llms|large language models]] requires sustained high-power operation, leading to higher thermal loads than traditional web hosting workloads. This shift is driving a disproportionate increase in water usage relative to electricity consumption in new facilities. Regions with [[concepts/water-scarcity|water scarcity]] are facing constraints on new data center development due to the high volume of water required for thermal management, [[concepts/prompting|prompting]] a reevaluation of site selection criteria and cooling strategies.
