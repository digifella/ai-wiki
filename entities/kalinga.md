# Kalinga

Kalinga was an ancient region of India located in what is now Odisha and parts of Andhra Pradesh. Situated on the eastern coast of the Indian subcontinent, it occupied a strategically important position that facilitated trade and cultural exchange. The region developed its own distinctive political structures and maintained significant economic influence throughout ancient Indian history.

## Historical Significance

Kalinga's most extensively documented period corresponds with the Mauryan Empire, when it became integrated into the imperial system. The region is particularly remembered for the Kalinga War (circa 261 BCE), fought between the Mauryan emperor Ashoka and the independent kingdom of Kalinga. This military campaign resulted in significant casualties and became a turning point in Ashoka's reign, prompting his conversion to Buddhism and his subsequent adoption of a policy of non-violence (Dhamma).

## Mathematical and Numerical Legacy

Kalinga contributed to the development of mathematical and numerical systems in ancient India. The region's scholars and administrators participated in the broader Indian tradition of mathematical advancement, which included developments in arithmetic, geometry, and numerical notation that would eventually influence mathematical thinking across Asia and the medieval world.
