# Dr Robert Ballard

Dr. Robert Ballard is an American oceanographer and underwater explorer best known for discovering the wreck of the RMS Titanic in 1985. His work advanced deep-sea exploration technology and methodology, establishing techniques that became foundational for subsequent underwater archaeological expeditions.

## The Titanic Discovery

In 1985, Ballard led a joint French-American expedition to locate the Titanic in the North Atlantic Ocean. Operating at depths exceeding 12,000 feet, his team employed remotely operated vehicles (ROVs) and sonar technology to map and document the site. This mission was originally part of a US Navy effort to locate two lost nuclear submarines, the USS Thresher and USS Scorpion, but shifted focus to the historic shipwreck after initial searches proved unfruitful.

## Scientific and Technical Contributions

Ballard’s approach to deep-sea exploration emphasized the use of autonomous underwater vehicles and real-time data transmission, which allowed for more efficient surveying of large areas of the ocean floor. His development of these methods significantly lowered the cost and increased the accessibility of deep-ocean research. Beyond the Titanic, Ballard has led numerous expeditions to explore other significant shipwrecks and underwater geological features, contributing extensively to the fields of marine archaeology and oceanography.
