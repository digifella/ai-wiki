## Bumblebees: Advanced Cognition, Cumulative Culture, and Flood Resilience
**Clip title:** Bumblebees Can Do What?! Incredible Discoveries Nobody
Expected
**Author / channel:** [[entities/anton-petrov|Anton Petrov]]
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wPy-8gOUl-A

### Summary
The video delves into recent groundbreaking research that reveals
[[concepts/bumblebees|bumblebees]] are far more complex and intelligent than previously understood,
challenging long-held biological assumptions. Despite having brains the
size of poppy seeds, these insects exhibit astonishing abilities that have
only recently come to [[concepts/light|light]], fundamentally altering our perception of their
capabilities and the broader insect kingdom. The [[entities/speaker|speaker]] highlights several
studies from 2022-2024 that showcase their unexpected cognitive and
physiological prowess.

One of the initial misconceptions addressed is the long-debunked myth that
bumblebees shouldn't be able to fly due to their body shape and wing size,
based on flawed aerodynamic calculations. More significantly, recent
studies have overturned the view of bumblebees as mere biological robots
driven solely by instinct. Research published in "Nature" demonstrated
their capacity for "[[concepts/cumulative-culture|cumulative culture]]," a complex form of [social learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Learning)
previously thought unique to large-brained animals like primates and
certain birds. In a two-step puzzle box experiment, individual bees
initially struggled, but once shown the [[concepts/solution|solution]] by a human demonstrator,
they quickly mastered it. Crucially, these "learner" bees then taught other
"naive" bees the complex sequence, proving a [social transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Transmission) of
knowledge. Beyond this, bumblebees have also been observed using tools,
counting, understanding the concept of [[concepts/zero|zero]], and performing basic
mathematical equations.

Perhaps the most "shocking" discovery highlighted is the bumblebee queen's
remarkable [[concepts/resilience|resilience]] to flooding. Accidental observation during a
[hibernation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibernation) study revealed that queens could survive submerged underwater
for several days. Subsequent research confirmed this, showing approximately
90% survival even after eight days of submersion in cold water. Their
survival mechanisms involve three key factors: a profound metabolic
[[concepts/depression|depression]], where their metabolic rate drops by up to 95%, significantly
reducing oxygen demand; [anaerobic metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaerobic_Metabolism), allowing cells to generate
energy without oxygen; and a clever method of underwater respiration, where
they seemingly utilize a "[physical gill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_Gill)" – a thin layer of trapped air
against their body – to facilitate [gas exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_Exchange) directly with the water.
This extraordinary adaptation is attributed to evolutionary resilience, as
hibernating underground makes them vulnerable to unpredictable spring
floods from heavy rains and melting snow, which could otherwise wipe out
entire generations.

These revelations underscore the vast extent of what remains unknown about
the natural world, even in our immediate surroundings. Bumblebees are not
just vital pollinators but complex, social, and intelligent creatures with
unique [[concepts/adaptations|adaptations]]. This deeper understanding is particularly critical
given that bumblebee populations are currently in decline, facing threats
from [habitat loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_Loss) and pesticides known to impair their [[concepts/memory|memory]], brain
function, and colony-building abilities. Recognizing their intricate
intelligence and resilience serves as a potent reminder of the need for
greater conservation efforts and continued scientific exploration to
protect these fascinating insects and the delicate ecosystems they support.
