# General Problem Solving Capabilities

General [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] capabilities refer to the ability of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems to tackle diverse tasks and challenges across different domains without task-specific [[concepts/training|training]] or optimization. For small language models (SLMs) operating within constrained computational budgets—typically around 4GB in size—assessing these capabilities has become increasingly important as these models are deployed in resource-limited environments such as edge devices and mobile systems.

## Benchmarking Approaches

Evaluating general problem-solving in SLMs involves standardized benchmarking methodologies that test [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]], task adaptation, and performance across varied problem types. These benchmarks measure how well models can transfer knowledge from training to novel situations, maintain coherence in multi-step reasoning, and handle unfamiliar problem structures. The 4GB constraint creates a meaningful [[concepts/testing|testing]] ground, as it represents a practical threshold for [[concepts/deployment|deployment]] in consumer and industrial [[concepts/software|applications]] where [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]] are limited.

## Relevance and Applications

Understanding the general problem-solving performance of compact models has practical implications for real-world deployment [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]]. Organizations and developers need reliable data about what types of problems different SLM variants can effectively address, and where their limitations manifest. This information guides decisions about model selection, hybrid approaches combining multiple smaller models, and the viability of [[concepts/mobile-ai|on-device AI]] solutions across [[concepts/health|healthcare]], manufacturing, and consumer technology sectors.
