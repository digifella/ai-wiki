# Los Angeles

Los Angeles is the second-largest city in the United States, located in Southern California along the Pacific Coast. With a population exceeding 3.9 million residents and a greater metropolitan area of nearly 13 million people, it is one of the world's most populous urban centers. The city serves as a major economic, cultural, and technological hub, with significant influence in entertainment, international trade, aerospace, and technology sectors.

## Seismic Setting and Geological Context

Los Angeles sits within a highly seismically active region defined by complex fault systems. The San Andreas Fault, one of the world's most significant strike-slip faults, runs through Southern California approximately 35 miles northeast of the city. In addition to this major fault, Los Angeles is crossed by and situated near numerous local faults, including the Newport-Inglewood Fault, Elsinore Fault, and others. This geological setting results in frequent small earthquakes and poses considerable risk from larger seismic events.

## Infrastructure and Urban Resilience

The dense urban development and extensive critical infrastructure in Los Angeles—including major ports, transportation networks, water systems, and utilities—make the region particularly vulnerable to earthquake impacts. The concentration of population and infrastructure has established Los Angeles as a focal point for earthquake engineering research, building code development, and seismic resilience planning. Modern building standards in the city reflect decades of seismic design advancement, though retrofitting older structures remains an ongoing challenge for the region's long-term resilience.
