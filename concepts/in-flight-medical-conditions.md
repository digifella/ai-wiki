# In-Flight Medical Conditions

## Overview
In-flight medical conditions refer to [[concepts/health|health]] events or deteriorations occurring during Spaceflight missions. These conditions pose significant risks to mission [[concepts/success|success]] and crew safety, necessitating robust pre-flight screening, in-flight monitoring, and emergency response protocols.

## Key Risks and Evidence
Per the [[entities/national-academies|National Academies]]' review of [[concepts/nasa|NASA]]'s evidence reports, the primary concern is the potential for:
- **[[concepts/adverse-health-outcomes|Adverse Health Outcomes]]**: Immediate physiological harm or long-term health decrements.
- **Performance Decrements**: Reduction in crew capability affecting mission-critical tasks.

The 2017 Letter Report (reviewed 2018) confirms that while medical events are probabilistic, their occurrence is a certainty over extended mission durations. See [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Review-of-NASAs-Evidence-Reports-on-Human-Health-Risks-2|Review of NASA's Evidence Reports on Human Health Risks: 2017 Letter Report (2018)]] for detailed risk [[concepts/quantification|quantification]].
