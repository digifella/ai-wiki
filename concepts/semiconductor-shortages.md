# Semiconductor Shortages

Semiconductor shortages occur when global supply of chips falls below demand, constraining production across industries dependent on computing hardware. Supply chain disruptions became particularly acute following the [[concepts/covid-19|COVID-19]] pandemic, affecting automotive manufacturing, [[concepts/consumer-grade-hardware|consumer electronics]], and [[concepts/data-center-boom|data center expansion]]. The underlying [[concepts/causes|causes]] include concentrated manufacturing capacity in specific geographic regions, extended production lead times measured in months, and difficulty predicting demand fluctuations across different market segments.

## Structural Factors

The semiconductor industry's [[concepts/vulnerability|vulnerability]] to shortages stems from structural characteristics. Production capacity is concentrated among a small number of foundries, primarily in Taiwan, South Korea, and the [[entities/united-states|United States]]. Manufacturing advanced chips requires specialized facilities that take years and billions of dollars to construct, limiting the industry's ability to quickly scale production in response to demand surges. Additionally, the [[concepts/supply-chains|supply chains]] for raw materials and specialized equipment themselves face bottlenecks that can cascade through the entire production process.

## Impact on AI Development

Semiconductor constraints have directly affected [[concepts/computing-architecture|AI infrastructure]] deployment. [[entities/high-performance|High-performance]] chips required for training [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]] and running [[concepts/techno-economics|data centers]] compete with demand from consumer electronics and other industries. Companies developing [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] have faced delays in acquiring GPUs and specialized [[concepts/central-processing-units|processors]], influencing timelines for [[concepts/knowledge-acquisition|model development]] and deployment. The competition for limited chip supplies has also affected the distribution and cost of AI computing resources globally.

## Recovery Patterns

Recovery from shortage periods typically requires 18-24 months as manufacturing capacity expands and demand normalizes. However, new demand patterns can emerge before full recovery occurs, creating cyclical constraint periods. Recent years have seen mixed signals, with some chip categories returning to normal supply levels while others, particularly advanced processors for [[concepts/ai-powered-applications|AI applications]], remain in high demand relative to available capacity.
