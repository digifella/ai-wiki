# Parameter Models

Parameter models are machine learning systems whose behavior is determined by learned parameters—numerical values including weights and biases that are optimized during training. These models form the foundation of modern AI agents and generative systems, spanning architectures from large language models to specialized computer vision and video generation systems. The parameters are adjusted iteratively during training to minimize prediction errors, allowing the model to capture patterns in data without explicit programming of task-specific rules.

## Open Weight Models

OpenAI has released the GPT-OSS series as open-weight models, making the internal parameters of its generative architectures publicly accessible. This approach allows researchers and developers to inspect, modify, and fine-tune the underlying weights, facilitating greater transparency and customization compared to closed-source alternatives. By providing access to the model weights, OpenAI enables the community to build upon its foundational architectures for specific use cases and academic study.

## Local Video Generation

In the domain of generative media, Wan 2.2 has emerged as a significant model for text-to-video and image-to-video synthesis. Unlike cloud-only solutions, Wan 2.2 is designed for local deployment, allowing users to run inference on their own hardware. This capability is typically realized through ComfyUI, a node-based graphical user interface that facilitates the workflow of loading model weights, managing latent spaces, and generating video outputs. The local execution of Wan 2.2 provides users with greater control over privacy, computational resources, and the iterative refinement of generated content.
