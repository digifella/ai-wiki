# Digital Preservation

Digital [[concepts/preservation|preservation]] refers to the active maintenance and safeguarding of digital records, documents, and cultural materials to ensure their long-term [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]] and [[concepts/integrity|integrity]]. As institutions increasingly digitize their collections, the practice has become essential for archival work, [[concepts/cultural-heritage|cultural heritage]] management, and historical research. Digital preservation addresses the unique challenges posed by technological obsolescence, format degradation, and the inherent fragility of digital [[entities/storage|storage]] media.

## Key Challenges

Digital materials face distinct preservation obstacles that differ from physical records. Storage formats become obsolete as technologies evolve, [[concepts/visual-rendering|rendering]] files unreadable without specialized hardware or software. Digital media such as magnetic tapes and optical discs are also subject to physical degradation over time. Additionally, the rapid pace of [[concepts/technological-change|technological change]] complicates long-term access strategies.
