# Jungian Grief Model

The Jungian Grief Model is a mental health intervention framework developed at the University of Copenhagen that integrates principles from Jungian psychology with contemporary grief counseling practices. The model draws on Carl Jung's theoretical concepts regarding the unconscious mind and psychological integration to help individuals process grief and loss within a structured therapeutic context. Rather than viewing grief as a linear process to be "resolved," the framework emphasizes psychological integration and the meaning-making aspects of loss.

## Core Framework

The model is organized into distinct modules that address both the internal conflicts that arise during grief and the pathways toward resolution. These modules guide individuals through the psychological dimensions of loss, including the confrontation of shadow aspects of the grief experience and the integration of loss into one's ongoing identity. The structured approach allows practitioners to tailor interventions to individual circumstances while maintaining theoretical coherence grounded in depth psychology principles.

## Clinical Application

The Jungian Grief Model is used in counseling and therapeutic settings to support individuals navigating bereavement, loss, and major life transitions. By combining archetypal symbolism and unconscious exploration with practical counseling techniques, the framework offers both psychological insight and practical coping strategies for grief-related distress.
