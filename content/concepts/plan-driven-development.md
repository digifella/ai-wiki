# Plan Driven Development

Plan-driven development, commonly known as waterfall methodology, is a [[concepts/software-engineering|software engineering]] approach that emphasizes comprehensive upfront planning and sequential execution. The methodology divides development into distinct phases—typically requirements gathering, [[concepts/design|design]], [[concepts/adoption|implementation]], and [[concepts/testing|testing]]—where each [[concepts/phase|phase]] must be largely completed before the next begins. This linear progression prioritizes predictability, [[concepts/power|control]], and thorough documentation throughout the development lifecycle.

## Core Characteristics

The defining feature of plan-driven development is its reliance on detailed planning [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] the project outset. Requirements are extensively documented and analyzed before any design work commences, design is finalized before implementation begins, and testing occurs primarily after development is complete. This approach assumes that requirements can be well-understood and specified early, and that changes during development should be minimized. The methodology produces comprehensive artifacts at each stage, creating a detailed record of decisions and specifications.

## Contrast with Iterative Approaches

Plan-driven development differs substantially from agile and iterative methodologies, which embrace ongoing change and frequent [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] cycles. While plan-driven approaches work well for projects with stable, well-defined requirements and clear constraints—such as regulated industries or fixed-scope contracts—they can struggle with evolving requirements or emerging complexity. The upfront commitment to a detailed plan makes accommodating change more costly and disruptive than in adaptive frameworks.
