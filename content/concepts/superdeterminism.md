# Superdeterminism

Superdeterminism is a proposed interpretation of [[concepts/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]] that attempts to resolve the apparent conflicts between quantum theory and [[concepts/local-realism|local realism]]. The approach denies the freedom of experimenters to independently choose measurement settings, arguing instead that all events—including the decisions made by experimenters—are predetermined by prior physical conditions. Under this view, correlations between particle properties and the apparatus used to measure them are not mysterious or non-local, but rather the result of hidden deterministic processes established at the universe's origin.

## Bell's Theorem and Local Realism

The concept emerged in response to Bell's theorem and subsequent experimental tests of Bell inequalities, which seemed to rule out local hidden variable theories. Superdeterminism accepts determinism but rejects the assumption that experimenters have free choice in their measurement decisions. By eliminating this "freedom of choice" assumption—sometimes called the "free will" loophole—superdeterminism claims to preserve local realism and avoid the need for non-local influences or [[concepts/wave-function-collapse|wave function collapse]].

## Criticisms and Philosophical Implications

Superdeterminism remains controversial within the [[concepts/physics|physics]] community. Critics argue that denying experimenter freedom is philosophically unpalatable and practically undermines the [[concepts/scientific-method|scientific method]], which relies on the ability to choose independent variables. Additionally, the [[concepts/theory|theory]] has not yet produced novel predictions or led to significant experimental advances. Most mainstream physicists favor other interpretations of quantum mechanics, such as the Copenhagen interpretation or the many-worlds interpretation, though superdeterminism continues to be explored by a minority of researchers interested in preserving classical notions of locality and realism.
