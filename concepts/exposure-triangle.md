# Exposure Triangle

The **[[concepts/exposure|Exposure]] Triangle** is a fundamental concept in photography describing the relationship between three [[concepts/camera-settings|camera settings]] that determine the brightness and quality of an image:

- [[concepts/aperture|Aperture]]: Controls the size of the lens opening, affecting depth of field and [[concepts/light|light]] intake.
- [[concepts/shutter-speed|Shutter Speed]]: Determines the duration the sensor is exposed to light, affecting motion blur.
- ISO: Adjusts the sensor's sensitivity to light, affecting image noise/grain.

## Strategic Semi-Automatic Modes

While manual control is essential for [[concepts/learning|learning]], professional workflows often prioritize efficiency and [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] through strategic semi-automatic modes.

- **Efficiency over Perfection**: Professionals often avoid full manual mode to maintain shooting [[concepts/speed|speed]] and [[concepts/resilience|adaptability]] in dynamic environments [[lab-notes/2026-09-13-Why-Professional-Photographers-Prefer-Strategic-Semi-Aut|Why Professional Photographers Prefer Strategic Semi-Automatic Camera Modes]].
- **Priority Modes**:
  - **Aperture Priority (Av/A)**: Allows control over depth of field while the camera calculates shutter speed.
  - **Shutter Priority (Tv/S)**: Allows control over motion blur while the camera calculates aperture.
- **Workflow Integration**: Semi-automatic modes reduce [[concepts/cognitive-load|cognitive load]], allowing photographers to focus on [[concepts/writing|composition]] and timing rather than constant manual [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]].
