# Physics Simulation

Physics simulation refers to the computational modeling and prediction of physical systems and phenomena using mathematical equations and [[concepts/numerical-analysis|numerical methods]]. These simulations solve differential equations governing motion, forces, and energy transfer to approximate real-world behavior in digital environments. [[concepts/software|Applications]] range from engineering design and materials science to visual effects and [[concepts/robotics|robotics]], where simulating physical behavior before real-world implementation reduces costs and risks.

## Computational Methods

[[concepts/physics|Physics]] simulations employ various numerical techniques depending on the system's complexity and required [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]]. Common approaches include finite element analysis (FEA) for structural mechanics, computational [[concepts/fluid-dynamics|fluid dynamics]] (CFD) for fluid flow, and particle-based methods for dynamics. The choice of method involves tradeoffs between computational cost, precision, and the specific phenomena being modeled, such as rigid body dynamics, soft body deformation, or collision detection.

## Recent Developments

Recent advances in [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] and machine [[concepts/learning|learning]] have introduced new capabilities to physics simulation. AI systems are increasingly used to accelerate simulations through learned approximations, predict outcomes more rapidly than traditional solvers, and automate aspects of model setup and [[concepts/active-parameters|parameter optimization]]. Additionally, [[concepts/ai-coding-assistance|AI-assisted coding]] tools are enabling faster development of [[concepts/simulation|simulation]] software, while robotics research continues to bridge the gap between simulated environments and real-world physical systems through improved [[concepts/advanced-ai-techniques-that-improve-generalization-from-simulated-data|sim-to-real transfer]] techniques.
