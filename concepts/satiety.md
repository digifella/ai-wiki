# Satiety

Satiety is the physiological and psychological state of feeling full and satisfied after eating. It represents the opposite of hunger and functions as a key regulatory mechanism controlling eating behavior and food intake. When satiety is achieved, the body signals to the brain that adequate nutrition has been consumed, typically leading to the cessation of eating. This regulatory process is essential for maintaining energy balance and preventing overconsumption.

## Biological Mechanisms

Satiety is regulated through multiple biological pathways. Hormones such as leptin and peptide YY signal fullness to the brain's appetite centers, while the hormone ghrelin, conversely, promotes hunger. Gastric distension—the physical stretching of the stomach—also contributes to satiety signals. Additionally, nutrient absorption in the small intestine triggers chemical signals that reinforce the sensation of fullness. These mechanisms work in coordination to create the satiety response.

## Nutritional Factors

Certain nutrients and foods promote stronger satiety than others. Protein, dietary fiber, and complex carbohydrates generally produce greater satiation than simple sugars and refined foods. The volume, caloric density, and macronutrient composition of meals influence both the intensity and duration of satiety. Individual variations in satiety responsiveness can affect eating patterns and overall energy balance, making satiety an important consideration in [[concepts/diet|dietary]] planning and [[concepts/weight-management|weight management]].
