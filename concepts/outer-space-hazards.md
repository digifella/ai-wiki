# Outer Space Hazards

Outer space presents a hostile environment characterized by extreme conditions that are lethal to unprotected biological life. Primary hazards include vacuum [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], radiation, thermal extremes, and micrometeoroid impacts.

## Environmental Threats

- **Vacuum and Decompression**: Immediate risk of ebullism, hypoxia, and barotrauma.
- **Radiation**: High-energy [[concepts/cosmic-rays|cosmic rays]] and solar particle events causing cellular damage.
- **Thermal Extremes**: Rapid temperature fluctuations between direct sunlight and shadow.
- **Micrometeoroids**: High-velocity [[concepts/granular-matter|particulate matter]] capable of puncturing protective layers.

## Mitigation Technologies

[[concepts/secure|Protection]] against these hazards relies on multi-layered [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] solutions, particularly in personal protective equipment.

- **Space Suit Engineering**: Modern suits utilize complex material [[concepts/science|science]] to counteract vacuum and [[concepts/heat-stress|thermal stress]]. See [[lab-notes/2026-07-04-Space-Suit-Materials-and-Technology-for-Extreme-Space-Pr|Space Suit Materials and Technology for Extreme Space Protection]] for detailed analysis of [[concepts/nasa|NASA]] suit architecture.
- **Layered Defense**: Suits incorporate pressure garments, thermal micrometeoroid garment layers, and life support systems to maintain homeostasis.
