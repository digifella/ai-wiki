# Domestic Semiconductor Independence

Domestic semiconductor independence describes a national strategy aimed at reducing reliance on foreign suppliers by establishing local chip manufacturing capabilities. This initiative involves the construction of domestic fabrication plants (fabs), the support of specialized research institutions, and the development of integrated [[concepts/supply-chains|supply chains]] for semiconductor production. The primary [[concepts/purpose|objective]] is to achieve technological sovereignty, thereby mitigating vulnerabilities to [[concepts/global-trade|international trade]] disruptions or geopolitical pressures that could restrict access to critical [[concepts/computation|computing]] components.

The [[entities/pursuitunimelbeduau|pursuit]] of this independence is often driven by the need to [[concepts/secure|secure]] supply chains for [[entities/high-performance|high-performance]] computing, particularly in the context of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]]. For instance, [[entities/china|China]] has pursued the development of [[concepts/nvidia-gpu-alternatives|Nvidia-free AI models]], such as [[concepts/longcat-series|LongCat 2.0]], as part of a broader effort to decouple its advanced [[concepts/computing-infrastructure|computing infrastructure]] from foreign hardware dependencies. This approach highlights the intersection of hardware manufacturing and software [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] in achieving strategic autonomy.

Achieving this goal requires significant investment in both physical infrastructure and human capital. Nations focus on building robust ecosystems that span from raw material processing to final chip assembly, ensuring that critical technologies remain accessible regardless of external political climates. This comprehensive approach seeks to balance economic efficiency with [[concepts/export-control|national security]] interests, recognizing semiconductors as foundational elements of modern digital economies.
