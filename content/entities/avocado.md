# Avocado

Avocado is a fruit native to south-central Mexico, belonging to the laurel family (Lauraceae). The plant produces pear-shaped fruits with a large central seed surrounded by creamy flesh. Avocados are typically harvested while still firm and ripen after picking, a characteristic that influences their commercial [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] and consumption patterns.

## Culinary and Nutritional Uses

The fruit is widely consumed in cuisines around the world, particularly in Latin American, Mediterranean, and Asian cooking. Avocado flesh is commonly used in guacamole, salads, sandwiches, and as a garnish. Nutritionally, avocados are notable for their high fat content—primarily monounsaturated fats—along with fiber, potassium, and various vitamins including B vitamins and vitamin K. This nutritional profile has contributed to their popularity in [[concepts/health|health]]-conscious diets.

## Cultivation and Production

Major producers of avocados include Mexico, Indonesia, Colombia, and California. The crop requires specific climate conditions, preferring subtropical to tropical environments with moderate rainfall. Commercial cultivation has expanded significantly since the mid-20th century in response to growing international demand, though the crop remains sensitive to environmental factors such as water availability and frost.
