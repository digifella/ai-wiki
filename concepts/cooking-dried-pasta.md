# Cooking Dried Pasta

The fundamental method for cooking dried pasta begins with bringing a large pot of salted water to a rolling boil. The standard ratio is approximately four parts water to one part pasta by weight, providing sufficient space for even heat distribution and preventing the pasta from sticking together. Salt should be added to the water before the pasta enters, as this is when the starch structure most readily absorbs seasoning. The generous water volume maintains boiling temperature when pasta is added and allows starches to disperse into the cooking liquid rather than coating the pasta surface.

## Texture and Doneness

Pasta should be cooked until it reaches an al dente state—tender but still firm to the bite—typically achieved within the time range specified on the package, though individual preference and pasta shape affect the exact timing. Testing for doneness by tasting is more reliable than following package instructions precisely, as factors like water hardness, altitude, and pasta thickness influence cooking time. Overcooking results in mushy texture and loss of structural integrity, while undercooking leaves a hard, unpalatable core.

## Sauce Integration

Timing the completion of pasta with sauce preparation ensures both components reach optimal temperature simultaneously. Reserving one to two cups of starchy cooking water before draining allows adjustment of sauce consistency after combining. The starch in this water helps emulsify fat-based sauces and creates better adhesion between sauce and pasta. Finishing pasta in the sauce for one to two minutes, rather than serving them separately, facilitates better flavor integration and coating.
