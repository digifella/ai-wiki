# Cast Iron Cookware

Cast iron cookware consists of cooking vessels made from cast iron, a durable material that has been used in kitchens for centuries. Cast iron pans, Dutch ovens, and griddles are valued for their heat retention properties and longevity. When properly seasoned and maintained, cast iron develops a natural non-stick surface through the buildup of polymerized oils. The material's thermal mass allows it to maintain consistent temperatures during cooking, making it suitable for a wide range of cooking methods.

## Seasoning and Maintenance

Cast iron requires regular seasoning to maintain its non-stick properties and prevent rust. Seasoning involves coating the cookware with oil and heating it to a point where the oil polymerizes, bonding to the metal surface. This process creates a durable, slick finish that improves with use. Maintenance typically involves washing with mild soap and water, drying thoroughly, and applying a thin layer of oil after each use to protect the surface.

## Cooking Applications

Due to its excellent heat retention, cast iron is ideal for searing meats, baking bread, and slow-cooking stews. It can be used on stovetops, in ovens, and over open fires, provided the handle material allows for such temperatures. While generally durable, cast iron is brittle and can crack if subjected to sudden temperature changes or physical impact. It is also reactive to acidic foods, which can strip the seasoning and impart a metallic taste, so prolonged cooking of tomatoes or wine-based sauces is often discouraged unless the pan is well-seasoned.
