# Commercial Lunar Payload Services

Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) is a [[concepts/nasa|NASA]] initiative established in the late 2010s to contract private companies for the delivery of scientific instruments and equipment to the lunar surface. The program represents a [[concepts/strategic-pivot|strategic shift]] toward leveraging commercial spaceflight providers for [[concepts/lunar-exploration|lunar exploration]], aiming to reduce costs and increase the frequency of surface deliveries compared to relying solely on government-operated systems.

Under the CLPS framework, NASA issues task orders to selected vendors rather than procuring entire launch vehicles or landers. This structure allows NASA to procure specific payload delivery services, enabling multiple missions to proceed concurrently with different providers. The program supports the broader Artemis campaign by ensuring that scientific assets reach the Moon efficiently, complementing the agency's heavy-[[entities/lift|lift]] capabilities provided by the Space Launch System (SLS) and the [[concepts/human-landing-system|Human Landing System]] (HLS) developed for [[concepts/crewed-missions|crewed missions]].

The program addresses critical challenges related to Artemis 3 readiness by diversifying the supply chain for lunar surface logistics. By fostering a competitive commercial market, NASA seeks to establish a sustainable [[concepts/infrastructure|infrastructure]] for lunar exploration. This approach allows the agency to focus on high-level mission objectives while private [[concepts/nodes|entities]] manage the operational complexities of landing payloads, thereby accelerating the timeline for scientific return and long-term lunar presence.
