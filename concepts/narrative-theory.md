# Narrative Theory

Narrative [[concepts/theory|theory]] is an [[entities/tomasz-janowski|academic]] discipline that examines how stories function across different media, including literature, film, games, and [[concepts/corporate-platforms|digital platforms]]. The field investigates the fundamental structures, conventions, and techniques through which narratives are constructed and how they convey meaning to audiences. By analyzing core components such as plot, character, point of view, and temporal structure, narrative theory develops frameworks for understanding how audiences interpret and derive meaning from stories.

## Key Areas of Study

Narrative theory encompasses several key areas of study, primarily focusing on the distinction between story and discourse. Story refers to the chronological sequence of events and characters, while discourse concerns the specific manner in which these events are presented to the audience. This distinction allows scholars to analyze how manipulation of time, perspective, and information affects the reader's or player's [[concepts/experience|experience]]. In interactive media like games, this extends to examining how player agency influences narrative progression and the resulting interpretation of the plot.

The field also explores the cultural and contextual factors that shape narrative reception. Narratives do not exist in a vacuum; they are influenced by the historical period, social norms, and cultural background of both the [[concepts/creator|creator]] and the audience. Narrative theory investigates how these external factors interact with internal structural elements to produce meaning. This includes the study of genre conventions, which provide a shared set of expectations that guide audience interpretation, and the ways in which narratives can challenge or reinforce societal values.

## Interdisciplinary Connections

Narrative theory intersects with various other disciplines, including [[concepts/linguistics|linguistics]], [[concepts/psychology|psychology]], and media studies. From a linguistic perspective, it examines the role of language and [[concepts/semiotics|semiotics]] in constructing narrative reality. Psychological approaches focus on how audiences process narrative information, engage with characters, and experience emotional responses. In media studies, the theory adapts to account for the unique [[concepts/affordances|affordances]] of different platforms, such as the non-linear possibilities of digital [[concepts/storytelling|storytelling]] or the spatial narratives found in video games. This interdisciplinary nature ensures that narrative theory remains a dynamic field capable of addressing the evolving landscape of storytelling.
