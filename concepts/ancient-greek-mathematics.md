# Ancient Greek Mathematics

[[concepts/ancient-greek|Ancient Greek mathematics]] represents a foundational shift in mathematical thought, moving from practical computation toward abstract [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] and [[concepts/proof|formal proof]]. Developing primarily between the 6th century BCE and the 4th century CE, Greek mathematicians established many concepts and methods that remain central to [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] today. Key figures including Thales, Pythagoras, Euclid, and Archimedes transformed mathematics from a tool for commerce and surveying into a rigorous intellectual discipline pursued for its own sake.

## Geometry and Proof

The Greeks made geometry their primary mathematical focus, developing it into a formal system of definitions, axioms, and logical proofs. Euclid's *Elements*, compiled around 300 BCE, systematized geometric knowledge and became the dominant mathematical text for over two thousand years. Greek mathematicians proved fundamental theorems about triangles, circles, and polygons, and established the relationship between geometric magnitudes—work that laid groundwork for later developments in algebra and calculus.

## Number Theory and Logic

Beyond geometry, Greek mathematicians investigated the properties of numbers themselves. Pythagorean mathematicians discovered [[concepts/irrational_number|irrational numbers]]—quantities that could not be expressed as ratios of integers—a finding that challenged contemporary understanding. The Greeks also developed sophisticated logical methods for reasoning about mathematical objects, creating frameworks that influenced the development of formal [[concepts/open-source-philosophy|logic]] as a discipline.

## Legacy

Greek [[concepts/calculation-methods|mathematical approaches]] emphasized rigorous proof and abstract [[concepts/abstraction|generalization]] rather than mere calculation. These standards became embedded in Western mathematical practice and contributed significantly to the development of [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]] and formal logic. Many Greek [[concepts/mathematical-concepts|mathematical concepts]] and problems continued to [[concepts/ambition|drive]] mathematical investigation centuries after ancient Greece's political decline.
