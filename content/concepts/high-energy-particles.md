# High Energy Particles

High energy particles are [[concepts/subatomic-particles|subatomic particles]] that possess extremely high kinetic energy, typically studied through [[concepts/cosmic-rays|cosmic rays]] and particle accelerator experiments. These particles originate from various sources including [[concepts/supernovae|supernovae]], [[concepts/active-galactic-nuclei|active galactic nuclei]], and other violent cosmic phenomena. The detection and analysis of high energy particles provides insights into fundamental [[concepts/physics|physics]], stellar processes, and the [[concepts/structure|structure]] of the universe.

## Cosmic Ray Sources

The LHAASO (Large High Altitude Air Shower Observatory) observatory, located on the Tibetan Plateau, has made significant observations of cosmic ray sources throughout the galaxy. The facility detects [[concepts/gamma-rays|gamma rays]] and cosmic rays by observing the air showers they produce when entering Earth's atmosphere. Recent observations have identified exceptionally powerful cosmic ray acceleration sites, contributing to the understanding of how particles reach such extreme energies in natural cosmic environments.

## Laboratory Studies

Particle accelerators such as the [[entities/lhc|Large Hadron Collider]] (LHC) enable controlled study of high energy particle interactions. Experiments like the CMS detector investigate fundamental particle properties and test theoretical predictions about [[concepts/granular-physics|particle physics]]. These experiments complement observational cosmic ray studies by allowing researchers to examine particle behavior under precisely controlled conditions and explore questions about the substructure and composition of known particles.
