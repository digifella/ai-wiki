# Macro Photography

Macro photography is a technique for capturing highly magnified images of small subjects, revealing fine details invisible to the naked eye. The magnification ratio typically begins at 1:1, where the subject is reproduced at life-size on the camera sensor or film, and can extend to 10:1 or greater for extreme close-ups. This approach requires specialized equipment and careful technique to achieve sharp, usable images at such high magnifications.

## Equipment and Methods

Dedicated macro lenses are the primary tool for macro photography, designed to focus at very short distances while maintaining optical quality. These lenses typically have focal lengths between 50mm and 200mm and are optimized for close-focus work. Alternatively, macro extension tubes, close-up filters, or reversing rings can be adapted to standard lenses to achieve magnification. Lighting becomes critical in macro work, as depth of field is extremely shallow at high magnifications; photographers often use ring lights, diffused flash, or carefully positioned continuous [[concepts/light|light]] sources to properly illuminate subjects.

## Post-Processing and Workflow

High-magnification images often require significant post-processing to correct optical aberrations, enhance [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]], and manage noise introduced by high [[concepts/iso-settings|ISO settings]] or [[concepts/film-grain|film grain]].
