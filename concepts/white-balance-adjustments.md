# White Balance Adjustments

**White Balance (WB)** is the process of removing unnatural [[concepts/color-casts|color casts]] from images, ensuring that white objects appear truly white under different lighting conditions. It involves adjusting the relative intensity of red, green, and blue channels to match the color temperature (measured in **Kelvin**) and tint of the [[concepts/light|light]] source.

## Core Principles
- **Color Temperature**: Cool (blue) vs. Warm ([[concepts/amber|amber]]) shifts.
- **Tint**: Green vs. Magenta shifts.
- **Neutral Reference**: Establishing a baseline where RGB values are equal for neutral surfaces.

## Implementation & Tools
- **[[concepts/camera-settings|Camera Settings]]**: In-camera WB presets (Daylight, Cloudy, Tungsten, etc.) provide initial corrections.
- **Post-Processing**: Tools like [[concepts/lightroom|Lightroom]] and [[concepts/adobe-camera-raw|Adobe Camera Raw]] allow precise channel manipulation.
- **[[concepts/ai-automation-agents|AI Automation]]**: Recent advancements include [[concepts/ai-driven-automation|AI-driven automation]] of complex [[concepts/photoshop-control|Photoshop]] tasks.

## Strategic Color Grading
Beyond technical correction, [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|color grading]] is used to evoke emotion and add depth. A key technique is **Strategic Two-Color Grading**, which focuses on intentional mood setting rather than generic adjustments.

- **Emotional Intent**: Impactful editing requires consciously deciding how a photo should "feel" before applying adjustments.
- **Two-Color Technique**: Utilizing a complementary or contrasting two-color palette to shift the overall mood effectively.
- **Depth Creation**: Strategic grading adds visual layers, preventing flat or overly processed appearances.
