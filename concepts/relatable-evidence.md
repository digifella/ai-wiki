# Relatable Evidence

Relatable evidence refers to data or observations that bridge the gap between abstract [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|theoretical frameworks]] and tangible, interpretable reality. It is information that allows the observer to map specific empirical findings onto broader conceptual models, facilitating understanding through direct analogy or traceable [[concepts/evolutionary-lineage|lineage]].

In scientific and philosophical contexts, relatable evidence often serves as the anchor point for constructing mental models of complex systems. It transforms raw data into [[concepts/storytelling|narrative]] structure, making it "sticky" or memorable by linking it to existing knowledge structures.

## Key Characteristics
- **Traceability**: The evidence can be followed back to its source or physical [[concepts/presence|manifestation]].
- **Analogy**: It allows for comparison with known phenomena.
- **Tangibility**: Even abstract concepts are grounded in observable metrics or artifacts.

## Applications and Examples

### Geological Deep Time
Relatable evidence is critical in fields like [[concepts/geology]], where time [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] exceed human [[concepts/experience|experience]]. Researchers rely on physical traces to relate ancient processes to current observations.

- **Early [[concepts/tectonic-plate-shifts|Plate Tectonics]]**: Recent findings provide compelling physical anchors for deep-time geodynamics.
	- Study published in *[[concepts/science|Science]]* suggests plate tectonics may have started as early as 3.48 billion years ago.
	- Evidence derived from [[concepts/magnetic-traces|magnetic traces]] in ancient crustal pieces.
	- Source: [[entities/alec-brenner|Alec Brenner]] (Harvard/Yale), featured in *[[entities/scientific-american|Scientific American]]* (June 2026).

### Cognitive Mapping
In social sciences and [[concepts/psychology|psychology]], relatable evidence helps individuals empathize with statistical outliers or minority experiences by presenting them in narrative formats that mirror the observer's own life structure.
