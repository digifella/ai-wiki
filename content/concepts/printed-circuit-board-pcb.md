# Printed Circuit Board (PCB)

A Printed Circuit Board (PCB) is a flat board made of insulating material, typically fiberglass or composite resin, onto which electrical components are mounted and interconnected. The board contains conductive pathways, called traces, which are typically made of copper and serve as the electrical connections between components. PCBs form the structural and electrical foundation for most modern electronic devices, from consumer electronics to industrial equipment.

## Manufacturing and Design

PCBs are manufactured through a series of processes that involve etching copper layers, drilling holes for component leads, and applying solder masks and labels. The design phase uses specialized [[concepts/software|software]] to create circuit layouts before manufacturing begins. Single-sided boards contain conductive traces on one surface, while multi-layer boards stack multiple conductive layers with insulation between them, allowing for more complex circuitry in compact spaces.

## Function and Applications

The primary function of a PCB is to provide mechanical support and electrical connections for [[concepts/electronic-components|electronic components]] such as resistors, capacitors, transistors, and integrated circuits. PCBs are essential in virtually all modern electronics, including computers, smartphones, automotive systems, medical devices, and consumer appliances. The ability to standardize and mass-produce PCBs has been fundamental to the development and affordability of electronic technology.
