# Climate Science

Climate science is the systematic study of Earth's climate systems and the long-term atmospheric patterns that characterize them. It integrates knowledge from multiple disciplines—including atmospheric physics, oceanography, geology, and ecology—to understand how climate operates and changes over time. Climate scientists examine interactions between the atmosphere, oceans, ice sheets, and the biosphere to explain both current conditions and past climates, as well as to project future changes.

## Methods and Data

Climate research relies on diverse observational and analytical approaches. Scientists collect data through ground-based weather stations, satellite instruments, ice cores, tree rings, and ocean measurements that extend observations back centuries or millennia. This information is processed using mathematical models that simulate atmospheric circulation, ocean currents, and energy transfer. By comparing historical records with instrumental measurements, researchers can identify long-term trends and distinguish natural variability from human influences.

## Key Areas of Study

Major areas within climate science include understanding greenhouse gas cycles, solar radiation effects, ocean circulation patterns, and feedback mechanisms that amplify or dampen climate change. Researchers also investigate regional climate variations, extreme weather events, and the impacts of climate shifts on ecosystems and human societies. This work informs assessments of climate sensitivity—how much global temperatures respond to increased atmospheric carbon dioxide and other forcings.
