# Salmon Poaching

Salmon poaching is a classical French cooking technique in which salmon fillets or steaks are gently simmered in a flavored liquid and served with a cream-based sauce. The method originates from Normandy, a region in northern France renowned for both its salmon fisheries and cider production. This traditional approach emphasizes delicate, moist results through controlled application of low to medium heat, allowing the fish to cook gently while absorbing subtle flavors from the poaching liquid.

The poaching liquid typically consists of cider, wine, or a combination of both, often enriched with aromatics such as shallots, herbs, and spices. The salmon is submerged in this mixture and cooked at a temperature just below boiling to prevent the flesh from breaking apart. This gentle cooking process ensures that the protein coagulates slowly, preserving the natural texture and moisture of the fish while infusing it with the acidity and sweetness of the liquid.

Once cooked, the salmon is usually removed from the poaching liquid and plated. The remaining liquid is often reduced and emulsified with heavy cream or butter to create a rich, velvety sauce that complements the mild flavor of the fish. This dish is traditionally associated with Normandy and reflects the region's agricultural staples, combining local dairy products with its famous apple cider to create a distinctive regional specialty.
