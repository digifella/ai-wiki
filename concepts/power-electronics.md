# Power Electronics

## Core Concepts
- **Definition**: The field of engineering that deals with the control and conversion of electric power using solid-state devices.
- **Key Components**: IGBT, MOSFET, SiC, GaN, DC-DC Converter, AC-DC Inverter.
- **Applications**: [[concepts/renewable-energy|Renewable Energy]] Integration, Electric Vehicles, Smart Grid, HVDC Transmission.

## Solid-State Transformer (SST)
A power electronic device that replaces traditional magnetic transformers with high-frequency switching circuits, enabling bidirectional power flow and advanced grid control.

### Evolution & Context
- SSTs represent a shift from Electromagnetic Induction to High-Frequency Switching for voltage transformation.
- Recent analysis highlights the technology's potential to modernize the grid despite historical adoption barriers [[lab-notes/2026-08-26-Solid-State-Transformer-Evolution-Advantages-and-Obstacl|Solid-State Transformer: Evolution, Advantages, and Obstacles to Market Adoption]].

### Advantages
- **Size & Weight**: Significant reduction in physical footprint compared to conventional Power Transformers due to high-frequency operation.
- **Grid Flexibility**: Enables Power Quality improvement, Voltage Regulation, and Islanding capabilities.
- **Bidirectional Flow**: Essential for Microgrid integration and Distributed Generation.
- **Fault Current Limiting**: Inherent ability to limit short-circuit currents, enhancing grid stability.

### Obstacles to Market Adoption
- **Cost**: High initial capital expenditure due to complex power electronic components and control systems.
- **Reliability & Thermal Management**: Challenges in managing heat dissipation and ensuring long-term reliability of semiconductor switches.
- **Control Complexity**: Requires sophisticated Digital Signal Processing and real-time control algorithms.
- **Standardization**: Lack of unified industry standards for interoperability and safety.
