# Chest X Rays

Chest X-rays are radiographic images of the thorax used to diagnose and monitor a wide range of pulmonary, cardiac, and mediastinal conditions. They remain one of the most commonly ordered imaging studies in clinical practice due to their accessibility, low cost, and minimal radiation exposure compared to other imaging modalities. A standard chest X-ray examination typically includes posteroanterior (PA) and lateral views, which provide complementary perspectives for evaluating the structures within the chest cavity.

## Clinical Applications

Chest X-rays are employed to identify infections such as pneumonia, evaluate lung nodules and masses, assess cardiac size and contour, detect pleural effusions, and monitor the position of medical devices like central lines and endotracheal tubes. They are also useful for detecting pneumothorax, atelectasis, and signs of congestive heart failure. The simplicity and speed of chest radiography make it an effective first-line imaging tool in both acute and chronic clinical settings.

## Technical Considerations

The quality and diagnostic utility of a chest X-ray depend on proper patient positioning, adequate inspiration, and appropriate exposure settings. Standard views—posteroanterior (PA), lateral, and occasionally apical lordotic—allow radiologists to systematically evaluate the lungs, heart, mediastinum, and chest wall. Portable anteroposterior (AP) X-rays may be obtained at the bedside when patients cannot be transported to the imaging department, though these have reduced image quality compared to standard studies.
