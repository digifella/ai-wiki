# Color Casts

Color casts are unwanted color tints that uniformly shift the overall color balance of a photograph away from neutral or intended tones. They occur when a single color—such as intense reds, blues, or greens—dominates an image, typically caused by the lighting environment where the photo was taken. Digital cameras interpret light through white balance settings, which attempt to identify what should appear neutral in a scene. When actual lighting conditions deviate significantly from daylight, cameras may misinterpret the color information, resulting in a pervasive tint that obscures the true colors of the subject.

In concert photography, extreme color casts are common due to the use of artificial stage lighting, such as deep reds, blues, or greens, which are often far removed from the camera's default daylight white balance. These lighting conditions can cause the camera's automatic white balance to fail, leaving the image with a heavy, unnatural hue that does not reflect the actual appearance of the performers or the venue. Correcting these casts is essential for restoring visual accuracy and ensuring that skin tones and stage elements appear natural.

Adobe Lightroom Classic provides tools to address these issues by allowing precise control over white balance and color grading. Users can manually adjust the temperature and tint sliders to counteract the dominant hue, or use the eyedropper tool to set a neutral gray point within the image. For more complex scenarios, the HSL/Color panel can be used to selectively adjust specific color ranges, reducing the intensity of the offending cast while preserving other details. This targeted approach helps in recovering detail in shadows and highlights that may have been crushed or blown out by the intense stage lighting.
