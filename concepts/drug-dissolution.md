# Drug dissolution

The kinetic process by which a solid solute enters a solvent to form a homogeneous [[concepts/solution|solution]]. In pharmacokinetics, the dissolution rate is frequently the rate-limiting step for the systemic absorption of orally administered medications.

## Key Determinants
- **Solubility**: The equilibrium concentration of a solute in a solvent; highly dependent on temperature and pressure.
- Polymorphism: The existence of a substance in multiple crystalline forms. Different polymorphs possess distinct lattice energies, which directly impact solubility and dissolution kinetics.
- **Surface Area**: The availability of the solid-liquid interface; often manipulated via micronization to increase dissolution [[concepts/speed|speed]].
- **Environmental Factors**: Solvent pH, viscosity, and the pKa of the drug molecule.

## Clinical and Industrial Implications
- **Bioavailability**: Inadequate dissolution can lead to insufficient plasma concentrations, [[concepts/visual-rendering|rendering]] a drug therapeutically ineffective.
- **Stability and [[concepts/quality-control|Quality Control]]**: Unexpected [[concepts/phase|phase]] transitions can compromise drug manufacturing and safety.
