# Train Ridership

**Train ridership** refers to the volume of passengers using rail transit systems, serving as a key metric for network utilization, economic impact, and urban [[concepts/transport-efficiency|mobility efficiency]].

## Key Drivers
*   **[[concepts/infrastructure|Infrastructure]] Capacity:** New lines and tunnels significantly alter capacity constraints and travel times.
*   **Fare Policy:** Pricing structures directly influence mode choice and overall demand elasticity.
*   **Service Frequency:** Reliability and headway are critical for commuter retention.

## Case Study: Melbourne Metro Tunnel
The opening of the Melbourne [[concepts/metro-tunnel|Metro Tunnel]] represents a pivotal shift in regional ridership dynamics. Analysis of this event highlights the correlation between major infrastructure completion and fare policy adjustments.

*   **Impact Analysis:** Recent assessments focus on how the tunnel's full operational opening interacts with current fare structures to drive or suppress ridership growth [[lab-notes/2026-09-12-Melbourne-Metro-Tunnel-Opening-Fare-Policy-Train-Ridersh|Melbourne Metro Tunnel Opening & Fare Policy: Train Ridership Impact Analysis]].
