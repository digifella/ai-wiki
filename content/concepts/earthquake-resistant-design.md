# Earthquake Resistant Design

Earthquake resistant design encompasses structural engineering strategies that protect buildings and infrastructure from seismic damage. Rather than making structures rigidly resistant to ground motion, modern approaches focus on allowing controlled [[concepts/exercise|movement]] and energy dissipation during [[concepts/earthquakes|earthquakes]]. This [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]] recognizes that some flexibility and controlled deformation is often safer than attempting to resist all forces.

## Base Isolation Systems

Base isolation is a key technique that uses bearing mechanisms placed between a building's foundation and its superstructure. These bearings—typically made of elastomeric materials, springs, or [[concepts/friction|friction]] pendulum systems—decouple the building from ground motion by allowing the foundation to move while the building above remains relatively stable. This separation dramatically reduces the forces transmitted through the [[concepts/structure|structure]], minimizing damage to the building and its contents.

## Additional Strategies

Beyond isolation, earthquake resistant design incorporates other methods such as damping systems that absorb energy through specialized devices, flexible connections that allow structural elements to move without failure, and reinforced concrete and steel designs that provide ductility. The choice of approach depends on local seismic risk, building use, and economic factors. Critical infrastructure like hospitals and emergency response centers prioritize these technologies to maintain functionality immediately after earthquakes.
