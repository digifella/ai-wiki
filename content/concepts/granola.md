# Granola

[[entities/granola|Granola]] is a breakfast cereal made from rolled oats combined with nuts, seeds, and a sweetening [[entities/agent|agent]], typically honey or [[concepts/sugar|sugar]]. The mixture is baked until the ingredients bind together and brown, creating crunchy clusters. The resulting product can be eaten dry as a cereal with milk, yogurt, or plant-based alternatives, or consumed as a snack on its own.

## Composition and Variations

The basic formula for granola includes oats as the primary ingredient, supplemented with ingredients such as almonds, walnuts, sunflower seeds, and pumpkin seeds. Beyond these core components, manufacturers and home cooks frequently add dried fruits like raisins, cranberries, or dates; coconut flakes; chocolate chips; or spices such as cinnamon and vanilla. The ratio of components and choice of sweetener vary significantly between commercial brands and homemade recipes, affecting both flavor profile and nutritional content.

## Nutritional Considerations

Granola is calorie-dense due to its oil and sugar content, though homemade versions can be adjusted for lower sugar or fat. The oats and nuts provide fiber and protein, while seeds contribute various micronutrients. Commercial granola products vary widely in added sugars and sodium levels, making nutritional comparison between brands important for consumers monitoring their dietary intake.
