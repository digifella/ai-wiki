# Coma Cluster

The Coma Cluster (Abell 1656) is a massive, prominent galaxy cluster located in the direction of the constellation Coma Berenices. It serves as a primary laboratory for studying Large-scale structure, [[concepts/galaxy-formation-and-evolution|Galaxy Evolution]], and the distribution of [[concepts/dark-matter]].

## Key Features
- **[[concepts/writing|Composition]]**: Contains thousands of individual galaxies embedded in a dense Intra-cluster [[entities/medium|medium]] (ICM).
- **Mass Distribution**: Evidence of significant [[concepts/dark-matter]] presence is derived from Gravitational Lensing and X-ray observations of the hot gas within the cluster.
- **Cosmological Context**: A vital component of the Cosmic Web, representing high-[[concepts/density|density]] [[concepts/nodes|nodes]] in the universe's structural framework.

## Dark Matter Research & Observations
- Extensive studies of the cluster's mass-to-[[concepts/light|light]] ratio reinforce the dominance of non-[[concepts/baryonic-matter|baryonic matter]].
- Recent advancements in Gamma-Ray [[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]] provide new comparative data:
    - Potential detection of [[concepts/wimp]] [[concepts/annihilation-signals|annihilation signals]] in [[concepts/milky-way]] [[entities/gamma|Gamma]] [[concepts/rays|Rays]] offers a potential template for identifying similar dark matter signatures in massive clusters (2026 04 24 [[concepts/gamma-rays|Dark Matter WIMP Annihilation Signal]] Detected in Milky Way Gamma Rays).
