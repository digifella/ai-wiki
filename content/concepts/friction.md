# Friction

Friction is a resistive force that arises when two surfaces in [[entities/contact|contact]] attempt to move relative to one another. This force always opposes the direction of motion (or potential motion) and acts parallel to the surfaces involved. Friction is a fundamental phenomenon in [[concepts/physics|physics]] that affects nearly every mechanical system, from the operation of vehicles and machinery to the [[concepts/exercise|movement]] of everyday objects.

## Types and Mechanisms

There are two primary categories of friction: static friction and kinetic friction. Static friction prevents motion between surfaces at rest and can vary in magnitude up to a maximum threshold; once this threshold is exceeded, the surfaces begin to slide. Kinetic friction, also called sliding friction, acts between surfaces already in relative motion and typically has a lower magnitude than maximum static friction. The microscopic mechanisms behind friction involve surface irregularities and intermolecular forces between materials; even apparently smooth surfaces are rough at the microscopic scale.

## Coefficient and Practical Applications

The strength of friction between two materials is quantified by the coefficient of friction, a dimensionless value that depends on the [[entities/nature|nature]] of both surfaces in contact. This coefficient allows friction to be calculated as a function of the normal force pressing the surfaces together. Understanding friction is essential for engineering applications ranging from brake design to structural stability. Related concepts such as the [[concepts/angle-of-repose|angle of repose]]—the steepest angle at which a material remains stable on an inclined surface—directly depend on friction between the material and the surface.
