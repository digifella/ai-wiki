# Seismic Data

Seismic data comprises recordings of elastic waves propagating through Earth's interior, captured by global networks of seismometers. These instruments detect ground motion generated by natural events, such as earthquakes and volcanic activity, as well as controlled sources like explosions. The raw data is converted into digital signals that record the amplitude, frequency, and arrival times of compressional (P) and shear (S) waves, providing a primary dataset for understanding the planet's internal dynamics.

Data acquisition relies on dense arrays of seismometers distributed across continents and ocean floors. Processing techniques filter noise and align waveforms to reconstruct the path and velocity of seismic energy as it travels through different layers of the Earth. This analysis allows geophysicists to map the boundaries between the crust, mantle, and core, identifying regions where wave speeds change due to variations in material composition and state.

Recent analysis of seismic anomalies in Earth's inner core has suggested the potential existence of a new state of matter. These anomalies manifest as unexpected variations in wave propagation speeds and attenuation patterns that do not align with standard models of solid iron-nickel alloys. Researchers continue to investigate whether these signals indicate a distinct phase of matter, such as a superionic state, or complex structural heterogeneities within the core.
