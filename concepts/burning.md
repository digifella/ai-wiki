# Burning

**Burning** is a [[concepts/photo-retouching|photo retouching]] technique originally derived from the darkroom process of selectively increasing [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] in specific areas of a print. In [[concepts/digital-post-processing|digital post-processing]], it involves darkening regions to create [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], define edges, and enhance three-dimensional depth. It is the counterpart to [[concepts/dodging]].

## Technical Definition
- **Traditional**: Prolonged exposure of specific film areas during printing to darken them.
- **Digital**: Lowering exposure, luminance, or brightness values locally within an [[concepts/image-editing|image editing]] software (e.g., [[entities/adobe|Adobe]] [[concepts/lightroom]], [[concepts/photoshop]]).

## Application in Digital Workflow
- **Purpose**: To guide the viewer’s eye, suppress distracting highlights, and add volume/depth to flat subjects.
- **Tools**:
  - Adjustment brushes, [[concepts/gradient-tools|gradient filters]], and radial filters.
  - Luminance masks for precise selection.
  - Curve [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] for tonal control.

## Related Concepts
- [[concepts/dodging]]: Lightening specific areas to draw [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] or reveal shadow detail.
- Chiaroscuro: The strong [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] between [[concepts/light|light]] and dark, often achieved through balanced dodging and burning.
- [[concepts/local-adjustments]]: [[concepts/non-destructive-editing|Non-destructive editing]] confined to specific image regions.
