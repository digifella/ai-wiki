## Kim Percy's Visualising the Invisible Exhibition: Art and Dyslexia Strengths
**Clip title:** Kim Percy - Visualising the Invisible Exhibition
**Author / channel:** Federation University Australia
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ss57GR1CMjs

### Summary
[[entities/kim-percy|Kim Percy]], an artist, designer, and sessional lecturer at Federation University, introduces her [[entities/phd|PhD]] exhibition titled "Visualising the Invisible" at the [[entities/post-office-gallery|Post Office Gallery]] in Ballarat, Australia. The core of her work revolves around investigating dyslexia through the lens of [[concepts/visual-art|visual art]] and personal exploration. Having been late-diagnosed with dyslexia, Percy embarked on this [[entities/phd|PhD]] journey to understand how her cognitive differences have shaped her identity and art practice, discovering that what she once considered personal strengths are often attributes of dyslexia.

Percy's research delves into the correlations between dyslexia and visual art, revealing how this [neurodivergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurodivergence) contributes to unique ways of [[concepts/human-cognition|thinking]], including strong [visual-spatial awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual-spatial_awareness), pattern recognition, narrative [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]], and multi-dimensional [[concepts/human-cognition|thinking]]. Far from being solely about challenges, her exhibition is a celebration of difference, [[concepts/resilience|resilience]], and creative strength. Each aspect of her extensive thesis has been translated into a series of artworks, serving as visual "chapters" that respond to her research topics.

The exhibition features various pieces that explore different facets of the dyslexic experience. For instance, "The Oscillation of Disclosure" uses figures submerged in water to represent the dilemma of disclosing an [invisible disability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invisible_disability) and its potential impact on one's career. Other works address the challenges of language perception and the [[concepts/stress-management|coping]] strategies, often involving "[[concepts/masking|masking]]" or hiding, adopted by individuals with dyslexia. These diverse art forms, including a children's book titled "The Girl Who Tried," provide a multifaceted insight into living with dyslexia.

Ultimately, "Visualising the Invisible" serves as a powerful platform to open up crucial conversations. [[concepts/visual-arts|Kim Percy]] expresses pride and privilege in creating a body of work that allows people—whether dyslexic, [ADHD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADHD), autistic, or otherwise neurodivergent—to explore, explain, or reveal their own experiences. The exhibition creates a space for shared understanding and destigmatizes neurodiversity, highlighting its creative potential and fostering a sense of community and acceptance.
