# Manual Settings

Manual settings are essential controls available on cameras and scanning equipment that allow users to override automatic functions and have direct command over [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], focus, and other technical [[concepts/parameters|parameters]]. In the context of [[concepts/camera-scanning|home film scanning]], manual settings give photographers precise control over how their negatives or [[entities/google-slides|slides]] are digitized, resulting in higher quality scans tailored to individual film stocks and lighting conditions.

## Application in Film Scanning

When [[concepts/high-quality-images|scanning film at home]], manual settings typically include aperture, shutter speed (or scan resolution), white balance, and focus [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]]. Rather than relying on automatic detection, which may misinterpret film characteristics or [[concepts/color-casts|color casts]], manual control allows the scanner operator to optimize each scan based on the specific film type, density, and desired output. This approach is particularly useful for unusual or expired film stocks that [[concepts/automations|automated systems]] might struggle to process correctly.

## Learning Manual Settings

Resources like the [[entities/bh-photo|B&H Photo]] [[concepts/tutorial|tutorial]] by [[entities/lotta-milde|Lotta Milde]] provide practical guidance on understanding and applying manual settings when scanning film at home. By mastering these controls, photographers can achieve consistent results, save on professional lab costs, and develop a deeper understanding of their film's characteristics and how they translate to digital formats.
