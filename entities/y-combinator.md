# Y Combinator

Y Combinator is a startup accelerator and venture capital firm founded in 2005 by Paul Graham, Jessica Livingston, Robert Morris, and Trevor Blackwell. Based in San Francisco, the organization provides seed funding, mentorship, and resources to early-stage technology companies, typically in exchange for a small equity stake. The accelerator operates through intensive batch programs lasting approximately three months, during which founders receive guidance on product development, fundraising, and business strategy.

## Operating Model

Y Combinator's primary mechanism is its batch program, which admits cohorts of startups multiple times per year. Selected companies receive initial seed funding, office space, and access to a network of experienced mentors and investors. The program culminates in Demo Day, where founders present their progress to a large audience of potential investors. Beyond the batch program, Y Combinator has evolved to offer additional services including growth-stage funding and ongoing support for portfolio companies.

## Impact and Scope

The organization has funded thousands of companies across diverse sectors including software, hardware, biotech, and financial services. Notable Y Combinator alumni include Airbnb, Dropbox, Stripe, and Reddit, among many others. Y Combinator's influence on startup culture and the venture capital ecosystem has been substantial, establishing a model for accelerators that has been replicated globally. The firm manages multiple funds and continues to expand its investment activities beyond the core batch program.
