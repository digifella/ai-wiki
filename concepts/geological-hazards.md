# Geological Hazards

Geological hazards are naturally occurring Earth processes that pose direct risks to human life, property, and infrastructure. These phenomena stem from the dynamic nature of Earth's crust, internal heat flow, and surface processes, operating across timescales ranging from seconds to centuries. Unlike environmental hazards driven by weather or biological systems, geological hazards are fundamentally caused by planetary-scale physical forces within and at the surface of the Earth.

## Primary Types

Major geological hazards include volcanic eruptions, earthquakes, tsunamis, landslides, and ground subsidence. Volcanic hazards encompass not only lava flows but also pyroclastic flows, lahars, and flank collapses that can rapidly alter landscapes and displace populations. Earthquakes release accumulated stress along tectonic plate boundaries and faults, causing ground shaking and triggering secondary hazards such as landslides and tsunamis. Landslides themselves represent a significant hazard, often triggered by heavy rainfall, earthquakes, or volcanic activity on steep slopes.

## Human Vulnerability

The impact of geological hazards on human populations depends on both the frequency and magnitude of events in a given region, as well as the density and preparedness of nearby settlements. Areas with high population density near active plate boundaries, volcanoes, or unstable slopes face elevated risk. Modern hazard assessment combines historical records, geological mapping, and monitoring technology to estimate risk and inform land-use planning and emergency preparedness strategies.
