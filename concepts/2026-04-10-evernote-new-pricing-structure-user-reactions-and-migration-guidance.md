# 2026 04 10 Evernote New Pricing Structure User Reactions And Migration Guidance

In April 2026, [[entities/evernote|Evernote]] announced a significant restructuring of its subscription [[concepts/pricing-model|pricing model]], consolidating its previous multi-tiered offering into a simplified two-tier system. The new structure consisted of a free basic plan and a single premium subscription tier. Evernote's [[concepts/leadership|leadership]] indicated that the redesign was driven by operational sustainability requirements and the need to allocate resources toward platform development and [[concepts/infrastructure|infrastructure]] improvements.

The revised free plan retained core note-taking capabilities but introduced stricter limits on device synchronization and monthly upload allowances. The premium tier, which replaced the former Professional and Business plans, offered unlimited device sync, advanced search functionality, and offline access. This [[concepts/consolidation|consolidation]] aimed to reduce customer confusion regarding feature availability across different subscription levels, although it resulted in the removal of certain niche features previously available only to higher-tier subscribers.

User reactions were mixed, with a segment of long-term customers expressing frustration over the loss of [[concepts/granular-control|granular control]] over their data and the perceived reduction in value for the premium price point. Conversely, some users welcomed the simplified billing structure, noting that it reduced the complexity of managing multiple subscriptions. Social media discussions and tech forums highlighted concerns about data portability and the potential impact on teams that had relied on the previous business-specific features.

[[concepts/user-feedback|Migration guidance]] provided by Evernote emphasized the [[concepts/value|importance]] of exporting data before the transition deadline to ensure compatibility with alternative platforms. The company offered a grace period for existing subscribers to adjust to the new terms, with prorated refunds available for those who chose to cancel. Technical support resources were updated to assist users in transferring [[concepts/notes|notes]], tags, and [[concepts/notebook-tools|notebooks]] to competing services such as [[entities/notion|Notion]], OneNote, and [[concepts/obsidian|Obsidian]], reflecting the anticipated shift in user behavior following the pricing overhaul.
