# Low Frequency Radio Range

**[[concepts/camera-raw|Low Frequency Radio Range]]** (LFR) was a pioneering aviation navigation system used in the early-to-mid 20th century to guide aircraft along airways. It relied on the pilot's auditory interpretation of overlapping radio beams to maintain course.

## Overview
The system transmitted two overlapping radio signals with distinct [[concepts/audio-modality|audio]] modulations (typically Morse code letters 'A' and 'N'). Pilots listened via headphones; a continuous [[concepts/tone|tone]] indicated they were on the correct centerline, while alternating tones signaled deviation.

## Key Characteristics
- **Auditory [[concepts/recommendations|Guidance]]:** Unlike later visual Instrument Landing System (ILS) approaches, LFR required pilots to interpret sound patterns.
- **Frequency Band:** Operated in the low frequency spectrum, allowing for long-range propagation but requiring large antenna arrays.
- **Limitations:** Susceptible to atmospheric interference and required precise aircraft equipment.
