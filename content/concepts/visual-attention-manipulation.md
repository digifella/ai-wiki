# Visual Attention Manipulation

Visual attention manipulation refers to the deliberate application of design techniques to guide where viewers look within an image or composition. In [[concepts/aesthetic-design|visual design]] and photography, these methods control the viewer's eye [[concepts/exercise|movement]] and determine which elements receive focus first, second, and third. The goal is to communicate [[concepts/hierarchy|hierarchy]], emphasize subject matter, and create a more intentional viewing experience.

## Applications in Photography and Editing

In [[concepts/portrait-photography|portrait photography]], [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] manipulation techniques are commonly applied during post-processing using software like [[concepts/photoshop|Photoshop]] and Lightroom. Common methods include selective sharpening to emphasize the subject's eyes or face while softening the background, strategic use of [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] to make key areas stand out, and careful [[concepts/exposure-adjustment|exposure adjustment]] to draw focus to specific regions. Darkening peripheral areas or enhancing subject brightness are also effective approaches. These techniques work by exploiting how human [[concepts/computer-vision|vision]] naturally gravitates toward sharp, bright, and high-contrast elements.

## Related Techniques

Attention manipulation overlaps with broader photo processing approaches such as [[concepts/dark-and-moody-photo-processing|dark and moody]] edits, where deliberate tonal choices create drama while simultaneously directing focus toward lit areas of the composition. Technical precision, such as achieving tack-[[concepts/sharpness|sharp focus]] in critical areas, provides the foundation for these directional techniques to work effectively.
