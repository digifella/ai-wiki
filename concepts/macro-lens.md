# Macro Lens

A macro lens is a specialized optical instrument designed to achieve high magnification ratios, typically 1:1 or greater, which allows the subject to be projected onto the camera sensor at life-size or larger. This capability enables photographers to capture minute details with extreme precision, revealing textures and structures that are often invisible to the naked eye. The primary distinguishing feature of these lenses is their ability to maintain sharp focus at very short working distances, often just centimeters from the subject, while preserving image quality across the entire frame.

The optical design of macro lenses prioritizes flat field correction and minimal distortion to ensure that the entire subject remains in focus and accurately represented. Unlike standard lenses, macro optics are engineered to perform optimally at close range, where depth of field becomes critically shallow. This requires precise control over aperture and focus stacking techniques to manage the plane of sharpness effectively.

In the context of film scanning, macro lenses serve as a cost-effective alternative to dedicated film scanners. By mounting a macro lens on a digital camera and using a light table, users can digitize negatives or slides at home. This method reduces laboratory costs while offering greater control over resolution and color grading, provided the photographer has the necessary steady-hand techniques or tripod support to mitigate camera shake during close-up exposures.
