# Image Adjustments

Image [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] refer to digital modifications applied to photographs to enhance visual quality, correct technical problems, or achieve specific aesthetic goals. These techniques form a core component of [[concepts/smartphone-camera-apps|digital photography]] workflows and are typically performed using [[concepts/photo-editing|photo editing]] software such as [[concepts/photoshop|Adobe Photoshop]], [[concepts/lightroom|Lightroom]], or comparable applications. Adjustments can address fundamental imaging issues like [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] and color balance, or serve creative purposes such as stylistic enhancement or artistic interpretation.

## Common Adjustment Categories

Corrective adjustments address technical deficiencies in the original photograph. Exposure adjustments brighten or darken images, while white balance corrections address color temperature issues caused by different lighting conditions. [[concepts/contrast|Contrast]], saturation, and [[concepts/clarity-slider|clarity]] adjustments refine tonal range and visual impact. [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|Color grading]] allows for selective modification of specific [[concepts/color-ranges|color ranges]], and sharpening enhances perceived detail and definition.

Creative adjustments enable artists to develop a particular look or mood. These include color grading for stylistic effect, [[concepts/vignette-effects|vignetting]] to draw focus inward, and curve adjustments for fine-tuned tonal control. Many photographers apply consistent adjustment presets across image series to establish a recognizable [[concepts/style|visual style]] or maintain [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] within a project.

## Workflow Considerations

Most professional workflows apply non-destructive adjustments, meaning original image data remains unmodified. This approach allows photographers to revise decisions or [[concepts/scientific-experiment|experiment]] with alternative treatments. Understanding how adjustments interact—for example, how increased saturation affects perceived brightness—helps achieve balanced results. Effective image adjustment requires both technical knowledge of available tools and trained visual judgment about when adjustments enhance rather than degrade an image.
