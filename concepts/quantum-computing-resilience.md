# Quantum Computing Resilience

**[[concepts/quantum-computing|Quantum Computing]] Resilience** refers to the capacity of cryptographic systems, data integrity protocols, and computational [[concepts/infrastructure|infrastructure]] to withstand or recover from threats posed by quantum [[concepts/computation|computing]] capabilities and associated AI-driven attack vectors. It encompasses both post-quantum cryptography (PQC) migration and the re-evaluation of threat models regarding vulnerability management.

## Core Concepts

*   **Shift from Volume to Impact**: Traditional threat models often focused on the sheer volume of vulnerabilities. Modern resilience strategies, influenced by recent analyses, prioritize **[[concepts/validated-impact|validated impact]]** over vulnerability count [[lab-notes/2026-09-24-AI-Vulnpocalypse-Rethink-From-Vulnerability-Volume-to-Va|AI Vulnpocalypse Rethink: From Vulnerability Volume to Validated Impact]].
*   **AI-Driven Threat Landscape**: The integration of AI in cybersecurity creates a dual-use environment where AI can both defend against and exploit quantum-era vulnerabilities.
*   **Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC)**: The foundational layer of resilience, ensuring that current encryption standards remain secure against quantum algorithms like Shor's algorithm.
*   **Hybrid Security Models**: Combining classical cryptographic methods with PQC algorithms during the transition period to mitigate "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks.

## Strategic Implications

*   **Re-evaluating the "Vulnpocalypse"**: Recent discourse suggests that the immediate catastrophic impact of quantum computing on all cybersecurity may be overstated if focus remains on vulnerability volume rather than validated, exploitable impact [[AI Vulnpocalypse Rethink: From Vulnerability Volume to Validated Impact](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t8Y-eqZ5_-0)].
*   **Prioritization**: Resources should be allocated to high-impact, validated threats rather than attempting to patch every theoretical vulnerability.
*   **Interoperability**: Ensuring that PQC implementations do not break existing systems during the transition phase.
