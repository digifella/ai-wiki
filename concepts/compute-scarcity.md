# Compute Scarcity

Compute scarcity in [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] refers to a structural imbalance where the demand for [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]] exceeds the available supply of [[entities/high-performance|high-performance]] hardware, such as GPUs and TPUs. This constraint primarily affects the training of [[concepts/demystifying-llms|large language models]] and the [[concepts/ai-inference|inference]] of deployed services. When request volumes surpass physical capacity, service providers face operational bottlenecks that manifest as increased latency, reduced availability, or degraded service quality for end-users.

The phenomenon is driven by the [[concepts/exponential-growth|exponential growth]] in user adoption of AI tools, which has outpaced the deployment timeline for new [[concepts/data-center-infrastructure|data center infrastructure]]. This mismatch creates a strategic [[concepts/vulnerability|vulnerability]] for providers like Anthropic, whose rapid demand for Claude has highlighted the fragility of [[concepts/supply-chains|supply chains]] dependent on specialized silicon. The scarcity forces providers to make difficult trade-offs between scaling capacity, managing costs, and maintaining service [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] during peak usage periods.

Strategically, compute scarcity influences market dynamics by creating high barriers to entry for new competitors and increasing the leverage of hardware manufacturers. It compels AI firms to prioritize efficiency and [[concepts/algorithm-optimization|optimization techniques]] to maximize the utility of existing resources. Consequently, the ability to secure reliable compute access has become a critical determinant of [[concepts/unique-value-proposition|competitive advantage]] in the development and deployment of advanced [[concepts/ai-agents|AI agents]] and models.
