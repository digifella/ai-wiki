# simulated home theater sound

**Simulated home theater sound** refers to audio processing techniques that create the perception of immersive, multi-directional audio using fewer physical speakers than required by the original object-based format. This is distinct from true [[concepts/object-based-audio]] rendering, which relies on specific speaker configurations or binaural rendering for headphones.

## Core Concepts

*   **Object-Based Audio**: The underlying technology (e.g., [[concepts/dolby-atmos]]) where audio elements are treated as independent objects with metadata describing their position and movement, rather than being mixed into fixed channels.
*   **The "Deception"**: Many consumer implementations of "[[entities/dolby|Dolby]] Atmos" do not use height speakers or sufficient channel counts to physically reproduce the object-based mix. Instead, they use psychoacoustic algorithms to simulate height and surround effects.
*   **Perceptual vs. Physical Immersion**: While true object-based audio requires specific hardware, simulated sound aims to trick the brain into perceiving immersion through phase manipulation, frequency filtering, and dynamic range compression.

## Critical Perspectives

*   **Misrepresentation of Technology**: Critics argue that marketing "simulated" sound as "Dolby Atmos" misleads consumers about the fidelity and spatial accuracy of the experience.
*   **Hardware Dependency**: The effectiveness of simulated sound varies wildly based on the quality of the soundbar or AV receiver's processing engine.
*   **Loss of Intent**: The director's intended spatial placement of audio objects is often flattened or distorted when downmixed to simulated stereo or 5.1 outputs.
