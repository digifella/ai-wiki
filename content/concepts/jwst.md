# JWST

The James Webb Space Telescope ([[entities/jwst|JWST]]) is a space-based observatory developed through international collaboration, primarily by [[concepts/nasa|NASA]], the European Space Agency (ESA), and the Canadian Space Agency (CSA). Launched on December 25, 2021, it serves as the successor to the Hubble Space Telescope and operates primarily in the infrared spectrum, allowing it to detect light from distant and ancient objects that would be invisible to optical telescopes.

## Scientific Capabilities

JWST's infrared imaging [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] enable observations of the [[concepts/primordial-universe|primordial universe]], including some of the earliest galaxies and [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]] formed after the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]]. The telescope can observe through cosmic dust that blocks [[concepts/light|visible light]], revealing [[concepts/dense-gas-clouds|star-forming regions]] and the composition of distant astronomical objects. Its large primary mirror, composed of 18 hexagonal segments made of beryllium coated with gold, collects infrared radiation with unprecedented sensitivity and resolution.

## Operations

The telescope operates from the second Lagrange point (L2), approximately 1.5 million kilometers from Earth, where gravitational forces allow it to maintain a stable position relative to Earth and Sun. A large deployable sunshield protects its instruments from thermal radiation, maintaining operating temperatures near absolute [[concepts/concept-of-nothingness|zero]]. Since its launch, JWST has transmitted observations that have contributed to understanding of [[concepts/galaxy-formation|galaxy formation]], [[concepts/stellar-evolution|stellar evolution]], and exoplanet atmospheres.
