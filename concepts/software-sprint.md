# Software Sprint

A **Software Sprint** is a fixed time period (typically 1–4 weeks) during which a specific set of work must be completed and made ready for review in Agile and [[concepts/scrum]] methodologies. Sprints are the core iterative cycle of development, aiming to deliver incremental value through potentially shippable product increments.

## Core Characteristics

- **Time-boxed**: Strict duration limits prevent scope creep and enforce prioritization.
- **Goal-oriented**: Defined by a Sprint Goal that provides focus and [[concepts/cohesion|cohesion]].
- **Immutable Scope**: Once the sprint begins, scope is generally locked to protect the team's focus, barring critical emergencies.
- **Deliverable**: Results in a working product increment, not just documentation or code.

## AI-Assisted Development Integration

Modern sprints increasingly leverage [[concepts/development-speed|ai-assisted-development]] to accelerate delivery and enhance code quality. Key tools and strategies include:

- **[[concepts/ai-assisted-coding|Claude Code]]**: Utilized for automation and [[concepts/editing-workflow-optimization|workflow optimization]] within the sprint lifecycle.
- **Codex [[concepts/ai-performance-optimization|AI Optimization]]**: Advanced usage of Codex involves strategic model selection and leveraging post-GPT 5.6 update features for maximum [[concepts/productivity|productivity]] and safety. ## Lifecycle

1. **Sprint Planning**: Team selects items from the product backlog to commit to for the sprint, defining the Sprint Goal.
2. **Daily Stand-ups**: Short, time-boxed meetings to synchronize activities and identify impediments.
3. **Development & AI Augmentation**: Execution of tasks, potentially augmented by AI tools for [[concepts/code-generation|code generation]], review, and testing.
4. **Sprint Review**: Demonstration of the completed increment to stakeholders.
5. **Sprint Retrospective**: Reflection on the process to identify improvements for the next cycle.
