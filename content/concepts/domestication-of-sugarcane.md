# Domestication Of Sugarcane

Sugarcane originated in [[entities/papua-new-guinea|Papua New Guinea]] and the surrounding Pacific islands, where it grew wild before being domesticated by indigenous populations. Archaeological evidence suggests cultivation began around 8000 BCE, with the plant gradually spreading westward through trade routes and human migration. Early cultivators developed techniques for processing the cane to extract and concentrate its juice, laying the foundation for [[concepts/sugar|sugar]] production as a deliberate agricultural practice.

## Ancient and Medieval Development

By around 500 BCE, sugarcane cultivation had reached India, where it became integral to local [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]] and [[concepts/diet|diet]]. Indian producers refined methods for crystallizing sugar, creating a product that could be stored and traded over long distances. Knowledge of sugar production spread through the Islamic world during the medieval period, where it became a luxury commodity in Mediterranean and European markets, commanding high prices due to its [[concepts/limited-resources|scarcity]] and the labor-intensive methods required for its manufacture.

## Colonial Expansion and Enslavement

When European powers colonized tropical regions of the Americas from the 16th century onward, they established vast sugarcane plantations as a cornerstone of colonial economic systems. Planters imported enslaved African laborers to work these fields under brutal conditions, transforming sugarcane cultivation into one of the most consequential drivers of the transatlantic slave trade. This period marked the transformation of sugarcane from a regionally important crop into a [[concepts/global-commodity|global commodity]] whose production became inextricably linked to human trafficking and enslavement, fundamentally shaping colonial economies and societies for centuries.
