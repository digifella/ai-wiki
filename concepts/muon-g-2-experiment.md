# Muon G 2 Experiment

The Muon g-2 experiment is a precision measurement study conducted at Fermilab that determines the anomalous magnetic moment of the muon, a fundamental particle similar to the electron but approximately 200 times heavier. The g-2 factor quantifies how the muon's magnetic moment deviates from theoretical predictions based solely on its spin. This experiment serves as one of the most stringent tests of the Standard Model of particle physics, comparing experimental data with theoretical calculations to identify potential discrepancies.

The experiment utilizes a storage ring to circulate polarized muons in a uniform magnetic field. As the muons orbit, their spin precesses at a rate slightly different from their cyclotron frequency due to quantum fluctuations involving virtual particles. By measuring this precession frequency with extreme precision, physicists can calculate the anomalous magnetic moment and compare it against Standard Model predictions to search for signs of new physics.

Current results from Fermilab have shown a persistent deviation from theoretical expectations, suggesting possible interactions with undiscovered particles or forces. The collaboration continues to analyze data from multiple runs to reduce statistical and systematic uncertainties. Final results from the complete dataset are expected to be published in 2025, which will provide a definitive test of the Standard Model's validity.
