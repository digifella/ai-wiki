# Kalinga War

The Kalinga War was a significant military [[concepts/conflict|conflict]] fought between the [[concepts/mauryan-empire|Mauryan Empire]] under [[entities/emperor-ashoka|Emperor Ashoka]] and the kingdom of [[entities/kalinga|Kalinga]] in 261 BCE. This war marked one of the most brutal conquests during this period, leading to immense suffering on both sides.

- Resulted in immense destruction and loss of life.
- Sparked profound remorse in [[concepts/emperor-ashoka|Emperor Ashoka]] after witnessing its devastation.
- Led to Ashoka's conversion to [[concepts/buddhism|Buddhism]] and subsequent advocacy for non-violence.

### Historical Context
- Part of the expansionist [[concepts/policies|policies]] of the [[entities/mauryan-empire|Mauryan Empire]].
- Played a crucial role in shaping Ashoka’s later policies and [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]].
- Influenced the spread of Buddhist teachings beyond India, contributing to its global [[concepts/power|influence]].

### Impact on Culture and Science
- The war's aftermath saw significant shifts towards pacifism and humanitarian efforts within the empire.
- Inspired reforms that focused on [[concepts/religious-tolerance|religious tolerance]], public welfare, and moral [[concepts/governance|governance]].
- Contributed to advancements in [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] with the development of the concept of [[concepts/concept-of-nothingness|zero]] by ancient Indian scholars.
