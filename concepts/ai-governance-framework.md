# AI Governance Framework

An [[concepts/ai-governance|AI Governance]] Framework is a structured approach to translating abstract [[concepts/core-principles-of-ethics|ethical principles]] into concrete operational processes that enable [[concepts/health-sector|healthcare organizations]] to safely adopt and oversee [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] technologies. Rather than treating [[concepts/ai-ethics|AI ethics]] as purely philosophical concern, [[concepts/governance|governance]] frameworks establish the institutional [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]]—including [[concepts/policies|policies]], review procedures, [[concepts/accountability|accountability]] structures, and [[concepts/monitoring-systems|monitoring systems]]—that ensure [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] operate within defined ethical and safety boundaries.

## Core Components

Effective frameworks typically include several interconnected elements: clear [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] authority and accountability chains; processes for evaluating AI systems before deployment; ongoing performance monitoring and audit procedures; and mechanisms for addressing failures or unintended consequences. [[concepts/health|Healthcare]] organizations implementing these frameworks must define [[concepts/roles-and-responsibilities|roles and responsibilities]], establish criteria for assessing algorithmic bias and fairness, and create channels for stakeholder [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] including clinicians, patients, and affected communities.

## Implementation in Healthcare

Healthcare contexts present particular governance challenges because AI decisions directly impact patient safety and outcomes. Frameworks address clinical validation requirements, regulatory [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] (such as FDA oversight), integration with existing [[concepts/quality-assurance|quality assurance]] processes, and [[concepts/opacity|transparency]] in how AI [[concepts/recommendations|recommendations]] influence [[concepts/clinical-reasoning|clinical decision-making]]. Documentation and traceability become essential components, enabling organizations to explain and justify [[concepts/algorithmic-decision-making|algorithmic decisions]] when problems emerge.

The effectiveness of an [[concepts/ai-policy|AI governance]] framework depends on organizational commitment, adequate resourcing, and ongoing adaptation as technology and understanding evolve. Frameworks function best when embedded within broader [[concepts/organizational-culture|organizational culture]] rather than existing as isolated compliance exercises.
