# Commercial Jet Aviation

**Commercial Jet Aviation** refers to the operation of jet-powered aircraft for the transport of passengers and cargo. It marked a [[concepts/mindset-shift|paradigm shift]] from propeller-driven Propeller Aircraft in the mid-20th century, characterized by higher speeds, greater altitudes, and increased efficiency over long distances.

## Historical Context & Key Developments

The transition to jet propulsion in commercial aviation was driven by advancements in turbojet and turbofan [[concepts/engine|engine]] technology. Early adopters included the De Havilland Comet and Boeing 707, which established the standards for modern air travel.

### Canadian Aerospace Contributions

Canada played a significant, though often truncated, role in the early development of jet aviation technology.

*   **Avro C102 Jetliner**: A proposed jet airliner by Avro Canada that never entered production.
    *   Often overshadowed by the more famous Avro Arrow interceptor program.
    *   Represents a "tragic and largely forgotten chapter" in Canadian aerospace history.

## Technical Characteristics

*   **Propulsion**: Reliance on Turbojet and later Turbofan engines.
*   **Airframe Design**: Pressurized cabins, swept wings for high-[[concepts/speed|speed]] stability, and advanced materials.
*   **[[concepts/efficient-operation|Operational Efficiency]]**: Higher cruise speeds (Mach 0.7–0.85) compared to piston-engine contemporaries.
