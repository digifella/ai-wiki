# Post-quantum cryptography

[[concepts/post-quantum-security|Post-quantum cryptography]] (PQC) refers to [[concepts/cryptographic-algorithms|cryptographic algorithms]]—specifically public-key primitives—designed to remain [[concepts/secure|secure]] against the computational capabilities of a [[entities/quantum-computing]]-enabled adversary.

### Key Concepts
- Critical area of research and development aimed at mitigating Quantum Threats to existing Digital [[concepts/security|Security]] infrastructures.
- Focuses on protecting data even amidst the current hardware and [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]] limitations of [[entities/quantum-computing]].
- Addresses the [[concepts/vulnerability|vulnerability]] of current Classical [[concepts/cryptography|Cryptography]] standards to future quantum-scale attacks.
- **Policy & Transition**: Recent US Executive Orders mandate accelerated migration to [[concepts/encryption-standards|PQC standards]] to counter the evolving threat of [[concepts/Q-Day|Q-Day]] (the hypothetical date when quantum computers can break current encryption).
- **Strategic Urgency**: The transition is critical not just for future-proofing, but for mitigating "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks where adversaries store encrypted data today to decrypt once quantum capabilities mature.
