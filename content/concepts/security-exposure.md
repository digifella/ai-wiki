# Security Exposure

[[concepts/security|Security]] [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] refers to vulnerabilities or weaknesses in systems, [[concepts/software|applications]], or organizational processes that can be exploited by malicious actors or inadvertent misuse to compromise confidentiality, [[concepts/integrity|integrity]], or availability of assets. These exposures may stem from technical flaws in software or infrastructure, misconfigured systems, inadequate access controls, or gaps in security [[concepts/policies|policies]] and procedures. The consequences of unmitigated security exposures typically include data breaches, unauthorized system access, financial loss, operational disruption, or regulatory non-[[concepts/compliance|compliance]].

## Common Sources

Security exposures arise from multiple sources within an organization. Technical exposures include unpatched software, weak encryption, or misconfigured cloud [[entities/storage|storage]]. Procedural exposures emerge from insufficient access controls, lack of audit trails, or inadequate [[concepts/incident-response|incident response]] plans. Human and organizational factors—such as inadequate security [[concepts/training|training]], shadow operations, or pressure to bypass security controls—also create significant risk surfaces.

## Shadow IT as an Example

[[concepts/shadow-it|Shadow IT]] and unsanctioned tool [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] represent a growing class of security exposure in modern enterprises. When employees deploy unauthorized software or [[concepts/cloud-computing|cloud services]] to circumvent perceived inefficiencies, they often bypass security reviews, data [[concepts/governance|governance]] controls, and compliance oversight. This creates unmanaged risk vectors including potential [[concepts/data-leakage|data leakage]], system infiltration pathways, and regulatory violations.

## Risk Management

Addressing security exposure requires systematic identification through [[concepts/vulnerability|vulnerability]] assessments and penetration [[concepts/testing|testing]], prioritization based on likelihood and impact, and remediation through technical fixes, [[concepts/policy-updates|policy updates]], or process redesign. Ongoing monitoring, regular security audits, and employee awareness training help organizations reduce their exposure surface and respond more effectively to emerging threats.
