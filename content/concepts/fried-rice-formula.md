# Fried Rice Formula

Fried rice is a versatile dish that succeeds or fails based on two critical factors: proper rice [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] and thoughtful ingredient selection. The foundation of good fried rice depends on using the right type of cooked rice and preparing it correctly before frying. This formula approach allows cooks to create successful fried rice consistently, regardless of which specific ingredients are available or preferred.

## Rice Treatment

The most important step in making fried rice is ensuring the rice has the proper [[concepts/texture|texture]] before cooking. Freshly cooked warm rice contains too much moisture and [[entities/will|will]] become mushy when fried. Day-old rice that has been refrigerated is ideal, as the grains have dried out and separated, allowing them to fry individually rather than clump together. If fresh rice must be used, it should be spread on a sheet pan to cool and dry before frying. The rice grains should be distinct and slightly firm when they go into the pan.

## Mix-in Selection

While the rice technique is non-negotiable, the mix-ins can be tailored to whatever ingredients are on hand. Fried rice works well with proteins such as eggs, chicken, [[concepts/shrimp|shrimp]], or tofu, along with vegetables like peas, carrots, onions, and green onions. Aromatics like garlic and ginger add depth of flavor. The key principle is balancing textures and flavors rather than following a rigid recipe—the formula remains constant even as specific ingredients change.
