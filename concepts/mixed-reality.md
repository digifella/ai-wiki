# Mixed Reality

Mixed Reality (MR) is a technology that blends digital content with the physical world, enabling interactive experiences where virtual and real elements coexist and interact in real time. Unlike Virtual Reality, which creates entirely digital environments, or Augmented Reality, which overlays digital elements on a camera feed, Mixed Reality anchors digital objects to physical spaces with spatial awareness and persistent interaction capabilities. This positioning allows users to place, manipulate, and interact with virtual objects as though they exist in the real environment.

## Key Characteristics

Mixed Reality systems use spatial mapping and environmental understanding to integrate digital content meaningfully into physical spaces. Objects placed in an MR environment remain in consistent positions relative to physical landmarks, persist across multiple sessions, and can be occluded by real-world obstacles. This requires real-time depth sensing, motion tracking, and processing to maintain coherent interaction between digital and physical elements.

## Applications and Implementation

Mixed Reality has applications across design, education, manufacturing, and entertainment. Architects can visualize building designs in actual spaces, engineers can overlay instructions on equipment during maintenance, and teams can collaborate using shared virtual models in shared physical environments. Implementation typically relies on head-mounted displays, spatial computing platforms, or mobile devices equipped with necessary sensors and processing capabilities.
