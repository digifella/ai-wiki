# Pixels Per Inch

Pixels Per Inch (PPI) is a measurement of pixel density that expresses how many pixels are contained within one linear inch of a display or digital image. It is calculated by dividing the total number of pixels along one dimension by the physical size in inches along that same dimension. Higher PPI values indicate greater pixel density, which generally results in sharper, more detailed visual output when viewed at typical distances.

## Display and Imaging Applications

PPI is commonly used as a specification for digital displays, including computer monitors, smartphones, and tablets. In this context, it determines the sharpness of the screen and the clarity of text and graphics. A higher PPI means individual pixels are smaller and less visible to the human eye, creating a smoother appearance. This metric is particularly critical for mobile devices where viewing distance is short, necessitating high density to prevent the "screen door effect."

In digital imaging and printing, PPI relates to the resolution of an image file relative to its physical print size. While often confused with Dots Per Inch (DPI), which refers to the ink dots a printer places on paper, PPI specifically concerns the data density of the digital source. When preparing images for print, adjusting the PPI setting allows users to control the physical dimensions of the output without altering the total pixel count, thereby affecting the level of detail retained in the final hard copy.
