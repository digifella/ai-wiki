# Success Rates

Success rates in the context of [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] refer to the empirical measurement of how effectively repository-level context files improve agent performance. These specialized files—such as [[concepts/agents|AGENTS]].md and [[concepts/claude-ai|CLAUDE]].md—are designed to provide AI systems with structured information about a [[concepts/code|codebase]], its conventions, and operational guidelines. Measuring their effectiveness requires systematic evaluation of agent task completion, [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]], and decision-making quality with and without access to these contextual resources.

## Context Files and Agent Performance

Repository-level context files serve as a bridge between human knowledge about a project and [[concepts/ai-agent|AI agent]] capabilities. By documenting architectural decisions, [[concepts/coding|coding]] [[concepts/open-standards|standards]], available tools, and project-specific constraints in a standardized format, these files aim to guide agent behavior and improve outcome quality. The degree to which such documentation actually enhances agent success depends on factors including file completeness, clarity, relevance to specific tasks, and the agent's ability to effectively parse and utilize the information provided.

## Measurement Considerations

Empirical evaluation of success rates requires careful experimental design to isolate the impact of context files from other variables. Relevant metrics may include task completion rates, error reduction, decision quality alignment with project goals, and [[concepts/agent-efficiency|agent efficiency]]. Studies in this area examine whether the overhead of maintaining detailed context files produces measurable improvements in agent performance, and under what conditions such benefits are most significant.
