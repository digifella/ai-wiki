## Claude Fable 5.2 for Construction — Should you switch from Opus?
**Clip title:** Claude Fable 5.2 for Construction — Should you switch from Opus?
**Author / channel:** Tim Fairley
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ChYnZwJS498
**Credibility:** Commentary (tier 1 of 5). YouTube video from Tim Fairley: not edited or peer reviewed, so its claims are the speaker's own. Summary written by Gemini.

### Summary
The video discusses the capabilities and use cases of Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 model, using a construction scheduling simulation as a case study. The speaker ran 5,000 simulations of a warehouse construction schedule under various conditions and discovered that the initial schedule had less than a 1% chance of meeting the finish date, proving the original critical path incorrect. Fable 5.2 topped several industry benchmarks, including drawing analysis and takeoff.

The video highlights the differences between Fable 5.2 and Opus 5.5, noting that Fable is designed for long, complicated work and acts as a manager to orchestrate other sub-agents (like Opus and Sonnet), whereas Opus is better suited for smaller, everyday tasks. In a head-to-head comparison to generate a 3D model from a 2D construction drawing, Fable focused more on getting the underlying data file correct, while Opus focused on the visual rendering.

The speaker outlines a five-step architecture for using Fable on complex projects:
1. **Grill:** The user gives Fable a simple prompt, and Fable asks questions until it fully understands the goal and definition of "done."
2. **Plan:** Fable breaks the job down into steps and creates a plan.
3. **Set the tests:** Fable writes independent tests for each step to define success criteria.
4. **Hand out the work:** Fable acts as the manager, handing tasks to Opus or Sonnet sub-agents, which test their work before reporting back.
5. **Check:** A final instance of Fable checks the completed simulation against the original brief to catch any errors.

The speaker concludes that Fable is goal-oriented and excels at long-horizon tasks, keeping the goal in view and managing sub-agents, effectively changing the way work is done by allowing users to simulate and test multiple project schedules at zero cost.

#### Description
I used Claude Fable 5.2 to build a warehouse construction simulation and run it 5,000 times, varying weather, crew productivity and late deliveries. I show what drove the finish date and what happened when we tested 128 combinations of changes to the plan.

The simulation uses assumed delivery conditions to demonstrate the method. It is not a forecast for a live project. Our take-off tests also show why the quantities still need checking before you price the work.
