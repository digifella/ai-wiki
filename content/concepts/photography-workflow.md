# Photography Workflow

Photography workflow refers to the systematic process photographers follow from capture through final delivery of [[concepts/images|images]]. A typical workflow encompasses several stages: image ingestion and [[concepts/organization|organization]], culling and selection, editing and enhancement, and export or publication. The specific steps and tools used vary depending on the photographer's genre, output requirements, and personal preferences.

## Digital Organization and Culling

After importing images, photographers sort and organize their digital files for efficient management. This stage includes reviewing images, removing duplicates and poor shots, and rating or flagging keepers. Modern tools increasingly incorporate AI-[[concepts/assisted-culling|assisted culling]] features that help identify technically sound images or automatically stack similar shots, reducing manual review time for large batches.

## Post-Processing and Editing

The editing phase involves adjusting [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], color, [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], and other properties to achieve the desired final appearance. Common [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] include exposure correction, white balance, local [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] for selective edits, and [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|color grading]]. Many photographers use dedicated [[concepts/software|software]] such as Lightroom or [[concepts/photoshop|Photoshop]], which offer preset-based workflows and increasingly automated tools powered by AI for tasks like intelligent masking and tonal adjustments.

## Output and Delivery

The final stage prepares images for their intended use—whether for client delivery, print, web publication, or archival. This typically involves selecting appropriate file formats, applying sharpening and compression settings, and organizing exports. Workflow efficiency at this stage depends on having established export presets and clear organizational systems from earlier steps.
