# Historical Oversight

**Historical Oversight** refers to the systematic neglect, marginalization, or erasure of significant events, technologies, or narratives from mainstream historical [[concepts/conscious-thought|consciousness]]. This phenomenon often occurs when dominant narratives prioritize certain geopolitical actors or technological successes, leaving parallel developments—particularly those that failed or were cancelled—under-documented.

## Key Mechanisms
- **[[concepts/storytelling|Narrative]] Dominance:** Focus on "winners" (e.g., Boeing, Lockheed [[entities/martin-keen|Martin]]) obscures "losers" or cancelled projects.
- **Institutional [[concepts/amnesia|Memory Loss]]:** Lack of [[concepts/archival-preservation|archival preservation]] for defunct [[concepts/nodes|entities]].
- **Cultural Amnesia:** Public interest shifts rapidly, leaving niche historical topics without sustained engagement.

## Case Studies in Aerospace History

### The Avro C102 Jetliner
A primary example of historical oversight is the **Avro C102 Jetliner**, a Canadian commercial jet project that remains largely forgotten despite its significance.

- **Context:** Often overshadowed by the more famous Avro Arrow cancellation, the C102 represents a distinct failure in Canadian commercial aviation ambitions.
- **Significance:** It highlights the "unfulfilled promise" of Canada's aerospace industry in the jet age, illustrating how domestic [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] was stifled by political and economic decisions.
- **Source Integration:** Detailed analysis of this oversight can be found in [[lab-notes/2026-07-15-Avro-C102-Jetliner-Canadas-Unfulfilled-Promise-in-Commer|Avro C102 Jetliner: Canada's Unfulfilled Promise in Commercial Jet Aviation History]].

## Implications
Understanding historical oversight is crucial for a complete picture of technological evolution. Ignoring projects like the C102 leads to an incomplete understanding of:
1. The risks and failures inherent in aerospace development.
2. The specific geopolitical pressures on nations like Canada during the Cold War.
3. The potential alternative trajectories of global [[concepts/aviation-history|aviation history]].
