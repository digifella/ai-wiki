# Satellite Communication

Satellite communication involves the transmission of signals between ground stations and orbiting satellites, enabling global connectivity independent of terrestrial infrastructure. The technology functions by relaying radio signals from ground-based transmitters through orbiting satellites back to receiving stations, allowing communication across geographic areas where traditional network infrastructure is impractical or unavailable. This approach supports applications in telecommunications, broadcasting, weather monitoring, and internet connectivity.

## Orbital Architecture

Modern satellite communication systems, such as SpaceX's Starlink, utilize low Earth orbit (LEO) constellations to reduce latency and increase bandwidth compared to traditional geostationary systems. The technical evolution of these systems has progressed through distinct hardware generations, moving from the V1.5 configuration to the more advanced V3 satellites. These iterations involve significant changes in orbital architecture, including increased satellite density and refined orbital planes to optimize coverage and signal reliability.

## Signal Physics

The operation of satellite networks relies on precise signal physics to maintain link integrity across vast distances. As systems evolve from earlier versions to current standards, adjustments in antenna design, frequency bands, and beamforming techniques are implemented to mitigate atmospheric interference and signal degradation. The continuous refinement of these physical parameters ensures stable data transmission between the ground segment and the orbital layer, supporting the high-throughput requirements of modern broadband services.
