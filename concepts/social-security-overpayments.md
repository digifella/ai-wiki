# Social Security Overpayments

Social security overpayments occur when a government agency disburses more benefit funds to a recipient than they are entitled to receive. These discrepancies can result from administrative errors, failures to update records when a recipient's circumstances change, or calculation mistakes in determining benefit eligibility. Once identified, overpayments typically become the financial responsibility of the recipient, with recovery pursued through debt collection mechanisms or automatic deductions from future benefit payments.

## The Robodebt Scheme

Australia's Robodebt Scheme, formally known as the Income Compliance Program, utilized an automated algorithm to calculate social security debts. The system averaged income reported on tax returns over a year and applied that figure to fortnightly payment periods, assuming a linear distribution of income. This method ignored the actual fluctuation of earnings within those periods, leading to significant inaccuracies where individuals were deemed to have debts despite having no income in specific fortnights.

The scheme operated from 2016 to 2019 and affected hundreds of thousands of Australians. It was widely criticized by legal experts, auditors, and human rights advocates for its lack of due process and the presumption of guilt placed on recipients. In 2023, the Federal Court of Australia ruled that the averaging method used to calculate debts was unlawful, marking a significant legal precedent regarding the use of automated systems in welfare administration.
