# User Experience Design

User Experience [[concepts/design|Design]] (UX Design) is a foundational discipline focused on creating functional and intuitive interfaces that serve user needs effectively. Rather than prioritizing aesthetics alone, UX design emphasizes how users interact with digital products, systems, and services. This approach combines principles from [[concepts/psychology|psychology]], design, and technology to shape experiences that feel natural and purposeful.

## Core Design Principles

Several foundational concepts underpin effective UX design. **[[concepts/affordances|Affordances]]** refer to the visual and functional properties that signal how an element should be used—a button that looks clickable, or a slider that appears draggable. **[[concepts/hierarchy|Visual hierarchy]]** organizes information by importance, guiding users' [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] through size, color, [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], and positioning. **[[concepts/grids|Grids]]** provide structural frameworks that align elements consistently, creating coherence and reducing cognitive load. **[[concepts/typography|Typography]]** involves selecting and arranging typefaces to enhance readability and establish visual character, making [[concepts/text|text]] both accessible and expressive.

## Practical Application

These concepts work together to create interfaces that are both aesthetically coherent and functionally transparent. A well-designed interface anticipates user needs, communicates intent clearly, and minimizes [[concepts/friction|friction]] in completing tasks. By understanding and applying these core principles, designers can craft experiences that feel intuitive rather than requiring extensive instruction or guidance.
