# Water Scarcity

Water scarcity occurs when the available supply of fresh water is insufficient to meet the demands of human populations and natural ecosystems. Although Earth contains approximately 1.386 billion cubic kilometers of water, about 97% exists as saltwater in oceans. Of the remaining freshwater, roughly 69% is frozen in ice sheets and glaciers, while much of the rest is stored in groundwater aquifers. This leaves less than 1% of Earth's total water readily accessible as surface freshwater in lakes, rivers, and precipitation.

## Drivers and Global Distribution

Water scarcity is driven by multiple factors, including population growth, urbanization, and increased agricultural and industrial demand. The problem is not uniformly distributed globally; some regions experience chronic water stress due to arid or semi-arid climates, while others face seasonal scarcity linked to precipitation patterns. Climate change is intensifying water stress in many areas by altering rainfall distribution, accelerating glacial melt, and increasing evaporation rates. Over 2 billion people currently live in water-stressed regions, and this number is projected to rise significantly.

## Technological and Management Responses

Various approaches aim to address water scarcity, including desalination technologies that convert saltwater to freshwater, though these remain energy-intensive and costly. Water conservation measures, improved irrigation efficiency, groundwater management, and wastewater treatment and reuse offer more sustainable solutions. Infrastructure development, such as dams and water transfer systems, can redistribute water but often involves complex environmental and social trade-offs. Integrated water resource management that balances human needs with ecosystem requirements represents an important framework for long-term solutions.
