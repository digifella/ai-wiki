# Fabric Chemistry

Fabric chemistry encompasses the modification of textile properties through chemical treatments, focusing on surface tension alteration, molecular [[concepts/link-aggregation|bonding]], and hydrophobicity. Key areas include finishing agents, mordants, and historical [[concepts/heritage-preservation-methods|preservation techniques]].

## Hydrophobic Treatments & Metal Soaps
*   **[[concepts/aluminum-stearate|Aluminum Stearate]]**: A [[concepts/metal|metal]] soap formed by reacting aluminum salts with fatty acids (e.g., stearic acid). It creates a water-repellent barrier on fibers.
*   **Historical Application**: Used historically for waterproofing [[concepts/canvas|canvas]] and wool; often marketed deceptively as "permanent" solutions despite inherent instability [[lab-notes/2026-06-08-Waterproofing-Fabrics-with-Soap-and-Alum-History-Chemist|Waterproofing Fabrics with Soap and Alum: History, Chemistry, and Limitations]].
*   **Mechanism**: Aluminum ions ($Al^{3+}$) cross-link with carboxylate groups in soap molecules, depositing an insoluble salt layer on the fabric surface that repels water via high [[entities/contact|contact]] angle.

## Mordants & Reactive Agents
*   **Alum (Potassium Aluminum Sulfate)**: Primary mordant in natural dyeing; also acts as a catalyst in forming metal soaps when combined with fatty acids in alkaline conditions.
*   **Reactivity**: Facilitates binding of dyes to protein/cellulose fibers while modifying surface energy for water resistance.

## Limitations & Stability
*   **Hydrolysis**: Metal soaps degrade under acidic conditions or prolonged moisture [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], losing efficacy over time.
*   **Breathability Trade-off**: Dense [[concepts/metal-soap|aluminum stearate]] coatings can reduce fabric breathability compared to modern fluorocarbon-based DWRs (Durable Water Repellents).
