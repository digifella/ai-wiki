# Civilian Infrastructure Targeting

**Civilian [[concepts/infrastructure|infrastructure]] targeting** refers to the deliberate destruction or disruption of non-military assets, including [[concepts/supply-lines|logistics networks]], energy [[concepts/grids|grids]], and commercial [[concepts/supply-chains|supply chains]], to degrade an adversary's economic capacity, morale, or operational readiness. In modern asymmetric conflicts, this strategy increasingly focuses on dual-use infrastructure and commercial logistics hubs that support military mobilization.

## Strategic Evolution: Logistics as a Target

Recent operational trends indicate a shift from broad [[concepts/strategic-long-range-attacks|strategic bombing]] to [[concepts/accuracy|precision]] strikes against specific commercial logistics [[concepts/nodes|nodes]]. This approach exploits the interdependence between civilian e-commerce and [[concepts/military-logistics|military supply]] chains.

### Case Study: Wildberries Logistics Network
The integration of commercial logistics into the war effort has made major e-commerce platforms primary targets. A notable example is the Ukrainian campaign against Wildberries, a dominant Russian e-commerce retailer.

- **Strategic Rationale:** Wildberries warehouses serve as critical distribution hubs for both civilian goods and military supplies, including uniforms, equipment, and raw materials. Disrupting these nodes creates cascading failures in the Russian domestic supply chain.
- **Operational Impact:** Long-range strikes on Wildberries facilities have forced the company to halt operations in multiple regions, demonstrating the [[concepts/vulnerability|vulnerability]] of centralized commercial logistics to asymmetric air power.
- **[[concepts/economic-warfare|Economic Warfare]]:** By targeting high-volume logistics centers, [[entities/ukraine|Ukraine]] aims to increase the cost of war for [[entities/russia|Russia]], disrupt mobilization efforts, and signal the reach of Ukrainian long-range capabilities into deep rear areas.
- **Russian Response:** The Russian government has responded with increased [[concepts/air-defence|air defense]] deployments around commercial hubs and propaganda efforts to frame these strikes as attacks on civilians, attempting to shift international perception despite the dual-use nature of the targets.
