# Interview Techniques

Interview techniques are structured methods for evaluating candidates during hiring processes. In the context of generative [[concepts/ai-advancement|AI advancement]], traditional interview approaches face new challenges as AI systems can assist candidates in preparing [[concepts/responses|responses]] and answering certain categories of questions. This has prompted reassessment of how organizations identify and assess genuine talent, [[concepts/skills|skills]], and [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] independent of AI assistance.

## AI-Era Adaptations

Contemporary interview techniques increasingly focus on assessments that differentiate between AI-assisted and authentic human performance. These methods prioritize real-time problem solving, contextual [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]], and demonstrations of domain knowledge that require lived experience or deep understanding rather than [[concepts/pattern-matching|pattern-matching]] from [[concepts/training-data|training data]]. Organizations have shifted toward evaluating how candidates think through novel [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]] rather than assessing memorized information or standard responses.

## Assessment Focus Areas

Effective modern interview techniques emphasize practical application of knowledge, ethical [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]], interpersonal dynamics, and the ability to work within organizational constraints. Interviewers increasingly employ scenario-based questioning, live technical problem-solving under observation, and discussion of past project decisions to evaluate genuine capability. The goal is to assess qualities that reflect individual judgment, experience, and character rather than testable facts or prepared answers.
