Exposure refers to the amount of light captured by a camera's sensor or film, determining the overall brightness and visibility of an image. It is one of the most fundamental concepts in photography, as proper exposure is essential for creating clear, well-balanced images. An image can be underexposed (too dark), overexposed (too bright), or correctly exposed depending on how much light reaches the sensor during capture.

## The Exposure Triangle

The exposure of a photograph is controlled through three interdependent variables known as the exposure triangle: aperture, shutter speed, and ISO. Aperture controls the size of the lens opening and affects both exposure and depth of field. Shutter speed determines how long the sensor is exposed to light, directly influencing brightness and motion blur. ISO represents the sensor's sensitivity to light, with higher values producing brighter images but introducing more digital noise. Photographers adjust these three elements in combination to achieve desired exposure levels while maintaining control over other visual properties.

## Exposure in Post-Processing

Modern photo editing software provides exposure adjustment tools that allow photographers to modify brightness and light intensity after capture. These tools range from basic exposure sliders that uniformly brighten or darken an entire image to more advanced controls like curves and levels that offer precise adjustments to specific tonal ranges. Exposure correction in post-processing can rescue underexposed or overexposed shots within limits, though recovery is generally more successful for slightly underexposed images where detail remains in the highlights.
