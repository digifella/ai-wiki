## Fundamental UI/UX Design Concepts: Affordances, Hierarchy, Grids,
[[concepts/typography|Typography]] Explained
**Clip title:** Every UI/UX Concept Explained in Under 10 Minutes
**Author / channel:** [[entities/kole-jain|Kole Jain]]
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcbgbKtOELY

### Summary
This video provides a comprehensive overview of fundamental UI/UX [[concepts/design|design]]
concepts, emphasizing how to create intuitive, user-friendly, and
aesthetically pleasing digital interfaces. The main topic revolves around
mastering the basics of [[concepts/visual-communication|visual communication]] and interaction to build
effective designs.

The video begins by explaining **[[concepts/affordances|Affordances]] and [Signifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signifiers)**, highlighting
how UI elements should inherently communicate their function and state
without explicit [[concepts/instructions|instructions]]. Examples include [[concepts/active-tabs|active tabs]], [[concepts/hover-states|hover states]],
and grayed-out inactive items. This leads into **Visual [[concepts/hierarchy|Hierarchy]]**, which
focuses on arranging information to be easily scannable and understandable.
Techniques discussed include using size, position, and color to emphasize
important elements (e.g., larger, bolder titles at the top), incorporating
[[concepts/images|images]] for visual appeal and quick scanning, and utilizing icons and
alignment to convey [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] (like delivery routes).

Next, the video delves into **[[concepts/grids|Grids]], [Layouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layouts), and Spacing**, stressing that
grids are guidelines for [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] and responsiveness (e.g., 8-column for
tablets, 4-column for mobile) rather than rigid rules, especially for
custom designs. Critically, it advocates for ample **[[concepts/whitespace|whitespace]]** to let
elements "breathe" and introduces the [4-point grid system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-point_grid_system) for consistent
[[concepts/scaling|scaling]] and element spacing. Following this, **[[concepts/typography|Typography]] and Font Sizing**
are covered, emphasizing that design is primarily [[concepts/text|text]]. The recommendation
is to select one high-quality sans-serif font and stick to it, carefully
adjusting letter spacing and line height for optimal readability. The video
also differentiates font size hierarchies between landing pages (wider
[[concepts/range|range]]) and information-dense dashboards (tighter range, smaller maximum
sizes).

The discussion then moves to **Color [[concepts/theory|Theory]]**, suggesting starting with one
primary brand color and creating variations for backgrounds and text. It
highlights the importance of **[semantic colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_Colors)** (e.g., blue for trust, red
for urgency, green for success) to provide meaningful signifiers rather
than merely decorative elements. Designing for **[Dark Mode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Mode)** presents
specific challenges, requiring lighter-toned cards for depth instead of
[[concepts/shadows|shadows]], and dimming saturation/brightness for UI elements. Speaking of
shadows, in **[[concepts/light|Light]] Mode**, they are valuable for depth but should be
subtle (reduced [[concepts/opacity|opacity]], increased blur) to avoid distraction. The video
also covers **Icons and [[concepts/buttons|Buttons]]**, advising that icons should align with
text line height, and buttons require consistent padding and states.

Finally, the video emphasizes **Feedback and States**, stating that every
user interaction must elicit a response. This includes outlining essential
[button states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Button_States) (default, hover, pressed, disabled, loading) and [input states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Input_States)
(focus, error, warning). These [[concepts/responses|responses]] can be enhanced through
**[Micro-interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micro-interactions)**, subtle animations or visual cues that confirm
actions (like a "Copied!" chip sliding up). The last concept is
**[Overlays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overlays)**, crucial for displaying text on images. It suggests using
linear gradients or progressive blurs to ensure text readability without
fully obscuring the underlying visual content. Ultimately, the video
concludes that a strong grasp of these fundamental UI/UX concepts is
essential for creating compelling and intuitive digital experiences.
