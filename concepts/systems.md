# Systems & Feedback Loops

A system is a set of interconnected components that work together to achieve a common goal. Systems can be natural or artificial, simple or complex, and can be analyzed using systems [[concepts/human-cognition|thinking]]. **Feedback loops** are critical [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] within these systems that regulate behavior, driving emergence and enabling complex adaptive behaviors.

## Key Concepts

- **Components**: Individual parts of a system.
- **Interconnections**: [[concepts/relationships|Relationships]] between components.
- **[[concepts/emergent-behavior|Emergence]]**: Properties that arise from the interactions of components, often unpredicted by individual part analysis.
- **[[concepts/feedback|Feedback]] [[concepts/loops|Loops]]**:
    - Mechanisms that regulate system behavior through positive (amplifying) or negative (stabilizing) cycles.
    - In social and cognitive contexts, feedback loops enable Collective Intelligence, where [[concepts/team-performance|group performance]] exceeds individual capabilities via continuous [[concepts/learning|learning]] [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]].

## Types of Systems

- **Natural Systems**: Ecosystems, biological systems.
- **Artificial Systems**: Machines, software, organizations.
- **Complex Systems**: Systems with many [[concepts/interconnected-parts|interconnected parts]], such as [[concepts/large-language-models|large language models (LLMs)]].
    - These systems often operate on the "edge of chaos," balancing order and randomness to maximize learning and [[concepts/resilience|adaptability]].
