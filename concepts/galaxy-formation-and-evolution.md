# Galaxy Formation And Evolution

Galaxies are believed to have formed through the [[concepts/gravitational-collapse|gravitational collapse]] of primordial [[concepts/density|density]] fluctuations in the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]], emerging approximately 100 to 200 million years after the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]]. These initial perturbations were seeded by [[concepts/virtual-particles|quantum fluctuations]] during the period of [[concepts/cosmic-inflation|cosmic inflation]] and subsequently grew via gravitational instability as the universe expanded. As [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] [[concepts/halos|halos]] accumulated mass, they created potential wells that drew in baryonic gas, providing the necessary conditions for structure formation.

The collapsing gas clouds within these dark matter halos fragmented into smaller, denser regions that eventually ignited to form the first [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]] and stellar clusters. These early stellar populations, known as [[concepts/first-generation-of-stars|Population III stars]], were massive and short-lived, enriching the interstellar [[entities/medium|medium]] with heavy elements through [[concepts/supernovae|supernova explosions]]. Over time, these smaller structures merged and accreted additional material, assembling into the larger, gravitationally bound systems recognized as galaxies today.

[[concepts/lrds|Galaxy evolution]] is driven by a complex interplay of stellar processes, dynamical interactions, and environmental factors. Internal processes such as star formation rates, supernova [[concepts/feedback|feedback]], and [[concepts/active-galactic-nuclei|active galactic nuclei]] activity regulate the consumption of gas and the growth of supermassive [[concepts/black-holes|black holes]]. Externally, galaxy mergers and interactions with neighboring structures significantly alter morphology, triggering bursts of star formation or transforming spiral galaxies into elliptical ones.

The current cosmological model, Lambda-CDM, describes a hierarchical formation scenario where small structures form first and merge to create larger ones. This process continues to shape the large-scale structure of the universe, with [[concepts/galaxy-clusters|galaxy clusters]] and superclusters forming along filaments of dark matter. Observational evidence from [[concepts/deep-field|deep-field]] surveys and redshift measurements supports this timeline, illustrating how galaxy properties such as mass, size, and [[concepts/writing|composition]] have evolved over [[concepts/cosmic-time|cosmic time]].
