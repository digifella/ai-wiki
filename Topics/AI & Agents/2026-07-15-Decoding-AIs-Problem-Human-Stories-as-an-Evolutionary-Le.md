## Decoding AI's Problem: Human Stories as an Evolutionary Learning Algorithm
**Clip title:** AI's Biggest Problem Was Hiding in Plain Sight
**[[entities/tasia-custode|Author]] / channel:** [[concepts/dr-know-it-all-knows-it-all|Dr. Know-it-all Knows it all]]
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m9SKz3Ta0r0

### Summary
In a thought-provoking video, Dr. Know It All posits that the critical missing element in the advancement of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] is the understanding and application of "stories." He argues that stories are far more than just entertainment; they represent an evolutionary [[concepts/algorithm|algorithm]] that human intelligence has leveraged for hundreds of thousands of years to acquire and transmit complex knowledge and [[concepts/skills|skills]]. This profound insight, initially conceived during his effort to create a compelling YouTube title, highlights the deep [[concepts/connection|connection]] between human [[concepts/human-cognition|cognitive processes]], our survival [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]], and the future potential of AI.

Dr. Know It All explains the human phenomenon of stories by describing them as a mechanism for "psychic energetic collapse." This refers to the "aha!" or "eureka" moment when various ideas suddenly connect, releasing cognitive [[concepts/stress|stress]] and leading to a deeper understanding. Stories are highly effective at compressing reality, not just conveying isolated [[concepts/factual-knowledge|facts]], but more importantly, capturing the entire "journey." This journey includes an inciting incident that disrupts a state of equilibrium, a period of navigation through challenges, and ultimately, a cathartic [[concepts/solution|resolution]]. By engaging with these narratives, humans perform a type of "Monte Carlo [[concepts/simulation|simulation]]" of potential future [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]], vicariously experiencing outcomes and [[concepts/learning|learning]] optimal "[[concepts/policies|policies]]" (action trajectories) without incurring real-world risks. Emotions, he emphasizes, are integral to this process, [[concepts/acting|acting]] as [[concepts/recommendations|guidance]] and "loss functions" within these [[concepts/environment-simulation|simulated environments]], reinforcing appropriate actions and teaching individuals how to endure "temporary pain" for a better long-term outcome.

The video then translates this [[concepts/human-understanding|human understanding]] of stories to the realm of artificial intelligence. Dr. Know It All asserts that current [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]], both embodied (like robots) and disembodied (like [[concepts/demystifying-llms|large language models]]), primarily learn from "snapshots" – [[concepts/dead-files|static data]] points and loss functions. This approach limits their ability to tackle long-horizon [[concepts/complex-tasks|complex tasks]] and develop robust common-sense [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]]. The next significant leap in AI, he suggests, will come from training these systems to comprehend and generate "structured long-horizon perturbation-response trajectories," which are, in essence, stories. Such a [[concepts/mindset-shift|paradigm shift]] would enable AI to pre-act rather than merely react to events, fostering better [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] in dynamic environments (e.g., [[concepts/autonomous-driving-technology|autonomous vehicles]] understanding other drivers' intentions) and facilitating more nuanced human-robot interactions. Ultimately, this would equip AI with a more developed "common sense" and the capacity for continuous learning beyond initial training.
