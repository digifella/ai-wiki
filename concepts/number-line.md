# Number Line

A number line is a fundamental visual representation of numbers arranged along a straight, one-dimensional axis. It typically displays numbers marked at regular intervals along a horizontal or vertical line, serving as a foundational tool for understanding numerical relationships and operations. The standard configuration places zero at a central point, with positive numbers extending to the right and negative numbers extending to the left, although vertical variations exist for specific contexts.

In the context of mathematical logic and cryptography, the number line serves as a discrete coordinate system for analyzing the distribution of prime numbers. By mapping primes onto this linear structure, researchers can visualize gaps between consecutive primes and identify patterns relevant to primality testing algorithms. This geometric perspective aids in the study of prime number theorems and the statistical properties that underpin cryptographic key generation.

The linear arrangement allows for the application of analytic number theory techniques to discrete sets. It facilitates the comparison of prime density against theoretical models, such as the logarithmic integral function. This visualization is critical for evaluating the security of cryptographic systems that rely on the computational difficulty of factoring large integers, as the distribution of primes directly influences the probability of selecting secure keys.
