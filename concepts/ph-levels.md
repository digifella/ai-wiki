# Ph Levels

pH is a logarithmic scale used to specify the acidity or alkalinity of a substance, ranging from 0 to 14. It measures the concentration of hydrogen ions in a solution, where a pH of 7 indicates neutrality. Values below 7 denote acidity, while values above 7 indicate alkalinity. Because the scale is logarithmic, each whole number change represents a tenfold difference in hydrogen ion concentration; for instance, a substance with a pH of 5 is ten times more acidic than one with a pH of 6 and one hundred times more acidic than a neutral substance with a pH of 7.

In the context of food and nutrition, pH levels play a critical role in food safety, preservation, and sensory characteristics. Acidic environments inhibit the growth of many pathogenic bacteria, making pH control essential for canning and fermenting processes. The acidity of food also significantly influences flavor profiles, texture, and the effectiveness of certain preservatives. Understanding pH helps in determining appropriate storage conditions and processing methods to maintain quality and prevent spoilage.
