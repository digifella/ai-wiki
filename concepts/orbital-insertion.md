# Orbital Insertion

**Orbital insertion** is the process by which a spacecraft adjusts its trajectory to enter a stable orbit around a celestial body. This maneuver typically involves a critical burn to achieve the necessary [[concepts/velocity|velocity]] and altitude, transitioning from a sub-orbital or transfer trajectory into a closed orbit.

## Key Concepts
- **Delta-v Budget**: The change in velocity required to achieve insertion, heavily dependent on the target orbit's altitude and inclination.
- **Periapsis/Apoapsis**: The lowest and highest points of the orbit, respectively, often used to define the insertion parameters.
- **Circularization**: A secondary burn often performed at apoapsis to raise the periapsis and achieve a circular orbit.

## Recent Developments
- **[[concepts/starship-flight-14|SpaceX Starship Flight 14]]**: Marked a significant milestone with the vehicle's first successful orbital insertion during its 14th flight test.
- **Mission Payload**: The flight included the successful deployment of 26 [[concepts/starlink-v3|Starlink V3]] satellites.
- **Recovery**: Demonstrated controlled splashdowns for the booster stages.
