# Point And Shoot Camera

A point and shoot camera is a compact camera designed for casual photography, characterized by automatic settings and fixed or modestly variable lens [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]]. These cameras prioritize simplicity and portability over manual [[concepts/power|control]], making them accessible to photographers who do not require extensive technical adjustment. The category encompasses a wide [[concepts/range|range]] of [[concepts/models|models]], from basic fixed-lens designs to more advanced compact cameras with substantial zoom ranges.

## Superzoom Variants

Superzoom [[concepts/point-and-shoot-cameras|point and shoot cameras]] extend the [[concepts/focal-length|focal length]] range significantly beyond standard compact models, often featuring 20x [[concepts/optical-zoom|optical zoom]] or greater. The [[entities/zs200|Panasonic ZS300]] represents this category, offering extensive zoom capability in a portable form factor. However, as documented in a PetaPixel video analysis, successive generations of such cameras have introduced only incremental improvements over multi-year periods, raising questions about the practical value of upgrades relative to their [[concepts/cost|cost]].

## Market Context

Point and shoot cameras have declined in mainstream use following the widespread [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of [[concepts/mobile-photography|smartphone photography]], which offers comparable convenience with integrated processing and sharing capabilities. Remaining models in this category typically target enthusiasts seeking dedicated imaging devices with superior optical systems or those preferring dedicated cameras for specific shooting situations. The superzoom niche in particular has faced pressure to justify its continued development and premium [[concepts/pricing|pricing]].
