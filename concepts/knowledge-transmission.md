# Knowledge Transmission

**Knowledge Transmission** refers to the [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]], structures, and processes by which information, skills, and cultural norms are passed between individuals or systems. In biological contexts, this occurs through [[concepts/social-learning]] and Language; in artificial systems, it involves [[concepts/web-scraping|data ingestion]], [[concepts/training-process|model training]], and inference patterns.

## Core Mechanisms

*   **Explicit Transmission**: Codified knowledge via [[concepts/writing]], Documentation, and formal education.
*   **Implicit Transmission**: [[concepts/experience|Tacit knowledge]] acquired through observation, imitation, and Embodied [[concepts/cognition|Cognition]].
*   **Narrative Encoding**: The use of [[concepts/storytelling]] to compress complex causal [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] into memorable, transmissible units.

## AI and Narrative Integration

Recent analysis suggests that current [[concepts/ai-technologies|Artificial Intelligence]] systems lack a fundamental component of human knowledge transmission: the structural utility of stories.

*   **The Narrative Gap**: [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] often process data as isolated facts rather than integrated narratives, leading to difficulties in contextual reasoning and long-term coherence.
*   **Stories as [[concepts/algorithms|Algorithms]]**: [[concepts/human-stories|Human stories]] function as an [[concepts/evolutionary-learning-algorithm]], allowing for rapid [[concepts/simulation|simulation]] of outcomes and [[concepts/social-dynamics|social dynamics]] without direct physical experience.
*   **Integration Strategy**: Incorporating narrative structures into AI training may bridge the gap between [[concepts/data-formatting|raw data processing]] and [[concepts/contextual-understanding|contextual understanding]].
