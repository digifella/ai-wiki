# Lasagna Preparation

Lasagna [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] is a multi-layered cooking technique that combines three core components: ragout (a slow-cooked meat sauce), pasta sheets, and béchamel (a creamy [[concepts/white-sauce|white sauce]]). The method requires sequential assembly and baking to create the finished dish. [[entities/andy-cooks|Andy Cooks]]' detailed recipe provides a systematic approach to combining these elements.

## Core Components

The ragout forms the base flavor layer, built through slow cooking of meat and tomatoes to develop depth. Pasta sheets provide structural support and [[concepts/texture|texture]] throughout the dish. Béchamel, made from butter, flour, and milk, creates the creamy binding element that holds the layers together while cooking.

## Assembly and Baking

The preparation process involves layering these components in a baking dish, typically alternating between ragout, pasta, and béchamel until the dish reaches the top. The assembled lasagna is then baked until the layers meld and the surface develops a golden finish. Proper timing and temperature [[concepts/power|control]] during baking are essential to achieving even cooking throughout the layered [[concepts/structure|structure]].
