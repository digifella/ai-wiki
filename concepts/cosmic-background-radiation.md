# Cosmic Background Radiation

**[[concepts/cosmic-microwave-background|Cosmic Background Radiation]]** refers to the [[concepts/light|electromagnetic radiation]] filling the universe, primarily observed as the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB). It serves as the thermal afterglow of the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]], providing critical evidence for the universe's hot, dense origin and subsequent expansion.

## Key Concepts

*   **Thermal Remnant**: The CMB is a nearly uniform blackbody radiation at ~2.7 K, representing the cooled remnant of the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]]'s recombination epoch.
*   **Isotropy and Anisotropy**: While largely isotropic, tiny temperature fluctuations in the CMB map the seeds of large-scale structure formation.
*   **[[concepts/olbers-paradox|Olbers' Paradox]] [[concepts/solution|Resolution]]**: The darkness of the [[concepts/night-sky|night sky]] is explained by the [[concepts/finite-age-of-the-universe|finite age of the universe]] and the expansion of space, which redshifts light and limits the observable horizon. This connects directly to the discussion on why the sky is not infinitely bright: [[lab-notes/2026-08-10-Olbers-Paradox-Why-the-Night-Sky-is-Dark|Olbers' Paradox: Why the Night Sky is Dark]].
*   **Cosmological Principle**: The uniformity of background radiation supports the assumption that the universe is homogeneous and isotropic on large [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]].
