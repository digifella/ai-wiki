# First Stage Engine

A first stage [[concepts/engine|engine]] is a propulsion unit that operates during the initial [[concepts/phase|phase]] of a launch vehicle's flight, providing the [[concepts/engine-thrust|thrust]] necessary to [[entities/lift|lift]] the vehicle and its payload from the launch pad through the lower [[concepts/earths-atmosphere|atmosphere]]. These engines are typically the largest and most powerful on a launch vehicle, as they must generate sufficient thrust to overcome the vehicle's total mass and gravitational forces during the most fuel-intensive portion of ascent.

First stage engines typically burn for several minutes, depending on the vehicle's design and mission profile. This duration allows the rocket to achieve the [[concepts/velocity|velocity]] and altitude required for subsequent stages to take over. The operational characteristics are optimized for high thrust-to-weight ratios to efficiently clear the dense lower atmosphere before transitioning to more fuel-efficient upper stages.

In certain mission architectures, the first stage engine continues [[concepts/burning|burning]] after [[concepts/payload-fairing-separation|payload fairing separation]]. This extended operation can allow for continued flight of the [[concepts/payload-structure|payload structure]], potentially enabling the vehicle to maintain stability or trajectory correction even if the fairing sustains damage. This capability highlights the versatility of first stage propulsion systems in managing complex ascent profiles and mitigating structural risks during the critical early phases of launch.
