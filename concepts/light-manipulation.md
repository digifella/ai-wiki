# Light Manipulation

Light manipulation in portrait photography involves the deliberate control and adjustment of illumination to enhance a subject's appearance and the overall composition. This practice spans two distinct phases: the capture phase, where physical lighting equipment is arranged to shape shadows and highlights, and the post-processing phase, where digital tools refine or alter existing light data. The primary objective is to guide the viewer's eye, correct technical imbalances, and establish a specific mood that aligns with the photographer's creative intent.

## Capture Phase

During the capture phase, photographers utilize physical lighting equipment such as strobes, continuous lights, reflectors, and diffusers to sculpt the subject. Key techniques include controlling the direction, quality, and color temperature of light to define facial features and create depth. By manipulating the ratio between key and fill light, photographers can emphasize texture or smooth skin tones, while the use of modifiers like softboxes or grids allows for precise control over the spread and intensity of the illumination.

## Post-Processing Phase

In the digital domain, light manipulation refers to the editing of luminance and color values to simulate or enhance natural lighting effects. Tools such as Dodge and Burn are employed to selectively lighten or darken specific areas, thereby altering the perceived shape and dimensionality of the subject. Advanced techniques may involve frequency separation to adjust skin tones independently of texture, or the use of gradient maps and color grading to unify the lighting atmosphere and correct white balance inconsistencies introduced during the shoot.
