# Whites

Whites in [[concepts/photo-editing|photo editing]] refer to the brightness and tonal values of the lightest areas in an image. In digital photography workflows, the [[concepts/whites-adjustment|whites slider]] is a primary tool for adjusting [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] in the highlights without affecting the overall image [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] or darker tones. This adjustment is distinct from overall exposure correction, as it targets only the bright regions of the photograph.

## Adjustment in Post-Processing Software

The whites control is a standard feature in professional editing applications including Adobe Lightroom and Adobe [[concepts/camera-raw|Camera Raw]]. These tools allow photographers to recover detail in overexposed highlights, brighten underexposed light areas, or compress the tonal range as needed. The adjustment works non-destructively, meaning changes can be modified or reversed without degrading image quality. Photographers typically use the whites slider in conjunction with other [[concepts/exposure-settings|exposure controls]] like [[concepts/shadows|shadows]], [[concepts/blacks|blacks]], and highlights to achieve comprehensive tonal correction.

## Practical Application

When adjusting whites, photographers must balance visibility and detail in bright areas against the risk of losing highlight information if pushed too far. This is particularly important in scenes with significant sky exposure or reflective surfaces. The whites adjustment is commonly one of the first steps in the exposure correction workflow, applied after basic white balance has been established but before [[concepts/local-adjustments|local adjustments]] or other advanced edits are made.
