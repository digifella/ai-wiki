Exposure refers to the total amount of [[concepts/light|light]] captured by a camera's sensor or film, which determines the overall brightness and visibility of an image. It is a fundamental concept in photography, as proper exposure is essential for creating clear, well-balanced visuals. Depending on the quantity of light that reaches the recording [[entities/medium|medium]] during capture, an image may be correctly exposed, underexposed (too dark), or overexposed (too bright).

## The Exposure Triangle

The exposure of a photograph is controlled through three interdependent variables known as the [[concepts/exposure-triangle|exposure triangle]]: [[concepts/aperture|aperture]], [[concepts/shutter-speed|shutter speed]], and [[concepts/iso-settings|ISO sensitivity]]. Aperture regulates the size of the lens opening, controlling both the volume of light entering the camera and the depth of field. Shutter speed dictates the duration for which the sensor is exposed to light, affecting motion blur and light accumulation. ISO sensitivity measures the sensor's or film's responsiveness to light, allowing for brighter images in low-light conditions at the cost of potential noise or grain.

## Digital Adjustment

In [[concepts/photo-editing|photo editing]] software, exposure [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] modify the brightness and light intensity of an existing image. While this can correct minor capture errors, significant deviations from the original capture data often result in loss of detail in the [[concepts/shadows|shadows]] or highlights. Digital exposure tools allow photographers to fine-tune the visual balance after the image has been recorded, providing flexibility in post-production workflows.
