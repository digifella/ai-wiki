# Auditory Nerve

The **auditory nerve** (also known as the vestibulocochlear nerve or cranial nerve VIII) is a paired cranial nerve responsible for transmitting auditory and balance information from the [[concepts/inner-ear|inner ear]] to the brain. It consists of two distinct functional components: the **cochlear nerve** (hearing) and the **vestibular nerve** (balance).

## Anatomy and Physiology

- **Origin**: Arises from the brainstem at the **cerebellopontine angle**.
- **Cochlear Division**: Carries sensory information regarding sound from the **spiral ganglion** cells in the **cochlea**.
- **Vestibular Division**: Carries sensory information regarding head position and [[concepts/exercise|movement]] from the **vestibular ganglion** in the **semicircular canals** and otolith organs.
- **Pathway**: Synapses in the cochlear nuclei of the medulla, then projects via the lateral lemniscus to the inferior colliculus, medial geniculate body, and finally the **primary auditory cortex**.

## Clinical Significance

- **Auditory Neuropathy**: A disorder where sound enters the inner ear normally but the transfer of signals from the inner ear to the brain is disrupted.
- **Acoustic Neuroma**: A benign tumor (schwannoma) that develops on the vestibular portion of the auditory nerve, potentially compressing the cochlear portion and causing hearing loss.
- **Cochlear Implants**: Devices that bypass damaged hair cells in the cochlea to directly stimulate the auditory nerve fibers, restoring perception of sound.
