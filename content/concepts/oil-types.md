# Oil Types

Oils are liquid fats derived from plants, seeds, nuts, or animal sources, commonly used in cooking and food [[concepts/preparation|preparation]]. They serve multiple culinary functions including sautéing, frying, roasting, and as ingredients in dressings and baked goods. The choice of oil significantly affects both the flavor and nutritional profile of prepared foods.

## Common Culinary Oils

Vegetable oils like canola, soybean, and sunflower oil are neutral-flavored options widely used for general cooking. Olive oil, extracted from olives, ranges from [[concepts/light|light]] and mild to robust and fruity depending on processing methods. Coconut oil and palm oil are tropical sources with distinct flavor profiles. Seed oils such as sesame and flaxseed are typically used for finishing dishes or dressings rather than high-heat cooking due to their lower smoke points.

## Heat Tolerance and Cooking Applications

Different oils have varying smoke points—the temperature [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] which they begin to break down and release smoke. Refined oils with higher smoke points, such as canola and refined vegetable oils, are better suited for frying and high-heat [[concepts/cooking-methods|cooking methods]]. Unrefined oils like extra virgin olive oil have lower smoke points and are generally reserved for low-heat [[concepts/software|applications]], drizzling, or use in cold preparations. Matching oil selection to cooking temperature helps maintain food quality and oil stability.
