# Self Hosted AI Assistant

A [[concepts/local-ai-hosting|self-hosted AI]] assistant is a personal [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] system that operates on your own hardware or infrastructure rather than relying on [[concepts/cloud-based-services|cloud-based services]]. This approach grants users direct control over their data, [[concepts/privacy|privacy]] settings, and [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]] allocated to the assistant. Self-hosted solutions typically run on [[concepts/open-source|open-source]] frameworks and can operate on various devices, from personal computers to dedicated servers.

## Key Characteristics

[[concepts/on-premise-deployment|Self-hosted AI]] assistants differ from commercial alternatives by eliminating dependence on third-party cloud providers. Users maintain full ownership of their data, avoiding transmission to external servers and reducing [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] to data collection or service changes. The computational load is managed locally, allowing [[concepts/customization|customization]] of the assistant's capabilities based on available hardware resources.

## Common Use Cases

These systems serve individuals and organizations seeking privacy-focused automation, [[concepts/local-data-processing|local data processing]], and independence from [[concepts/subscription-based-services|subscription-based services]]. They are suitable for tasks like personal scheduling, [[concepts/document-processing|document processing]], research assistance, and custom [[concepts/ai-driven-workflow-automation|workflow automation]]. Organizations may [[concepts/deployment|deploy]] self-hosted assistants to maintain confidentiality of sensitive information or to operate in environments with limited internet connectivity.

## Technical Considerations

Running a self-hosted [[concepts/ai-assistant|AI assistant]] requires sufficient hardware resources and technical knowledge for setup and maintenance. Users must handle [[concepts/app-updates|software updates]], [[concepts/security|security]] patching, and [[concepts/monitoring-and-alerting|system monitoring]] independently. Performance depends on local computational capacity, which may be more limited than cloud-based alternatives but offers greater [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] and predictability in operation.
