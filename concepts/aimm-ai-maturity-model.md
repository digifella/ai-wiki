# Aimm Ai Maturity Model

The Aimm (Ai Maturity Model) is a framework for evaluating how individuals develop and integrate [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] capabilities into their personal workflows and [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] processes. Introduced by security researcher and AI strategist [[entities/daniel-miessler|Daniel Miessler]], the model conceptualizes AI adoption as a progressive development journey rather than a binary adoption event. Rather than viewing [[concepts/ai-integration|AI integration]] as something people either do or do not do, the framework emphasizes the evolution of skills, tools, and mental models required to [[concepts/leverage-ai|leverage AI]] effectively.

The model outlines distinct stages of maturity, beginning with basic awareness and moving toward sophisticated, autonomous agent-based systems. Early stages focus on using AI as a passive tool for [[concepts/knowledge-bases|information retrieval]] and content generation, where the user maintains full control over the interaction. As individuals progress, they begin to automate specific tasks and integrate AI into their [[concepts/daily-routines|daily routines]], requiring a deeper understanding of [[concepts/prompt-based-modeling|prompt engineering]] and [[concepts/context-management|context management]].

Advanced maturity involves the creation of [[concepts/kai|personal AI infrastructure]], where users build custom systems that operate with varying degrees of autonomy. This stage includes the development of [[concepts/local-llm|local AI models]], secure data pipelines, and agent architectures that can execute [[concepts/complex-workflows|complex workflows]] without constant human intervention. The framework serves as a guide for security professionals and knowledge workers to assess their current position and identify the technical and strategic steps needed to achieve higher levels of [[concepts/ai-proficiency|AI proficiency]].
