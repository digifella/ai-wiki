# Deep Space Exploration

[[concepts/space-exploration|Deep space exploration]] encompasses the scientific investigation of regions beyond the solar system, including distant star systems, interstellar space, and the broader galactic environment. Unlike planetary exploration, which focuses on objects orbiting our sun, deep space exploration extends humanity's understanding to the nearest [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]] and the structure of the [[concepts/milky-way|Milky Way]] and other galaxies. This field of study addresses fundamental questions about stellar formation, galactic dynamics, and the distribution of matter throughout the universe.

## Methods and Observations

The primary tools for deep space exploration are space-based observatories and robotic probes designed to withstand the harsh conditions of interstellar travel. Telescopes such as the [[concepts/hubble-space-telescope|Hubble Space Telescope]] and the [[concepts/james-webb-space-telescope|James Webb Space Telescope]] utilize [[concepts/light|electromagnetic radiation]] across various spectra to observe [[concepts/distant-galaxies|distant galaxies]], nebulae, and exoplanets. These instruments allow astronomers to analyze the [[concepts/writing|composition]], temperature, and motion of celestial bodies billions of light-years away, providing data on the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]] and the lifecycle of stars.

Robotic missions, such as the Voyager program, have ventured into the outer reaches of the heliosphere and interstellar [[entities/medium|medium]]. These probes carry scientific instruments to measure magnetic fields, [[concepts/cosmic-rays|cosmic rays]], and plasma [[concepts/density|density]] in regions unreachable by human astronauts. While current technology limits direct physical exploration of other star systems, these missions provide critical in-situ data that complements remote sensing observations, helping to map the boundary between the solar system and interstellar space.
