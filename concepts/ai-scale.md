# AI Scale

**AI Scale** refers to the trajectory of increasing computational, parameter, and capability thresholds in [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems, particularly the tension between proprietary closed-source dominance and the emerging viability of large-scale [[concepts/model-customization|open-weight models]].

## Current Landscape: The Open-Weight Shift
The paradigm of "open-source AI" is undergoing a critical inflection point, moving from small, [[concepts/custom-models|specialized models]] to massive, general-purpose architectures that challenge the performance gap with proprietary leaders.

### Key Developments (2026)
*   **[[concepts/kimi-k3|Kimi K3]]**: Released by [[entities/china|China]]'s [[entities/moonshot-ai]], this model represents a significant leap in [[concepts/open-weight|open-weight]] capability, demonstrating competitive performance in [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] and [[concepts/200k-token-context-window|long-context processing]].
*   **[[concepts/openclaw|Inkling]]**: Developed by the US-based [[entities/thinking-machines-lab]], Inkling complements the global open-weight ecosystem, highlighting cross-border collaboration and competition in high-scale [[concepts/training-process|model training]].
*   **Strategic Implications**: These releases signal that [[concepts/open-weight-language-models|open-weight models]] are no longer merely "distilled" or "small" alternatives but are becoming viable, high-scale competitors, forcing a re-evaluation of deployment strategies and [[concepts/hardware-compatibility|hardware requirements]].
*   **Deployment Challenges**: As models grow, the barrier to running them locally increases, leading to discussions about the sustainability of open-weight ecosystems against the [[concepts/computational-resources|compute]] advantages of major tech giants.
