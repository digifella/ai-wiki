# Game Theory

Game [[concepts/theory|theory]] is a mathematical framework for analyzing strategic interactions between rational decision-makers, known as players, who each seek to optimize their own outcomes. Developed formally in the mid-20th century by mathematicians including [[entities/chef-john|John]] von Neumann and Oskar Morgenstern, the field models situations where the result for any participant depends not only on their own choices but also on the choices of others. This interdependence distinguishes game theory from traditional optimization problems, where an agent's outcome is determined solely by their own actions.

The framework applies to both competitive [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]] where players have opposing interests and cooperative situations where players can benefit from coordinated action. In competitive settings, such as zero-sum games, one player's gain is exactly balanced by another's loss. In cooperative contexts, the focus shifts to how groups form coalitions and distribute resources or payoffs among members. Key concepts include the Nash equilibrium, a state where no player can benefit by unilaterally changing their strategy, and the [[concepts/minimax|minimax]] theorem, which provides a [[concepts/solution|solution]] for zero-sum games.

Applications of game theory extend beyond [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] into economics, political [[concepts/science|science]], biology, and computer science. In economics, it is used to model market competition and auction designs. In [[concepts/evolutionary-biology|evolutionary biology]], it helps explain the development of altruistic behaviors and species strategies. In computer science, particularly in [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]], game theory informs the development of [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]] for [[concepts/expertise-based-ai-assistants|multi-agent systems]] and [[concepts/internet-security|network security]]. The discipline continues to evolve, addressing complex dynamics in large-scale systems and bounded rationality.
