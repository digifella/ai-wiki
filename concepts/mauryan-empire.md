# Mauryan Empire

The [[entities/mauryan-empire|Mauryan Empire]] was a major ancient Indian state that flourished from approximately 322 to 185 BCE, spanning much of the Indian subcontinent. Centered in the Gangetic valley with its capital at Pataliputra (modern-day Patna), the empire represents one of the earliest and most extensive political organizations in South Asian history. Founded by Chandragupta Maurya after the decline of the Nanda dynasty, the empire expanded rapidly under subsequent rulers, establishing diplomatic relations with Hellenistic kingdoms and controlling significant territorial reach across the subcontinent.

## Administration and Society

The Mauryan state developed a sophisticated administrative system organized into provinces governed by officials appointed from the center. The empire maintained a substantial military force and created networks for tax collection and resource management. Cities served as centers of commerce and administration, while [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]] remained the foundation of the [[concepts/economic-system|economy]]. The empire's infrastructure, including roads and [[entities/public-works|public works]], facilitated trade and communication across diverse regions.

## Cultural and Intellectual Contributions

The Mauryan period witnessed significant developments in art, literature, and [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]]. The reign of Ashoka (circa 268–232 BCE), the third major emperor, marked a transformative moment when he adopted [[concepts/buddhism|Buddhism]] and promoted it throughout the empire. Ashoka issued [[concepts/public-edicts|public edicts]] inscribed on stone pillars and rock surfaces across the realm, disseminating moral and administrative [[concepts/recommendations|directives]]. The empire patronized scholarship, including early mathematical and astronomical work, contributing to the intellectual foundations of classical [[concepts/indian-civilization|Indian civilization]]. The spread of Buddhism during this period influenced cultural developments throughout Asia.

The Mauryan Empire declined gradually after Ashoka's reign, eventually fragmenting into smaller regional powers by 185 BCE. However, its administrative models, cultural achievements, and historical significance established it as a formative period in Indian history.
