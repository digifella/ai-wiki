# Color Labels

Color labels in [[concepts/lightroom|Lightroom]] serve as a fundamental organizational tool for categorizing and managing photographs within a [[concepts/catalog|catalog]]. They provide a quick visual system for flagging images during the [[concepts/creative-workflow|editing workflow]], allowing photographers to group similar images or mark them according to custom criteria. This system works alongside other Lightroom features like [[concepts/star-ratings|star ratings]] and [[concepts/flags|flags]] to create a comprehensive [[concepts/organizational-structure|organizational structure]].

The five standard color options—red, yellow, green, blue, and purple—offer distinct visual cues that can be mapped to specific stages of post-processing or thematic elements within a shoot. For instance, a photographer might use green to indicate images ready for export, red for those requiring significant retouching, and yellow for preliminary selections. This flexibility enables users to adapt the labels to their unique workflow needs rather than adhering to a rigid [[concepts/universal-standard|universal standard]].

In the context of the [[concepts/master-of-editing|Master of Editing]] course, the application of color labels is emphasized as a method for streamlining large-scale projects. By assigning colors based on technical quality, emotional impact, or [[concepts/storytelling|narrative]] role, editors can rapidly filter and sort thousands of images. This visual layering reduces [[concepts/cognitive-load|cognitive load]] during the culling process, ensuring that critical decisions are made efficiently without relying solely on [[concepts/metadata|metadata]] or file names.
