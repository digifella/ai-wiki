# Telephoto Zoom

Telephoto zoom in panoramic photography involves using longer focal lengths to create stitched images, diverging from the traditional wide-angle approach. While conventional panoramas prioritize capturing expansive landscapes and breadth of view, this technique emphasizes magnification and detailed framing. It allows photographers to isolate specific subjects or textures within a larger scene, offering a distinct compositional alternative to standard wide-field captures.

The use of telephoto lenses results in increased magnification and compressed perspective within the final image. This compression flattens the visual depth, bringing distant elements closer together visually compared to wide-angle shots. Consequently, the technique is particularly useful for abstracting complex scenes or highlighting patterns that might be lost in a broader context.

Stitching these high-magnification segments requires precise alignment to maintain sharpness and avoid parallax errors. The resulting image often exhibits a unique aesthetic characterized by tight framing and reduced environmental context. This method expands the utility of panoramic photography beyond landscape documentation, enabling detailed study of architectural details, natural textures, and isolated subjects.
