# restaurant-style cooking

Techniques used in professional kitchens to maximize efficiency, [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]], and sensory appeal of dishes. Key principles include high heat, proper [[concepts/salt-timing|seasoning timing]], and ingredient [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] (mise en place).

## Key Techniques

- **Velveting**: Coating proteins in a mixture of cornstarch, egg white, and wine to retain moisture and create a silky [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]].
- **Wok Hei**: The "breath of the wok," achieved through high-heat stir-frying that imparts a smoky flavor.
- **Oil Sealing**: Pouring hot oil over aromatics or finished dishes to [[concepts/deployment|release]] essential oils and enhance aroma.
- **Cornstarch Slurry**: Using starch-water mixtures to thicken sauces quickly without lumps, common in stir-fry dishes.

## Ingredient Handling

- **Pre-chopping**: Uniform cuts ensure even cooking and professional presentation.
- **[[concepts/marinating|Marinating]]**: Early seasoning allows flavors to penetrate proteins deeply.
- **Fat Management**: Using neutral oils with high smoke points for frying; finishing with aromatic oils (e.g., sesame) for flavor.

## Case Study: Chinese Scrambled Eggs

Professional [[concepts/chinese-scrambled-eggs|Chinese scrambled eggs]] differ significantly from Western versions in [[concepts/texture|texture]] and flavor profile.

- **Fluffiness**: Achieved by beating eggs thoroughly and adding a small amount of water or cooking wine before cooking.
- **High Heat**: Cooking quickly over intense heat prevents overcooking and maintains a tender, moist curd.
- **Oil Usage**: Generous oil is used to coat the pan and the eggs, creating a glossy, rich mouthfeel.
- **Aromatics**: Scallions are often added at the end to preserve freshness and color.
- **Seasoning**: Salt is added at the right moment to avoid drawing out too much moisture prematurely.
