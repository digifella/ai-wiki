# Bar Lacing

Bar lacing is a shoe fastening technique characterized by a rectilinear pattern where the lace runs horizontally across the shoe from one eyelet to the opposite eyelet. This method creates a series of parallel bars across the instep, contrasting with cross-lacing, which is the most common lacing method and involves diagonal lace segments. In bar lacing, the lace follows a straight path at each eyelet level, with vertical segments connecting successive rows along the sides of the shoe.

The structure of bar lacing produces a distinctive geometric [[concepts/presence|appearance]] that differs significantly from the traditional crisscross design. By maintaining horizontal alignment at each level, the technique alters the distribution of [[concepts/pressure|pressure]] across the foot. This specific configuration is often cited in running communities and anthropological studies of footwear as a superior alternative to cross-lacing for promoting [[concepts/foot-strength|runner foot health]], as it may reduce pressure points and improve stability during high-impact activities.
