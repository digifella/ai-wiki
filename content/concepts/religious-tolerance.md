# Religious Tolerance

Religious tolerance is the practice of accepting and respecting the beliefs, practices, and rights of individuals who hold different religious views. It operates on the principle that people of various faiths should coexist peacefully without persecution, discrimination, or forced conversion. Tolerance does not necessarily require agreement with or endorsement of other religions, but rather a commitment to allowing others freedom of conscience and worship.

## Historical Development

The concept of religious tolerance has evolved significantly across different societies and time periods. While some ancient and medieval societies developed frameworks for managing religious pluralism, systematic protections for religious freedom emerged more prominently during the Enlightenment and influenced modern democratic constitutions. Historical developments include the Edict of Nantes in 17th-century France and the First Amendment to the [[entities/united-states|United States]] Constitution, both of which sought to reduce religiously motivated [[concepts/conflict|conflict]] by establishing legal protections.

## Modern Practice

Contemporary religious tolerance is typically embedded in international human rights frameworks and national laws that protect freedom of religion and belief. Many countries recognize religious tolerance as essential to social stability and individual liberty. Implementation varies widely, with some societies achieving greater pluralism than others, and ongoing tensions between religious communities and secular [[concepts/governance|governance]] structures remain common in many regions.
