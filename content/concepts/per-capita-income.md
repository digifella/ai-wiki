# Per Capita Income

## Definition
Average GDP or total national income divided by resident population. Primary quantitative proxy for [[concepts/quality-of-life|standard of living]], economic development, and aggregate wealth [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] across geopolitical entities.

## Calculation & Variants
- Base formula: `Total National Income ÷ Mid-year Population`
- Nominal vs. PPP (Purchasing [[concepts/power|Power]] Parity) [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] for inflation and [[concepts/cost|cost]] of living
- Distinct from median income and disposable income; highly sensitive to wealth concentration

## Analytical Utility
- Benchmarks economic growth and productivity trends over fiscal cycles
- Informs fiscal policy, social welfare targeting, and poverty alleviation frameworks
- Enables cross-national comparison via World Bank and IMF standardizations

## Structural Limitations
- Averages obscure income inequality and Gini coefficient disparities
- Excludes informal economy [[concepts/output|output]], unpaid labor, and environmental externalities
- Fails to capture regional price level variations without PPP normalization

## Empirical Application: Cuba & Urban Economic Shifts
- [[entities/havana|Havana]] pre-1959 operated as a high-per capita income hub, historically termed "The Paris of the Caribbean" due to robust foreign investment, tourism, and [[concepts/sugar|sugar]] exports
- Post-[[concepts/revolution|revolution]] state nationalization and trade embargoes triggered severe economic decline, demonstrating how institutional restructuring directly compresses aggregate income metrics
- Urban infrastructure degradation, currency controls, and black market expansion further decoupled nominal per capita income from actual standard of living
- Reference: [[lab-notes/2026-05-12-Havanas-Transformation-Prosperity-Revolution-and-Economi|Havana's Transformation: Prosperity, Revolution, and Economic Decline]]
