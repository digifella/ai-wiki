# Battery Swapping

**Battery swapping** is a method of electric vehicle (EV) refueling where a depleted battery pack is replaced with a fully charged one, significantly reducing downtime compared to conventional charging. This approach is particularly critical for heavy-duty-trucks and commercial fleets where operational [[concepts/continuity|continuity]] is paramount.

## Key Applications & Case Studies

### Janus Electric Retrofit Solution
Recent developments highlight the viability of retrofitting existing diesel fleets rather than manufacturing new EVs from scratch. A notable example is the work by [[entities/janus-electric|Janus Electric]], an Australian company focusing on [[concepts/heavy-duty-truck-electrification|heavy-duty truck electrification]].

- **[[concepts/retrofit|Retrofit]] Approach**: Janus Electric converts existing diesel trucks to electric powertrains, enabling immediate deployment without waiting for new vehicle manufacturing cycles.
- **Swapping Infrastructure**: The system utilizes a standardized battery swapping mechanism, allowing trucks to be refueled in minutes rather than hours.
- **Operational Impact**: This method addresses range [[concepts/anxiety|anxiety]] and [[concepts/charging-infrastructure|charging infrastructure]] gaps in remote or industrial settings, such as those found in Australia.
- **Source Analysis**: Detailed [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] insights and feasibility assessments are available in [[lab-notes/2026-07-30-Janus-Electric-Battery-Swapping-Retrofit-for-Heavy-Duty|Janus Electric: Battery Swapping Retrofit for Heavy-Duty Truck Electrification]].

## Advantages
- **Reduced Downtime**: Refueling time is comparable to diesel refueling.
- **Grid Stability**: Batteries can be charged slowly at off-peak hours, reducing strain on local power [[concepts/grids|grids]].
- **Battery Longevity**: Centralized charging allows for better battery management and [[concepts/health-surveillance|health monitoring]].
- **[[concepts/cost-efficient-solutions|Cost Efficiency]]**: Lower upfront vehicle costs through retrofitting and potential battery-as-a-service models.

## Challenges
- **Standardization**: Lack of universal battery form factors across manufacturers.
- **Infrastructure Cost**: High initial investment for swapping stations.
- **Weight & Complexity**: Added weight of the swapping mechanism and battery packs.
