# Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR)

Ground Penetrating Radar is a geophysical [[concepts/survey|survey]] technique that uses radar pulses to image the subsurface. The method transmits electromagnetic waves into the ground and records the reflected signals, creating a detailed map of buried structures and objects without requiring excavation. GPR operates across a range of frequencies, typically between 10 MHz and 2.6 GHz, with lower frequencies penetrating deeper but at reduced resolution, while higher frequencies provide finer detail in shallower surveys.

## Applications in Archaeology

GPR has become a standard tool in archaeological practice for prospecting and site assessment. It can detect buried artifacts, structures, soil disturbances, and voids beneath the surface, allowing archaeologists to plan excavations more efficiently and identify areas of greatest interest. The technique is particularly valuable in agricultural landscapes where surface surveys alone may miss subsurface deposits. Denmark has notably integrated GPR technology with a [[concepts/citizen-science|citizen science]] approach, engaging private metal detectorists as part of a collaborative program to systematically survey farm fields and recover archaeological finds.

## Technical Advantages

The non-invasive [[entities/nature|nature]] of GPR makes it cost-effective for large-area surveys compared to traditional test excavation. It provides real-time subsurface visualization and can work in various soil and geological conditions, though results are affected by soil moisture and conductivity. Combined with other survey methods such as [[concepts/metal-detecting|metal detection]] and magnetometry, GPR contributes to a more comprehensive understanding of archaeological sites and the landscapes they occupy.
