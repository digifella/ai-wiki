# Dna Rewriting

DNA rewriting encompasses a class of emerging [[concepts/gene-editing|gene-editing]] technologies that extend beyond the foundational capabilities of [[concepts/crispr|CRISPR]] systems. While CRISPR-Cas9 primarily functions by creating double-strand breaks in DNA to facilitate insertion or deletion, successor technologies aim to expand the range of editable mutations, improve [[concepts/accuracy|precision]], and enhance overall efficiency. These advances represent the next generation of molecular tools designed to modify genetic material in living organisms with greater control and reduced off-target effects.

Base editing and [[concepts/prime-lens|prime]] editing are the primary methodologies within this domain. Base editing allows for the direct conversion of one DNA base pair into another without inducing double-strand breaks, thereby reducing the risk of unintended genomic damage. Prime editing offers even greater versatility by enabling precise insertions, deletions, and all possible base-to-base conversions without relying on donor DNA [[concepts/templates|templates]] or double-strand breaks. These techniques address key limitations of earlier CRISPR applications, particularly regarding accuracy and the scope of achievable genetic modifications.

Research into these successor technologies is prominently conducted at the [[entities/broad-institute|Broad Institute]], where [[entities/feng-zhang|Dr. Feng Zhang]] has played a pivotal role in their development and refinement. His work focuses on optimizing these tools for therapeutic applications, aiming to correct genetic defects associated with various diseases. The ongoing evolution of DNA rewriting technologies continues to [[concepts/ambition|drive]] progress in [[concepts/viral-defense-mechanism|molecular biology]], offering new avenues for treating [[concepts/genetic-disorders|genetic disorders]] that were previously considered intractable.
