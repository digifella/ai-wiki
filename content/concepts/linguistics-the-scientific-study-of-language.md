# Linguistics: The Scientific Study Of Language

Linguistics is the scientific discipline that examines human language in its [[concepts/structure|structure]], use, and historical development. As an empirical field, it applies systematic methods to understand how languages work, how they change over time, and how they function in [[concepts/human-cognition|human cognition]] and society. Linguists study both the universal properties shared across all human languages and the specific features that distinguish individual languages from one another.

## Core Areas of Study

The field encompasses several interconnected domains. Phonetics and phonology examine the sounds of language and their [[concepts/organization|organization]] within linguistic systems. Syntax investigates how words and phrases combine to form grammatical sentences. Semantics and pragmatics address meaning—both the literal meaning of words and expressions, and how context shapes interpretation. Historical and comparative linguistics trace how languages evolve and relate to one another through common ancestry.

## Broader Applications

Beyond examining [[concepts/language-grammar|language structure]] itself, linguistics intersects with psychology, [[concepts/neuroscience|neuroscience]], and anthropology to understand how humans acquire language, process it mentally, and use it to communicate. Applied linguistics draws on these insights to inform fields such as language education, clinical speech-language pathology, and [[concepts/nlp|natural language processing]] in computer science. The discipline also contributes to documentation and [[concepts/preservation|preservation]] efforts for endangered languages.
