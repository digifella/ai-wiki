# Version Numbers

Version numbers are numerical or alphanumeric identifiers assigned to different releases of software, hardware, or other products to [[concepts/redlining|track changes]] and [[concepts/software-updates|updates]] over time. They serve as a concise way to distinguish between iterations, allowing users and developers to understand which variant they are using and whether updates are available. Version numbering systems vary widely across industries and organizations, ranging from simple sequential integers to complex multi-part schemes.

## Common Versioning Schemes

The most prevalent approach in software is semantic versioning, which uses three numbers in the format MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH (for example, 2.5.3). The major number indicates significant changes that may break compatibility, minor indicates new features, and patch indicates bug fixes.
