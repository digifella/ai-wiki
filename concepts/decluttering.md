# Decluttering

Decluttering is the process of removing unnecessary items from a space to create a more organized and functional environment. While initial decluttering projects can significantly improve a space, the primary challenge for most people is maintaining that order over time. Without systematic approaches, spaces naturally tend to accumulate clutter again, undoing previous efforts and creating a cycle of repeated reorganization.

## Preventing Clutter Recurrence

Effective decluttering requires both initial action and ongoing habits. Rather than relying solely on periodic major cleanup efforts, sustainable organization depends on integrating small, consistent behaviors into daily routines. A key strategy for preventing clutter recurrence is applying Gretchen Rubin’s One-Minute Rule, which suggests that if a task takes less than one minute to complete, it should be done immediately.

This approach targets the specific point where new items enter the environment. By handling incoming objects—such as mail, keys, or clothing—within sixty seconds, individuals prevent these items from becoming temporary piles that eventually solidify into permanent clutter. This method shifts the focus from reactive, large-scale organizing to proactive, micro-management of possessions, thereby reducing the cognitive load and physical effort required for future maintenance.

The long-term efficacy of this technique lies in its low barrier to entry. Because the required action is minimal, it bypasses the procrastination often associated with larger cleaning tasks. Over time, these immediate actions accumulate to maintain a baseline of order, ensuring that the initial gains from decluttering are preserved without the need for constant, intensive intervention.
