# Compute Capacity

The total quantifiable processing [[concepts/power|power]] ([[concepts/cpu|CPU]], GPU, TPU) available within a distributed system or cloud environment to execute computational workloads. It serves as the primary physical bottleneck for [[entities/ai]] [[concepts/training-process|model training]] and large-scale [[concepts/inference|inference]].

## Core Components
- **[[concepts/hardware|Hardware]] Architectures**: Includes general-[[concepts/purpose|purpose]] GPUs (e.g., [[entities/nvidia]]) and application-specific integrated circuits like [[entities/tpus|TPUs]].
- **Infrastructure [[concepts/scaling|Scaling]]**: The ability of [[concepts/cloud-computing]] environments to provision [[entities/high-performance|high-performance]] clusters on demand.

## Strategic Trends & Developments
- **Specialized [[concepts/silicon|Silicon]]**: Increasing strategic focus on [[concepts/tpu-development|TPU development]] to optimize [[concepts/computing-architecture|AI infrastructure]] efficiency and performance-per-watt.
- **Ecosystem Dependencies**: The necessity of [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]] capacity to support the massive [[concepts/training|training]] requirements of major model developers (e.g., [[entities/anthropic]]).
- **Economic Drivers**: The shift in Monetization Strategy for cloud providers, focusing on the orchestration and availability of specialized, high-demand [[concepts/hardware|hardware]].
- **Hardware Competition**: The interplay between general-[[concepts/motivation|purpose]] hardware (e.g., [[entities/nvidia]]) and custom-designed, proprietary [[concepts/silicon|silicon]] in large-scale AI workloads.
