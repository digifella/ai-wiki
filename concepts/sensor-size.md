# Sensor size

Physical dimensions of a camera's image sensor, directly influencing [[concepts/light|light]] capture, noise levels, [[concepts/dynamic-range|dynamic range]], and depth of field. Larger sensors generally enable superior performance in low-light conditions and wider dynamic range.

Key implications:
- **Full-frame sensors** (36×24mm) maximize light gathering and dynamic range (common in [[concepts/dslr-photography|DSLR]] and [[concepts/mirrorless-photography|Mirrorless camera systems]]).
- **Smartphone sensors** (typically 1/2.3" or smaller) prioritize compactness but sacrifice [[concepts/low-light-performance|low-light performance]] and dynamic range compared to larger sensors.
- **Dynamic range** and **low-light performance** are significantly enhanced by larger sensor area due to greater photon capture capacity.
- Noise management is critical when utilizing smaller sensors or high [[concepts/iso-settings|ISO settings]]; understanding the [[concepts/causes|causes]] and [[concepts/post-processing-solutions|post-processing solutions]] for [[concepts/digital-image-noise|digital noise]] is essential for maintaining image quality regardless of sensor constraints, as detailed in [[lab-notes/2026-06-04-Demystifying-Digital-Image-Noise-Causes-Prevention-and-P|Demystifying Digital Image Noise: Causes, Prevention, and Post-Processing Solutions]].
