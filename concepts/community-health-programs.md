# Community Health Programs

[[concepts/community-health|Community health]] [[concepts/software|programs]] are organized health interventions and services designed to improve the health and [[concepts/wellbeing|wellbeing]] of defined populations at the community level. Rather than focusing solely on individual clinical treatment, these programs address [[concepts/patient-needs|health needs]] through coordinated efforts across diverse settings such as schools, workplaces, clinics, and public spaces. They represent a systems-based approach that recognizes how social, environmental, and economic factors shape health outcomes.

## Scope and Delivery

[[concepts/public-health|Community health]] programs typically encompass [[concepts/health-promotion|disease prevention]], health education, screening services, and management of chronic conditions. They may be delivered by government agencies, non-profit organizations, [[concepts/health|healthcare]] providers, or partnerships between these [[concepts/nodes|entities]]. Programs often employ community health workers, nurses, educators, and other professionals who understand local contexts and can build [[concepts/trust|trust]] with residents.

## Core Functions

These programs commonly focus on identified [[concepts/population-health|population health]] priorities such as maternal and child health, communicable disease control, [[concepts/mental-health-support|mental health support]], and management of non-communicable diseases like diabetes and hypertension. Effective community health programs conduct needs assessments, establish measurable objectives, and monitor outcomes to ensure services meet actual community requirements rather than assumed needs.

## Integration and Sustainability

Successful community health programs typically integrate with existing healthcare systems while remaining responsive to local priorities. Sustainability depends on adequate funding, trained [[entities/employees|personnel]], community engagement, and [[concepts/coordination|coordination]] with other social services. Programs that involve community members in planning and [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] tend to achieve better health outcomes and stronger community ownership.
