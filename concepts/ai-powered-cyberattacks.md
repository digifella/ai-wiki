# AI-Powered Cyberattacks

## Overview
The convergence of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] and malicious intent has shifted the threat landscape from manual exploitation to automated, scalable, and adaptive campaigns. Attackers leverage [[concepts/large-language-models]] (LLMs) and [[concepts/agentic-systems|autonomous agents]] to lower the barrier to entry for sophisticated attacks while increasing their [[concepts/speed|speed]] and [[concepts/accuracy|precision]].

## Key Attack Vectors

### Dark Sourcery & LLM Hacking
*   **Prompt Injection & [[concepts/jailbreaking|Jailbreaking]]:** Adversaries manipulate LLMs to bypass safety filters, extracting sensitive data or generating malicious code.
*   **[[concepts/data-hallucination|Hallucination]] Exploitation:** Attackers exploit model inaccuracies to trick systems into executing unauthorized actions or revealing internal [[concepts/open-source-philosophy|logic]].
*   **[[concepts/code-generation|Code Generation]]:** [[concepts/ai-tools|AI tools]] are used to rapidly generate polymorphic malware, obfuscate payloads, and identify vulnerabilities in real-time.

### Agent Swarms
*   **Autonomous Orchestration:** Multiple [[concepts/ai-agents|AI agents]] coordinate to perform complex multi-stage attacks, such as reconnaissance, exploitation, and lateral [[concepts/exercise|movement]], without human intervention.
*   **Scalability:** [[concepts/swarm-intelligence|Swarm intelligence]] allows for distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks and mass phishing campaigns that adapt to defenses dynamically.
*   **[[concepts/resilience|Resilience]]:** Decentralized agent structures make takedown efforts difficult, as the loss of individual [[concepts/nodes|nodes]] does not collapse the attack [[concepts/infrastructure|infrastructure]].
