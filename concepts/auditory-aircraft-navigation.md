# Auditory Aircraft Navigation

**Auditory aircraft navigation** refers to early aviation techniques where pilots relied on sound signals transmitted via radio waves to determine their position and course, particularly before the widespread [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of visual instrument flight rules (IFR) and modern GPS.

## Core Concepts

*   **[[concepts/low-frequency-radio-range|Low Frequency Radio Range]] (LFR):** A pioneering system that transmitted directional radio signals audible to pilots.
    *   Integrated from [[lab-notes/2026-08-03-Low-Frequency-Radio-Range-Pioneering-Auditory-Aircraft-N|Low Frequency Radio Range: Pioneering Auditory Aircraft Navigation System]].
    *   Featured in analysis by [[entities/scott-manley|Scott Manley]], contrasting early auditory methods with modern avionics in aircraft like the [[entities/cirrus|Cirrus]] Aircraft.
    *   Source: [Low Frequency Radio Range: Pioneering Auditory Aircraft Navigation System](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Q4Jsy0LGeE)

## Historical Context

*   Enabled Instrument Flight Rules operations in poor visibility conditions prior to advanced electronic navigation aids.
*   Relied on the pilot's ability to interpret [[concepts/audio-modality|audio]] cues (such as Morse code dots and dashes) to stay on course.
*   Represented a critical transition [[concepts/phase|phase]] between visual navigation and fully automated electronic navigation systems.

## Related Systems

*   NDB (Non-Directional Beacon)
*   VOR (VHF Omnidirectional Range)
*   ADF (Automatic Direction Finder)
