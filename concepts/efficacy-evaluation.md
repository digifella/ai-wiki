# Efficacy Evaluation

**Definition:** The systematic assessment of the extent to which an intervention, policy [[concepts/success|achievement]], or other intervention produces intended results under controlled or real-[[entities/earth|world]] conditions. In clinical and nutritional contexts, it distinguishes between **efficacy** (performance in ideal/controlled settings) and **effectiveness** (performance in routine practice).

## Core Principles
- **[[concepts/hypothesis-driven-experimentation|Hypothesis Testing]]:** Requires null [[concepts/hypothesis-testing|hypothesis testing]] to determine statistical significance.
- **Confounding Variables:** Must control for placebo effects, lifestyle factors, and pre-existing conditions.
- **Reproducibility:** Findings must be replicable across independent studies Reproducibility [[concepts/crisis|Crisis]].
- **Dose-Response:** Establishes relationship between substance quantity and biological response.

## Methodologies
- **Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs):** [[concepts/major-gold-deposit|Gold]] standard for establishing causality.
- **Meta-Analysis:** Statistical combination of results from multiple studies to increase power.
- **Systematic Reviews:** Comprehensive identification of all relevant research on a specific question.
- **Observational Studies:** Cohort or case-control designs when RCTs are unethical or impractical.

## Application in Nutritional Science
Evaluation of dietary supplements often suffers from high heterogeneity in study quality, funding bias, and lack of standardization in active ingredients.
