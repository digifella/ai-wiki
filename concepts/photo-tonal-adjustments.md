# Photo Tonal Adjustments

Photo tonal adjustments refer to modifications made to the brightness, contrast, and color balance of digital photographs. These adjustments are fundamental to post-processing workflows in photography and digital image editing. By altering the tonal range of an image—from shadows and midtones to highlights—photographers can enhance visual impact, correct exposure issues, and establish a consistent aesthetic across a series of images.

## Camera Raw Filter

The Photoshop Camera Raw Filter is a specialized tool for making non-destructive tonal adjustments to digital images. It provides access to a range of controls including exposure, highlights, shadows, whites, blacks, clarity, and vibrance, allowing photographers to refine the tonal qualities of their work. The Camera Raw interface displays a histogram and preview in real-time, enabling immediate visual feedback as adjustments are made. Because Camera Raw processing is non-destructive, original image data remains intact, and adjustments can be revisited and modified at any point in the editing process.

## Common Adjustment Controls

Standard tonal adjustment controls include exposure (overall brightness), contrast (difference between light and dark areas), and shadow/highlight sliders (targeting specific tonal ranges). White balance adjustments correct color casts related to lighting conditions, while vibrance and saturation controls modify color intensity. The ability to selectively target shadows, midtones, and highlights separately allows photographers to make precise corrections without affecting the entire image uniformly. These tools work together to refine both technical exposure issues and creative color grading choices.
