# Allied Health

Allied health encompasses a broad category of healthcare services provided by professionals distinct from physicians and nurses. This diverse workforce includes physiotherapists, occupational therapists, dietitians, speech-language pathologists, and radiographers, among other specialized practitioners. These professionals operate collaboratively with medical doctors and nursing staff to deliver comprehensive patient care across various settings, including clinical hospitals, rehabilitation facilities, and community-based programs.

The scope of allied health practice addresses specific aspects of patient well-being that complement traditional medical treatment. Practitioners focus on rehabilitation, prevention, diagnosis, and therapeutic interventions tailored to individual patient needs. Their work is integral to multidisciplinary teams, ensuring that care plans are holistic and address the physical, mental, and social dimensions of health.

Recent advancements in technology have significantly influenced allied health practices, particularly through the integration of wearable devices and embedded sensors. These tools enable continuous monitoring and precise data collection, allowing practitioners to track patient progress in real-time. This technological integration supports more accurate assessments and personalized care strategies, enhancing the effectiveness of interventions in both clinical and remote environments.
