Natural Language Processing (NLP) is a subfield of artificial intelligence and computational linguistics dedicated to the interaction between computers and human language. Its primary objective is to enable machines to understand, interpret, and generate human language in a way that is both meaningful and useful. By bridging the gap between human communication and computer data, NLP allows systems to process vast amounts of unstructured text and speech data.

The discipline integrates principles from linguistics, computer science, and machine learning. Modern NLP systems typically rely on deep learning models, such as transformers, to analyze language at various levels, including phonetics, morphology, syntax, and semantics. These models learn patterns from large datasets to perform tasks like tokenization, part-of-speech tagging, named entity recognition, and sentiment analysis.

In the context of AI agents, NLP serves as the critical interface for perception and action. It enables agents to parse user intents, extract relevant information, and formulate coherent responses. This capability is foundational for applications ranging from virtual assistants and chatbots to automated translation services and information retrieval systems, effectively allowing agents to operate within human-centric environments.
