# 2d Grid Simulation

The 2D Grid Simulation is a computational framework developed by Sakana AI to model the survival and coexistence of artificial intelligence species within a digital ecosystem. This method utilizes a discrete two-dimensional lattice where each AI agent occupies a single cell. The environment is designed to restrict agent behavior to immediate neighborhoods, preventing global communication and forcing interactions to rely solely on local rules and spatial proximity.

## Local Interaction Dynamics

Agents operate under strict locality constraints, meaning their decisions and state updates are determined exclusively by the configuration of adjacent cells. This design eliminates the possibility of long-range coordination, effectively simulating an evolutionary pressure where agents must adapt to their immediate surroundings to persist. The lack of global information forces the emergence of complex behaviors from simple, localized decision-making processes.

## Emergent Coexistence

The primary objective of the simulation is to observe how different AI architectures or strategies can coexist within a shared, resource-constrained space. By limiting interaction ranges, the system creates a competitive environment where survival depends on efficient local resource management and adaptation. The resulting dynamics provide insights into the stability of multi-agent systems and the conditions necessary for diverse AI species to maintain equilibrium without centralized control.
