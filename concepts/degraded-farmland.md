# Degraded Farmland

**Degraded farmland** refers to agricultural land that has lost its [[concepts/productivity|productivity]], ecological function, or [[concepts/payload-structure|structural integrity]] due to intensive [[concepts/agriculture|farming]] practices, erosion, nutrient depletion, or contamination. [[concepts/preservation|Restoration]] often involves reversing soil compaction, rebuilding organic matter, and reintroducing native [[concepts/biodiversity|biodiversity]].

## Characteristics
- **Soil Degradation**: Loss of topsoil, reduced microbial diversity, and compaction.
- **Biodiversity Loss**: Replacement of complex ecosystems with monocultures.
- **Hydrological Disruption**: Altered drainage patterns and increased runoff.

## Restoration Strategies
- **Agroforestry**: Integrating trees and shrubs into crop systems.
- **Cover Cropping**: Using non-harvested plants to protect and enrich soil.
- **[[concepts/invasive-species|Invasive Species]] Management**: Strategic removal or repurposing of non-native species.
- **Rewilding**: Allowing natural processes to restore ecosystem balance with minimal human intervention.

## Case Studies
- **[[concepts/banks-peninsula|Banks Peninsula]], New Zealand**: [[lab-notes/2026-07-10-Banks-Peninsula-Rewilding-Hugh-Wilsons-Gorse-Based-Ecosy|Banks Peninsula Rewilding: Hugh Wilson's Gorse-Based Ecosystem Restoration]] demonstrates an unconventional approach where [[concepts/gorse]] (an invasive species) was utilized as a foundational component for [[concepts/ecosystem-restoration|ecosystem restoration]] rather than immediate eradication, facilitating the return of [[concepts/native-flora|native flora]] and fauna on degraded land.
