# Global Trade

Global trade refers to the exchange of goods, services, and resources between distant regions and peoples across history. Rather than a modern invention, long-distance trade networks emerged thousands of years ago as societies developed surplus production and sought access to resources unavailable in their local environments. These exchanges fundamentally shaped cultural [[entities/contact|contact]], technological diffusion, and economic development across civilizations.

## Early Trade Networks

The earliest forms of global trade developed along strategic geographical corridors. The Silk Road, linking East Asia with the Mediterranean [[entities/earth|world]] from around the 2nd century BCE onward, facilitated not only the [[concepts/exercise|movement]] of silk and spices but also [[concepts/ideas|ideas]], technologies, and religions. Maritime trade routes connected Indian Ocean civilizations, while overland routes crossed Central Asia and the Sahara Desert. These networks created interdependencies between distant regions long before modern [[concepts/industrialization|industrialization]].

## Colonial Expansion and Commodification

The Age of Exploration from the 15th century onward transformed global trade patterns and intensity. European expansion into the [[concepts/new-world|Americas]], Africa, and Asia established new trade routes and introduced new commodities—particularly [[concepts/sugar|sugar]], spices, and precious [[concepts/metal|metals]]—into global commerce. This period saw the [[concepts/emergent-behavior|emergence]] of [[concepts/colonial_trade|colonial trade]] systems that concentrated wealth in European hands while restructuring economies across the colonized world, often through coercive labor systems.

## Impact and Legacy

Global trade networks have consistently served as channels for both material goods and immaterial forces—spreading technologies, diseases, languages, and institutional practices. The structures and inequalities established during earlier periods of global trade continue to influence contemporary economic [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] and development patterns across regions.
