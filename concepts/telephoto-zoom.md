# Telephoto Zoom

Telephoto Zoom is a photographic technique that combines telephoto lenses with panoramic image stitching to create extended wide-angle compositions. Rather than relying on a camera's built-in panorama mode, the method involves capturing multiple overlapping frames using a telephoto lens, then digitally stitching the images together into a single panoramic photograph. This approach circumvents the limitations of standard smartphone panorama functions and produces results with greater detail and tonal consistency.

The process typically begins with the photographer selecting a telephoto lens to isolate specific sections of a scene. By capturing a series of overlapping shots, the photographer ensures sufficient data for seamless alignment during post-processing. This method allows for higher resolution outputs than standard smartphone modes, which often downsample or compress images to maintain real-time processing speeds.

Digital stitching software aligns the individual frames based on overlapping features, correcting for perspective distortion and exposure differences. The resulting composite image maintains the sharpness and depth of field characteristics of the telephoto lens while achieving a field of view wider than the lens's native capabilities. This technique is particularly useful for landscape and architectural photography where detail retention is critical.
