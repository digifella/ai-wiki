# Scale Effect

The **scale effect** refers to how the performance and capabilities of [[concepts/artificial-intelligence-models|artificial intelligence models]], particularly transformer-based architectures, are heavily influenced by [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]]. Larger datasets, more powerful [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] (such as GPUs), and increased [[concepts/model-size|model size]] generally lead to better results due to an improvement in generalization ability, capacity for capturing complex patterns, and [[concepts/parallel-processing|parallel processing]] speed.

### Key Points
- **Larger Models**: The performance of [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] increases with scale up until a certain point where [[concepts/diminishing-returns|diminishing returns]] are observed.
- **Computational Power**: Modern systems rely on [[entities/high-performance|high-performance]] GPUs and large datasets to train complex [[concepts/neural-networks|neural networks]] efficiently.
- **[[concepts/limited-resources|Resource Constraints]]**: Limited [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]] can significantly impact the [[concepts/training|training]] time, efficiency, and overall effectiveness of an AI model.

## Demystifying AI: Transformer Training on a 1979 PDP-11

**Clip title:** EXPOSED: The Dirty Little Secret of AI (On a 1979 PDP-11)
**Author / channel:** [[entities/dave|Dave]]'s Garage
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OUE3FSIk46g

### Summary
The video, presented by Dave, aims to demystify the [[concepts/training-process|training process]] of a [[concepts/neural-network|neural network]] by [[concepts/running|running]] a transformer on a vintage 1979 [[concepts/pdp-1144|PDP-11/44]] computer. Unlike modern cloud clusters with thousands of GPUs, this system operates with a single 6MHz [[concepts/cpu|CPU]] and a mere 64KB of [[concepts/ram|RAM]] (though later upgraded to 4MB). The core idea, Dave argues, is not magical or new; it highlights the scale effect in AI training, showing that even small-scale hardware can train models but at an extremely slow pace compared to modern [[concepts/open-standards|standards]].

### Bullet Points
- Demonstrates transformer model training on a PDP-11 with [[concepts/limited-resources|limited resources]].
- Contrasts modern AI training practices with those possible using 1970s technology.
- Emphasizes the role of computational power in [[concepts/scaling|scaling]] AI performance.
- Addresses misconceptions about AI's complexity and necessity for high-end hardware.
