# Emotion Theory

Emotion [[concepts/theory|Theory]] encompasses scientific frameworks for understanding emotions as complex psychological and physiological phenomena. These theories examine how emotions arise, what purposes they serve, and how they [[concepts/power|influence]] human behavior and [[concepts/cognition|cognition]]. Emotion theory integrates insights from [[concepts/psychology|psychology]], [[concepts/neuroscience|neuroscience]], physiology, and behavioral [[concepts/science|science]] to explain emotional experience across different contexts and populations.

## Core Dimensions

Most emotion theories address several fundamental aspects: the subjective experience of feeling, the physiological [[concepts/responses|responses]] accompanying emotions (such as changes in heart rate or hormone levels), the behavioral expressions emotions produce, and the cognitive appraisals that trigger emotional responses. Different [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|theoretical frameworks]] emphasize different combinations of these elements, leading to various competing [[concepts/models|models]] about which components are primary in emotional experience.

## Major Theoretical Approaches

Prominent emotion theories include appraisal theories, which emphasize cognitive evaluation of situations; dimensional theories, which represent emotions along continuous [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] like valence and arousal; discrete emotion theories, which propose distinct categories of basic emotions; and constructionist approaches, which view emotions as constructed from more basic psychological components. Each framework offers explanatory power for different aspects of emotional life and has empirical support from laboratory and naturalistic studies.
