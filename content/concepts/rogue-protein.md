# Rogue Protein

A rogue protein, or prion, is an infectious agent composed entirely of protein with no genetic material. Unlike conventional pathogens such as bacteria or viruses, [[concepts/infectious-proteins|prions]] propagate by inducing normal cellular proteins to misfold into the same abnormal shape, creating a chain reaction of protein corruption. This mechanism of disease transmission was revolutionary in biology, challenging the central dogma that all infectious [[concepts/agents|agents]] must contain DNA or RNA.

## Historical Discovery: Kuru in the Fore Tribe

The existence of prions was first demonstrated through the study of kuru, a fatal neurodegenerative disease that affected the Fore people of [[entities/papua-new-guinea|Papua New Guinea]] in the mid-20th century. The disease spread through the practice of mortuary cannibalism, specifically the consumption of infected brain tissue during funeral rites. Epidemiological investigation and transmission experiments revealed that kuru was caused by a prion and could be transmitted through contaminated neural tissue, providing the first concrete evidence of protein-only infection.

## The Protein World Hypothesis

The discovery of prions has led to broader speculation about the [[concepts/start-of-life|origins of life]] itself. The Protein World Hypothesis considers whether self-propagating proteins might have played a role in the earliest stages of molecular evolution, potentially preceding or working alongside RNA in the emergence of biological systems. While prions are now understood as pathological agents rather than life-giving molecules, their unique replication mechanism raises fundamental questions about what constitutes living matter and how complexity might have arisen from simple chemical interactions.
