## Singapore Fried Vermicelli Recipe and Origin Clarification

**Author / channel:** Flo Lum

### Summary
The video provides a detailed, step-by-step recipe for making [[concepts/singapore-fried-vermicelli|Singapore Fried Vermicelli]], a popular noodle dish often found in [[concepts/hong-kong-style-cafes|Hong Kong-style cafes]]. The host, [[entities/flo-lum|Flo Lum]], clarifies early on that despite its name, this dish is not actually Singaporean in origin but was created by the Cantonese and became a beloved staple in Hong Kong. She emphasizes that while it looks complex, it's surprisingly easy and cost-effective to prepare at home, promising viewers they'll never order it from a restaurant again.

The [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] [[concepts/phase|phase]] focuses heavily on "[mise en place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mise_en_place)," showcasing the ingredients and their preliminary steps. These include preparing thin [rice vermicelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_vermicelli) noodles according to package [[concepts/instructions|instructions]] (soaking and draining, then covering with a wet towel to retain moisture), [julienning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julienning) vegetables like red bell pepper, onion, and green onions, mincing garlic, and having [bean sprouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bean_sprouts) ready. Additionally, [char siu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Char_siu) (Chinese barbecued pork) is sliced, shrimp are peeled and deveined, and two eggs are beaten, ensuring all components are ready for the quick [stir-frying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stir-frying) process.

The cooking process involves several rapid stir-frying stages in a hot wok. First, a portion of the prepared noodles is briefly fried to achieve a toasted flavor, then set aside. Next, shrimp are quickly cooked until pink and removed from the wok to prevent overcooking. The vegetables – onions, red bell pepper, garlic, and bean sprouts – are then stir-fried, followed by the sliced char siu and the special blend [curry powder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry_powder) that gives the dish its distinctive yellow color and aromatic flavor. Finally, the pre-fried noodles, cooked shrimp, and scrambled egg are returned to the wok, tossed with [soy sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy_sauce) and [sesame oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame_oil), and mixed with the remaining green onions until thoroughly combined.

The video concludes by showcasing the vibrant, generous portion of Singapore [[concepts/vermicelli-cooking|Fried Vermicelli]], garnished with fresh green onions. The host's partner tastes the dish, praising its flavors, textures, and overall mouthfeel, highlighting the deliciousness of the Chinese curry. The overarching takeaway is the immense satisfaction and savings gained from preparing this restaurant-quality dish at home. Flo encourages viewers to embrace home cooking for both culinary enjoyment and financial prudence, especially in times when dining out can be quite expensive.
