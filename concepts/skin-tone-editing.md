# Skin Tone Editing

Skin tone editing in Lightroom is the process of color-correcting and enhancing how skin appears in photographs. Rather than making isolated adjustments to individual images, photographers often use the Calibration Panel to establish foundational color corrections that affect how the camera's sensor data is interpreted across an entire shoot. This approach provides a non-destructive baseline adjustment that influences all subsequent edits and helps maintain consistency across a series of images.

## The Calibration Panel Approach

The Calibration Panel allows photographers to adjust how Lightroom interprets color information from the camera's red, green, and blue channels. By modifying the shadows, midtones, and highlights within each channel, editors can shift skin tones toward warmer or cooler palettes without affecting the underlying image data. This method is particularly useful for compensating for different lighting conditions or camera characteristics that may cast unwanted color casts across skin tones.

## Foundational vs. Selective Adjustments

Foundational color corrections made through the Calibration Panel serve as a starting point for an entire photo session, creating visual consistency before any localized adjustments are applied. This differs from selective skin tone adjustments made later in the editing workflow, such as targeted HSL (Hue, Saturation, Luminance) slider changes or local adjustments to specific areas. Using both approaches in combination allows photographers to achieve refined, natural-looking skin tones while maintaining overall image coherence.
