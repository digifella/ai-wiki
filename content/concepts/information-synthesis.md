# Information Synthesis

Information synthesis is the process of converting source materials—such as research documents, articles, or [[concepts/infographic-design|infographics]]—into new formats and interactive [[concepts/software|applications]] using [[entities/ai-tools|AI tools]]. This approach leverages language [[concepts/models|models]] and autonomous [[concepts/agents|agents]] to extract, reorganize, and present information in ways that serve different purposes or audiences. Rather than simply summarizing sources, synthesis creates derivative works that add functional value, such as [[concepts/interactive-html-web-applications|interactive web applications]], multi-format educational content, or knowledge systems.

## Tools and Platforms

Several AI platforms facilitate information synthesis workflows. [[concepts/ai-integrated-notebooks|NotebookLM]], a research tool developed by [[concepts/google-search|Google]], enables users to upload documents and generate summaries, outlines, and [[concepts/infographic-design|infographics]]. When combined with additional tools like [[entities/gemini-models|Gemini AI]] and [[concepts/ai-assisted-coding|Claude Code]], these [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] extend to creating interactive [[concepts/software|applications]] and integrating synthesized content into existing [[concepts/knowledge-management|knowledge management]] systems like [[concepts/obsidian|Obsidian]]. [[concepts/agentic-systems|Autonomous agents]] can automate portions of this [[concepts/workflow|workflow]], handling repetitive tasks in [[concepts/content-transformation|content transformation]] and multi-format [[concepts/output|output]] generation.

## Practical Applications

Common applications of information synthesis include converting static research materials into [[concepts/interactive-learning|interactive learning]] tools, generating multiple content formats from a single source for [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] across platforms, and building augmented research workflows that reduce manual processing time. The approach is particularly useful for researchers, educators, and content creators who work with large volumes of information and need to repurpose materials efficiently.
