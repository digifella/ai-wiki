[[entities/google-search|Google Search]] is a web search [[concepts/engine|engine]] developed and operated by Google that indexes and retrieves information from billions of web pages, images, videos, news articles, and other digital content across the internet. Launched in 1998, it has become the dominant search engine globally, handling the majority of internet search queries worldwide. The service processes user queries and returns ranked results intended to match what users are seeking.

## How It Works

Google Search uses algorithmic ranking to determine the order of results presented to users. The system crawls and indexes the web by deploying automated [[concepts/software|programs]] known as spiders or bots to discover and update content. It then analyzes this data using complex [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]], such as PageRank, to evaluate the relevance and authority of pages relative to a specific query. These algorithms consider numerous factors, including keyword usage, site structure, and user engagement metrics, to sort results from most to least relevant.

## Features and Evolution

Over time, [[entities/google-search|Google Search]] has expanded beyond traditional text-based results to include specialized verticals like Google Images, Google News, and [[entities/google-maps|Google Maps]]. The platform integrates [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] and [[concepts/machine-learning|machine learning]] to refine [[concepts/search-precision|search accuracy]] and personalize results based on user history and location. Recent developments have introduced features like [[concepts/ai-overviews|AI Overviews]], which synthesize information from multiple sources to provide direct answers to complex queries, marking a shift toward more conversational and comprehensive search experiences.
