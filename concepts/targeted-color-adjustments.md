# Targeted Color Adjustments

Targeted color adjustments allow photographers and editors to modify specific colors within an image while leaving others unchanged. Rather than applying global corrections that affect all tones uniformly, this approach isolates particular color ranges—such as reds, oranges, yellows, greens, cyans, and blues—for independent editing. This selective method proves useful when only certain colors require correction or enhancement, such as adjusting skin tones without affecting background foliage, or deepening blue skies while preserving other hues.

## Color Mixer Tool

In Lightroom Classic, the Color Mixer panel provides the primary interface for targeted adjustments. Users can select individual color channels and modify three key parameters: hue (the actual color), saturation (color intensity), and lightness (brightness). Adjustments made to one color range do not automatically affect adjacent colors, though there is some overlap in the ranges themselves. This precision enables editors to solve common problems like correcting off-color skin tones, restoring faded colors, or creating stylistic effects with specific color groups.

## Practical Applications

Common uses for targeted color adjustments include correcting orange or yellow casts in portraits, intensifying or muting vibrant clothing colors, and adjusting foliage to appear more natural or stylized. The technique is particularly valuable in post-processing workflows where global adjustments prove insufficient or would create undesired side effects. By working within defined color channels rather than luminosity or saturation masks, editors achieve results that feel natural and maintain tonal relationships within the adjusted color range.
