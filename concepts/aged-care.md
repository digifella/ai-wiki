# Aged Care

**Aged Care** refers to the system of [[concepts/health|health]] and social support services provided to older adults, encompassing residential facilities, community-based support, and palliative care. The sector focuses on maintaining autonomy, dignity, and [[concepts/quality-of-life|quality of life]] while managing complex chronic conditions and social [[concepts/disconnection|isolation]].

## Core Principles
- **[[concepts/person-centred-care|Person-Centred Care]]**: Tailoring support to individual preferences, history, and values rather than [[concepts/standardized-communication|standardized protocols]].
- **[[concepts/wellbeing|Holistic Health]]**: Integrating physical, mental, and [[concepts/social-well-being|social well-being]].
- **Dignity of Risk**: Allowing seniors to make choices that may involve risk to preserve autonomy.

## Technological Integration and Ethics
The integration of **[[concepts/ai-technologies|Artificial Intelligence]]** presents significant opportunities for efficiency and monitoring but raises critical ethical concerns regarding surveillance, data [[concepts/privacy|privacy]], and the dehumanization of care.

- **Humanity First**: Technology must augment, not replace, [[concepts/human-connection|human connection]]. [[entities/ai|Artificial Intelligence]] should handle administrative burdens or [[concepts/artificial-intelligence-in-healthcare|predictive analytics]] to free up [[entities/employees|staff]] for [[concepts/social-interaction|interpersonal interaction]].
- **[[concepts/person-led-flourishing|Person-Led Flourishing]]**: AI implementations must prioritize the subjective [[concepts/experience|experience]] and flourishing of the care recipient, ensuring [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]] do not optimize for efficiency at the cost of dignity.
- **Ethical Frameworks**: Deployment requires strict [[concepts/governance|governance]] to prevent bias in diagnostic tools and ensure transparent [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] processes.
