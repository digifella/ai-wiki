## OpenAI Astra Cancellation, Sonnet 5.5, and Meta Muse's Impact on AI
**Clip title:** [[concepts/whisper-transcription|OpenAI]] cancels Astra [[concepts/deployment|release]], [[concepts/sonnet-5|Sonnet 5]].5 & what [[concepts/muse-assistant|Meta Muse]] means for work

### Summary
This episode of the "[[concepts/mixture-of-experts|Mixture of Experts]]" podcast, hosted by [[entities/david|David]] Zax with IBM experts [[entities/chris-hay|Chris Hay]], [[entities/madison-gooch|Madison Gooch]], and [[entities/ash-minhas|Ash Minhas]], delves into recent developments in the [[concepts/ai-landscape|AI landscape]], focusing on strategic decisions by major players, the evolving utility of different [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]], and the transformative impact of [[concepts/action-oriented-ai|agentic AI]] on both consumer and enterprise spheres. The discussion highlights the complexities of [[concepts/ai-development|AI development]], deployment, and [[concepts/governance|governance]] in a rapidly advancing field.

The panel first examined OpenAI's reported cancellation of its [[concepts/gpt-61|GPT-6.1]] Astra model. Ash Minhas expressed skepticism, viewing the announcement, made shortly before OpenAI's DevDay, as a calculated marketing move lacking transparent performance data. He suggested that such selective disclosure, merely stating the model "underperformed" on metrics like "deception" and "scope [[concepts/authorization|authorization]]" without evidence, aimed to generate buzz rather than truly inform. Chris Hay offered a counter-perspective, arguing that given OpenAI's recent regulatory scrutiny, publicly halting a model that failed safety requirements was a necessary and responsible decision, especially with the recent release of other models like "Sole." This segment underscored the tension between rapid [[concepts/innovation|innovation]], public perception, and [[concepts/ethical-considerations|ethical AI development]].

The conversation then shifted to [[entities/anthropic-institute|Anthropic]]'s release of its mid-tier Sonnet 5.5 model, which surprisingly demonstrated performance comparable to, or even exceeding, higher-tier models on certain tasks. Chris emphasized that different models serve different purposes; while top-tier models like [[concepts/opus|Opus]] are powerful, they are expensive. Sonnet 5.5 emerges as a cost-effective "workhorse" for everyday tasks that don't require maximum intelligence, thus preventing excessive "token burn" and associated costs. Madison Gooch elaborated on this from a business standpoint, noting that developers often have a bias towards using the largest, most expensive models. She highlighted IBM's watsonx platform, which, through an [[concepts/full-stack-applications|agentic IDE]] like "Bob," aims to intelligently select the optimal model based on an understanding of intent, context, and a balance of price and performance, thereby optimizing value for enterprises.

Finally, the panel discussed Meta Muse, a consumer-focused [[concepts/ai-agent|AI agent]] that recently topped app charts due to its seamless, low-[[concepts/friction|friction]] [[concepts/user-experience-design|user experience]]. Ash praised its [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]] and utility for boosting personal [[concepts/productivity|productivity]]. Chris saw Meta Muse as a harbinger of the future of agentic AI, where AI assistants perform tasks autonomously on users' behalf, similar to OpenAI's "[[concepts/dots-agent|dots]]." Madison concluded that this consumer-driven, frictionless agent experience [[entities/will|will]] inevitably shape expectations within the enterprise. She stressed the critical need for robust [[concepts/trusted-frameworks|AI governance frameworks]] to manage token consumption, measure value, ensure security, and maintain [[concepts/compliance|compliance]], rather than resisting these emerging tools. Embracing agentic AI with proper governance will be key for businesses to unlock its full potential while mitigating risks.
