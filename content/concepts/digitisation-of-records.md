# Digitisation Of Records

Digitisation of records is the systematic conversion of physical documents, papers, photographs, and other archival materials into digital formats. This process involves scanning, imaging, or otherwise capturing the content of paper-based and analog materials to create electronic versions that can be stored, accessed, and preserved in digital systems. Organizations undertake digitisation projects to improve [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]], reduce physical [[concepts/storage-requirements|storage requirements]], and enhance long-term [[concepts/preservation|preservation]] of important documents.

## Implementation and Process

The digitisation workflow typically includes several stages: assessment of materials to be converted, [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] of physical documents, scanning or imaging using appropriate technology, [[concepts/quality-assurance|quality assurance]] of digital files, and [[concepts/organization|organization]] within a database or content management system. Institutions must decide on appropriate file formats, resolution [[concepts/open-standards|standards]], and [[concepts/metadata|metadata]] requirements based on the [[entities/nature|nature]] of the materials and intended use. Proper planning ensures [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] across large-scale projects and maintains the [[concepts/integrity|integrity]] of the original information.

## Benefits and Considerations

Digitised records enable broader access to archival materials, allowing multiple users to view documents simultaneously without risk to fragile originals. Digital formats facilitate searchability, cross-referencing, and integration with other information systems. However, successful digitisation requires ongoing [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to [[concepts/digital-preservation|digital preservation]], as file formats and [[entities/storage|storage]] technologies become obsolete. Organizations must balance the upfront costs and technical expertise required against the long-term advantages of accessibility and reduced deterioration of physical materials.
