# Alan Turing

**Alan Mathison Turing** (23 June 1912 – 7 June 1954) was an English mathematician, computer scientist, logician, cryptanalyst, philosopher, and theoretical biologist. He is widely considered to be the father of computer science and [[concepts/artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]].

## Key Contributions

*   **Turing Machine:** Proposed the concept of a universal computing machine in his 1936 paper *"On Computable Numbers"*, providing a formalization of the concepts of algorithm and [[concepts/computation]].
*   **Turing Test:** Proposed the Turing Test in 1950 as a criterion of machine intelligence.
*   **Codebreaking:** Played a pivotal role in cracking the Enigma cipher during World War II at Bletchley Park.
*   **Artificial Intelligence:** Early advocate for the possibility of machine thinking and the development of [[concepts/neural-networks|neural networks]].

## Universal Machine & Early Computing

Turing's theoretical work on the Universal Turing Machine laid the groundwork for modern stored-program computers. His insights bridged the gap between abstract mathematical logic and physical computing hardware.

*   **Conceptual Foundation:** The idea that a single machine could simulate any other Turing machine, effectively making it a general-purpose computer.
*   **Early Applications:** His work directly influenced the design of the Automatic Computing Engine (ACE) and the Colossus computers used for codebreaking.
