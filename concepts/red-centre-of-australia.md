# Red Centre Of Australia

The Red Centre of Australia encompasses the arid interior regions of central Australia, primarily located in the Northern Territory around Alice Springs and Uluru. This vast region covers approximately 643,500 square kilometers and represents one of Australia's most iconic and geologically significant areas. The distinctive red coloration of the landscape's rocks and soil results from iron oxide, commonly known as rust, which formed through the oxidation of iron-rich minerals over geological time.

The geological formation of the Red Centre is linked to a high oxygen period that coincided with the creation of coal and oil. During this era, the oxidation process was particularly active, leading to the extensive deposition of iron oxides that characterize the region's current appearance. This chemical transformation of iron-rich minerals established the foundational geology that defines the arid interior today.
