# Telephoto Lens

A telephoto lens is a camera lens with an extended focal length, typically defined as 70mm or greater, designed to magnify distant subjects and bring them closer within the photographic frame. This optical characteristic allows photographers to capture fine details of faraway objects without physically moving closer to the subject. The magnification effect is directly proportional to the focal length; for instance, a 200mm lens provides approximately twice the magnification of a 100mm lens. These lenses are distinguished by their narrow field of view and the resulting compression of perspective, which flattens the apparent distance between foreground and background elements.

## Optical Design and Characteristics

Telephoto lenses utilize a specific optical design that allows the physical length of the lens to be shorter than its focal length. This compact construction makes them more portable than simple long-focus lenses of equivalent power. The narrow angle of view inherent to telephoto optics isolates subjects from their surroundings, making them ideal for wildlife, sports, and portrait photography where background separation is desired. However, this narrow field of view also makes framing more challenging and increases the visibility of camera shake, often necessitating the use of image stabilization systems or faster shutter speeds.

## Common Applications

Due to their ability to compress space and magnify distant details, telephoto lenses are essential tools in several photographic genres. In wildlife photography, they allow for capturing animals from a safe and non-intrusive distance. In sports photography, they enable the framing of action from the sidelines or press boxes. Portrait photographers often use focal lengths between 85mm and 135mm to achieve flattering perspective and smooth background blur, while landscape photographers may use longer telephotos to isolate specific details within a broader scene.
