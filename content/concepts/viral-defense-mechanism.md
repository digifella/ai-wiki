# Viral Defense Mechanism

Viral Defense Mechanism is a [[concepts/gene-editing|gene-editing]] technology developed by [[entities/feng-zhang|Dr. Feng Zhang]] [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] the [[entities/broad-institute|Broad Institute]]. It represents a proposed advancement in the field of genetic engineering, positioning itself as a successor to [[concepts/crispr|CRISPR]]-based approaches. The technology draws inspiration from viral defense systems found in [[entities/nature|nature]], leveraging biological mechanisms that organisms use to protect themselves against viral infection.

## Development and Context

The technology emerged from research into naturally occurring defense systems that cells employ against viral threats. By studying these biological processes, Zhang's team sought to develop a gene-editing platform that could offer improvements over existing CRISPR methodologies, potentially addressing limitations in precision, efficiency, or applicability to different genetic targets.
