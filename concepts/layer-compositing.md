# Layer Compositing

Layer compositing is a fundamental technique in digital image editing that involves combining multiple image layers to create a final composite image. This process typically includes arranging layers in a specific order, adjusting their opacity, and applying blending modes to control how pixels from different layers interact. The core advantage of layer-based compositing is its non-destructive workflow, allowing editors to preserve original image data while experimenting with various visual configurations without permanently altering the source files.

## Color and Tonal Integration

A key aspect of advanced compositing is achieving visual cohesion through color and tonal adjustments. Tools such as Photoshop's Harmonize feature facilitate this by automatically analyzing the color histograms of selected layers and adjusting them to match a target layer. This process ensures that disparate elements share a consistent lighting environment and color palette, reducing the artificial appearance often associated with digital manipulation.

## Workflow and Application

The technique relies on the strategic use of adjustment layers, masks, and blending modes to refine the integration of elements. By isolating specific areas for correction and applying global tonal shifts, editors can simulate realistic lighting conditions and atmospheric perspective. This method is widely used in photography, graphic design, and visual effects to create seamless images that appear as if they were captured in a single exposure.
