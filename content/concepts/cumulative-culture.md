# Cumulative Culture

The process by which information, [[concepts/skills|skills]], or behaviors are transmitted across generations, enabling the accumulation of knowledge and the development of increasingly complex social strategies, tools, or survival mechanisms.

## Key Mechanisms
- **Social [[concepts/learning|Learning]]**: The primary vehicle for transmitting learned behaviors within a population.
- **The Ratchet Effect**: The [[concepts/preventive-care|prevention]] of information loss, ensuring that each generation builds upon the successes of the previous one.

## Recent Findings
- **[[concepts/bumblebees|Bumblebees]]**: Recent research reveals advanced [[concepts/cognition|Cognition]] in these insects, challenging long-held biological assumptions regarding brain size and complexity. [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wPy-8gOUl-A)
  - [[concepts/bumblebees|Bumblebees]] exhibit sophisticated [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] skills.
  - They demonstrate the ability to learn from and build upon previous successes, indicating cumulative culture.
  - These cognitive abilities contribute to Flood [[concepts/resilience|Resilience]] and other survival-critical behaviors.
- **Adaptive Complexity**: Evidence suggests these cognitive abilities may enhance survival strategies and social learning.

### New Findings
- Bumblebees exhibit advanced cognition, challenging long-held biological assumptions about brain size and complexity. Despite their small brains, they show sophisticated [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] skills and the ability to build upon previous successes.
- These findings suggest that bumblebees can develop cumulative culture, enhancing their survival strategies and [[concepts/resilience|resilience]].
