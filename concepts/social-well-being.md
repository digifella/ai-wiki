# Social Well Being

Social well-being refers to the conditions and factors within societies that enable people to maintain and improve their health. It encompasses the social determinants of health, including income, education, employment, housing, food security, and access to healthcare. These elements shape both individual and community health outcomes by influencing the opportunities and resources available to individuals.

Rather than focusing solely on individual behaviors or medical interventions, social well-being recognizes that population health is fundamentally influenced by the social, economic, and environmental contexts in which people live. This perspective shifts the focus from treating illness to addressing the root causes of health disparities through systemic change and policy development.

The concept acknowledges that health is not merely the absence of disease but a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being. Consequently, improving social well-being requires coordinated efforts across multiple sectors, including public health, urban planning, and social services, to create equitable environments that support healthy living for all members of society.
