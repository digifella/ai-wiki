# Cybersecurity Principles

Core tenets for protecting [[concepts/knowledge-management|information systems]] against evolving threats, including those driven by [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]].

## Key Principles

- **Defense in Depth**: Layered [[concepts/security|security]] controls to prevent single points of failure.
- **Least Privilege**: Restricting user and system access to only what is necessary.
- **[[concepts/zero-trust|Zero Trust]]**: Never trust, always verify; assume breach and validate explicitly.
- **[[concepts/resilience|Resilience]]**: Ability to recover quickly from incidents.
- **Adaptive Security**: [[concepts/continuous-monitoring|Continuous monitoring]] and response to emerging threats.

## Emerging Threat Landscape: AI-Powered Attacks

The integration of AI into offensive [[concepts/cybersecurity|cybersecurity]] operations has introduced new vectors that challenge traditional defense-in-depth models.

- **[[concepts/llm-hacking|LLM Hacking]]**: Attackers exploit vulnerabilities in [[concepts/demystifying-llms|Large Language Models]], such as prompt injection or [[concepts/data-leakage|data leakage]], to manipulate outputs or access sensitive data [[lab-notes/2026-10-01-AI-Powered-Cyberattacks-Dark-Sourcery-LLM-Hacking-and-Ag|AI-Powered Cyberattacks: Dark Sourcery, LLM Hacking, and Agent Swarms]].
- **[[concepts/cybersecurity-threats|Dark Sourcery]]**: Use of AI to automate and scale sophisticated phishing and social [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] campaigns, making them harder to detect.
- **[[concepts/agent-collaboration|Agent Swarms]]**: Coordinated [[concepts/ai-agents|AI agents]] performing distributed attacks, increasing scalability and reducing the need for human intervention.
- **Trust Erosion**: Growing concern over the [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] of AI-driven security tools and chatbots, which may be compromised or manipulated.
