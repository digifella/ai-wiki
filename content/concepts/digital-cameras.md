# Digital Cameras

Digital cameras are electronic devices that capture and store [[concepts/images|images]] in digital format, replacing traditional film-based photography. They use image sensors to convert [[concepts/light|light]] into electrical signals, which are then processed and saved as digital [[concepts/files|files]]. Digital cameras [[concepts/range|range]] from smartphone cameras to professional DSLRs and mirrorless systems, each offering different levels of [[concepts/power|control]], image quality, and functionality.

## Film Scanning at Home

For photographers working with analog film, home scanning offers an accessible way to digitize physical negatives and [[entities/google-slides|slides]] without relying on commercial scanning services. The process involves using a dedicated film scanner or a camera with macro [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] to capture high-quality digital versions of film originals. Home scanning can be cost-effective for those with substantial film archives and provides control over the final digital image quality, [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|color grading]], and resolution.

Basic [[concepts/camera-scanning|home film scanning]] typically requires a light source, film holder, and either a dedicated scanner or camera capable of [[concepts/macro-photography|macro photography]]. The choice of equipment depends on the volume of film to be scanned, desired [[concepts/output|output]] resolution, and budget. Proper technique—including correct lighting, focus, and [[concepts/exposure-settings|exposure settings]]—is essential for achieving good results that preserve the detail and character of the original film.
