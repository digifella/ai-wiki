# Lightroom Foundations

Core principles and workflows for [[entities/adobe-lightroom|Adobe Lightroom]], focusing on [[concepts/exposure|exposure control]], tonal balance, and color [[concepts/science|science]]. This concept serves as the entry point for understanding [[concepts/non-destructive-editing|non-destructive editing]] pipelines, particularly regarding how global [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] interact with local tools.

## Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/non-linear-editing|Non-Destructive Editing]]**: All adjustments are [[concepts/metadata|metadata]] stored in sidecar [[concepts/files|files]] or catalogs, preserving original pixel data.
- **Tonal [[concepts/range|Range]]**: Managing [[concepts/highlights|highlights]], [[concepts/shadows|shadows]], whites, and [[concepts/blacks|blacks]] to establish [[concepts/dynamic-range|dynamic range]] before color correction.
- **White Balance**: The foundational step in [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|color grading]]; neutralizing [[concepts/color-casts|color casts]] ensures subsequent hue/saturation adjustments behave predictably.

## Color Grading & Cinematic Techniques
Color dramatically influences photo emotion and aesthetic. Proper grading relies on a solid foundation of exposure and white balance.

- **Source**: [[lab-notes/2026-05-23-Lightroom-Color-Grading-Foundations-Tools-and-Cinematic|Lightroom Color Grading: Foundations, Tools, and Cinematic Techniques]]
- **Core [[concepts/philosophy|Philosophy]]**: Achieving natural and cinematic looks requires understanding how color tools interact with tonal [[concepts/distribution|distribution]].
- **[[concepts/workflow|Workflow]] Priority**: Establish correct exposure and white balance before applying HSL or Color Grading Mixer adjustments to avoid shifting neutral tones unexpectedly.
- **Tools**: Utilize the Color Grading panel for targeted [[concepts/hue|hue]] shifting in [[concepts/whites|highlights]], midtones, and shadows to create depth and mood.
