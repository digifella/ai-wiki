# Flatbed Scanner

A flatbed scanner is a device that converts physical film negatives and [[entities/google-slides|slides]] into digital [[concepts/images|images]] by capturing light reflected from or transmitted through the film. Unlike dedicated film scanners, flatbed scanners are multi-[[concepts/motivation|purpose]] machines designed primarily for documents and prints, but many models can accommodate film with appropriate adapters or holders. This makes them an accessible option for photographers who want to digitize their film collection without investing in specialized equipment.

## Scanning Film at Home

Scanning film at home using a flatbed scanner offers several practical advantages. The process requires minimal additional equipment beyond the scanner itself, making it cost-effective compared to professional lab scanning services. Home scanning also provides greater creative control over image [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] during the scanning process, allowing you to optimize results for individual frames rather than relying on batch processing.

The general workflow involves securing the film in a holder or carrier designed for your scanner model, positioning it on the scanner glass or film holder, and using scanning [[concepts/software|software]] to capture the image. Settings such as resolution, color profile, and [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] can typically be adjusted to suit the specific film type and desired output quality. Resolution of 2400 DPI or higher is generally recommended for capturing adequate detail from film originals.
