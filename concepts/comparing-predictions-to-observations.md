# Comparing Predictions To Observations

Comparing predictions to observations is the third and critical step in [[concepts/feynman|Richard Feynman]]'s formulation of the [[concepts/scientific-method|scientific method]]. After making an initial guess about how nature works and computing the logical or mathematical consequences of that guess, a scientist must test whether those predictions match what actually occurs in the natural [[entities/earth|world]]. This comparison serves as the empirical check that determines whether a hypothesis has merit or must be rejected or revised.

## The Validation Process

The comparison is inherently binary in its outcome: either predictions align with observations, or they do not. If observations contradict predictions, the hypothesis fails and must be abandoned or modified. If observations consistently match predictions across multiple independent tests, the hypothesis gains credibility as a valid description of natural phenomena. This [[concepts/software-sprint|iterative cycle]]—guess, [[concepts/compute|compute]], compare—forms the backbone of experimental [[concepts/science|science]] and distinguishes scientific claims from mere speculation.

## Practical Importance

The strength of this approach lies in its objectivity. A hypothesis cannot be considered scientifically valid based on theoretical elegance, intuition, or authority alone; it must ultimately [[concepts/solution|answer]] to nature itself. By grounding scientific validation in observable reality rather than subjective [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]], this method provides a mechanism for correcting human error and exposing flawed assumptions. This is why precise measurement and careful [[concepts/experimental-design|experimental design]] are essential components of this final validation step.
