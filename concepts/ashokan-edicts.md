# Ashokan Edicts

The Ashokan Edicts are a series of inscriptions commissioned by [[concepts/emperor-ashoka|Emperor Ashoka]] of the [[concepts/mauryan-empire|Mauryan Empire]] during the 3rd century BCE. Carved into stone pillars, rocks, and cave walls across the Indian subcontinent, these texts represent some of the earliest surviving written records of Indian history. They provide direct evidence of Ashoka’s reign, his philosophical outlook, and his administrative [[concepts/policies|policies]] following the [[concepts/kalinga-war|Kalinga War]].

The edicts were composed in multiple languages and scripts, primarily Prakrit using the Brahmi and Kharosthi scripts, with a few in Greek and Aramaic. They outline Ashoka’s conversion to [[concepts/buddhism|Buddhism]] and his promotion of *Dhamma* (Dharma), a code of ethical conduct emphasizing non-violence, tolerance, and social welfare. The inscriptions also detail his efforts to improve [[concepts/infrastructure|infrastructure]], such as the construction of roads, wells, and hospitals, reflecting his commitment to public service.

These artifacts serve as crucial historical sources for understanding the political and cultural landscape of [[concepts/ancient-india|ancient India]]. They illustrate the extent of the Mauryan Empire’s reach and the emperor’s attempt to unify his diverse subjects through moral and administrative reforms. The edicts remain significant for their linguistic value and their insight into the early spread of Buddhist ideals.
