# Atmospheric Circulation Model

An atmospheric circulation model is a mathematical and computational representation of how air moves across large [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] in Earth's atmosphere. These [[concepts/models|models]] simulate wind patterns, pressure systems, and heat [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] driven by solar radiation and the Coriolis effect from planetary rotation. They form the foundation of weather prediction, climate research, and understanding persistent global patterns such as trade winds, jet streams, and monsoon systems.

## Physical Foundation

Atmospheric circulation models are based on fundamental principles of [[concepts/fluid-dynamics|fluid dynamics]] and thermodynamics. Solar energy heats the equatorial region more intensely than the poles, creating pressure gradients that [[concepts/ambition|drive]] air [[concepts/exercise|movement]]. The Earth's rotation deflects moving air masses, organizing global wind patterns into distinct cells and bands. Models must account for variables including temperature, pressure, humidity, and the interactions between the atmosphere and underlying ocean and land surfaces.

## Applications and Development

These models operate across multiple scales, from regional weather systems to planetary climate patterns. They are essential tools for [[concepts/weather-forecasting|meteorological forecasting]], climate projection, and investigating how atmospheric circulation responds to changes in solar activity or atmospheric composition. [[concepts/empirical-evidence|Observational data]] from satellites, weather stations, and field experiments—such as the [[concepts/controlled-climate-experiment|Biosphere 2 experiment]], which provided controlled atmospheric measurements—help refine model [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]] and validate their predictions. Modern atmospheric circulation models continue to improve through increased computational [[concepts/power|power]] and better incorporation of [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] processes.
