# Adaptive Reuse

Adaptive reuse is the practice of converting existing buildings or structures to new purposes while retaining their original architectural and historical character. Rather than demolishing outdated or underutilized structures, adaptive reuse preserves built [[concepts/cultural-heritage|heritage]] and directs resources toward maintaining culturally significant spaces. This approach has become an important strategy in urban conservation, allowing cities to retain their architectural identity while meeting contemporary functional needs.

## Environmental and Economic Benefits

Adaptive reuse projects reduce waste by keeping existing structures in use rather than sending them to demolition sites. This practice also requires less energy and raw materials compared to new construction, as the building's foundation, framework, and many structural elements remain intact. Economically, adaptive reuse can be cost-effective when existing buildings are structurally sound, as developers may avoid some expenses associated with new construction while potentially accessing tax incentives and grants dedicated to heritage [[concepts/preservation|preservation]].

## Urban and Cultural Impact

By repurposing vacant or abandoned buildings, adaptive reuse can revitalize underutilized areas and contribute to urban renewal. Historic structures often carry cultural significance and community [[concepts/memory|memory]], and preserving them through adaptive reuse maintains a visible [[concepts/connection|connection]] to a city's past. This approach allows neighborhoods to evolve functionally while retaining their distinctive character, supporting both heritage conservation and contemporary urban life.
