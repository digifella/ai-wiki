# Gandhi

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (1869–1948) was an Indian political and spiritual leader who pioneered nonviolent resistance as a method for achieving independence and social reform. Born in Gujarat, he studied law in [[entities/united-kingdom|England]] and practiced as a barrister in [[entities/south-africa|South Africa]] from 1893 to 1915. During his two decades in South Africa, Gandhi developed *satyagraha* (literally "truth-force"), a [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]] of nonviolent civil disobedience formulated in response to racial discrimination against Indian immigrants.

## Campaign for Indian Independence

Returning to [[entities/india|India]] in 1915, Gandhi became the leading figure in the Indian National Congress and the Indian independence [[concepts/exercise|movement]] against British rule. He organized mass campaigns of non-cooperation and civil disobedience, including the 1930 Salt March, to challenge colonial authority. His strategies mobilized millions of Indians across caste and religious lines, significantly weakening British political control and accelerating the path to India's independence in 1947.

## Legacy and Assassination

Gandhi was assassinated in January 1948 by Nathuram Godse, a Hindu nationalist who opposed Gandhi's tolerance of Pakistan and his interfaith efforts. Despite his death, his principles of *[[concepts/non-violence-policy|ahimsa]]* (nonviolence) and *satyagraha* influenced subsequent civil rights movements worldwide, including those led by [[entities/martin-luther-king-jr|Martin Luther King Jr]]. and Nelson Mandela. He remains a central figure in modern political thought and is revered in India as the "Father of the Nation."
