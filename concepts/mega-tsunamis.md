# Mega Tsunamis

Mega tsunamis are exceptionally large tsunami events characterized by wave heights exceeding 100 meters. Unlike conventional tsunamis, which typically result from seafloor earthquakes and produce waves of 10–30 meters, mega tsunamis are driven by sudden, massive displacements of water caused by extreme geological phenomena. These rare occurrences represent some of the most powerful water movements in Earth's systems.

## Generation Mechanisms

The primary generation mechanism for mega tsunamis is the catastrophic collapse of volcanic flanks or massive landslides into the ocean. This process involves the rapid displacement of large volumes of rock and debris, which transfers significant kinetic energy to the water column. A notable historical example is the 1888 Ritter Island eruption in Papua New Guinea, where a volcanic flank collapse generated a wave estimated to be 100 meters tall. Such events differ fundamentally from tectonic tsunamis in their source dynamics and the localized, yet devastating, nature of the resulting wave energy.
