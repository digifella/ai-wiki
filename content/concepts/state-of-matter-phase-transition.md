# State Of Matter Phase Transition

A state of matter [[concepts/phase|phase]] transition is a transformation between different physical states of matter—such as solid, liquid, gas, or plasma—that occurs when conditions like temperature or pressure change sufficiently. During these transitions, the properties of matter undergo discontinuous changes, including alterations to density, crystal [[concepts/structure|structure]], and atomic arrangement.

## Inner Core Observations

Recent [[concepts/seismic-data|seismic data]] from [[concepts/inner-core|Earth's inner core]] has revealed unexpected wave propagation patterns that do not align with predictions based on conventional solid iron behavior. These anomalies—including unusual anisotropy and wave velocity variations—suggest that iron under the extreme pressures and temperatures of the inner core may exist in a previously unknown state of matter, distinct from ordinary solid, liquid, or known exotic states.

## Significance

Understanding phase transitions under extreme conditions is central to geophysics and material [[concepts/science|science]]. If a new state of matter exists within Earth's core, it would require revision of current [[concepts/models|models]] of planetary structure and composition, while also informing [[concepts/theoretical-physics|theoretical physics]] about how matter behaves under conditions rarely achieved in laboratories.
