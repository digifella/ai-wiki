# History Of Science And Mathematics

The development of [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] and [[concepts/science|science]] represents humanity's systematic effort to understand both the abstract and physical worlds. Mathematics emerged as a formal discipline in [[concepts/urban-planning|ancient civilizations]], with evidence of mathematical notation and calculation appearing in Mesopotamia around 3000 BCE and in Egypt shortly thereafter. Early mathematics was fundamentally practical, addressing practical needs in [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]], trade, and construction. The ancient Greeks, particularly from the 6th century BCE onward, shifted mathematics toward abstract [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] and logical [[concepts/proof|proof]], establishing geometry as a rigorous discipline through figures like Euclid and Pythagoras.

## Classical and Medieval Development

The Islamic Golden Age (8th-14th centuries) preserved and extended Greek mathematical knowledge while introducing algebra and advancing trigonometry and [[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]]. Meanwhile, European medieval scholarship gradually recovered classical texts through translation, particularly after [[entities/contact|contact]] with Islamic centers of [[concepts/learning|learning]]. Mathematics during this period remained largely separate from empirical science, though both disciplines benefited from shared logical frameworks.

## The Scientific Revolution

The 16th and 17th centuries witnessed a fundamental transformation as mathematics became the language of [[concepts/scientific-method|scientific inquiry]]. Figures like [[concepts/galileo|Galileo]], Kepler, and Newton demonstrated that mathematical descriptions could predict physical phenomena with remarkable accuracy. Newton's development of calculus and laws of motion exemplified this integration, establishing mathematics as essential to [[concepts/scientific-advancement|scientific progress]] rather than a separate intellectual [[entities/pursuitunimelbeduau|pursuit]].

## Modern Era

From the 18th century onward, mathematics and science have evolved in increasingly sophisticated directions. New mathematical fields emerged to address scientific challenges, while experimental methods became more rigorous and quantitative. The 20th century saw revolutionary developments in both domains—from [[concepts/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]] and relativity in [[concepts/physics|physics]] to abstract algebra and topology in mathematics—demonstrating the continuing interdependence of mathematical [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] and [[concepts/scientific-discovery|scientific discovery]].
