# Infinite Universe

The hypothesis that the universe extends without bound in space and/or time. This concept is central to several cosmological models and paradoxes regarding the nature of light, matter, and the observable horizon.

## Key Implications & Paradoxes

### Olbers' Paradox
A fundamental contradiction arising from the assumption of an infinite, static, and eternal universe.
- **The Paradox:** In an infinite universe uniformly filled with [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]], every [[concepts/line-of-sight|line of sight]] should eventually intersect a star, making the [[concepts/night-sky|night sky]] as bright as the surface of the sun.
- **[[concepts/solution|Resolution]]:** The night sky is dark because the universe has a finite age (~13.8 billion years) and is expanding. Light from distant stars has not had time to reach us, and [[concepts/galaxy-redshift|cosmological redshift]] reduces the energy of [[concepts/light|photons]] from distant sources.
- **Related Note:** [[lab-notes/2026-08-10-Olbers-Paradox-Why-the-Night-Sky-is-Dark|Olbers' Paradox: Why the Night Sky is Dark]]

### Observable Universe
- Due to the [[concepts/finite-speed-of-light|finite speed of light]] and the [[concepts/universe-age|age of the universe]], we can only observe a spherical region centered on [[entities/earth|Earth]].
- The boundary is defined by the **particle horizon**.
- An infinite universe may contain regions forever causally disconnected from us.

### Cosmological Principle
- Assumes the universe is **homogeneous** (same everywhere) and **isotropic** (same in all directions) on large [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]].
- Supports the idea that local observations can be extrapolated to the global structure.
