# Electricity Grid

An electricity grid is an interconnected network of power generation facilities, transmission lines, and [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] systems that work together to supply electrical energy to consumers across a geographic region. The grid operates as a complex system balancing real-time [[concepts/electricity-supply-and-demand|electricity supply and demand]], with power flowing from generation sources through high-voltage transmission networks and finally through lower-voltage distribution systems to end users including homes, businesses, and industries.

## Generation and Transmission

Power generation occurs at diverse facilities including coal plants, natural gas generators, hydroelectric dams, nuclear reactors, and renewable sources like wind and solar farms. Generated electricity is stepped up to high voltages for efficient long-distance transmission across regional transmission networks. These interconnected transmission systems allow power to be routed flexibly across service areas and between regions, enabling [[concepts/load-balancing|load balancing]] and reducing localized shortages.

## Distribution and Reliability

The distribution phase steps voltage down to safer levels suitable for local delivery through neighborhood substations and power lines to individual consumers. Grid reliability depends on maintaining consistent frequency and voltage, which requires continuous balancing of generation and consumption. Modern grids face challenges from aging infrastructure, variable renewable energy sources, [[concepts/extreme-weather-events|extreme weather events]], and growing peak demand periods. Regional grid operators monitor system conditions and coordinate generation schedules to prevent outages and maintain service [[concepts/continuity|continuity]].
