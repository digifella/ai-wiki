# Island nation

An **island nation** is a sovereign state whose primary territory consists of one or more islands, completely surrounded by water. These states are often distinct from mainland nations due to geographic [[concepts/disconnection|isolation]], which influences their [[concepts/economic-system|economy]], culture, and political stability.

## Characteristics
*   **Geographic Isolation**: Lack of land borders with other sovereign states; reliance on maritime boundaries and exclusive economic zones (EEZs).
*   **Economic [[concepts/vulnerability|Vulnerability]]**: Often dependent on imports for essential goods (food, fuel) due to limited arable land or industrial capacity. Susceptibility to global supply chain disruptions is high.
*   **Environmental Sensitivity**: High [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] to [[concepts/climate-change]] impacts such as [[concepts/sea-level-rise|sea-level rise]], coral bleaching, and [[concepts/extreme-weather-events|extreme weather events]].
*   **Small Island Developing States (SIDS)**: A subset of island nations designated by the UN, characterized by small population, geographical remoteness, and limited economic resources.

## Key Examples & Case Studies
*   [[entities/japan]]: Large island nation with significant industrial capacity; archipelagic structure.
*   [[entities/united-kingdom]]: Island nation with historical colonial ties; complex relationship with neighboring mainland [[entities/europe|Europe]] (e.g., [[entities/european-union]]).
*   Madagascar: Largest island nation; high [[concepts/biodiversity|biodiversity]] but significant poverty and infrastructure challenges.
*   [[entities/sri-lanka]]: Southern Asian island nation heavily reliant on [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]] (specifically tea, rubber, and coconuts) and tourism. Recent policy decisions have highlighted the risks of abrupt agricultural shifts in small economies.
