# Madau Plot

The **Madau Plot** is a graphical representation of the cosmic star formation rate [[concepts/density|density]] (SFRD) as a function of redshift ($z$). It illustrates how the rate at which [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]] are formed in the universe has evolved over [[concepts/cosmic-time|cosmic time]], serving as a critical tool for understanding [[concepts/galaxy-formation-and-evolution|galaxy evolution]] and chemical enrichment.

## Key Characteristics
- **Peak Activity:** The plot reveals that star formation was not constant but peaked around $z \approx 2$, a period known as **[[concepts/cosmic-noon|Cosmic Noon]]**.
- **Temporal Evolution:**
	- Post-[[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]]: A rise in star formation during the epoch of reionization.
	- Peak: Maximum efficiency in converting baryons into stars.
	- Decline: A steady decrease in SFRD from $z \approx 2$ to the present day ($z=0$), often attributed to fuel depletion and [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]].

## Integrated Insights
- Derived from observations including data from the Hubble Space Telescope.
- Essential for calculating the total mass of stellar material produced since the Big Bang and understanding the metallicity evolution of the universe.
