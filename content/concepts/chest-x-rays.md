# Chest X Rays

Chest X-[[concepts/rays|rays]] are radiographic [[concepts/images|images]] of the thorax used to diagnose and monitor a wide [[concepts/range|range]] of pulmonary, cardiac, and mediastinal conditions. They remain one of the most commonly ordered imaging studies in clinical practice due to their [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]], low [[concepts/cost|cost]], and minimal radiation [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] compared to other imaging modalities. A standard chest X-ray examination typically includes posteroanterior (PA) and lateral views, though portable anteroposterior (AP) views are often obtained for hospitalized patients.

## Clinical Applications

Chest X-rays are employed to evaluate suspected pneumonia, tuberculosis, pneumothorax, pleural effusion, pulmonary edema, and malignancy. They also provide useful information about cardiac silhouette size and contour, mediastinal structures, and bone density. In clinical settings, chest radiographs serve as an initial screening tool before more advanced imaging such as CT or MRI is considered.

## AI and Image Analysis

Recent advances in machine [[concepts/learning|learning]] have enabled automated and semi-automated analysis of chest X-rays. [[concepts/models|Models]] like [[concepts/dermatology-images|MedGemma 27B]] demonstrate how large [[concepts/multimodal-language-models|multimodal language models]] can process both medical images and associated [[concepts/text|text]] data to support radiological interpretation and [[concepts/clinical-reasoning|clinical decision-making]]. These systems can assist in identifying abnormalities, flagging critical findings, and summarizing relevant clinical features, though they remain tools that complement rather than replace expert radiologist review.
