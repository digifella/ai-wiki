# Native Flora

**Native flora** refers to plant species that naturally occur in a specific region without human introduction. In ecological contexts, particularly in New Zealand, the term often contrasts with [[concepts/invasive-species]] and is central to Rewilding and [[concepts/ecosystem-restoration]] efforts.

## Key Concepts
- **Ecological [[concepts/honesty|Integrity]]**: The presence of native species maintains soil [[concepts/health|health]], water cycles, and habitat for Native Fauna.
- **[[concepts/preservation|Restoration]] Challenges**: Removing invasive species often leaves degraded land requiring active intervention to re-establish native cover.

## Case Studies & Applications

### Banks Peninsula Rewilding
A notable example of unconventional restoration involves the work of botanist [[entities/hugh-wilson|Hugh Wilson]] on [[concepts/banks-peninsula]].

- **Methodology**: Wilson utilized [[concepts/gorse]] (*Ulex europaeus*), typically classified as a noxious weed, as a nurse crop rather than immediately eradicating it.
- **Outcome**: The gorse provided structural cover and [[concepts/nitrogen-fixation|nitrogen fixation]], allowing native seedlings to establish beneath it before the gorse was managed or died back.
- **Significance**: This approach challenges conventional "eradicate-first" wisdom, demonstrating that invasive species can be leveraged as transitional tools in [[concepts/ecological-succession|Ecological Succession]].
