# Gandhi

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (1869–1948) was an Indian lawyer, political organizer, and spiritual leader who became the preeminent figure in India's struggle for independence from British colonial rule. Born in Gujarat, Gandhi trained as a barrister in England before practicing law in South Africa, where he first developed his [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]] of *satyagraha* (nonviolent resistance) in response to racial discrimination against Indian immigrants. This experience shaped his approach to political activism and informed his return to India in 1915.

## Political Leadership and Independence Movement

Gandhi emerged as a dominant figure in the Indian National Congress during the 1920s and led several major campaigns of civil disobedience against British rule, including the Salt March of 1930 and the Quit India [[concepts/exercise|Movement]] of 1942. His strategy of nonviolent protest—involving boycotts, strikes, and peaceful demonstrations—mobilized millions of Indians across class and religious lines. This approach proved instrumental in weakening British resolve to maintain colonial [[concepts/power|control]], ultimately contributing to Indian independence in 1947.

## Philosophy and Legacy

Gandhi's philosophy of *satyagraha* combined political resistance with spiritual principles rooted in Hindu, Buddhist, and Christian traditions. He advocated for self-sufficiency, communal [[concepts/musical-harmony|harmony]] between Hindu and Muslim communities, and the dignity of manual labor. Though he was assassinated in 1948 by a Hindu nationalist opposed to his communal [[concepts/computer-vision|vision]], Gandhi's methods of nonviolent resistance influenced subsequent social movements worldwide, including the Civil Rights Movement in the [[entities/united-states|United States]] and anti-apartheid activism in South Africa.
