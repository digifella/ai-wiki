[[concepts/apache-2-license|Apache 2.0]] is a permissive [[concepts/open-source|open-source software]] [[concepts/license|license]] maintained by the Apache Software Foundation. Released in 2004, it grants users broad freedoms to use, modify, and distribute software while establishing clear legal protections for both developers and users. The license has become one of the most widely adopted in the software industry, used by major projects and organizations across commercial and non-commercial contexts.

## Core Permissions and Conditions

The [[concepts/apache-2-0|Apache 2.0 license]] permits unrestricted use of software for any purpose, including commercial applications. Users may modify the source code and distribute it, either in original or modified form. The primary requirement is that users include a copy of the license, a notice of modifications, and a statement of any changes made to the original code. Unlike copyleft licenses such as GPL, [[concepts/apache-2.0-license|Apache 2.0]] does not require derivative works to be released under the same license.

## Patent Protection

A distinguishing feature of [[concepts/apache-license|Apache 2.0]] is its explicit grant of patent rights. Contributors provide a license to any patents they hold that are necessarily infringed by their contribution, offering [[concepts/secure|protection]] against patent claims. If a user initiates patent litigation against the licensor, their patent rights under the license terminate automatically. This provision addresses a significant legal concern in [[concepts/coding|software development]] and is one [[concepts/purpose|reason]] the license is favored by organizations contributing to collaborative projects.

## Practical Application

Apache 2.0 is used by numerous high-profile projects including Apache web server, Kubernetes, [[entities/android|Android]], and various [[concepts/machine-learning|machine learning]] frameworks. Its balance between permissiveness and legal [[concepts/clarity-slider|clarity]] makes it suitable for both individual developers and large enterprises seeking to share code while retaining some control over how their work is used and attributed.
