# Cardiovascular Health

Cardiovascular [[concepts/health|health]] refers to the functional state of the heart and blood vessels, encompassing the heart's ability to pump blood effectively and the circulatory system's capacity to deliver oxygen and nutrients throughout the body. This physiological condition is assessed through measurable [[concepts/indicators|indicators]] including blood [[concepts/pressure|pressure]], resting heart rate, cholesterol levels, and arterial elasticity. Good cardiovascular health reduces the risk of heart disease, stroke, and other circulatory conditions, which remain among the leading [[concepts/causes|causes]] of mortality globally.

## Assessment and Indicators

Clinical evaluation of cardiovascular status typically relies on standard biometric markers. Blood pressure measures the force of circulating blood against artery walls, while resting heart rate indicates the efficiency of the heart muscle. Lipid profiles, specifically low-[[concepts/density|density]] and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol levels, provide insight into plaque buildup risks. Additionally, arterial elasticity, or [[concepts/compliance|compliance]], reflects the vessels' ability to expand and contract with each heartbeat, serving as a key indicator of vascular aging and stiffness.

## Influence of Physical Activity

Regular [[concepts/exercise|physical activity]] is a primary determinant of cardiovascular function. Aerobic exercise strengthens the myocardium, improving stroke volume and lowering resting heart rate. It also enhances endothelial function, promoting better blood [[concepts/flow|flow]] [[concepts/regulation|regulation]] and reducing [[concepts/systemic-inflammation|systemic inflammation]]. Consistent engagement in moderate-to-vigorous physical activity helps maintain healthy blood pressure and lipid profiles, thereby mitigating the development of atherosclerosis and other degenerative circulatory diseases.
