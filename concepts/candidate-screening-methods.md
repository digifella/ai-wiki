# Candidate Screening Methods

Candidate screening methods constitute the systematic procedures organizations employ to evaluate and filter job applicants during the initial stages of recruitment. These techniques serve to reduce large applicant pools into manageable numbers of qualified candidates suitable for deeper assessment. By applying standardized criteria aligned with specific job requirements and organizational needs, companies can eliminate unsuitable applicants before engaging in more resource-intensive evaluation phases. This early filtering process significantly improves hiring efficiency and reduces overall recruitment costs.

Common screening [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] include resume and application reviews, where recruiters assess qualifications against predefined job descriptions. Automated applicant tracking systems often facilitate this process by using keyword matching and algorithmic scoring to prioritize candidates. Additionally, pre-employment assessments, such as [[concepts/skills|skills]] tests or personality inventories, provide [[concepts/purpose|objective]] data to supplement traditional document reviews. These tools help identify candidates who possess the necessary technical competencies and cultural fit before they reach [[entities/the-interview|the interview]] stage.

The effectiveness of screening methods relies heavily on the validity and [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] of the chosen criteria. Organizations must ensure that screening tools are legally compliant and free from bias to maintain fairness and diversity in hiring. As recruitment processes evolve, the integration of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] and [[entities/big-data|data analytics]] has expanded the scope of automated screening, allowing for more nuanced evaluation of candidate potential. However, human oversight remains critical to interpret context and prevent the exclusion of qualified applicants due to rigid algorithmic filters.
