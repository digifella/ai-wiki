# Synthetic Biology

**Synthetic Biology** refers to the interdisciplinary field combining biology and [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] to design and construct novel biological parts, devices, and systems, or to redesign existing natural biological systems for useful purposes. This domain spans from speculative [[concepts/plant-animal-hybridization|plant-animal hybridization]] to advanced [[concepts/digital-gene-technology|synthetic biology]] applications aiming to bridge metabolic divides and create autonomous artificial life forms.

## Key Developments

### 2026 Breakthrough: Mammalian Photosynthesis
Recent research has moved beyond theoretical models to practical medical applications, specifically targeting the introduction of photosynthetic capabilities into [[concepts/mammalian-cells|mammalian cells]].

- **Core [[concepts/innovation|Innovation]]**: Successful integration of photosynthetic pathways into animal and human cells, effectively bridging the traditional separation between plant and animal kingdoms.
- **Application**: Utilization of this hybrid [[concepts/yeast-fermentation|metabolism]] to enhance [[concepts/energy-efficiency|energy efficiency]] in mammalian tissues.

### 2026 Breakthrough: Bottom-Up Artificial Cells
Parallel to top-down genetic engineering, significant progress has been made in bottom-up synthetic biology, culminating in the creation of fully functional artificial cells.

- **Spud Cell**: A landmark [[concepts/success|achievement]] in [[concepts/artificial-cells|artificial cell]] construction, described as the most complete artificial cell ever built.
- **Capabilities**: Demonstrates autonomous biological functions including "eating" (nutrient uptake), growth, and replication.
- **Significance**: Represents a critical step toward understanding the minimal requirements for life and enabling programmable biological systems from non-living components.
