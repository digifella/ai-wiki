# pH Levels

pH is a logarithmic scale that measures the concentration of hydrogen ions in a substance, ranging from 0 to 14. A pH of 7 indicates neutrality, values below 7 indicate acidity, and values above 7 indicate alkalinity. Because the scale is logarithmic, each unit change represents a tenfold difference in hydrogen ion concentration. For example, a substance with pH 5 is ten times more acidic than one with pH 6, and one hundred times more acidic than one with pH 7.

## Relevance in Food and Nutrition

pH levels significantly affect food safety, preservation, and flavor. Acidic foods with pH below 4.6, such as citrus fruits, vinegar, and yogurt, naturally inhibit the growth of harmful bacteria like botulism. This property makes them safer for certain preservation methods like canning. Alkaline foods are less common but include some leafy greens and certain processed foods. Most foods fall somewhere between these extremes, with neutral or slightly acidic profiles.

The pH of foods also influences taste perception and texture. Acidic foods taste tangy or sour, while alkaline foods can taste bitter. During cooking and food preparation, pH changes can affect how ingredients interact—for instance, acidic ingredients can prevent the breakdown of proteins, while alkaline conditions may alter color and nutrient availability. Understanding pH helps food manufacturers and home cooks control fermentation, prevent spoilage, and achieve desired culinary outcomes.
