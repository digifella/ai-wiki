# Prime Lenses

Prime lenses are optical devices characterized by a fixed focal length, meaning they cannot zoom in or out. Common focal lengths include 35mm, 50mm, and 85mm. Because the perspective is static, photographers must physically move closer to or farther from their subject to adjust the framing, rather than manipulating the lens mechanics. These lenses typically offer superior optical quality compared to zoom lenses of equivalent focal length.

The optical design is optimized for a single focal length rather than a variable range, allowing manufacturers to reduce aberrations and improve sharpness. This specialization often results in wider maximum apertures, which facilitate better performance in low-light conditions and allow for greater control over depth of field. The simplicity of the internal construction also contributes to a more compact and lightweight form factor compared to complex zoom counterparts.

While the lack of zoom capability requires the photographer to change position to compose shots, this constraint can encourage more deliberate framing and movement. The trade-off between physical mobility and optical precision makes prime lenses a preferred choice for portrait, street, and low-light photography where image clarity and light gathering are prioritized over convenience.
