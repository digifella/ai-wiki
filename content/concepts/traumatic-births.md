# Traumatic Births

Traumatic birth refers to childbirth experiences that result in significant psychological distress for those present, including the birthing person and their partner or support person. Research indicates that approximately 30% of births may be experienced as traumatic by attendees, particularly fathers or male partners. This psychological impact can have lasting effects on [[concepts/mental-health|mental health]], relationship dynamics, and bonding with the newborn.

## Impact on Fathers

Fathers who attend traumatic births face unique challenges in processing their experience. Witnessing complications, emergency interventions, or unexpected outcomes during labour and delivery can result in post-traumatic [[concepts/stress|stress]] symptoms, [[concepts/anxiety|anxiety]], and [[concepts/depression|depression]]. Despite the significance of their experience, fathers often receive limited psychological support or acknowledgment of their distress in clinical settings.

## Service Delivery Enhancement

Improving [[concepts/service-delivery|service delivery]] for fathers during and after traumatic births involves recognizing their role as support persons and ensuring their own [[concepts/health|wellbeing]] is addressed. This includes providing clear communication about clinical procedures, offering access to debriefing or counselling services, and creating pathways for fathers to process their experience. Healthcare systems that acknowledge the impact of traumatic births on all attendees tend to achieve better outcomes for family adjustment and long-term mental health.
