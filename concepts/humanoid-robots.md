# Humanoid Robots

Humanoid robots are mobile robotic systems designed with a body structure resembling human anatomy, typically featuring a torso, head, arms, and legs. This form factor enables them to operate in environments designed for human workers and to perform tasks requiring human-like dexterity and mobility. Because they can use existing tools and navigate standard spaces without requiring specialized adaptation, humanoid robots have potential applications in manufacturing, construction, healthcare, and domestic settings.

## Design and Development

The humanoid form presents both advantages and engineering challenges. The bipedal locomotion common to humanoid designs requires sophisticated balance and control systems, while articulated arms and hands enable manipulation of objects and tools. Development of humanoid robots involves research across multiple disciplines, including mechanical engineering, control systems, computer vision, and artificial intelligence. Major research institutions and companies worldwide have invested in humanoid robot projects at various stages of development and deployment.

## Control and Operation

Modern humanoid robots employ various control approaches, ranging from autonomous navigation and task execution to teleoperated systems where human operators direct the robot's actions. Teleoperation systems allow skilled operators to control complex movements and decision-making in real-time, which is particularly valuable for tasks in uncertain or hazardous environments where autonomous systems may be insufficient. AI systems designed for robot control can process sensory feedback and operator input to execute coordinated movements and task sequences.
