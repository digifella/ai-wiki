# Integrative Review

**Integrative review** is a research methodology that allows for the inclusion of diverse study designs and sources of evidence (experimental, non-experimental, and theoretical) to provide a comprehensive understanding of a broad phenomenon. Unlike [[concepts/systematic-study|Systematic Review]], which often prioritizes quantitative data for meta-analysis, integrative reviews aim to synthesize mixed methodologies to inform practice and [[concepts/theory|theory]].

## Key Characteristics
- **Broad Scope**: Incorporates multiple types of evidence (empirical, grey literature, theory).
- **Flexibility**: Does not adhere strictly to a single protocol, allowing for iterative searching and [[concepts/data-extraction|data extraction]].
- **Data Integration**: Uses rigorous data analysis techniques to categorize, critique, and synthesize findings into a coherent [[concepts/storytelling|narrative]] or framework.

## Methodological Steps
1. **[[concepts/issue-identification|Problem Identification]]**: Defining the research question.
2. **Literature Search**: Comprehensive search across multiple databases using truncation and wildcards.
3. **Data Evaluation**: Critiquing the quality and relevance of included studies.
4. **Data Analysis**: Categorizing data into concepts or themes.
5. **Presentation**: Synthesizing findings into a clear narrative structure.
