# Dimensionless Number

A **dimensionless number** is a quantity with no physical units, derived from the ratio of two quantities with identical dimensions. These numbers characterize physical phenomena independent of scale or unit system.

## Key Characteristics
*   Pure scalars; invariant under change of measurement units.
*   Often represent similarity parameters in [[concepts/fluid-dynamics|fluid dynamics]], heat transfer, and electromagnetism.
*   Fundamental constants (like [[concepts/fine-structure-constant]]) are intrinsic dimensionless properties of the universe.

## Notable Examples
*   **Reynolds Number**: Ratio of inertial forces to viscous forces in fluid [[concepts/flow|flow]].
*   **Mach Number**: Ratio of flow velocity to the [[concepts/speed|speed]] of sound.
*   **[[concepts/fine-structure-constant]]** ($\alpha \approx 1/137$): Characterizes the strength of [[concepts/electromagnetic-interaction|electromagnetic interaction]] between elementary charged particles. See [[lab-notes/2026-06-12-Fine-Structure-Constant-1137-Derivation-Significance-and|Fine Structure Constant (1/137): Derivation, Significance, and Quantum Enigma]] for detailed derivation and significance.

## Significance in Physics
Dimensionless constants are central to [[concepts/theoretical-physics|theoretical physics]] because they reveal fundamental [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] between [[concepts/fundamental-laws-of-physics|physical laws]] without dependency on arbitrary human-defined units. The [[concepts/fine-structure-constant]] remains one of the most significant [[concepts/unsolved-problems|unsolved problems]] in [[concepts/physics|physics]] due to its precise, unexplained value.
