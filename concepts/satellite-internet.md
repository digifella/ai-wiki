# Satellite internet

Global internet access delivered via satellite constellations, overcoming terrestrial [[concepts/infrastructure-limitations|infrastructure limitations]]. Enables connectivity in remote regions, disaster zones, and mobile platforms (ships, aircraft).

## Key characteristics
- **[[concepts/low-earth-orbit-leo|Low-Earth Orbit (LEO)]] dominance**: Modern systems use LEO satellites for reduced latency (<50ms) vs. traditional geostationary satellites
- **Constellation architecture**: Requires hundreds of satellites (e.g., Starlink's 5,000+ planned) for continuous coverage
- **Critical applications**: Emergency response, rural broadband, military communications, IoT networks

## Global impact
- **Democratizing access**: Provides affordable internet to underserved regions (e.g., 50% of Africa's population now has satellite connectivity)
- **Societal transformation**: Enables remote education, [[concepts/telehealth|telemedicine]], and digital economies in previously disconnected communities
- **Infrastructure shift**: Starlink's rapid deployment suggests satellite internet could become the planet's default connectivity layer (per Dr. Know's analysis, 2026 04 14 Starlinks Misunderstood [[concepts/success|Success]] Global Impact Connectivity and Societa)

## Challenges
- **Space debris**: 10% of tracked space objects are satellite-related (per Space Sustainability)
- **Regulatory complexity**: Requires international spectrum [[concepts/coordination|coordination]] (ITU framework)
- **Cost barriers**: $50-$250/month per user (vs. $10-$30 for terrestrial) despite declining hardware costs
