# Australian Geology

Australian [[concepts/geology|geology]] is dominated by ancient Precambrian cratons and extensive sedimentary basins that reflect the continent's complex tectonic history. The continent consists largely of stable continental shields, with much of the bedrock composed of [[entities/granite|granite]], metamorphic rocks, and ancient volcanic formations. Many of these rocks are among the oldest on Earth, with the Australian Shield representing some of the world's most geologically stable regions. The interior and margins have experienced repeated cycles of sedimentation, uplift, and erosion over billions of years, creating a diverse geological landscape.

## Structural Regions

Australia's geological [[concepts/structure|structure]] divides into three main provinces: the Archean and Proterozoic cratons of the west and centre, the Phanerozoic sedimentary basins and fold belts along the margins, and the younger volcanic fields in the southeast. The cratons are among the oldest known continental nuclei, while the peripheral regions contain younger sedimentary sequences deposited during various transgression and regression cycles. The Great Dividing [[concepts/range|Range]] in the east represents a major structural feature resulting from ancient orogeny and subsequent erosion.

## Mineral and Energy Resources

Australia's geological history has produced significant deposits of iron ore, gold, and other minerals, particularly within the Archean greenstone belts. The continent also hosts major coal and petroleum reserves in sedimentary basins such as the Bowen Basin and the Browse Basin, formed during different depositional episodes over the Phanerozoic. These economic mineral deposits reflect specific periods of crustal activity and favorable depositional conditions throughout Australian geological time.
