# Context Injection

Context injection is a technique for optimizing [[concepts/demystifying-llms|large language models]] (LLMs) by embedding targeted [[concepts/instructions|instructions]] and domain-specific information into prompts before requesting output. Rather than relying on an [[concepts/ai-system|AI system]]'s default behavior, this approach involves deliberately configuring the model with customized instructions, examples, constraints, and background information. The goal is to guide responses toward specific professional standards or [[concepts/scenarios|use cases]], ensuring that the generated content aligns with particular requirements.

This method recognizes that LLMs can be significantly more effective when given explicit [[concepts/recommendations|guidance]] tailored to specific domains. By providing structured context, users can mitigate the variability of [[concepts/general-purpose-models|general-purpose models]] and steer them toward more accurate and relevant outputs. This is particularly useful in fields requiring [[concepts/accuracy|precision]], such as legal or technical [[concepts/writing|writing]], where standard responses may lack necessary nuance or adherence to professional norms.

The practice extends beyond simple [[concepts/prompt-based-modeling|prompt engineering]] by integrating persistent or semi-persistent context elements. These may include [[concepts/custom-instructions|custom instructions]], [[concepts/coding-instructions|system prompts]], or [[concepts/few-shot-examples|few-shot examples]] that establish a consistent framework for interaction. This allows for the optimization of models like [[entities/chatgpt|ChatGPT]], [[concepts/claude-ai|Claude]], and Gemini for specialized tasks, reducing the need for extensive post-processing or [[concepts/iterative-learning|iterative refinement]] of the AI's initial responses.
