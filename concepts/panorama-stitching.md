# Panorama Stitching

Panorama stitching is a photographic technique that combines multiple overlapping images into a single, wider composition than a standard camera frame allows. Instead of relying on in-camera panorama modes or ultra-wide-angle lenses, photographers capture a series of distinct shots and merge them using specialized software. This method provides greater creative control over the final image and enables precise composition planning during the shooting phase.

The process typically involves capturing a sequence of images with consistent exposure and white balance settings to ensure seamless blending. By using a longer focal length lens for individual frames, photographers can achieve long lens compression, which flattens perspective and reduces distortion compared to wide-angle alternatives. This approach is particularly useful for landscape and architectural photography where maintaining sharpness and minimizing barrel distortion are priorities.

Software algorithms align the overlapping regions of the input images, correcting for parallax errors and geometric distortions. The resulting composite image retains the high resolution and dynamic range of the original individual files, offering superior quality compared to traditional single-shot panoramic methods. This technique allows for the creation of extremely wide aspect ratios while preserving detail across the entire field of view.
