# Ethanol Removal

**Ethanol removal** refers to the industrial and laboratory processes used to extract Ethanol from liquid mixtures, primarily for the production of [[concepts/non-alcoholic-beverages|non-alcoholic beverages]], pharmaceutical preparations, or fuel purification. The goal is to reduce alcohol content while preserving the sensory profile (aroma, taste, color) of the original matrix. This process shares chemical principles with [[concepts/solvent-extraction|solvent extraction]] and volatility management, closely mirroring techniques used in [[concepts/caffeine-removal|Decaffeination]].

## Core Principles
The separation relies on the difference in volatility between ethanol (boiling point ~78°C) and water (boiling point 100°C), as well as solubility differences in specific solvents. Key challenges include:
- **Azeotrope formation**: Ethanol and water form an azeotrope at ~95.6% ethanol, requiring specialized techniques for complete removal.
- **Volatile loss**: Risk of stripping desirable aromatic compounds alongside ethanol.
- **Thermal degradation**: Heat-sensitive compounds may degrade during distillation.

## Common Methods

### 1. Vacuum Distillation
- **Mechanism**: Reduces pressure to lower the boiling point of ethanol, allowing removal at temperatures typically below 40°C.
- **Application**: Standard for non-alcoholic beer and wine production to minimize thermal damage to flavor compounds.

### 2. Membrane Filtration (Reverse Osmosis)
- **Mechanism**: Uses semi-permeable membranes to separate ethanol and water from larger flavor molecules.
- **Process**: The permeate (ethanol/water mix) is distilled to recover ethanol, while the retentate (flavor compounds) is recombined with water.
- **Advantage**: Preserves volatile aromatics better than thermal methods.

### 3. Spinning Cone Column
- **Mechanism**: A multi-stage distillation system using centrifugal force and steam to separate volatiles at low temperatures.
- **Application**: High-end non-alcoholic spirits and wines where aroma [[concepts/storing|retention]] is critical.

## Context and History
The development of de-alcoholization parallels the history of Decaffeination, driven by similar consumer demands for reduced psychoactive content without sacrificing taste. Recent advancements focus on hybrid methods combining membrane technology with gentle distillation to address the "flavor gap" in non-alcoholic products.
