# USB 4

USB 4 is a high-[[concepts/speed|speed]] connector standard that builds upon [[concepts/usb-30|USB-C]] technology, offering significantly increased data transfer rates and improved [[concepts/power|power]] delivery [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]]. Introduced in 2019 and finalized by the USB Implementers Forum, USB 4 is based on [[entities/intel|Intel]]'s Thunderbolt 3 protocol, which was made available for [[concepts/licensing|licensing]] to other manufacturers.

## Technical Specifications

USB 4 supports data transfer speeds of up to 40 gigabits per second, double that of USB 3.2. It maintains backward compatibility with USB 3.2, [[concepts/usb-20|USB 2.0]], and Thunderbolt 3 devices through the use of appropriate adapters. The standard supports multiple data and display protocols simultaneously, allowing a single USB 4 port to carry video, data, and power on the same [[concepts/connection|connection]].

## Adoption and Use Cases

USB 4 has been adopted in laptops, external drives, and docking stations, though [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] has been gradual compared to earlier USB [[concepts/open-standards|standards]]. The standard is particularly valued for high-bandwidth [[concepts/software|applications]] such as external graphics cards, high-resolution displays, and fast [[entities/storage|storage]] devices. However, USB 4 devices and cables remain more expensive than their USB 3.2 counterparts, which has slowed mainstream adoption.
