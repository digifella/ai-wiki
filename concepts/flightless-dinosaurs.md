# Flightless Dinosaurs

**Flightless dinosaurs** are [[concepts/dinosaurs|non-avian dinosaurs]] that lacked the ability to fly, despite many possessing feathers. This group includes large theropods like [[entities/tyrannosaurus-rex]] and smaller maniraptorans like Oviraptor and Caudipteryx. The presence of feathers in these animals challenges the traditional view that feathers evolved solely for flight.

## Evolutionary Functions of Feathers

Recent research suggests that feathers in flightless lineages served functions other than aerial locomotion. Key hypotheses include:

- **Hunting and Predation**: Feathers may have evolved to enhance hunting capabilities, such as improving [[concepts/stealth|stealth]], maneuverability in dense vegetation, or sensory functions. See [[lab-notes/2026-06-26-Flightless-Dinosaurs-Feathers-Evolution-for-Hunting-Not|Flightless Dinosaurs' Feathers: Evolution for Hunting, Not Flight]] for detailed analysis on this hypothesis.
- **[[concepts/thermal-regulation|Thermoregulation]]**: Insulation against cold environments or [[concepts/heat-dissipation|heat dissipation]].
- **Display and Communication**: Visual signaling for mating, species recognition, or intimidation.
- **Brooding**: [[concepts/secure|Protection]] of eggs and hatchlings.

## Key Species and Evidence

- **Caudipteryx**: A turkey-sized oviraptorosaur from ~124 million years ago. Possessed well-developed feathered wings and tails but was flightless. Its anatomy suggests feathers were used for display or hunting rather than flight.
- **Velociraptor**: Likely covered in feathers, with wing-like structures on forelimbs that may have aided in balance or grasping prey.
- **Yutyrannus**: A large tyrannosauroid with filamentous feathers, indicating that insulation was a primary driver for feather evolution in large-bodied dinosaurs.
