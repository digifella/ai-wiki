# Indian Civilization

Indian civilization encompasses the complex societies and cultures that developed across the Indian subcontinent over more than five thousand years. The earliest known urban civilization in this region, the Indus Valley Civilization, flourished from approximately 3300 to 1300 BCE across what is now Pakistan and northwestern India. Contemporary with ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia, it featured planned cities with standardized drainage systems, consistent weights and measures for trade, and a writing system that remains only partially deciphered. Major settlements such as Harappa and Mohenjo-daro demonstrated sophisticated urban planning and evidence of extensive maritime and overland trade networks extending to Mesopotamia and Central Asia.

## Vedic and Classical Periods

Following the decline of the Indus Valley Civilization, Vedic societies emerged in the Indian subcontinent around 1500 BCE, characterized by pastoral communities and the composition of the Vedas, foundational texts of Hindu philosophy. The subsequent classical period, beginning around 500 BCE, saw the rise of major empires including the Maurya Empire (322–185 BCE) under figures like Ashoka, which expanded administrative systems and facilitated the spread of Buddhism across Asia. The Gupta Empire (320–550 CE) is often regarded as a golden age marked by advances in mathematics, astronomy, literature, and art.

## Trade and Global Connections

Throughout its history, Indian civilization maintained significant trade relationships that shaped its development and cultural influence. The Silk Road and maritime trade routes connected Indian merchants and goods to Central Asia, China, the Middle East, and East Africa. Spices, textiles, precious stones, and intellectual traditions from the Indian subcontinent influenced distant regions, while external trade also introduced new technologies, crops, and ideas that were integrated into Indian societies.
