# Wendelstein 7-X

The **Wendelstein 7-X** (W7-X) is a stellarator fusion research device located [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] the Max [[entities/planck|Planck]] Institute for Plasma [[concepts/physics|Physics]] in Greifswald, [[entities/germany|Germany]]. It is designed to prove the feasibility of stellarators as a [[concepts/power|power]] plant concept, addressing limitations found in tokamak designs such as disruption risks and continuous plasma operation.

## Overview

*   **[[concepts/design|Design]]:** A modular, 5-field-periodic stellarator with optimized magnetic fields to minimize neoclassical transport.
*   **[[concepts/motivation|Purpose]]:** To demonstrate that stellarators can confine plasma effectively for long durations without the disruptions common in tokamaks.
*   **Location:** Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics, Greifswald, Germany.
*   **Status:** Operational for scientific experiments; ongoing campaigns aim to extend plasma confinement times and temperatures.

## Significance

*   **Continuous Operation:** Unlike tokamaks, stellarators like W7-X are capable of steady-state operation, which is crucial for future commercial power generation.
*   **Optimization:** Advanced [[concepts/computational-efficiency|computational optimization]] allows for complex 3D magnetic coil structures, improving plasma stability and confinement efficiency.
*   **Path to ITER and Beyond:** Provides critical data for understanding plasma behavior in optimized configurations, complementing data from ITER and other fusion projects.
