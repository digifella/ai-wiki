# Targeted Color Adjustments

Targeted color adjustments enable editors to modify specific hues within an image while leaving other color ranges unaffected. Unlike global corrections that apply uniform changes to all tones, this method isolates individual color channels—such as reds, oranges, greens, and blues—for independent manipulation. This selective approach is essential for precise corrections, such as refining skin tones without altering background foliage or enhancing sky saturation while preserving natural landscape colors.

## Color Mixer Tool

The primary interface for these adjustments in Lightroom Classic is the Color Mixer panel, located within the HSL/Color/Black & White module. This tool provides three distinct controls for each of the eight primary color ranges: Hue, Saturation, and Lightness. Adjusting the Hue shifts the color toward adjacent tones on the color wheel, such as moving a red toward orange. Saturation controls the intensity of the color, allowing for desaturation to reduce vibrancy or increased saturation for greater vividness. Lightness adjustments alter the brightness of the specific color range, enabling editors to lighten shadows within a particular hue or darken highlights without impacting the overall exposure of the image.

## Practical Applications

These adjustments are frequently used to correct color casts or enhance specific elements of a scene. For instance, an editor might reduce the saturation of greens to prevent foliage from appearing overly artificial, or increase the lightness of blues to make a sky appear brighter and more dynamic. By targeting specific colors, editors can achieve a more balanced and natural look, ensuring that corrections do not introduce unwanted artifacts or affect areas of the image that are already satisfactory. This precision allows for nuanced editing that respects the original intent of the photograph while addressing specific technical or aesthetic issues.
