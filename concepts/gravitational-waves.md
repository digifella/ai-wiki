# Gravitational Waves

Gravitational waves are ripples in the curvature of spacetime that propagate as waves outward from their source at the [[concepts/finite-speed-of-light|speed of light]]. Predicted by Albert Einstein in 1916 as a consequence of his general [[concepts/theory|theory]] of relativity, these waves are generated by the acceleration of massive objects, particularly during violent cosmic events such as the merger of [[concepts/black-holes|black holes]] or [[concepts/neutron-stars|neutron stars]]. They carry unique information about their origins and the nature of [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]] that cannot be obtained from [[concepts/light|electromagnetic radiation]].

For nearly a century, gravitational waves remained a theoretical [[concepts/user-attention-prediction|prediction]] due to their extremely weak interaction with matter, making them difficult to detect. Direct confirmation was finally achieved in 2015 by the Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory ([[concepts/ligo|LIGO]]), which observed waves from the merger of two [[concepts/stellar-mass|stellar-mass]] black holes. This discovery validated a major prediction of general relativity and inaugurated the field of gravitational-wave [[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]], allowing scientists to observe the universe through a new [[entities/medium|medium]].

Recent research has expanded the scope of gravitational wave analysis to include evidence for sub-solar mass [[concepts/primordial-black-hole|primordial black holes]]. By examining the frequency and amplitude of detected signals, physicists investigate whether these compact objects originated in the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]] rather than from [[concepts/gravitational-collapse|stellar collapse]]. Such findings would provide critical insights into the conditions of the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]] and the distribution of [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]], further refining our understanding of cosmic evolution and the fundamental properties of gravity.
