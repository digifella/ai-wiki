# Removal Strategies

## Overview
Removal strategies refer to the methodologies and technologies employed to eliminate, degrade, or contain hazardous substances from environmental matrices (water, soil, air) or industrial processes. Key domains include water-treatment, waste-management, and environmental-remediation.

## Key Concepts
- **Adsorption**: Utilization of materials like activated-carbon or ion-exchange-resins to bind contaminants.
- **Membrane Filtration**: Techniques such as reverse-osmosis and nanofiltration to physically separate pollutants.
- **Advanced Oxidation Processes (AOPs)**: Use of reactive species to degrade persistent organic pollutants.
- **Thermal Treatment**: Incineration or pyrolysis for destruction of non-biodegradable compounds.

## Case Study: PFAS Removal
Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances ([[concepts/pfas|PFAS]]), known as "[[concepts/forever-chemicals|forever chemicals]]," present unique challenges due to their chemical stability.

- **Contamination Source**: Man-made compounds originating from the late 1940s, widely distributed in the environment Toxic PFAS in Water: Contamination, Health Risks, and Removal Strategies.
- **Health Risks**: Associated with significant human health impacts due to bioaccumulation and persistence.
- **Removal Challenges**: Standard water treatment often fails to fully remove PFAS; specialized strategies are required.
- **Current Approaches**: Research focuses on advanced filtration and destruction methods to mitigate exposure.
