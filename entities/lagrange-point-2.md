# Lagrange Point 2

**[[concepts/lagrange-point-2|Lagrange Point 2]]** (L2) is a gravitationally stable point in the Sun-Earth system, located approximately 1.5 million kilometers (1 million miles) directly "behind" Earth relative to the Sun. It is a primary location for deep-space observatories due to its thermal stability and unobstructed view of the cosmos.

## Key Characteristics
- **Gravitational Balance**: At L2, the combined gravitational pull of the Sun and Earth allows a spacecraft to orbit the Sun with the same period as Earth, remaining in a fixed position relative to the Earth-Sun line.
- **Thermal Environment**: The constant orientation away from the Sun allows for efficient passive cooling, critical for infrared astronomy.
- **Communication**: Requires continuous [[concepts/line-of-sight|line-of-sight]] to Earth for data transmission.

## Notable Missions
- **[[concepts/james-webb-space-telescope|James Webb Space Telescope]] (JWST)**: Operates in a halo orbit around L2, utilizing its sunshield for infrared observations.
- **Euclid**: ESA mission studying [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] and dark energy, also stationed at L2.
- **Nancy Grace [[concepts/falcon-heavy|Roman Space Telescope]]**: A [[entities/nasa|NASA]] observatory designed to survey the sky in visible and infrared light.
