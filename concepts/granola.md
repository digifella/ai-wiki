# Granola

[[entities/granola|Granola]] is a breakfast cereal composed primarily of rolled oats, nuts, seeds, and a sweetening agent such as honey or [[concepts/sugar|sugar]]. The ingredients are mixed together and baked until they bind into crunchy clusters and brown. This process distinguishes it from other oat-based cereals by creating a textured, toasted product that is often denser and sweeter than traditional oatmeal.

The [[concepts/writing|composition]] of granola varies widely depending on regional preferences and dietary needs. While oats form the base, common additions include almonds, walnuts, sunflower seeds, and pumpkin seeds. Sweeteners may range from refined sugar and maple syrup to agave nectar, and some varieties incorporate dried fruits like raisins, cranberries, or apricots. Modern iterations often include superfoods such as chia seeds or coconut flakes, though these are not part of the traditional definition.

Granola is versatile in its consumption and application. It is commonly eaten dry as a cereal with milk or yogurt, served as a standalone snack, or used as a topping for smoothie bowls and parfaits. Additionally, the clusters are frequently incorporated into baked goods, energy bars, and trail mixes to add [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]] and flavor. Its high fiber and protein content, derived from the oats and nuts, contributes to its popularity as a nutrient-dense food option.
