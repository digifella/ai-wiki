# Quantum Measurement

[[concepts/observation-collapse|Quantum measurement]] refers to the process of obtaining information about a quantum system, which fundamentally alters the system's state. This process lies at the heart of [[concepts/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]] and has prompted decades of philosophical and scientific debate. The [[concepts/wave-function-collapse|measurement problem]]—the apparent discontinuity between the continuous evolution of a quantum system and its sudden collapse upon measurement—remains one of the most significant [[concepts/unsolved-problems|unresolved questions]] in [[concepts/physics|physics]].

## Local Realism and Hidden Variables

Traditional interpretations of quantum mechanics appear to [[concepts/conflict|conflict]] with [[concepts/local-realism|local realism]], the principle that [[concepts/material-properties|physical properties]] exist independently of observation and that influences cannot travel faster than [[concepts/light|light]]. Bell's theorem and subsequent experiments demonstrated that no local hidden variable [[concepts/theory|theory]] can reproduce all predictions of quantum mechanics. This has led physicists to either abandon local realism or seek alternative frameworks that preserve it under modified assumptions.

## Superdeterminism as an Alternative

[[concepts/superdeterminism|Superdeterminism]] proposes that the universe is completely deterministic, with all events—including the choices made in quantum experiments—predetermined from the beginning of time. Under this framework, apparent randomness in quantum measurement outcomes emerges not from fundamental [[concepts/indeterminacy|indeterminacy]] but from correlations between the quantum system and the measurement apparatus that were established at the universe's origin. This interpretation potentially preserves local realism while maintaining [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] with quantum mechanical predictions, though it requires abandoning free [[entities/will|will]] in a strong sense.

Superdeterminism remains highly controversial within the physics community, as it challenges conventional assumptions about [[concepts/experimental-design|experimental design]] and raises profound questions about causality and determinism that extend beyond the purely physical realm.
