# Digital Scanning

Digital scanning is the process of converting physical film negatives or [[concepts/google-slides|slides]] into digital image files using specialized equipment and software. This technique allows photographers and enthusiasts to preserve and work with analog film archives in digital formats, making them compatible with modern editing, [[entities/storage|storage]], and sharing systems.

## Equipment and Methods

[[concepts/camera-scanning|Home film scanning]] typically involves dedicated film scanners or flatbed scanners equipped with [[concepts/opacity|transparency]] adapters. Dedicated film scanners use [[concepts/light|light]] sources and sensors designed specifically for film, generally producing higher [[concepts/solution|resolution]] output than flatbed alternatives. The scanning process involves placing the film in the scanner bed, selecting appropriate resolution and color settings, and allowing the device to capture the image data. Resolution is typically measured in dots per inch (DPI), with higher DPI settings producing larger digital files but requiring longer scan times.

## Workflow and Preservation

The digital scanning workflow generally includes organizing physical film materials, performing the scans, and then processing the resulting files through editing software if desired. Many users also employ cleaning and [[concepts/preservation|restoration]] techniques before scanning to improve image quality. Once digitized, the files can be stored on hard drives, [[concepts/cloud-computing|cloud services]], or other media, creating backups of irreplaceable photographs. Digital formats also enable sharing through [[entities/email|email]], social media, and printing services that may no longer support physical film projection or printing.

## Practical Considerations

Effective digital scanning requires [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to color accuracy, dust removal, and appropriate file formats for long-term storage. Users must balance scanning [[concepts/speed|speed]] against output quality based on their intended use—web sharing typically requires lower resolutions than [[concepts/archival-preservation|archival preservation]] or large-format printing. The initial investment in scanning equipment can be offset by the ability to process entire film archives without recurring lab fees.
