# Service Delivery

Service delivery in the context of traumatic birth experiences refers to the support systems and interventions designed to assist fathers and partners during and after childbirth complications. Research indicates that approximately 30% of births are experienced as traumatic by attendant partners, highlighting a significant gap in current healthcare provision. This high prevalence suggests that many maternity services do not adequately account for the psychological and emotional needs of non-birthing partners during labor and delivery.

## Current Gaps

Most maternity services are primarily structured around the physiological needs of the birthing person and the infant, often overlooking the distinct mental health requirements of fathers and partners. Consequently, these individuals frequently lack access to immediate psychological first aid or structured debriefing sessions following a traumatic event. The absence of dedicated protocols means that partners may experience secondary traumatic stress without appropriate recognition or referral pathways to specialized care.

## Proposed Interventions

Enhancing service delivery requires the integration of mental health screening for partners into standard postnatal care routines. Healthcare providers should be trained to identify signs of acute stress or post-traumatic stress disorder in non-birthing parents and offer timely referrals to counseling services. Additionally, creating inclusive support groups and providing clear information on available resources can help mitigate long-term psychological impacts for families affected by traumatic births.
