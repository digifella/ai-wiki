# Engineering Process

The **[[entities/national-academies|Engineering]] Process** is the systematic application of scientific and mathematical principles to design, develop, and validate systems, structures, or products. It encompasses requirements gathering, design, analysis, testing, manufacturing oversight, and lifecycle management. A robust process mitigates risk by ensuring that theoretical models align with physical realities through rigorous [[concepts/verification|verification]] and validation.

## Core Principles
- **Verification vs. Validation**: Ensuring the product is built correctly (verification) and that the correct product is built (validation).
- **[[concepts/iterative-design|Iterative Design]]**: Continuous refinement based on testing data and [[concepts/systems|feedback loops]].
- **Safety Margins**: Incorporating factors of safety to account for uncertainties in [[concepts/material-properties|material properties]] and load conditions.
- **Traceability**: Maintaining clear links between requirements, design decisions, and test results.

## Failure Modes in Engineering Processes
Failures often stem not from material defects alone, but from breakdowns in the process governing design and manufacturing:
- **Inadequate [[concepts/document-review|Peer Review]]**: Lack of independent verification of critical design assumptions.
- **Manufacturing Deviations**: Failure to detect or correct deviations from design specifications during production.
- **Risk Normalization**: Gradual acceptance of higher risk levels due to lack of immediate negative [[concepts/feedback|feedback]].
- **Insufficient Testing**: Skipping or inadequately [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]] physical tests in favor of theoretical models.

## Case Study: OceanGate Titan
The implosion of the [[concepts/oceangate-titan|OceanGate Titan]] submersible serves as a critical case study in process failure. Investigations concluded that the disaster was not caused by the inherent properties of carbon fiber, but by systemic failures in the engineering and manufacturing oversight.

- **Process Over Material**: The root cause was identified as an **inadequate engineering process** rather than a material [[concepts/science|science]] failure.
- **Manufacturing Oversight**: Failures in manufacturing [[concepts/quality-control|quality control]] allowed structural weaknesses to persist.
- **Source Analysis**: Detailed analysis of the incident highlights how bypassing standard engineering protocols leads to catastrophic outcomes.
