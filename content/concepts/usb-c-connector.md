# Usb C Connector

USB-C is a 24-pin connector standard introduced by the USB Implementers Forum in 2013. Unlike previous USB [[concepts/open-standards|standards]] which defined both the connector shape and the electrical protocol together, USB-C separates these concerns—the physical connector can carry multiple protocols including [[concepts/usb-20|USB 2.0]], [[concepts/usb-30|USB 3.0]], USB 3.1, Thunderbolt 3, and others. This flexibility has made USB-C increasingly common across consumer electronics, from smartphones and laptops to peripherals and power delivery systems.

## Cable Confusion and Safety Issues

The disconnect between connector appearance and actual capability has created significant consumer confusion. Two USB-C cables may look identical but support vastly different data speeds, power delivery capabilities, or even [[concepts/product-safety|safety standards]]. Poorly manufactured cables can damage devices or pose fire risks, particularly when handling high-power charging. Unlike older USB standards where the connector type clearly indicated the protocol, USB-C requires additional research or labeling to determine what a cable actually supports, leading many consumers to purchase unreliable or incompatible products.

## Standardization Challenges

While USB-C [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] has accelerated globally—partly due to regulatory mandates requiring common charging standards—manufacturers have inconsistently implemented the full specification. Some devices support only USB 2.0 speeds despite using USB-C connectors, while others advertise USB 3.1 speeds but lack proper certification. This fragmentation persists because the standard itself is complex, and [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] [[concepts/testing|testing]] remains inconsistent across different regions and manufacturers.
