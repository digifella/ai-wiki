# Cochlear Implant

A Cochlear Implant is a complex electronic bio-[[concepts/medical-device|medical device]] that provides a sense of sound to a person who is profoundly deaf or severely hard-of-hearing. Unlike Hearing Aids, which amplify sound, cochlear implants bypass damaged portions of the ear and directly stimulate the [[concepts/auditory-nerve|Auditory Nerve]].

## Mechanism of Action
- **Sound Processing:** External speech [[concepts/cpu|processor]] captures sound and converts it into digital signals.
- **Transmission:** Signals are transmitted via radio waves to an internal receiver/stimulator.
- **Stimulation:** The internal device converts signals into electrical impulses that stimulate the Auditory Nerve fibers within the Cochlea.
- **Perception:** The brain interprets these electrical signals as sound.

## Development History
- The development trajectory was characterized by a long, arduous journey involving significant scientific and ethical hurdles.
- Early experiments focused on the feasibility of [[concepts/electrical-stimulation|Electrical Stimulation]] of the auditory nerve to restore hearing perception.
- Modern iterations allow hundreds of thousands of individuals worldwide to perceive sound, marking a revolutionary milestone in Rehabilitation Medicine.
- See detailed historical analysis in [[lab-notes/2026-07-17-Cochlear-Implant-Development-From-Electrical-Stimulation|Cochlear Implant Development: From Electrical Stimulation to Hearing Restoration]].

## Clinical Indications
- Profound sensorineural hearing loss in both ears.
- Limited benefit from conventional Hearing Aids.
- Candidates include both adults and children (early implantation improves language development outcomes).
