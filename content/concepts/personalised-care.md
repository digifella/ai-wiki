# Personalised Care

Personalised care in [[concepts/allied-health|allied health]] refers to [[concepts/health|health]] interventions and monitoring tailored to individual patient needs, circumstances, and preferences. [[concepts/wearable-devices|Wearable devices]] equipped with [[concepts/embedded-sensors|embedded sensors]] have become increasingly important tools in delivering this approach, enabling continuous data collection about a patient's health status, activity levels, and physiological [[concepts/parameters|parameters]] throughout daily life.

## Wearable Devices in Allied Health

Wearable devices are electronic instruments designed to be worn on the body, typically on the wrist, chest, or other accessible locations. These devices integrate embedded sensors that measure variables such as heart rate, [[concepts/exercise|movement]] patterns, [[concepts/sleep-quality|sleep quality]], and other relevant [[concepts/health-metrics|health metrics]]. Unlike traditional clinical assessments that capture a single moment in time, [[concepts/internet-of-things|wearables]] provide ongoing data streams that reflect real-world patient behaviour and health patterns.

## Clinical Applications

Allied health professionals—including physiotherapists, occupational therapists, and exercise physiologists—use wearable data to monitor patient progress, adjust treatment plans, and encourage adherence to rehabilitation protocols. The continuous [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] from these devices allows practitioners to make more informed decisions about care and enables patients to engage more actively in their own health management. This real-time information supports a more responsive and individualised approach to treatment than conventional periodic check-ins alone.
