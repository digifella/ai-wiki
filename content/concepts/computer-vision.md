# Computer Vision

Computer vision is a field of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] concerned with enabling computers to interpret and understand visual information from [[concepts/images|images]] and video. It involves processing, analyzing, and extracting meaningful data from visual inputs, allowing AI systems to perform tasks that typically require human [[concepts/visual-perception|visual perception]]. Common [[concepts/software|applications]] include [[concepts/object-detection|object detection]], image classification, facial recognition, and scene understanding.

## Core Techniques

Computer vision systems typically rely on [[concepts/deep-learning-models|deep learning models]], particularly convolutional [[concepts/neural-networks|neural networks]] (CNNs), to identify patterns and features in visual data. These [[concepts/models|models]] are trained on large datasets to recognize objects, detect [[concepts/text|text]], estimate motion, and understand spatial [[concepts/relationships|relationships]]. The field has benefited significantly from advances in [[concepts/neural-network|neural network]] architectures and increased [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]] that enable [[concepts/training|training]] on massive image collections.

## Practical Applications

In practice, computer vision powers numerous real-world systems including autonomous vehicles, medical imaging analysis, [[concepts/quality-control|quality control]] in manufacturing, and content moderation platforms. It enables image search, augmented reality applications, and automated [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]] tools. As [[concepts/foundational-models|foundational models]] and [[concepts/multimodal-ai-agents|multimodal AI systems]] continue to advance, computer [[concepts/vision-capabilities|vision capabilities]] are becoming increasingly integrated with other AI modalities to support more [[concepts/complex-reasoning|complex reasoning]] tasks.
