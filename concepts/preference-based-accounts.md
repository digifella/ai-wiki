# Preference Based Accounts

Preference-based [[concepts/philosophical-debates|accounts of wellbeing]] define an individual's wellbeing in terms of the satisfaction of their preferences, desires, or values. Under this framework, a person's life goes well to the extent that their actual experiences align with what they prefer or want. This approach treats preference satisfaction as the primary measure of human flourishing, making subjective alignment between desires and reality central to understanding wellbeing.

## Core Principles

The preference satisfaction model rests on the intuition that wellbeing is inherently subjective and tied to what matters to each person. Rather than imposing external standards of what constitutes a good life, preference-based accounts [[concepts/respect|respect]] individual autonomy by grounding wellbeing in each person's own values and choices. This makes the framework particularly appealing from liberal and individualistic perspectives that prioritize personal agency in defining flourishing.

## Key Challenges

Preference-based accounts face several philosophical objections. Critics raise questions about whether all preferences should count equally—such as whether preferences formed under ignorance, manipulation, or adaptive circumstances (where people adjust their desires downward due to constraint) should equally contribute to wellbeing. There is also debate about whether preference satisfaction alone captures what makes a life genuinely good, or whether [[concepts/purpose|objective]] conditions and achievements matter independently of whether people desire them.

## Contemporary Discussion

Philosophers and economists continue to examine preference satisfaction as a measure of wellbeing, particularly in relation to [[concepts/happiness|happiness]] research and policy-making. These accounts remain influential in areas such as welfare economics, where preference satisfaction provides a foundation for evaluating [[concepts/policies|policies]] and individual outcomes, though often in combination with other wellbeing frameworks.
