The study of the evolution of human settlements, focusing on the intersection of architecture, economic history, and [[concepts/urban-planning|urban planning]]. It examines how cities transition through periods of growth, decay, and renewal.

### Key Themes
- [[concepts/architecture]]: The tension between modern development and the [[concepts/secure|protection]] of [[concepts/cultural-heritage|heritage]] structures.
- [[concepts/adaptive-reuse|Adaptive Reuse]]: The integration of historical layers into contemporary urban fabric.
- Urban [[concepts/resilience|Resilience]]: The ability of built environments and social structures to withstand [[concepts/financial-crises|financial crises]] and economic shifts.
- Architectural Layering: The phenomenon where new construction encapsulates or modifies older historical sites.
- Hidden Histories: The existence of significant but overlooked structures within the urban fabric, such as Melbourne's "secret buildings" which reveal original purposes often obscured by time.
