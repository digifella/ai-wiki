# Reproducibility Crisis

The **Reproducibility [[concepts/crisis|Crisis]]** refers to the widespread inability of independent researchers to replicate the results of published scientific studies. This phenomenon challenges the [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] of scientific literature across disciplines, including [[concepts/psychology|psychology]], medicine, and increasingly, high-tech fields like [[entities/quantum-computing]].

## Core Issues
- **Publication Bias**: Journals preferentially publish positive results, skewing the perceived efficacy of interventions or technologies.
- **P-Hacking**: Manipulating data analysis to find statistically significant patterns by [[concepts/probability|chance]].
- **Lack of [[concepts/opacity|Transparency]]**: Insufficient detail in methods prevents exact replication.

## Case Study: Quantum Computing Claims
The crisis extends to emerging technologies where theoretical claims often outpace empirical [[concepts/verification|verification]]. A prominent example is the skepticism surrounding [[concepts/qubits|topological qubit]] advancements.

- **[[entities/microsoft|Microsoft]]'s [[entities/majorana-2|Majorana 2]] Chip**: In July 2026, Microsoft unveiled the Majorana 2 quantum chip, claiming significant progress toward scalable [[concepts/quantum-computing|topological quantum computing]] by 2029. However, these claims face scrutiny regarding the unproven nature of the underlying topological qubit [[concepts/physics|physics]].
- **Skepticism and Verification**: Independent verification of such high-stakes technological milestones is critical to prevent the "hype cycle" from obscuring actual [[concepts/scientific-advancement|scientific progress]]. See [[lab-notes/2026-07-09-Microsofts-Majorana-2-Quantum-Chip-Unproven-Topological|Microsoft's Majorana 2 Quantum Chip: Unproven Topological Qubit Claims and Skepticism]] for detailed analysis of the claims and community skepticism.

## Implications
- Erosion of public [[concepts/trust|trust]] in scientific institutions.
- Wasted resources on building upon non-replicable findings.
- Necessity for pre-registration of studies and open data practices.
