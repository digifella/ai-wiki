# AI in Design

Artificial intelligence and generative techniques are increasingly applied throughout the design process to support and extend designer capabilities. These tools assist with activities from initial ideation and concept exploration through to refinement and production. By analyzing large datasets of existing designs, AI systems can generate visual variations, suggest layouts, optimize compositions, and accelerate iteration cycles. This enables designers to explore a broader solution space more rapidly than manual methods alone.

## Common Applications

AI-assisted design tools are used across multiple disciplines including graphic design, user interface design, product design, and architecture. Generative algorithms can produce multiple design options based on specified parameters, allowing designers to quickly evaluate alternatives. Some systems help optimize designs for manufacturability, cost, or performance criteria. Others assist with routine tasks such as resizing assets for different formats, color palette generation, or layout suggestions based on design principles.

## Designer and Tool Relationship

Rather than replacing designers, these tools typically function as assistants that handle specific subtasks or generate starting points for human refinement. Designers retain responsibility for making aesthetic and strategic decisions, while AI handles computational or generative work. Effectiveness depends on clear input parameters and designer judgment about which generated options merit further development. The skills required of designers have shifted to include effectively directing and evaluating AI outputs rather than exclusively performing manual execution.
