A significant improvement detailed in the video is the enhanced subject and background masking capabilities. Morganti demonstrates this with a portrait, showcasing Lightroom Classic's ability to precisely select and adjust the background, even around intricate details like flyaway hair – an area where previous versions often struggled. He further illustrates this by selecting the background behind a bicycle, noting the accurate masking around the spokes. This advancement allows for more refined and automated local adjustments, saving photographers considerable time in post-processing.

Another key update is the improved Assisted Culling feature, particularly beneficial for event photographers. The video demonstrates how Lightroom Classic can now more effectively identify subjects with closed eyes within group photos, which was previously a challenge. This improved AI detection helps streamline the culling process, allowing photographers to quickly identify and reject suboptimal shots. Additionally, new AI filters have been integrated into the Metadata panel, enabling users to sort their images based on whether they've had AI edits applied, such as AI masking or generative AI removals.

Finally, Morganti introduces the highly anticipated Duplicate Finder. This feature allows users to scan their entire Lightroom library (or specific folders/collections) to identify identical images. Crucially, the duplicate detection is based on pixel data rather than just filenames, making it more accurate in finding true duplicates, even if they have different names or minor metadata variations. The detected duplicates are presented in stacks, offering an organized way to review and remove them, thereby helping photographers manage storage and declutter their extensive libraries.

In conclusion, while the updates in Lightroom Classic version 15.4 might not be revolutionary, they represent practical and valuable enhancements to core functionalities. The improved masking tools offer greater precision, Assisted Culling streamlines the selection of group photos, the new AI metadata filters provide better organization for AI-enhanced images, and the Duplicate Finder is a powerful tool for efficient library management. These features collectively contribute to a more efficient and precise workflow for photographers.
