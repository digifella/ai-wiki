## Australia's Long-Range Climate and Water Outlook: October-December 2026
**Clip title:** Climate and water long-range forecast, issued 1 October 2026
**[[entities/tasia-custode|Author]] / channel:** Bureau of Meteorology
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J0eHXbQ9D88

### Summary
The [[entities/bureau-of-meteorology|Bureau of Meteorology]]'s [[concepts/long-range-forecast|long-range forecast]] for October to December 2026 details expected rainfall and [[concepts/temperature-trends|temperature trends]] across Australia, highlighting a period of varied weather conditions with a notable leaning towards warmer temperatures. Climatologist [[entities/caitlin-minney|Caitlin Minney]] presented the outlook, emphasizing regional differences in precipitation and the widespread likelihood of [[concepts/above-average-temperatures|above-average temperatures]].

September 2026 served as a precursor to these trends, registering as Australia's second warmest September on record, driven by dominant high-[[concepts/pressure|pressure]] systems. This resulted in warmer-than-average temperatures across most of the continent, with specific daily records broken in southern locations like Albany, WA (31.8°C), and Parafield, SA (35.6°C). Rainfall patterns were varied, with above-average precipitation in the western interior and far north, but below-average conditions across the southwest and much of the eastern half, particularly the Murray-Darling Basin, which experienced its second driest September on record. These dry and warm conditions led to elevated fire danger and bushfires in Queensland and New South [[entities/united-kingdom|Wales]]. The 2026 snow [[entities/season|season]] in alpine regions also saw record warmth, with much of the precipitation falling as rain rather than snow, leading to an unprecedented [[concepts/concept-of-nothingness|zero]] snow depth at Spencer's Creek, NSW, for the first time in September since 1954. Soil moisture generally decreased across eastern and southwestern Australia, while inland southern regions maintained wetter-than-average soils, impacting streamflow which was largely below average in the south and east.

Looking ahead to October-December, the rainfall forecast indicates a significant divide: above-average rainfall is predicted for much of Australia's western half, extending into parts of Queensland and New South [[entities/united-kingdom|Wales]] later in the [[entities/season|season]]. Conversely, below-average rainfall is expected in the far north and southeast. October specifically is anticipated to have a drier start for [[concepts/northern-australia|northern Australia]]. Simultaneously, both daytime and nighttime temperatures are very likely to be above average across almost the entire continent. This translates to an increased [[concepts/probability|chance]] of unusually warm days, particularly across the southern two-thirds of Australia and the southeast, and a very high likelihood of unusually warm nights nationwide for the remainder of the year.

The overarching climate [[concepts/causes|drivers]] influencing this forecast include warming sea surface temperatures around the Australian region, which further increases the risk of marine heatwaves, especially in the southeast. El Niño is expected to strengthen further, likely peaking in late spring or summer, and a brief positive Indian Ocean Dipole event also remains a possibility. In summary, Australia [[concepts/faces|faces]] an increased risk of heatwaves and elevated fire danger over the coming warmer months, coupled with significant regional variations in rainfall, indicating a period requiring close monitoring of weather and water resources.
