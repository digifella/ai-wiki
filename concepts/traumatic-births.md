# Traumatic Births

Traumatic birth refers to childbirth experiences that result in significant psychological distress for those present, including the birthing person and their support partners. Research indicates that approximately 30% of births may be experienced as traumatic by attendees. These experiences can stem from various factors including medical emergencies, unexpected complications, loss of control during labour, inadequate pain management, or feeling unsupported during the birthing process. The psychological impact of traumatic birth can persist long after delivery, affecting mental health and bonding.

## Psychological Impact and Risk Factors

The distress associated with traumatic birth often manifests as post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), anxiety, or depression. Key risk factors include a lack of informed consent, perceived threat to life, and feelings of isolation or powerlessness during the procedure. For support partners, witnessing the distress of the birthing person without the ability to intervene effectively can also lead to secondary traumatic stress. The duration and intensity of symptoms vary, with some individuals recovering naturally while others require clinical intervention.

## Enhancing Service Delivery Through Male Attendance

Improving service delivery involves recognizing the role of support partners, particularly fathers, in mitigating trauma. Men's attendance during labour and birth has been linked to improved outcomes for both the birthing person and the newborn. Facilitating active participation allows fathers to provide emotional and physical support, which can enhance the birthing person's sense of control and safety. Healthcare systems are increasingly encouraged to adopt policies that welcome and prepare fathers for this role, ensuring they are equipped to support their partners through the birthing process.
