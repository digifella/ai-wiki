# Bernhard Riemann
Bernhard Riemann (1826-1866) was a German mathematician who made significant contributions to the fields of analysis, geometry, and [[concepts/number-theory|number theory]]. His work laid foundational elements for modern [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] and [[concepts/physics|physics]].

## Contributions
- **[[concepts/riemann-hypothesis|Riemann Hypothesis]]**: Proposed in 1859, this conjecture relates to the [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] of [[concepts/prime-numbers|prime numbers]], suggesting that the non-trivial zeros of the [[concepts/riemann-zeta-function|Riemann zeta function]] all have real part ½.
- **Geometry**: Introduced elliptic geometry as an alternative to Euclidean geometry, which influenced Einstein's [[concepts/theory|theory]] of general relativity.

## Legacy
Riemann's hypothesis continues to be one of the most famous [[concepts/unsolved-problems|unsolved problems]] in mathematics. The Clay Mathematics Institute offers a $1 million prize for its proof or disproof.
