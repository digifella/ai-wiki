# Deep Field

**Deep Field** refers to astronomical images of a small, seemingly empty region of the sky, taken with long [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] times to detect faint, distant objects. These observations are critical for studying [[concepts/galaxy-formation-and-evolution|galaxy evolution]], the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]], and the large-scale structure of the cosmos.

## Key Observations

*   **[[concepts/james-webb-space-telescope|James Webb Space Telescope]] ([[concepts/jwst|JWST]]) Deep Field (2026):** A recent 100-hour observation campaign targeting the [[concepts/fornax-constellation|Fornax constellation]] revealed previously unseen [[concepts/distant-galaxies|distant galaxies]].
    *   See detailed analysis: [[lab-notes/2026-09-16-James-Webbs-100-Hour-Deep-Field-Unveiling-Distant-Galaxi|James Webb's 100-Hour Deep Field: Unveiling Distant Galaxies in Fornax]]
    *   Source material: [James Webb's 100-Hour Deep Field: Unveiling Distant Galaxies in Fornax](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R3J36iLf2Q)
*   **[[concepts/hubble-deep-field|Hubble Deep Field]]:** The pioneering effort that established the methodology for deep sky surveys, revealing thousands of galaxies in a tiny patch of sky.
*   **Methodology:** Involves staring at a single patch of sky for extended periods (e.g., 100+ hours) to accumulate enough [[concepts/light|light]] from faint, high-redshift objects.
