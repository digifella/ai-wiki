# Iceberg Collision

In 1985, while conducting a classified military operation, the [[entities/us-navy|US Navy]] inadvertently discovered the wreck of the RMS Titanic on the floor of the [[concepts/north-atlantic|North Atlantic Ocean]]. The mission's primary objective was to locate and investigate two sunken nuclear submarines: the USS Thresher and the USS Scorpion, which had been lost decades earlier during the Cold War. To accomplish this task, the Navy employed advanced deep-sea imaging technology and sonar equipment that had been developed for submarine detection and naval research.

## The Discovery

During the search operations, the equipment and research team led by [[entities/dr-robert-ballard|Robert Ballard]] identified an unusual object on the seafloor. Further investigation revealed it to be the Titanic, which had sunk in 1912 after striking an iceberg. The wreck lay at a depth of approximately 3,700 meters (12,000 feet), far deeper than many had expected. The accidental discovery became one of the most significant archaeological finds of the twentieth century, providing the first visual confirmation of the ship's final resting place and extensive photographic evidence of its condition.

## Historical Significance

The discovery of the Titanic through a military operation highlighted the dual-use potential of naval technology and contributed substantially to understanding the mechanics of the ship's sinking. The classified [[entities/nature|nature]] of the mission meant that details about the discovery were initially restricted, though the findings eventually became public and generated worldwide interest in maritime archaeology and the history of the vessel.
