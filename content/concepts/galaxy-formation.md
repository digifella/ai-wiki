# Galaxy Formation

Galaxy formation is the process by which galaxies assemble and evolve in the universe, beginning shortly after the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]] and continuing to the present day. The formation and early development of galaxies remains a central question in [[concepts/cosmology|cosmology]], as observations reveal galaxies existed when the universe was only a few hundred million years old—earlier than many theoretical models predicted. Understanding how these first galaxies formed, what composed them, and how they grew over [[concepts/cosmic-time|cosmic time]] requires both advanced observational instruments and refinement of [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|theoretical frameworks]].

## Early Universe Observations

Recent observations from the [[concepts/jwst|James Webb Space Telescope]] (JWST) have provided new evidence regarding the composition of the earliest galaxies. Spectroscopic analysis of the galaxy [[entities/gn-z11|GN-z11]], located at a redshift indicating the universe was approximately 200 million years old, has revealed signatures consistent with [[concepts/first-generation-of-stars|Population III stars]]—the hypothetical first generation of stars composed almost entirely of hydrogen and helium. The detection of such primordial stellar populations in GN-z11 offers empirical support for theoretical predictions about early galactic composition and star formation timescales in the infant universe.

## Ongoing Questions

Current observations continue to refine our understanding of galaxy formation [[concepts/physics|physics]]. Recent studies examining [[concepts/galaxy-clusters|galaxy clusters]] have indicated that [[concepts/baryonic-matter|baryonic matter]] content may have been underestimated in previous surveys, which has implications for [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] models and the balance between ordinary and dark matter in large-scale cosmic structures. As observational capabilities improve, models of galaxy formation must be adjusted to account for these new measurements and the prevalence of surprisingly massive and mature galaxies in the early universe.
