# Skillet Cooking

Skillet cooking is a versatile cooking method that uses a flat-bottomed, typically [[concepts/rounding|round]] pan with sloped sides to prepare food over direct heat. Skillets are a fundamental tool in kitchens across many culinary traditions, offering efficient heat [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] and easy ingredient manipulation. The [[concepts/design|design]] allows cooks to quickly sauté, fry, braise, and finish dishes, making it particularly suitable for preparations that require both stovetop cooking and occasional oven finishing.

## Applications in Curry Preparation

Skillet cooking has proven effective for preparing [[concepts/curries|curry dishes]], particularly in North Indian cuisine. The pan's design facilitates the blooming of spices in hot oil or ghee, a critical step that develops the foundational flavors of curry. [[entities/neel-kajale|Chef Neel Kajale]] demonstrates this technique in his North Indian [[concepts/chicken-curry|chicken curry]] [[concepts/preparation|preparation]], using a skillet to layer flavors through controlled heat and sequential addition of aromatics, spices, and protein before simmering until the dish reaches desired [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]].

## Advantages for Home Cooking

The skillet method offers practical advantages for home cooks preparing complex dishes like curries. A single pan reduces cleanup while allowing for visual monitoring of ingredient development. The sloped sides facilitate [[concepts/stirring-technique|stirring]] and ingredient incorporation, while the concentrated cooking surface promotes [[concepts/even-heat-distribution|even heat distribution]]—important for achieving balanced flavor development and proper protein cooking in dishes that benefit from both quick browning and extended simmering.
