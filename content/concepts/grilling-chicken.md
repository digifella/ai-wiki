# Grilling Chicken

Grilling chicken presents a challenge in maintaining [[concepts/moisture-content|moisture content]], as the high, dry heat can quickly evaporate internal fluids and result in dry meat. Several established techniques address this issue, with marinade selection, cut choice, and cooking method forming the primary variables that [[concepts/power|influence]] the final [[concepts/texture|texture]] and juiciness of grilled chicken.

## Marinading

[[concepts/chicken-marination|Marinating chicken]] before [[concepts/bbq|grilling]] serves multiple functions. Acidic marinades containing ingredients such as vinegar, citrus juice, or yogurt can break down muscle proteins slightly, allowing the meat to retain more moisture during cooking. Marinades also add flavor throughout the meat rather than just on the surface. Optimal marinating time typically ranges from 30 minutes to several hours, depending on the marinade composition and chicken thickness.

## Cut Selection

The choice of cut significantly affects grilling outcomes. Bone-in, skin-on pieces such as thighs and drumsticks retain moisture more effectively than boneless, skinless breasts due to their higher fat content and the insulating properties of bone and skin. Thinner cuts cook faster and more evenly, reducing the risk of the exterior drying out before the interior reaches safe internal temperatures.

## Cooking Methods

Temperature control and [[concepts/cooking-time|cooking duration]] are critical to achieving juicy results. Cooking chicken over moderate heat rather than high heat allows the interior to reach proper doneness before the exterior becomes overdried. Using indirect heat—where chicken cooks next to rather than directly over flame—provides more even cooking. Monitoring internal temperature with a meat thermometer and removing chicken [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] 165°F (74°C) prevents overcooking. Allowing cooked chicken to rest for several minutes before serving permits redistribution of juices throughout the meat.
