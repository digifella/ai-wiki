## AI-Powered Cyberattacks: Dark Sourcery, LLM Hacking, and Agent Swarms
**Clip title:** Can you [[concepts/trust|trust]] your chatbot? Inside three AI-powered cyberattacks
**[[entities/tasia-custode|Author]] / channel:** IBM Technology
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dHn0qzSDMO0

### Summary
This "[[concepts/security|Security]] Intelligence" podcast episode delves into the evolving landscape of cyber threats, highlighting how [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] is being leveraged by attackers to create more sophisticated and scalable campaigns. The central theme revolves around the panelists' collective concern that people, and even organizations, are perhaps too quick to [[concepts/trust|trust]] [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]], forgetting fundamental [[concepts/cybersecurity|cybersecurity]] principles in the face of new technology. The discussion focuses on three primary AI-enabled [[concepts/cybersecurity-threats|attack vectors]] that are changing the [[concepts/cybersecurity|cybersecurity]] game: [[concepts/dark-sourcery|Dark Sourcery]], LLM-assisted hacking sprees, and agent swarm attacks.

The first major threat discussed is "Dark Sourcery," an attack identified by Vigilance Security where malicious actors employ "[[concepts/solution|Answer]] [[concepts/engine|Engine]] Optimization" (AEO) to poison the [[concepts/custom-dataset|training data]] of [[concepts/ai-chatbots|AI chatbots]] like ChatGPT and [[concepts/gemini|Google Gemini]]. This technique is akin to [[concepts/traditional-seo|traditional SEO]] poisoning but aims to manipulate AI-generated responses with misinformation and phishing links. For instance, a user might ask an AI for a company's customer support number and be provided with a fraudulent [[entities/contact|contact]]. A critical takeaway emphasized by the panelists is the alarming statistic that 91% of regular AI users do not verify the information they receive, making them highly susceptible. The experts [[concepts/stress|stress]] that this is an "old trick" of attacking trust, now with a new AI angle, underscoring the urgent need for users and [[concepts/ai-agents|AI agents]] alike to adopt a "trust but verify" [[concepts/mindset|mindset]].

The episode then explores two related attack types: LLM-assisted hacking sprees and agent swarm attacks. GreyNoise reported a threat actor who used a [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|Large Language Model (LLM)]] to develop 17 unique scripts, bypassing Microsoft's anti-malware, to exploit [[concepts/critical-security-risks|critical vulnerabilities]] in ubiquitous systems like WordPress and ZyXEL. This campaign resulted in the theft of thousands of documents from a Western government, executed with remarkable [[concepts/speed|speed]] – remote [[concepts/code-execution|code execution]] achieved in roughly four hours. In a similar vein, an "agent swarm attack" utilized hundreds of AI agents to breach PaperCut print management software, subsequently attacking [[concepts/microsoft-windows|Windows]] Active Directory environments across 48 countries. A startling revelation from both cases is that even the attackers sometimes struggle to control their AI agents, leading to unintended targets.

In conclusion, the panelists consistently underscore that while AI dramatically [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] and accelerates these attacks, the underlying vulnerabilities are often not novel. The core issue remains a lapse in fundamental cybersecurity hygiene. They urge individuals and businesses to prioritize basic [[concepts/risk-mitigation|security measures]] such as prompt patching of critical vulnerabilities, implementing robust identity and access management, and enforcing multi-factor authentication. [[entities/dave-plummer|Dave]] McGinnis succinctly summarizes AI as a "force multiplier" that can be wielded for both offense and defense. Ultimately, the message is a clear call to action: treat these AI-powered threats as a "wake-up call" to re-evaluate and strengthen existing defenses, reminding everyone that "locking your digital doors" is more crucial than ever in this rapidly evolving threat landscape.
