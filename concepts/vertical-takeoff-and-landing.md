# Vertical Takeoff And Landing

**[[concepts/vertical-takeoff-and-landing-vtol|Vertical takeoff and landing]]** ([[concepts/vertical-take-off-and-landing|VTOL]]) refers to an aircraft's ability to ascend, descend, and hover without requiring a runway. This capability distinguishes VTOL aircraft from conventional fixed-wing aircraft, which depend on extended runway infrastructure for [[concepts/crew-operations|flight operations]]. VTOL technology enables flight operations in environments where runway construction or maintenance is impractical, including urban areas, remote locations, and disaster zones.

## Design Approaches

Several mechanical approaches enable VTOL flight. Rotorcraft such as helicopters use rotating blades to generate [[entities/lift|lift]] in all directions. [[concepts/tiltrotor-technology|Tiltrotor]] aircraft, exemplified by the [[entities/v-22-osprey|V-22 Osprey]], employ tilting rotors that function as helicopter blades during vertical flight and transition to fixed-wing propellers during forward flight. Other designs include quadcopters and multicopter drones, which use multiple propellers controlled electronically to achieve vertical [[concepts/exercise|movement]] and hover.

## Applications

VTOL aircraft serve diverse operational roles across military, civilian, and emergency response sectors. Military applications include troop transport, reconnaissance, and combat support. In civilian contexts, VTOL technology supports search and rescue operations, medical evacuation, and transport to locations without developed airports. Urban air mobility and cargo delivery represent emerging applications as VTOL technology advances.

## Operational Considerations

VTOL aircraft typically consume more fuel than fixed-wing alternatives during forward flight due to increased aerodynamic drag and mechanical complexity. Hover capability and vertical maneuverability provide operational advantages that offset these efficiency costs in many applications. [[concepts/pilot-training|Pilot training]] requirements and maintenance complexity generally exceed those of conventional aircraft, reflecting the technical demands of VTOL systems.
