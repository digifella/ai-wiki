# A Video Titled P Vs Np

"[[concepts/complexity-classes|P vs NP]]" refers to videos, documentaries, or educational content that explain one of computer science's most significant [[concepts/unsolved-problems|unsolved problems]]. The P vs NP question asks whether every problem whose [[concepts/solution|solution]] can be quickly verified by a computer can also be quickly solved by a computer. P represents problems solvable in polynomial time, while NP represents problems whose solutions can be verified in polynomial time. This distinction has profound implications for [[concepts/cryptography|cryptography]], optimization, and computational [[concepts/theory|theory]].

## The Core Concepts

Videos exploring this topic typically break down the fundamental difference between these two complexity classes. P problems are those that a computer can solve directly in reasonable time—such as sorting a list or finding the shortest path between two points. NP problems, by [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], may be difficult to solve from scratch, but once a potential solution is provided, its correctness can be checked quickly. The famous question is whether these two classes are equivalent: if P = NP, it would mean every verifiable problem is also solvable in polynomial time, which most computer scientists believe is unlikely.

## Significance and Applications

The P vs NP problem carries substantial real-world weight. If P equals NP, modern cryptography would become fundamentally compromised, as many encryption systems rely on the assumption that certain problems are hard to solve but easy to verify. Educational videos on this topic often illustrate how the problem connects to practical fields like [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]], drug discovery, and logistics optimization. The question remains unsolved, with the Clay [[concepts/mathematics|Mathematics]] Institute offering a one-million-dollar prize for either a proof or disproof of P = NP.
