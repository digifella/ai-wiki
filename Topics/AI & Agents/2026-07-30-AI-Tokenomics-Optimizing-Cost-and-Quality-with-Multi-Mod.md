## AI Tokenomics: Optimizing Cost and Quality with Multi-Model Workflows
**Clip title:** You NEED to do this (HUGE AI SAVINGS)
**[[entities/tasia-custode|Author]] / channel:** Matthew Berman
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QNEo_tl-nhw

### Summary
This video explains how expert AI users are moving beyond basic [[concepts/prompt-based-modeling|prompt engineering]] to optimize their AI usage by understanding "tokenomics," aiming to increase quality while simultaneously decreasing cost. The core idea presented is that not all [[concepts/tokens|tokens]] or [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] are created equal in terms of quality, [[concepts/speed|speed]], or expense, and strategic selection is key.

The [[entities/speaker|speaker]] first defines "[[concepts/tokens|tokens]]" as the fundamental units of language models, often akin to words or sub-word units, which models use to process and generate text. He demonstrates how different words can comprise varying numbers of tokens. Crucially, the video emphasizes the concept of "intelligence [[concepts/density|density]]" within tokens. While some models might be cheaper per token, they may require significantly more tokens to complete a task with the same level of intelligence or accuracy as a more expensive model. For instance, a comparison between [[concepts/gpt-56-sol|GPT-5.6]] and the [[concepts/open-source|open-source]] [[concepts/kimi-k3|Kimi K3]] model shows that despite [[entities/kimi|Kimi]]'s lower per-token price, it costs more per completed task due to its lower [[concepts/intelligence-density|intelligence density]], meaning it needs more tokens to think through and solve a problem.

To achieve both high quality and cost-effectiveness, the video proposes a multi-model workflow: "Planner, Executor, Reviewer." The most intelligent, albeit expensive, models (like Fable or [[concepts/gpt-56-sol|GPT-5.6]]) should be used for complex planning tasks, which involve high input tokens but low output tokens. Cheaper, faster models (such as [[concepts/grok|Grok]] 4.5 or Composer) are then utilized for the execution [[concepts/phase|phase]], where the volume of output tokens (e.g., [[concepts/writing|writing]] code) is high, making [[concepts/cost-efficient-solutions|cost efficiency]] paramount. Finally, a [[concepts/excellence|high-quality]] model (like GPT-5.6) can review the output, again requiring high input but low output tokens. This blended approach significantly reduces the overall cost of a complex task (e.g., from $81 using one model to $25.55 using a mixture) while maintaining superior results, also factoring in the benefit of faster output speed from the cheaper models during execution.

The video concludes by discussing the broader [[concepts/economic-implications|economic implications]], highlighting the competition between [[concepts/proprietary-ai|closed-source AI]] [[entities/labs|labs]] (like [[concepts/whisper-transcription|OpenAI]] and [[entities/anthropic-institute|Anthropic]]) and the emerging [[concepts/reasoning-models|open-source models]]. Closed-source models currently offer high intelligence density but come with higher costs and larger profit margins for their creators. Conversely, open-source models, despite sometimes requiring more tokens per task, [[concepts/ambition|drive]] down [[concepts/inference|inference]] costs due to broader [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]] and competition among service providers. This competition ultimately benefits end-users by making AI more affordable and accessible, shifting profitability within the [[concepts/ai-ecosystem|AI ecosystem]] from token sales to hardware and application layers. Understanding these tokenomics is essential for developers and businesses to make informed decisions in the rapidly evolving [[concepts/ai-landscape|AI landscape]].
