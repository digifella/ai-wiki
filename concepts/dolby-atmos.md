# Dolby Atmos

**[[entities/dolby|Dolby]] Atmos** is an [[concepts/object-based-audio|object-based audio]] technology developed by Dolby Laboratories that allows sound engineers to place and move individual sounds in a three-dimensional space, including overhead. Unlike traditional channel-based formats (e.g., 5.1 or 7.1), Atmos treats [[concepts/audio-modality|audio]] as discrete objects with [[concepts/metadata|metadata]] defining their position and [[concepts/exercise|movement]].

## Core Technology
- **Object-Based Audio:** Sounds are not tied to specific channels but are rendered dynamically based on the playback environment.
- **Height Channels:** Utilizes ceiling-mounted or upward-firing speakers to create a vertical sound dimension.
- **Dynamic Metadata:** Allows for scene-by-scene or even sample-by-sample adjustment of audio levels and positioning.

## Home Theater Implementation & Criticism
While Atmos is designed for immersive, multi-[[entities/speaker|speaker]] setups, widespread [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] in home environments has led to significant debate regarding its actual implementation.

- **Simulated Atmos:** Many consumer soundbars and entry-level receivers use "simulated" Atmos, relying on psychoacoustic tricks and virtualization rather than true object-based [[concepts/fat-rendering|rendering]] through dedicated height channels Dolby Atmos Deception: Object-Based Audio vs. Widespread [[concepts/simulated-home-theater-sound|Simulated Home Theater Sound]].
- **The "Deception" Argument:** Critics argue that the marketing of Atmos often obscures the gap between the ideal object-based [[concepts/experience|experience]] and the widespread reality of simulated, channel-based upmixing in average home setups.
- **Hardware Dependency:** True Atmos benefits require specific hardware configurations (e.g., 7.1.4 or higher) to realize the intended spatial audio benefits.
