# Orbital Health Risks

Physiological and psychological threats to human [[concepts/health|health]] resulting from [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] to the [[concepts/vacuum-of-space|space environment]], including microgravity, cosmic radiation, and [[concepts/disconnection|isolation]]. These risks are compounded by mission duration and distance from [[entities/earth|Earth]], particularly in missions beyond [[concepts/low-earth-orbit-leo|Low Earth Orbit]].

## Primary Risk Categories

*   **Microgravity Effects**:
    *   Musculoskeletal degradation (bone [[concepts/density|density]] loss, muscle atrophy).
    *   Cardiovascular deconditioning and fluid shift anomalies.
    *   Neuro-otological disorders affecting balance and [[concepts/computer-vision|vision]] ([[entities/sans-institute|SANS]]).
*   **Radiation Exposure**:
    *   Galactic [[concepts/cosmic-rays|Cosmic Rays]] (GCRs) and Solar Particle Events (SPEs).
    *   Increased long-term cancer risk and potential acute radiation sickness.
    *   Central nervous system cognitive deficits.
*   **Psychosocial Stressors**:
    *   Confinement, isolation, and crew [[concepts/cohesion|cohesion]] dynamics.
    *   [[concepts/sleep|Sleep]] disruption and circadian rhythm misalignment.
*   **Operational Constraints**:
    *   Limited medical resources and inability for rapid evacuation.
    *   Delayed communication with ground-based medical support.

## Recent Developments and Capability Gaps

*   Current medical protocols are optimized for LEO but insufficient for [[concepts/deep-space-exploration|deep space]] missions where resupply and emergency return are not viable.
*   Research is shifting towards closed-[[concepts/loop|loop]] life support systems and autonomous diagnostic tools.
*   Key studies are focusing on mitigating cumulative [[concepts/radiation-damage|radiation damage]] and maintaining musculoskeletal [[concepts/integrity|integrity]] over extended durations.
