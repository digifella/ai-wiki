# Photo Culling

Photo culling is the process of reviewing and selecting photographs from a larger set, typically to identify the best or most usable [[concepts/images|images]] for a project. This is a standard workflow step in photography, following a shoot and preceding editing or delivery. Culling requires evaluating images based on criteria such as [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]], composition, [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], subject matter, and artistic intent.

## Traditional approach

Manual culling involves reviewing images one by one, often using [[concepts/software|software]] like Lightroom or Capture One to flag or rate selected images. This process can be time-consuming, particularly with large batches of photographs from events or sessions where hundreds or thousands of images may have been captured.

## AI-assisted culling

Recent developments in photography software, notably Adobe Lightroom's AI-powered assisted culling features, offer automated or semi-automated approaches to the culling process. These tools can analyze images and suggest selections based on technical quality, similarity detection, and other criteria, potentially reducing the time required to process large image libraries.
