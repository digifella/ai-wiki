# Sensor size

Physical dimensions of a camera's image sensor, directly influencing light capture, noise levels, [[concepts/dynamic-range|dynamic range]], and depth of field. Larger sensors generally enable superior performance in low-light conditions and wider dynamic [[concepts/range|range]].

Key implications:
- **Full-frame sensors** (36×24mm) maximize light gathering and dynamic range (common in DSLR and [[concepts/mirrorless-photography|Mirrorless camera systems]]).
- **Smartphone sensors** (typically 1/2.3" or smaller) prioritize compactness but sacrifice [[concepts/low-light-performance|low-light performance]] and dynamic range compared to larger sensors.
- **Dynamic range** and **low-light performance** are significantly enhanced by larger sensor area due to greater photon capture capacity.

Recent developments:
- [[entities/colin-smith]], founder of [[entities/photoshopcafe]], highlights in his introduction of Adobe Project Indigo that while traditional cameras (DSLRs/mirrorless) excel in low-light and dynamic range due to larger sensors, smartphones have surpassed [[concepts/point-and-shoot-cameras|point-and-shoot cameras]] through convenience, larger viewfinders, and integrated editing/sharing workflows.
