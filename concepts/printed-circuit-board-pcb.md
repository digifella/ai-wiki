# Printed Circuit Board (PCB)

A Printed Circuit Board (PCB) is a flat board composed of insulating material, typically fiberglass-reinforced epoxy resin, that serves as the structural and electrical foundation for electronic devices. The board's surface is layered with conductive copper traces that create electrical pathways between component connection points, eliminating the need for individual wiring. This design enables compact, reliable, and reproducible assembly of electronic circuits.

## Structure and Composition

PCBs range from simple single-layer designs to complex multi-layer configurations containing dozens of copper layers separated by insulation material. In multi-layer boards, internal copper layers are interconnected through small drilled holes called vias, which allow electrical signals to pass between layers. The insulating substrate provides mechanical support and electrical isolation between conductive elements, while the copper traces are typically etched away from a larger copper sheet using chemical processes or direct printing techniques.

## Manufacturing and Applications

PCB manufacturing involves several key steps: designing the circuit layout, transferring the design onto the copper surface, etching away unwanted copper, drilling component holes, and applying protective coating. Once manufactured, electronic components such as resistors, capacitors, and microchips are soldered onto the board at designated connection points. PCBs are fundamental to virtually all modern electronics, from consumer devices like smartphones and computers to industrial equipment and aerospace systems.
