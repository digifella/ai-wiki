# 256 QAM

256 [[concepts/quadrature-amplitude-modulation-qam|Quadrature Amplitude Modulation]] (256 QAM) is a digital modulation technique that encodes data by varying both the amplitude and phase of a carrier signal simultaneously. The scheme generates 256 distinct signal states, with each state corresponding to a unique 8-bit binary sequence. This allows 256 QAM to transmit 8 bits of information per symbol, achieving reasonable spectral efficiency for radio frequency communication channels.

## Technical Structure

In 256 QAM, the 256 constellation points are arranged in a 16×16 grid within the complex plane, where the in-phase (I) and quadrature (Q) components each take 16 discrete amplitude levels. This orthogonal arrangement enables efficient separation of information on two independent carrier signals at the same frequency. The modulation technique balances spectral efficiency gains against increased sensitivity to noise and signal distortion, as the closer spacing of constellation points requires more precise signal detection.

## Application in Satellite Communications

256 QAM is relevant to high-capacity [[concepts/satellite-communication|satellite communication]] systems, including configurations used in [[entities/spacex-starlink|SpaceX Starlink]] v3 [[concepts/architecture|architecture]]. In such [[concepts/software|applications]], the modulation scheme supports higher data throughput per transmission channel while operating within constrained bandwidth allocations. The technique's viability depends on maintaining adequate signal-to-noise ratio and managing multipath interference in the satellite-to-ground propagation environment.
