# Task Involvement

**Task Involvement** refers to the degree to which an individual identifies psychologically with a specific task, values task performance highly, and takes personal pride in doing a good job. It is a critical individual difference variable that moderates the relationship between job characteristics and work outcomes.

## Core Mechanics

- **Moderating Effect**: High task involvement strengthens the correlation between Job Diagnostic Model characteristics (e.g., [[concepts/skill|skill]] variety, task significance) and outcomes like internal work [[concepts/motivation|motivation]] and job satisfaction.
- **Psychological Identification**: Individuals with high involvement define themselves through their task performance; poor performance threatens their self-concept.
- **Prerequisite for Diagnosticity**: For job design interventions to be effective, [[entities/employees|employees]] must possess sufficient task involvement to care about the diagnostic [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] provided by the job structure.

## Integration with Team Effectiveness Frameworks

- **[[entities/hackman|Hackman]]'s Model**: According to [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Hackman-Five-Conditions-for-Team-Effectiveness-Teams| Teams]], [[concepts/team-effectiveness|team effectiveness]] relies on structural enablers. Task involvement operates at the individual level within these structures, influencing how team members engage with Task Interdependence and utilize [[concepts/performance-feedback]].
- **Individual-Team Alignment**: High individual task involvement ensures that members contribute meaningfully to the Shared Goal, reducing social loafing and enhancing overall Team [[concepts/cohesion|Cohesion]].
