# Avro Canada

**Avro Canada** (A.V. Roe Canada Limited) was a Canadian aircraft manufacturer based in Malton, Ontario, known for pioneering jet aircraft design in North [[entities/america|America]]. The company is most famous for the CF-100 Canuck interceptor and the cancelled Avro Arrow supersonic interceptor. Its history is marked by rapid [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] followed by abrupt government cancellation of major projects, leading to the dispersal of its [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] talent.

## Key Projects & Concepts

*   **Avro Arrow**: The company's most iconic project, a delta-winged, Mach 2 interceptor cancelled in 1959, causing a significant brain drain from the Canadian aerospace industry.
*   **CF-100 Canuck**: A successful twin-[[concepts/engine|engine]] jet interceptor that served as the backbone of the Royal Canadian Air Force during the early Cold War.
*   **Avro C102 Jetliner**: A proposed commercial jet airliner intended to compete with the Boeing 707 and Douglas DC-8.
    *   Details on this unfulfilled promise are documented in [[lab-notes/2026-07-15-Avro-C102-Jetliner-Canadas-Unfulfilled-Promise-in-Commer|Avro C102 Jetliner: Canada's Unfulfilled Promise in Commercial Jet Aviation History]].
    *   The project represents a largely forgotten chapter in Canadian aerospace history, overshadowed by the military focus of the Arrow program.

## Historical Context

Avro Canada emerged from the British A.V. Roe company's operations in Canada. Under the [[concepts/leadership|leadership]] of Roy Bieler and chief engineer [[entities/chef-john|John]] Frost, the firm achieved technological parity with leading US and UK manufacturers. However, political decisions regarding defense spending and the viability of the commercial jet market led to the termination of the Arrow and the C102 Jetliner projects, effectively ending Avro Canada's independent aircraft manufacturing operations.
