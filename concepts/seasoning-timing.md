# Seasoning Timing

Salt serves as a fundamental seasoning tool for home cooks, with its effectiveness largely determined by the specific type used and its physical properties. The most common culinary salts—table salt, kosher salt, and sea salt—differ significantly in crystal size and density. These variations directly impact how the salt measures and dissolves during the cooking process, making it essential for cooks to understand these distinctions to achieve consistent results.

Table salt is characterized by fine, tightly packed crystals that dissolve rapidly. Due to its high density by volume, a teaspoon of table salt contains significantly more sodium than the same volume of kosher or sea salt. This concentration requires careful measurement to avoid over-salting, particularly in recipes where precise seasoning is critical.

Kosher salt features larger, flakier crystals that are easier to pinch and distribute evenly by hand. Its lower density means that volume-based measurements yield less sodium per teaspoon compared to table salt. This characteristic allows for greater control during the cooking process, making it a preferred choice for seasoning meats and vegetables where immediate surface application is desired.

Sea salt varies widely in crystal structure depending on its source and processing method. While often used for finishing dishes due to its distinct texture and flavor profile, its irregular shape and density make it less predictable for baking or precise volumetric cooking. Understanding these physical differences ensures that cooks can adjust quantities appropriately when substituting one salt type for another.
