# Ethylene Production

Ethylene is a gaseous plant hormone that plays a crucial role in fruit ripening across many species, including bananas. It is produced naturally by ripening fruits and triggers a cascade of biochemical changes that alter fruit color, [[concepts/texture|texture]], and flavor. The gas diffuses through plant tissues and can also affect nearby fruits, making it a key factor in coordinating ripeness within fruit clusters and [[entities/storage|storage]] environments.

## Role in Banana Ripening

In bananas, ethylene production increases significantly as the fruit matures, initiating the transition from green to yellow. The hormone activates enzymes responsible for chlorophyll breakdown, starch conversion to sugars, and the development of characteristic banana flavor compounds. This process is temperature-dependent, proceeding more rapidly in warmer conditions and slowing considerably in refrigerated storage.

## Commercial Applications

The ability to control ethylene [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] allows producers to manage ripening timing across large shipments and retail environments. Ethylene generators are commonly used in ripening rooms to accelerate the process deliberately, while ethylene absorbers or controlled-atmosphere storage can slow ripening to extend shelf life. This control is essential for global banana [[concepts/distribution|distribution]], where fruits are typically harvested green and ripened near their point of sale.
