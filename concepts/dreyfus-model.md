# Dreyfus Model

The Dreyfus Model is a framework for understanding skill acquisition developed by Stuart and Hubert Dreyfus in the 1980s. It describes five stages of competence that practitioners progress through as they develop expertise: novice, advanced beginner, competent, proficient, and expert. The model emphasizes that skill development involves fundamental shifts in how learners perceive problems and apply knowledge, rather than simple linear accumulation of facts.

## Stages of Competence

The model delineates a progression from rule-based learning to intuitive decision-making. Novices rely heavily on context-free rules and lack the situational perception to handle complex scenarios. As individuals advance to the level of advanced beginner, they begin to recognize recurring situational aspects but still struggle with prioritizing tasks. Competent practitioners develop a conscious, deliberate plan and can handle complex situations by analyzing cause and effect, though this process remains cognitively demanding.

Proficient individuals perceive situations holistically rather than analytically, allowing them to recognize patterns and prioritize information effectively. This stage marks a shift from analytical processing to intuitive recognition. At the final stage of expertise, practitioners operate from an unconscious deep understanding. They no longer rely on rules or maxims but instead respond to situations based on a deep, contextual grasp of the domain, enabling rapid and accurate decision-making without conscious deliberation.
