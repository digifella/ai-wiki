# Bbq Chicken

BBQ chicken refers to chicken prepared using [[concepts/bbq|barbecue]] [[concepts/cooking-methods|cooking methods]], most commonly grilling or smoking, with the application of barbecue sauce, dry rubs, or other seasonings. The cooking technique imparts characteristic smoky flavors and develops a caramelized exterior while the meat remains moist inside. BBQ chicken is a staple across many regional barbecue traditions, particularly in North American cuisine, though variations exist globally in terms of sauce styles, wood choices, and cooking temperatures.

## Preparation and Cooking

BBQ chicken is typically prepared by placing whole birds, halves, or individual pieces on a grill or smoker [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] moderate to low temperatures. Grilling usually occurs at higher heat over direct or indirect flame, while smoking uses indirect heat with smoldering wood for several hours. Marinade, dry rubs, or wet seasoning may be applied before cooking, with barbecue sauce often added during the final stages to prevent excessive charring. Cooking times vary depending on the cut, temperature, and method, with whole birds requiring several hours while individual pieces cook more quickly.

## Regional Variations

Different regions have developed distinct approaches to BBQ chicken. North American barbecue traditions vary significantly by geography, with differences in sauce composition (ranging from vinegar-based to tomato-based to mustard-based), preferred wood types, and cooking temperatures. The dish also appears in international cuisines with local [[concepts/adaptations|adaptations]], incorporating regional spices and sauce styles while maintaining the core technique of smoke and heat cooking.
