# Tonal Music

Tonal music is a system of musical [[concepts/organization|organization]] centered around a primary pitch called the tonal center or tonic. This system establishes a [[concepts/hierarchy|hierarchy]] of pitches within a key or scale, where the tonic serves as the point of harmonic rest and resolution. Most Western popular music, classical music from the Baroque through Romantic periods, and many non-Western musical traditions employ tonal organization as their foundational structural principle.

## Harmonic Function

Within tonal music, chords and individual pitches function in relation to the tonal center. The major and minor [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] provide the pitch material, and harmonic progressions create tension and release by moving away from and returning to the tonic. Common functional progressions, such as [[concepts/exercise|movement]] from the dominant chord back to the tonic, reinforce the listener's sense of tonal stability and expectation. This harmonic vocabulary allows composers and performers to create coherent musical narratives with clear points of arrival and departure.

## Historical Context

Tonal organization became the predominant musical system in European classical music beginning in the Baroque period (circa 1600) and remained central through the 19th century. The system's clarity and flexibility made it well-suited to instrumental music, opera, and large-scale compositional forms. In the 20th century, some composers moved away from tonality entirely, developing atonal and serial techniques, while others continued to work within tonal frameworks or blend tonal and non-tonal elements.
