# Time Measurement Precision

**Time Measurement [[concepts/accuracy|Precision]]** refers to the fundamental and practical limits on the accuracy with which temporal intervals can be resolved. These limits arise from thermodynamic constraints, quantum mechanical uncertainty, and potentially, the granularity of spacetime itself at the [[entities/planck|Planck]] scale.

## Theoretical Limits

### Quantum Limits
Standard quantum mechanics imposes bounds via the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle. Precise time measurement requires high-energy probes, which introduces uncertainty in energy and potentially disturbs the system being measured.

### Planck Scale Constraints
At the scale of Planck Time ($t_P \approx 5.39 \times 10^{-44}$ s), it is hypothesized that spacetime may be discrete or "foamy," [[concepts/fat-rendering|rendering]] finer [[concepts/solution|resolution]] physically meaningless or impossible.

## Recent Developments: Quantum Gravity Bounds

Recent research suggests that the interplay between General Relativity and [[concepts/quantum-mechanics]] imposes a stricter, fundamental limit on clock precision than previously thought.
