# Pan Frying Bacon

Pan frying bacon is a stovetop cooking method in which bacon strips are placed in a skillet or frying pan and cooked over direct heat. As the bacon heats, its fat renders, creating both the cooking medium and much of the finished product's characteristic flavor. This method remains popular in home kitchens because it requires minimal equipment and technique, though it does demand attention to prevent burning.

## Technique

The process typically begins by placing bacon strips in a cold skillet without overlapping them. The pan is then heated over medium or medium-high heat. As the temperature rises, the fat gradually liquefies and distributes throughout the pan. Strips are occasionally turned to ensure even cooking on both sides, typically taking 8–12 minutes total depending on desired crispness and bacon thickness. The rendered fat can be reserved for other cooking purposes or discarded based on preference.

## Considerations

The main advantage of pan frying is its simplicity and the ability to cook multiple strips simultaneously. However, the method requires monitoring to prevent uneven cooking or charring, and splashing fat is a common hazard. Temperature control is important—too high heat risks burning the exterior while leaving the interior undercooked, while too low heat results in greasy rather than crisp bacon. Once cooked, strips are typically transferred to paper towels to drain excess fat.
