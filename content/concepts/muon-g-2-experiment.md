# Muon G-2 Experiment

The Muon g-2 experiment is a precision measurement study conducted at Fermilab that aims to measure the [[concepts/anomalous-magnetic-moment|anomalous magnetic moment]] of the muon particle. This property, expressed as the g-2 factor, describes how strongly a muon interacts with magnetic fields. The experiment represents one of the most precise tests of the [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|Standard Model of particle physics]], as theoretical predictions can be compared directly against experimental measurements to identify potential discrepancies that might indicate new [[concepts/physics|physics]].

## Methodology and Significance

The experiment involves storing muons in a carefully controlled magnetic field and observing how their spin precesses relative to their momentum. By measuring this precession frequency with extreme precision, researchers can determine the g-2 value and compare it against theoretical calculations. Any deviation between measured and predicted values could suggest the existence of undiscovered particles or forces not accounted for in current models. Previous measurements from Brookhaven National Laboratory showed hints of such a discrepancy, making confirmation at Fermilab particularly important.

The Fermilab collaboration expects to release results from its ongoing measurements in 2025, which will either reinforce or challenge the existing [[concepts/theory|theoretical framework]] and potentially provide insights into physics beyond the Standard Model.
