# Jwst

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) is a space-based infrared observatory developed through international collaboration between NASA, the European Space Agency (ESA), and the Canadian Space Agency (CSA). Launched on December 25, 2021, it succeeded the Hubble Space Telescope as humanity's primary space-based observatory for astronomical research. The telescope operates at the Sun-Earth L2 Lagrange point, approximately 1.5 million kilometers from Earth, where it maintains a stable orbit while remaining shielded from solar radiation.

## Design and Instrumentation

JWST features a 6.5-meter primary mirror composed of 18 hexagonal segments made of beryllium, coated with gold to optimize infrared reflection. This large aperture allows the telescope to collect more light than previous infrared observatories, enabling the observation of faint, distant objects. The instrument suite includes the Near Infrared Camera (NIRCam), the Near Infrared Spectrograph (NIRSpec), the Mid-Infrared Instrument (MIRI), and the Fine Guidance Sensor/Near Infrared Imager and Slitless Spectrograph (FGS/NIRISS). These instruments allow JWST to study every phase of cosmic history, from the luminous first stars following the Big Bang to the formation of galaxies, solar systems, and planetary systems.

## Scientific Objectives

The primary scientific goals of JWST involve investigating the early universe, galaxy evolution, star formation, and planetary systems. By observing in the infrared spectrum, the telescope can penetrate cosmic dust clouds that obscure visible light, revealing processes hidden from optical telescopes. It is designed to detect light from the first galaxies formed after the Big Bang, analyze the atmospheres of exoplanets for potential biosignatures, and study the physical properties of celestial bodies within our own solar system. The observatory's capabilities extend to understanding the lifecycle of stars and the chemical composition of interstellar medium.
