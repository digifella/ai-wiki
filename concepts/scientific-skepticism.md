# Scientific Skepticism

**Scientific skepticism** is the practice of examining claims critically by seeking verifiable evidence and applying standards of evidence. It relies on the [[concepts/scientific-method]] to evaluate hypotheses, distinguishing between extraordinary claims requiring extraordinary evidence and those supported by robust data.

## Core Principles

- **Empiricism**: Knowledge derives from sensory [[concepts/experience|experience]] and observable evidence.
- **Falsifiability**: Claims must be testable and potentially disprovable (Karl Popper).
- **Burden of [[concepts/proof|Proof]]**: Lies with the claimant, not the skeptic.
- **Extraordinary Claims**: Require proportionate levels of evidence.

## Application in Risk Assessment & Media Literacy

Skepticism is vital for evaluating existential risks and media narratives, preventing bias-driven panic or complacency. This is particularly relevant in high-stakes technological domains where hype often outpaces [[concepts/verification|verification]].
