# Nursing Roles

[[concepts/community-health-nursing|Community health nursing]] encompasses several distinct professional roles, each contributing to population-level [[concepts/health|health]] outcomes and individual care. These roles define the scope of practice, responsibilities, and focus areas for nurses working in community settings rather than hospital environments. Understanding these roles is essential for clarifying professional boundaries and ensuring appropriate allocation of nursing [[concepts/expertise|expertise]] within health systems.

## Common Community Nursing Roles

[[concepts/community-health|Community health]] nurses work across multiple settings including primary care clinics, [[concepts/public-health|public health]] departments, schools, and clients' homes. Public health nurses focus on [[concepts/health-promotion|disease prevention]], health promotion, and population-level intervention, often managing infectious [[concepts/contact-tracing|disease surveillance]] and [[concepts/vaccination|immunization]] [[concepts/software|programs]]. Home health nurses provide direct [[concepts/clinical-care|clinical care]] to individuals with chronic conditions or post-acute illness in domestic settings. School nurses address the [[concepts/patient-needs|health needs]] of school-age children, managing both acute health issues and preventive [[concepts/health-care|health services]]. Occupational health nurses work within workplace settings to protect employee health and safety.

## Specialist and Leadership Roles

Within [[concepts/fundamentals-of-nursing|community health nursing]], some nurses develop specialized expertise or assume [[concepts/leadership|leadership]] positions. Nurse practitioners and clinical nurse specialists may provide advanced assessment and treatment in community clinics. [[concepts/population-health|Community health]] nursing supervisors and program managers coordinate services and ensure quality standards across teams. Parish nurses integrate spiritual care with health services within faith communities. These expanded roles require additional education and certification beyond basic nursing [[concepts/preparation|preparation]].

All community nursing roles share common characteristics: emphasis on health promotion and [[concepts/primary-prevention-interventions-designed-to-prevent-disease-before-it-occurs|disease prevention]], focus on vulnerable or underserved populations, and [[concepts/coordination|coordination]] with other health and social services. The specific responsibilities and required qualifications vary by role, setting, and regulatory jurisdiction.
