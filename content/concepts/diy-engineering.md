# DIY Engineering

DIY engineering refers to the practice of designing, building, and modifying technical systems and devices outside of traditional institutional or commercial settings. This approach emphasizes [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]], experimentation, and hands-on [[concepts/learning|learning]], enabling individuals and small groups to engage in engineering work using available tools and resources. DIY engineering projects [[concepts/range|range]] from simple repairs and modifications to complex device development, and are facilitated by the availability of affordable components, online documentation, and community knowledge sharing.

## Open Source and Collaboration

A significant aspect of DIY engineering is its [[concepts/connection|connection]] to [[concepts/open-source|open-source]] principles, where designs, [[concepts/code|code]], and technical specifications are made publicly available for modification and redistribution. This model encourages collaborative [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] and accelerates [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] by allowing multiple contributors to build upon existing work. The [[entities/nano-banana|Nano-banana]] project exemplifies this approach, demonstrating how open-source [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] can be developed and distributed through community participation rather than conventional manufacturing channels.

## Tools and Methodologies

Modern DIY engineering has been enhanced by advances in digital fabrication, computational [[concepts/design|design]], and automated development tools. [[concepts/software|Software]] systems and AI-assisted technologies are increasingly employed to streamline [[concepts/design-workflows|design workflows]], improve [[concepts/testing|testing]] efficiency, and reduce barriers to entry for practitioners. These tools enable individuals to prototype [[concepts/ideas|ideas]] rapidly, simulate behavior before [[concepts/physical-construction|physical construction]], and document processes in ways that facilitate knowledge transfer within DIY communities.
