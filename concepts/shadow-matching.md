# Shadow Matching

Shadow matching is the process of ensuring shadows in a compositing workflow align with the environmental light source, color temperature, and intensity. When combining visual elements from different sources—such as placing a photographed object into a new background—the shadows cast by that element must correspond to the same lighting conditions as the scene. This alignment is essential for maintaining visual consistency, as viewers intuitively recognize how light behaves in physical space.

## Technical Considerations

Effective shadow matching requires analyzing several interdependent variables to achieve photorealism. The direction of the shadow must match the angle of the primary light source in the target environment, while the softness of the shadow edge depends on the size and distance of that light source. Additionally, the color of the shadow is rarely pure black; it is influenced by ambient light bounce and the color temperature of the surrounding environment, requiring careful tinting to avoid a "floating" appearance.

Intensity and opacity adjustments are also critical components of the process. Shadows must adhere to the inverse square law, appearing darker closer to the object and fading as they extend outward. In digital compositing, artists often use gradient maps or blur filters to simulate this falloff, ensuring that the shadow integrates seamlessly with the ground plane’s texture and lighting. Failure to account for these factors results in visual dissonance, breaking the illusion of depth and realism.
