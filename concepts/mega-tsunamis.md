# Mega Tsunamis

Mega tsunamis are exceptionally large tsunami events characterized by wave heights exceeding 100 meters. Unlike conventional tsunamis, which typically result from seafloor earthquakes and produce waves of 10–30 meters, mega tsunamis are driven by sudden, massive displacements of water caused by extreme geological phenomena. These rare occurrences represent some of the most powerful water movements in Earth's systems.

The primary generation mechanism for mega tsunamis is volcanic flank collapse, where a significant portion of a volcano's structure fails and slides into the ocean. This process displaces vast volumes of water instantaneously, creating waves of catastrophic scale. A documented example is the 1888 Ritter Island eruption in Papua New Guinea, which triggered a wave estimated to be 100 meters tall. Other potential causes include massive landslides and asteroid impacts, though volcanic activity remains the most frequently cited geological source for these events.
