# Seasoning Timing

Salt serves as a fundamental seasoning tool for home cooks, with its effectiveness largely determined by the specific type used and its physical properties. The most common culinary salts—table salt, kosher salt, and sea salt—differ significantly in crystal size and density. These variations directly impact how the salt measures and dissolves during the cooking process, making it essential for cooks to understand these distinctions to achieve consistent results.

Table salt is characterized by fine, compact crystals that result in a higher density compared to other varieties. Consequently, a teaspoon of table salt contains more salt by weight than the same volume of kosher or sea salt. This density difference means that substituting table salt for other types without adjusting the volume can lead to over-salting, as the smaller crystals pack more tightly into the measuring spoon.

In contrast, kosher salt and sea salt feature larger, irregular crystals with more air space between them. This structure results in a lower density, requiring larger volumes to deliver the same amount of sodium as table salt. Understanding these physical differences allows cooks to adjust their measurements appropriately, ensuring that the seasoning level remains accurate regardless of the specific salt variety chosen for a recipe.
