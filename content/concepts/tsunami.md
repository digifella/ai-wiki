# Tsunami

A tsunami is a series of large ocean waves caused by sudden, large-scale disturbances of water. The primary triggers include submarine [[concepts/earthquakes|earthquakes]], volcanic eruptions, underwater landslides, and meteorite impacts. Of these, undersea earthquakes account for the majority of destructive tsunamis. The waves can travel across entire ocean basins at speeds of 500–800 kilometers per hour in deep water, crossing ocean basins in just hours.

## Formation and Propagation

Tsunamis originate when a disturbance suddenly displaces a large volume of water. Subduction zone earthquakes are particularly effective at generating tsunamis because the sudden vertical [[concepts/exercise|movement]] of the seafloor transfers energy directly to the overlying water column. In deep ocean water, tsunami waves may be only a meter high but have wavelengths of hundreds of kilometers. As waves approach shallow coastal waters, they slow down and the water column compresses, causing the waves to increase dramatically in height—sometimes exceeding 30 meters or more.

## Coastal Impact

Unlike wind-driven surface waves, tsunami waves extend through the entire water column and can carry enormous energy onto shore. When a tsunami reaches the coast, it typically arrives as a series of waves rather than a single surge. Some tsunamis arrive with little warning, while others are preceded by a noticeable withdrawal of water from the shore. Coastal flooding, erosion, and the destructive force of the surge can cause significant loss of life and infrastructure damage. Early warning systems based on earthquake detection and ocean buoys have improved the ability to alert populations in vulnerable areas.
