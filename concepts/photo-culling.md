# Photo Culling

Photo culling is the process of reviewing and selecting photographs from a larger set to identify the best or most usable images for a project. It is a standard workflow step in photography that occurs after a shoot and before editing or final delivery. During culling, photographers evaluate images based on technical criteria such as sharpness and exposure, as well as subjective factors including composition, subject matter, and alignment with artistic intent.

## Manual and Assisted Culling

Traditionally, culling is a manual process where the photographer individually reviews each image, often using software tools to flag favorites, reject unusable shots, or rate images on a scale. This method requires significant time and attention to detail, particularly when dealing with high-volume shoots such as weddings or events.

Modern workflows increasingly utilize artificial intelligence to accelerate this phase. Software like Adobe Lightroom employs AI to automatically analyze images for technical quality, grouping similar shots and suggesting selections based on learned patterns. While AI assists in filtering out duplicates and obvious technical failures, the final artistic judgment typically remains with the photographer to ensure the selected images meet specific creative goals.
