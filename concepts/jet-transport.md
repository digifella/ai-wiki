# Jet Transport

**Jet transport** refers to the use of Jet Engines for the propulsion of aircraft designed primarily for the carriage of passengers or cargo. This technology marked a [[concepts/mindset-shift|paradigm shift]] in [[concepts/aviation-history]], drastically reducing travel times and increasing operational altitudes compared to Propeller Aircraft.

## Historical Context & Development
The transition from piston-[[concepts/engine|engine]] airliners to jet-powered transports occurred primarily in the mid-20th century. Key milestones include the introduction of the De Havilland Comet and the Boeing 707, which established the standard for modern commercial aviation.

## Notable Projects & Case Studies

### Avro C102 Jetliner
A significant, albeit unrealized, chapter in Canadian aerospace history is the **Avro C102 Jetliner**. Often overshadowed by the more famous Avro Arrow interceptor, the C102 represented a serious attempt by Avro Canada to enter the commercial jet market.

*   **Status:** Cancelled/Unfulfilled.
*   **Significance:** Represents a "tragic and largely forgotten" aspect of Canadian aerospace [[concepts/ambition|ambition]], highlighting the risks and political complexities of early jet development outside the US and UK.
*   **Source Integration:** Detailed analysis of this project is available in [[lab-notes/2026-07-15-Avro-C102-Jetliner-Canadas-Unfulfilled-Promise-in-Commer|Avro C102 Jetliner: Canada's Unfulfilled Promise in Commercial Jet Aviation History]].

## Technical Characteristics
*   **Propulsion:** Utilizes Turbojet or Turbofan engines.
*   **Performance:** Higher cruise speeds (typically Mach 0.7–0.85) and higher service ceilings than propeller-driven contemporaries.
*   **Efficiency:** Early jet transports suffered from high fuel consumption; modern High-Bypass Turbofans have significantly improved Fuel Efficiency.
