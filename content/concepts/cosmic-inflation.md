# Cosmic Inflation

Cosmic inflation is a [[concepts/theory|theoretical framework]] in [[concepts/cosmology|cosmology]] describing the universe's rapid exponential expansion in the fraction of a second following the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]]. This theory explains several observed properties of the universe, including its large-scale uniformity and the origin of [[concepts/structure|structure]] that eventually led to galaxies and [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]]. Inflation theory has become central to modern cosmology and continues to be refined through observations and theoretical research.

## Current Research

Research into cosmic inflation and related [[concepts/physics|physics]] takes place [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] institutions including the [[entities/perimeter-institute|Perimeter Institute]], where scientists investigate fundamental questions about the universe's creation and early evolution. A significant focus of contemporary research involves the search for [[concepts/wimp-particles|weakly interacting massive particles]] ([[concepts/wimps|WIMPs]]), hypothetical [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] candidates that could account for much of the universe's non-[[concepts/baryonic-matter|baryonic matter]].
