# Social Security Overpayments

Social [[concepts/security|security]] overpayments occur when a government agency disburses more benefit funds to a recipient than they are entitled to receive. These can arise from administrative errors, changes in a recipient's circumstances that were not communicated to the agency, or calculation mistakes. Recovery of overpaid amounts typically becomes the responsibility of the recipient, often through debt collection processes or deduction from future payments.

## The Robodebt Scheme

Australia's [[concepts/algorithmic-error|Robodebt Scheme]] represents a significant case of systematic [[concepts/overpayment-recovery|overpayment recovery]] through automated means. Implemented between 2015 and 2019 by the Department of Human Services, the scheme used an algorithm to identify and recover alleged overpayments from welfare recipients. The system cross-referenced income data from the Australian Taxation Office against declared income during welfare periods to identify discrepancies, automatically generating debt notices without human review.

The scheme was later found to be unlawful, with courts ruling that the algorithm's methodology was flawed and that many of the debts raised were invalid. The Australian government eventually abandoned the program and announced compensation for affected individuals. The case raised significant questions about the appropriate use of [[concepts/decision-making|automated decision-making]] in social security administration, particularly regarding due process and the [[concepts/verification|verification]] of data [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]] before debt recovery action.
