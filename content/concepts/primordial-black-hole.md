# Primordial Black Hole

Primordial black holes (PBHs) are hypothetical [[concepts/black-holes|black holes]] formed in the very [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]], within the first fraction of a second after the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]], rather than through stellar collapse as with conventional black holes. They would have formed from density fluctuations in the primordial plasma when the universe was extremely hot and dense. Unlike stellar black holes, which typically have masses of several solar masses, primordial black holes could [[entities/theoretically-media|theoretically]] span an enormous [[concepts/range|range]]—from microscopic masses up to millions of solar masses—depending on the conditions in the early universe.

## Gravitational Wave Evidence

Recent gravitational wave detections have renewed interest in primordial black holes as a viable candidate. The detection of sub-solar mass black hole mergers—objects lighter than our Sun—presents a puzzle for conventional stellar formation [[concepts/theory|theory]], since the standard processes that create black holes through stellar collapse cannot produce such low masses. Primordial black holes offer a plausible explanation for these observations, as they could naturally populate this mass range if they formed in sufficient abundance during the early universe.

## Significance and Constraints

If primordial black holes exist in significant numbers, they could account for some or all of the [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] in the universe, solving one of [[concepts/cosmology|cosmology]]'s major mysteries. However, observational constraints from various sources—including gravitational lensing surveys, pulsar timing arrays, and other electromagnetic observations—limit the abundance of PBHs across most of the mass spectrum. Current evidence suggests that if primordial black holes comprise dark matter, they must occupy relatively narrow mass [[entities/windows|windows]], with the sub-solar mass range being one of the most promising candidates based on recent gravitational wave data.
