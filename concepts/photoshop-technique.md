# Photoshop Technique

Focus stacking is a photographic method primarily employed in landscape photography to overcome the limitations of depth of field in a single exposure. By capturing a series of images with different focus points—typically targeting the foreground, middle ground, and background separately—photographers can ensure that every element within the scene is rendered sharply. This approach addresses the optical constraint where a wide aperture needed for low light results in a shallow depth of field, while a narrow aperture needed for sharpness may introduce diffraction or require impractically long exposure times.

The process begins during the shooting phase, where the photographer uses a tripod to maintain consistent framing across all shots. The focus point is shifted incrementally from the nearest object to the furthest visible point, ensuring that each frame captures a specific plane of the scene in sharp focus. Modern cameras often feature automated focus bracketing functions to streamline this capture sequence, reducing the risk of camera movement between frames.

In post-processing, the individual images are imported into software such as Adobe Photoshop. The software analyzes the sharpness of each layer and automatically blends the in-focus portions of each image into a single composite. This results in a final photograph where depth of field appears extended, allowing both near and distant subjects to remain crisp and detailed without the need for excessive stopping down of the lens aperture.
