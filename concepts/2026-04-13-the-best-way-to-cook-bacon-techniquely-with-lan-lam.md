By starting with raw bacon submerged in water, the process aims to control the rendering of fat and prevent the uneven burning often associated with high-heat frying.

This approach relies on the principle of boiling the bacon in water before the liquid evaporates, allowing the meat to cook gently and evenly. As the water boils away, the bacon begins to fry in its own rendered fat, resulting in a texture that is consistently crisp without the risk of charring. This method is highlighted for its ability to produce uniform results across multiple strips simultaneously, addressing common issues with hot spots in skillets or uneven heat distribution in ovens.

The technique is positioned as a practical alternative for home cooks seeking reliability over speed.
