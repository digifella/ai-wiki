# USB-C Connectivity

USB-C is a modern connector standard that provides bidirectional power delivery, high-speed data transfer, and support for multiple protocols through a single port. Unlike legacy connectors, USB-C's reversible design eliminates the frustration of orientation-dependent insertion and offers a standardized interface across consumer electronics. The [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of USB-C on tablets like iPads represents a shift away from proprietary connectors toward industry-standard connectivity.

## Impact on iPad Productivity

The integration of USB-C ports on iPads expands the device's functional capabilities by enabling direct [[concepts/connection|connection]] to external peripherals, displays, and [[entities/storage|storage]] devices. Users can connect high-speed external drives, external monitors, cameras, and other USB-C accessories without requiring adapters. This direct connectivity supports workflows that previously required workarounds or wireless alternatives, making iPads more suitable for professional and creative tasks.

## Technical Advantages

USB-C connectivity on iPads supports higher power delivery than previous [[concepts/open-standards|standards]], enabling faster charging and powering of external devices. The port simultaneously handles data transfer at speeds compatible with modern workflows, including [[concepts/video-editing|video editing]], photography, and file management. USB-C's support for multiple protocols—including DisplayPort and Thunderbolt on select models—allows for diverse [[concepts/scenarios|use cases]] through a single connector type, reducing accessory fragmentation and simplifying the [[concepts/user-experience-design|user experience]].
