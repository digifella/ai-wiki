# Territorial Gains

**Territorial gains** refer to the acquisition of land or strategic positions by a belligerent force during armed [[concepts/conflict|conflict]]. In the context of the Ukraine-[[entities/russia|Russia]] Conflict, these gains are measured not only by square kilometers but by their impact on [[concepts/supply-lines|supply lines]], defensive depth, and political leverage.

## Strategic Dynamics

*   **Offensive vs. Defensive Postures**: Gains are often temporary if not consolidated with fortifications. The shift from rapid maneuver warfare to attritional trench systems has slowed the rate of territorial change.
*   **Logistical [[concepts/vulnerability|Vulnerability]]**: Holding territory requires [[concepts/secure|secure]] supply routes. Disruption of logistics can negate territorial advantages, forcing retreats despite initial advances.
*   **Fortress Belts**: Defensive structures designed to slow enemy advance.

## Ecological Parallels: Land Reclamation

The concept of acquiring and holding territory extends to ecological [[concepts/preservation|restoration]], where "gaining" land involves reclaiming degraded ecosystems from [[concepts/invasive-species|invasive species]] or agricultural use.

*   **Gorse-Based Rewilding**: Contrary to conventional weed management, botanist [[entities/hugh-wilson|Hugh Wilson]] utilized [[concepts/gorse|gorse]] (*Ulex europaeus*) as a pioneer species to restore [[concepts/degraded-farmland|degraded farmland]] on [[entities/Banks-Peninsula|Banks Peninsula]], New Zealand.
*   **[[concepts/ecological-succession|Ecological Succession]]**: This approach leverages invasive species to stabilize soil and create microclimates, facilitating the return of [[concepts/native-flora|native flora]] and fauna, effectively "acquiring" ecological [[concepts/honesty|integrity]] over previously lost land.
*   **Case Study**: See [[lab-notes/2026-07-10-Banks-Peninsula-Rewilding-Hugh-Wilsons-Gorse-Based-Ecosy|Banks Peninsula Rewilding: Hugh Wilson's Gorse-Based Ecosystem Restoration]] for detailed analysis of this method.
