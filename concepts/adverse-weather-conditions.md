# Adverse Weather Conditions

**Adverse Weather Conditions** refer to [[concepts/atmospheric-phenomena|atmospheric phenomena]] that degrade [[concepts/crew-operations|flight operations]], increase pilot workload, and elevate accident risk. Key factors include reduced visibility, wind shear, turbulence, icing, and precipitation static. These conditions often act as catalysts for operational errors, particularly when compounded by Human Factors such as fatigue, [[concepts/stress|stress]], or poor [[concepts/crew-resource-management-crm|Crew Resource Management (CRM)]].

## Operational Impact & Risks
- **Visibility Reduction**: Fog, rain, and snow limit situational [[concepts/conscious-thought|awareness]], increasing reliance on instruments and raising the risk of controlled flight into terrain (CFIT) or runway excursions.
- **Aerodynamic Degradation**: Ice accumulation disrupts airflow, increasing drag and reducing [[entities/lift|lift]]; precipitation can cause [[concepts/engine|engine]] flameouts or sensor errors.
- **Wind Shear & Turbulence**: Sudden changes in wind speed/direction can cause rapid loss of airspeed and altitude, particularly during takeoff and landing phases.

## Case Studies & Historical Context
- [[entities/usair-flight-5050|USAir Flight 5050]]: LaGuardia Incident Exposes Critical Crew and CRM Failures: A 1989 incident where adverse weather at [[entities/laguardia-airport|LaGuardia Airport]] combined with severe CRM breakdowns and pilot incapacitation led to a runway excursion. This event highlights how weather stressors can mask or exacerbate critical crew [[concepts/coordination|coordination]] failures.

## Mitigation Strategies
- **Pre-flight Planning**: Rigorous assessment of METARs/TAFs and alternate route planning.
- **CRM Protocols**: Enhanced communication [[concepts/loops|loops]] and cross-checking under high-workload weather [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]].
- **Technology**: Utilization of Predictive Windshear Alert Systems (PWAS) and advanced autopilot modes for turbulence penetration.
