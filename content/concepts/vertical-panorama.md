# Vertical Panorama

Vertical panorama is a photography technique that combines multiple overlapping [[concepts/images|images]] taken in a vertical sequence to create an elongated composition. Unlike traditional panoramas that extend horizontally, vertical panoramas extend the frame vertically, allowing photographers to capture tall subjects or scenes that exceed the vertical field of view of a single [[concepts/exposure|exposure]]. The technique requires shooting multiple frames from bottom to top (or top to bottom) with sufficient overlap between consecutive images, typically 30-40% to ensure accurate stitching.

## Creation and Stitching

Creating a vertical panorama involves methodical framing and consistent [[concepts/exposure-settings|exposure settings]] across all shots. Photographers typically use a tripod with a level head to maintain consistent horizontal alignment while moving vertically between frames. [[concepts/software|Software]] stitching applications automatically align the overlapping regions and blend the images together, accounting for perspective distortion and exposure variations. Modern smartphone cameras and dedicated panorama apps have made the technique more accessible to casual photographers, though manual stitching with [[concepts/desktop-apps|desktop software]] often yields higher quality results for demanding compositions.

## Applications

Vertical panoramas are particularly suited to architectural photography, landscape vistas with prominent height elements, and situations where a standard tall crop proves insufficient. Examples include capturing entire building facades, deep canyons, towering trees, or dramatic skies above landscapes. The technique can also address the limitations of extreme [[concepts/telephoto-lens|telephoto]] focal lengths or ultra-wide-angle lenses when neither adequately frames the intended subject matter.
