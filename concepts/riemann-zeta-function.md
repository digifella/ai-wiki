# Riemann Zeta Function

The Riemann zeta function is a complex analytic function of fundamental importance to number theory and mathematical analysis. Formally defined for complex numbers with a real part greater than 1, it is expressed as the infinite series $\zeta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^s}$. Through the process of analytic continuation, mathematicians have extended its definition to nearly all complex numbers, revealing deep structural properties that are central to modern mathematics.

## Prime Number Distribution

The zeta function's primary significance lies in its connection to the distribution of prime numbers. The non-trivial zeros of the function are intimately linked to the error term in the prime number theorem, which describes the asymptotic distribution of primes. This relationship is encapsulated in the Riemann hypothesis, a famous unsolved problem that conjectures all non-trivial zeros lie on the critical line where the real part is 1/2.

## Analytic Continuation and Functional Equation

While the original series definition converges only for $\text{Re}(s) > 1$, the function can be extended to the entire complex plane except for a simple pole at $s = 1$. This extension satisfies a functional equation that relates the value of the function at $s$ to its value at $1-s$. This symmetry allows for the study of the function's behavior across the complex plane and is crucial for understanding its zeros and their implications for arithmetic progressions.
