# Abraham-Louis Breguet

**[[entities/abraham-louis-breguet|Abraham-Louis Breguet]]** (1747–1823) was a Swiss watchmaker, inventor, and entrepreneur who is widely regarded as one of the most important figures in the history of horology. He founded the Breguet brand and is credited with numerous innovations that defined modern watchmaking.

## Key Innovations
*   **The Tourbillon:** Invented in 1795 and patented in 1801, this mechanism was designed to counteract the effects of gravity on [[concepts/timekeeping-accuracy|timekeeping accuracy]] in pocket watches.
*   **Breguet Balance Spring:** Introduced the overcoil (pare-chute) end curve to improve isochronism.
*   **Skeleton Dials:** Popularized open-worked dials to showcase the movement's mechanics.
*   **Guilloché:** Mastered the art of engine-turning dials for aesthetic and functional durability.

## The Tourbillon Mechanism
The Tourbillon [[concepts/watch-mechanism|Watch Mechanism]]: Purpose, [[concepts/gravity-compensation|Gravity Compensation]], and 3D-Printed Model serves as a critical reference for understanding the mechanical complexity and historical context of Breguet's most famous invention.

### Purpose and Function
*   **Gravity Compensation:** The primary purpose of the tourbillon is to neutralize the effects of gravity on the escapement and balance wheel. By rotating the entire escapement assembly (balance wheel, pallet fork, and escape wheel) around a central axis, positional errors caused by gravity are averaged out over time.
*   **Accuracy:** Originally intended for pocket watches carried in vertical positions (e.g., waistcoats), the mechanism ensures consistent timekeeping regardless of orientation.
*   **Mechanical Complexity:** Described as the "heart of a watch," the tourbillon represents a pinnacle of micro-engineering, requiring precise assembly and adjustment.

## Legacy
Breguet's innovations laid the foundation for high-end mechanical watchmaking. His influence persists in modern Swiss Watch manufacturing, where the tourbillon remains a symbol of technical mastery and luxury.
