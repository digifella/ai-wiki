# Machine Learning Model

A [[concepts/machine-learning|Machine Learning]] Model is a computational system trained on data to perform specific tasks or [[entities/make|make]] predictions without being explicitly programmed for each outcome. [[concepts/models|Models]] learn patterns from [[concepts/training-data|training data]] and apply those patterns to new, unseen data. They form the foundation of most [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] [[concepts/software|applications]], from [[concepts/natural-language-processing-nlp|language processing]] to image recognition.

## Applications in Document Processing

[[concepts/artificial-intelligence-models|Machine learning models]] have proven particularly valuable for [[concepts/optical-character-recognition-ocr|optical character recognition (OCR)]] tasks, especially in structured document understanding. [[concepts/open-source|Open-source]] OCR models designed for table extraction enable systems to convert complex tabular data into machine-readable [[concepts/text|text]] formats. These models are especially useful in [[concepts/answer-generation|retrieval-augmented generation]] (RAG) applications, where accurate text extraction from documents is essential for feeding information into larger language systems.

## Model Types and Training

Different model architectures serve different purposes depending on the task requirements and computational constraints. [[concepts/custom-models|Specialized models]] like smaller, optimized variants are designed to balance [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]] with efficiency, making them suitable for [[concepts/deployment|deployment]] in production systems where resources may be limited. The choice of [[concepts/architecturetechnique|model architecture]], [[concepts/language-data|training data]] quality, and [[concepts/fine-tuning|fine-tuning]] processes all significantly [[concepts/power|influence]] a model's performance on [[concepts/real-world-tasks|real-world tasks]].
