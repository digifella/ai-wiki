## Melbourne Airport Rail Link: Sunshine Superhub Progress, Airport Connection Challenges

### Summary
[[concepts/melbourne-region|Melbourne]], Australia, remains an outlier among major Australian capital cities, lacking a direct rail link to its airport, 56 years after its opening. The video highlights this discrepancy, noting that cities like Sydney, Brisbane, and Perth have long-established airport rail connections. While Melbourne currently relies on bus services like [[concepts/skybus|SkyBus]], a dedicated [[concepts/melbourne-airport-rail-link|Melbourne Airport Rail Link]] has been planned and its initial construction [[concepts/phase|phase]] has "sort of started," though the full project [[concepts/faces|faces]] significant complexities and delays.

The airport rail project is divided into two key stages. Stage 1, currently underway, involves a substantial $4.1 billion upgrade (jointly funded by state and federal governments) to a 6-kilometre section of railway between West Footscray and Albion. This [[concepts/phase|phase]] is primarily focused on untangling a complex rail junction at Sunshine, where several metro, regional (Ballarat, Bendigo, Geelong), and freight lines currently intersect at grade. The aim is to transform Sunshine Station into a "superhub" by adding new tracks, rail flyovers, and two additional regional platforms, thereby improving efficiency and capacity across Melbourne's western and regional network. This stage is projected to be finished by 2030.

Crucially, Stage 2, which involves building the actual rail line from Sunshine directly to Melbourne Airport, has no construction contracts or a definitive start date. The video explains that the viability of the airport link hinges on the recently completed Melbourne Metro [[concepts/tunnel|Tunnel]]. This new [[concepts/tunnel|tunnel]] frees up capacity in the existing city [[concepts/loop|loop]] by redirecting the Sunbury and Dandenong lines, thus creating the necessary 'spare paths' for airport services to run from Sunshine through [[entities/san-francisco|the city]]'s new underground stations. The project also experienced delays due to a dispute between the State Government and Melbourne Airport over whether the airport station should be above or below ground, pushing the earliest completion date to 2033.

The video concludes by analysing the economics of the Melbourne Airport Rail Link. While airport rail projects often yield poor cost-benefit ratios when solely evaluated on airport passenger numbers (due to a small share of total trips, non-peak travel times, and passenger preference for taxis/cars with luggage), the stronger justification for this project lies in its broader impact. Stage 1, focused on the [[concepts/sunshine-superhub|Sunshine Superhub]] and related infrastructure, is presented as an essential "Western suburbs rail project with an airport badge." It enables future electrification of the Melton line and significantly improves regional train connections, thereby benefitting a rapidly growing population in Melbourne's west. The immediate investment in Stage 1 is deemed valuable for these network-wide benefits, even if Stage 2 (the direct airport [[concepts/connection|connection]]) encounters further delays or changes.
