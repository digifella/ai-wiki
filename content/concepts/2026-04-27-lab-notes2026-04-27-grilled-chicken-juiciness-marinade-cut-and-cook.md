## Marinade Selection and Function

Marinades influence chicken juiciness by altering protein structure and promoting moisture retention during cooking. Acidic components such as citrus juice or vinegar denature surface proteins, creating a barrier that slows moisture loss. Salt-based marinades work through osmosis, allowing the brine solution to penetrate muscle tissue and increase water-holding capacity. The composition and duration of marinating—typically 2 to 8 hours for chicken—affect the depth of moisture absorption and flavor development.

## Cutting Methods and Grain Orientation

The direction and thickness of cuts significantly impact perceived and actual juiciness. Cutting against the grain shortens muscle fibers, reducing the distance moisture must travel during cooking and making the cooked meat feel more tender and juicy. Uniform thickness across pieces ensures even heat distribution, preventing some sections from drying while others remain undercooked. Thinner cuts cook more quickly and retain surface moisture more effectively than thick sections.

## Cooking Techniques

Temperature control and cooking time are critical variables in preserving juiciness. Cooking to an internal temperature of 165°F (74°C)—the food safety minimum—rather than higher temperatures limits protein denaturation and moisture loss. Using moderate direct heat or indirect grilling methods reduces the risk of exterior drying before the interior reaches target temperature. Resting the chicken for 3 to 5 minutes after cooking allows residual heat to distribute evenly and allows muscle fibers to reabsorb some surface moisture before cutting.
