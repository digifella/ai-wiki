# Pi

Pi (π) is a mathematical constant representing the ratio of a circle's [[concepts/circumference|circumference]] to its diameter, approximately equal to 3.14159. It is a [[concepts/transcendental-number|transcendental number]], meaning it is not the root of any polynomial equation with rational coefficients, and its decimal representation never terminates or repeats. Pi appears throughout [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] and [[concepts/physics|physics]], from basic geometry to advanced calculus, making it one of the most fundamental constants in [[concepts/science|science]].

## Cultural and Fictional References

Pi gained particular cultural prominence through [[entities/carl-sagan|Carl Sagan]]'s 1985 science fiction novel *[[entities/contact|Contact]]*. In the narrative, the protagonist [[entities/ellie-arroway|Ellie Arroway]] discovers that when pi is converted to [[concepts/base-11-representation|base-11 representation]], a hidden sequence of 1s and 0s appears [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] a specific position, forming a recognizable message embedded within the digits themselves. This fictional concept explores themes of cosmic intelligence and the idea that mathematical truths might contain deliberately hidden information, though the premise remains speculative and has no basis in actual mathematical properties of pi.

The appearance of pi in *Contact* reflects a broader scientific and philosophical interest in whether fundamental constants might encode meaningful information about the universe or its origins. While pi's digits in any base are believed to be essentially random (a property consistent with its [[entities/nature|nature]] as a transcendental number), Sagan's use of the concept illustrates how [[concepts/mathematical-constants|mathematical constants]] can serve as vehicles for exploring deeper questions about knowledge, communication, and the [[concepts/structure|structure]] of reality itself.
