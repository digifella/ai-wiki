# Vermicelli Cooking

Vermicelli cooking encompasses techniques for preparing thin, delicate noodles used across Asian cuisines. The noodles themselves are typically made from wheat or rice flour and water, extruded into strands thinner than spaghetti. Due to their fine [[concepts/texture|texture]] and [[concepts/quick-cooking|quick cooking]] time, vermicelli dishes are prepared using high-heat, rapid methods that prevent the noodles from becoming mushy.

## Singapore Fried Vermicelli

[[concepts/singapore-fried-vermicelli|Singapore fried vermicelli]] is a stir-fried noodle dish that combines cooked vermicelli with vegetables, protein, and curry-based seasonings. The dish involves quickly tossing the noodles in a hot wok or large pan with oil, aromatics, and other ingredients to develop flavor while maintaining texture. Despite its name, the dish's actual origins are more complex than the Singapore attribution suggests, reflecting the cross-[[concepts/cultural-exchange|cultural exchange]] of recipes and techniques throughout Southeast Asia.

The [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] of Singapore fried vermicelli requires [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to timing and temperature [[concepts/power|control]]. Noodles are typically pre-cooked until just tender, then briefly cooled before the final stir-fry to prevent clumping and ensure even coating with sauce and seasonings. This two-stage cooking process distinguishes the dish from methods where dried noodles are cooked directly in the wok.
