# Scientific Evidence-Based Decision Making

## Definition
The systematic use of current best [[concepts/scientific-evidence|scientific evidence]] from research studies and clinical data to inform individual or organizational decisions. It integrates empirical findings with professional [[concepts/expertise|expertise]] and stakeholder values to minimize bias and maximize outcome [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]].

## Core Principles
*   **[[concepts/hierarchy|Hierarchy]] of Evidence:** Prioritizes [[concepts/excellence|high-quality]] studies (e.g., Randomized Controlled Trials, Systematic Reviews) over anecdotal reports or expert [[concepts/opinions|opinion]] alone.
*   **Critical Appraisal:** Rigorous evaluation of study methodology, sample size, potential conflicts of interest, and [[concepts/statistical-significance|statistical significance]].
*   **Reproducibility:** Preference for findings that are consistent across independent studies and meta-analyses.
*   **Contextual Application:** Adapting general scientific consensus to specific individual or situational constraints.

## Implementation Framework
1.  **Problem Formulation:** Define the decision question clearly (e.g., PICO framework).
2.  **Evidence Gathering:** Search reputable databases for relevant, [[concepts/peer-reviewed-research|peer-reviewed literature]].
3.  **Evaluation:** Assess validity, reliability, and applicability of sources.
4.  **Integration:** Combine evidence with practical constraints and [[concepts/ethical-considerations|ethical considerations]].
5.  **Review & Update:** Continuously monitor new data to refine decisions.

## Common Pitfalls
*   **Confirmation Bias:** Selecting evidence that supports pre-existing beliefs.
*   **Misinterpretation of Correlation:** Assuming [[concepts/causes|causation]] without mechanistic [[concepts/proof|proof]] or controlled testing.
*   **Outdated Information:** Relying on superseded studies without checking for recent meta-analyses.
