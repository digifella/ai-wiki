### Summary
This episode of "[[concepts/mixture-of-experts|Mixture of Experts]]" delves into several significant developments in the [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] landscape, focusing on strategic shifts within major tech companies, the evolving nature of [[concepts/ai-infrastructure-partnerships|AI infrastructure partnerships]], and the impact of AI on [[concepts/consumer-behavior|consumer behavior]] and marketing. The main topics covered include Apple's new CEO and its AI strategy, the deepening alliance between Amazon and [[entities/anthropic|Anthropic]], a customer intent study conducted by IBM and Adobe, and the recent leak of [[entities/anthropic-institute|Anthropic]]'s advanced "[[concepts/mythos|Mythos]]" model.

The discussion begins with Apple's recent [[concepts/leadership|leadership]] change, where [[entities/john-ternus|John Ternus]], with a strong background in hardware development ([[entities/ipad|iPad]], AirPods, [[entities/iphone|iPhone]], Apple Watch), takes the helm as CEO following [[entities/tim|Tim]] Cook's retirement. Experts ponder whether this signals Apple's continued focus on embedding AI primarily within its hardware ecosystem, leveraging its unique "AI moat" through [[concepts/on-device-processing|on-device processing]] and innovative material science like 3D-printed titanium for [[concepts/heat-dissipation|heat dissipation]] in [[concepts/local-large-language-models|local large language models]] (LLMs). While acknowledging Apple's historical conservatism and a perceived "underwhelming" approach to AI compared to competitors, the panelists suggest Ternus's [[concepts/leadership|leadership]] might emphasize [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] and [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]] in AI. However, questions arise about whether Apple will remain solely in the hardware lane or embrace a more expansive AI strategy, potentially transforming its App Store model or even facing challenges from a future where [[concepts/generative-ai|generative AI]] can create apps on the fly.

The conversation then shifts to the burgeoning partnership between Amazon and AI startup [[entities/anthropic-institute|Anthropic]], marked by Amazon's additional $5 billion investment, potentially reaching $25 billion. This deal is characterized as a "quid pro quo" where Anthropic, being compute-constrained, [[concepts/commits|commits]] to purchasing extensive [[concepts/cloud-computing|cloud services]] and specialized [[concepts/ai-chips|AI chips]] (Trainium 2 ASICs) from [[entities/amazon-web-services|Amazon Web Services]] ([[entities/amazon-web-services|AWS]]). This arrangement highlights the growing trend of tight coupling between cloud providers, model developers, and hardware manufacturers, leading to the formation of powerful AI "stacks" in what some refer to as a "Game of Thrones"-like battle for dominance. While this creates mutual dependency and efficiency benefits through specialized hardware, it also raises concerns about potential [vendor lock-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vendor_lock-in) and the future of an open [[concepts/ai-ecosystem|AI ecosystem]]. Amazon currently holds significant leverage in this relationship due to the sheer scale of Anthropic's computational needs.

A key takeaway from an IBM Institute for Business Value (IBV) study in collaboration with Adobe Summit reveals a significant surge in AI's influence on consumer purchasing decisions, increasing by 62% in the last two years. Surprisingly, this [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] is highest among older generations, with 82% of Gen X and 92% of Boomers using AI assistance for buying decisions. This trend is attributed to their lower tolerance for hostile online environments and their disposable income, leading them to delegate purchasing tasks to AI [[concepts/agents|agents]] for a "path of least resistance." This shift is creating an "agentic economy," transforming advertising strategies from targeting human eyeballs to optimizing content for AI [[concepts/agents|agents]], often through APIs and structured [[concepts/metadata|metadata]]. The panelists discuss how the concept of consumer "intent" is evolving, where [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] become proactive intermediaries, and future advertising will need to persuade agents, not just humans.

Finally, the podcast touches on the "funniest AI story of the week"—the alleged leak of Anthropic's highly guarded "[[concepts/mythos|Mythos]]" model, intended for restricted access under "[[entities/project-glasswing|Project Glasswing]]," by a small Discord group. This incident underscores the inherent messiness and challenges of controlling advanced AI, even when deemed too dangerous for public release. The name "[[entities/mythos|Mythos]]" itself evokes narratives of tricksters and stolen fire, suggesting a larger mythological context to the unfolding [[concepts/ai-landscape|AI landscape]]. The speakers conclude that despite efforts to keep powerful [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] under wraps and the formation of [[concepts/strategic-alliances|strategic alliances]], the space will likely eventually democratize, forcing greater transparency and adaptability from all players involved.
