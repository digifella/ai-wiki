# Beta Decay

**Beta decay** is a type of Radioactive Decay in which a Beta Particle (electron or [[concepts/positron|positron]]) and a Neutrino (or antineutrino) are emitted from an Atomic Nucleus. It is mediated by the Weak Interaction.

## Mechanisms

*   **Beta-minus ($\beta^-$) decay**: A neutron converts into a proton, emitting an electron and an electron antineutrino.
    *   $n \rightarrow p + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$
*   **Beta-plus ($\beta^+$) decay**: A proton converts into a neutron, emitting a positron and an electron neutrino.
    *   $p \rightarrow n + e^+ + \nu_e$
*   **Electron Capture**: A proton captures an inner-[[concepts/cli|shell]] electron, converting into a neutron and emitting a neutrino.

## Key Concepts

*   **[[concepts/preservation|Conservation]] Laws**: Beta decay conserves Lepton Number, Charge, and Energy. The emission of the neutrino was hypothesized by [[entities/wolfgang-pauli]] to resolve apparent energy non-conservation in beta spectra.
*   **Neutrino Properties**:
    *   Neutrinos have negligible mass and no electric charge.
    *   They interact only via the weak force and [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]], making them extremely difficult to detect.
    *   Recent insights highlight the role of neutrinos in nuclear stability and cosmic phenomena. See [[lab-notes/2026-07-18-Neutrinos-in-Nuclei-Beta-Decay-Oscillation-Mass-and-Cosm|Neutrinos in Nuclei: Beta Decay, Oscillation, Mass, and Cosmic Unveiling]] for detailed analysis on neutrino oscillation and mass implications.
*   **Q-Value**: The energy released in the decay, determined by the mass difference between parent and daughter nuclei.

## Significance

*   Fundamental to [[concepts/solar-nucleosynthesis|Stellar Nucleosynthesis]] and [[concepts/supernova|supernova]] dynamics.
*   Critical for understanding the [[concepts/standard-model]] of [[concepts/granular-physics|particle physics]].
*   Applications in radiometric dating and [[concepts/medical-imaging-technology|medical imaging]] (PET scans).
