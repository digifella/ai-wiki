# Zippers

A zipper is a fastening device consisting of two parallel strips of material fitted with interlocking teeth or coils that are joined or separated by a slider mechanism. The slider pulls the teeth together to close the zipper or pulls them apart to open it. This mechanical design allows for quick, reliable fastening of garments, bags, and other items. The basic principle—using small, precisely manufactured teeth that interlock under the action of a slider—has remained relatively consistent since the device's practical refinement in the early twentieth century.

## Development and History

The precursor to the modern zipper was the "clasp locker," invented by Whitcomb L. Judson in 1893, which utilized individual hooks and eyes. While innovative, it was cumbersome and prone to jamming. In 1914, Gideon Sundback, a Swedish-American engineer working for the Universal Fastener Company, developed the "Separable Fastener No. 1," which featured a more reliable design with interlocking teeth and a continuous chain. Sundback’s invention, patented in 1917, is widely considered the first practical zipper.

The technology gained widespread commercial adoption in the 1920s and 1930s, particularly in footwear and children's clothing. During World War II, zippers were utilized extensively in military uniforms and gear due to their durability and speed of use. Post-war, the invention of the coil zipper by YKK (Yoshida Kogyo Kabushikikaisha) in the 1930s and its subsequent mass production revolutionized the industry. YKK’s vertical integration and focus on precision manufacturing allowed it to dominate the global market, making the zipper a ubiquitous component in modern textile manufacturing.
