# Patient Data

Patient data refers to any information collected, stored, or processed about individuals within [[concepts/health|healthcare]] and digital health contexts. This encompasses personal identifiers, medical histories, diagnostic results, treatment plans, and ongoing [[concepts/health-metrics|health metrics]]. In digital health environments, patient data exists in electronic formats and becomes subject to specific security, [[concepts/privacy|privacy]], and regulatory requirements designed to protect sensitive personal information.

## Security and Privacy Considerations

The handling of patient data requires robust security infrastructure to prevent unauthorized access, breaches, or misuse. Organizations managing patient data must implement technical safeguards such as encryption, access controls, and audit trails, alongside administrative [[concepts/policies|policies]] that define who can access information and under what circumstances. [[concepts/compliance|Compliance]] with relevant regulations—such as those governing health information privacy—is a fundamental requirement for any system processing patient data.

## Patient Data in Digital Health Surveys

Patient data forms the foundation of digital health research and implementation studies. Surveys examining familiarity with digital health concepts often collect baseline information about respondents' understanding and experience with health-related technologies and [[concepts/terminology|terminology]]. Such surveys typically gather demographic and professional background data alongside [[concepts/responses|responses]] about digital health knowledge, allowing researchers to contextualize findings and identify patterns in [[concepts/adoption|technology adoption]] and awareness across different populations.
