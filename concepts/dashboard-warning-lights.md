# Dashboard Warning Lights

Dashboard warning lights are illuminated [[concepts/indicators|indicators]] on a vehicle's instrument panel that communicate the operational status of [[concepts/engine|engine]] and vehicle systems to the driver. These lights form part of the vehicle's onboard diagnostic system, alerting operators to mechanical, electrical, or fluid-related issues that require [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] or monitoring.

## Function and Color Coding

Warning lights are typically color-coded according to severity. Red lights indicate critical problems requiring immediate [[concepts/attention|attention]], such as [[concepts/engine-failure|engine failure]] or brake system failure, where continued operation may cause damage or safety hazards. Amber or yellow lights signal less urgent but important concerns—such as low oil pressure, check engine warnings, or service reminders—that warrant attention at the next convenient opportunity. Green lights and blue lights generally indicate active systems or available features rather than problems.

## Common Warning Indicators

[[concepts/oil-pressure|Engine oil pressure]] warning lights alert [[concepts/causes|drivers]] when oil pressure drops below safe operating levels, which can lead to engine damage if ignored. Temperature warning lights indicate overheating in the cooling system. Battery warning lights signal charging system failures. Brake system lights warn of low fluid levels or brake pad wear. Check engine lights indicate emissions system problems or other engine-related faults detected by the onboard diagnostic computer. Other common indicators include airbag status, anti-lock braking system (ABS) malfunctions, and transmission problems.

## Importance and Response

Drivers should familiarize themselves with their vehicle's warning [[concepts/light|light]] meanings, as ignoring critical warnings can result in expensive repairs or dangerous driving conditions. Most modern vehicles store diagnostic trouble codes when warning lights activate, allowing mechanics to identify specific problems using diagnostic scanning equipment.
