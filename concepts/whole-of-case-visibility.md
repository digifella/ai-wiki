# Whole Of Case Visibility

Whole of Case Visibility is a governance principle mandating comprehensive transparency and forensic-level auditability for artificial intelligence systems used in high-stakes decision-making, particularly within the criminal justice sector. This concept emerged as a direct response to the wrongful arrest of Farah Jama in Australia, where a face recognition match contributed to his detention despite lacking adequate human scrutiny and documentation. The incident highlighted critical gaps in understanding how algorithmic outputs influence individual liberty, necessitating a framework where every step of an AI’s contribution to a decision is fully traceable.

The principle requires that all data inputs, processing logic, and output generation within an AI system be documented with sufficient detail to allow for independent verification. In contexts involving potential deprivation of liberty, this level of visibility ensures that decisions are not treated as opaque "black boxes" but as auditable processes. It demands that the specific role of the AI in reaching a conclusion be clearly distinguished from human judgment, allowing investigators and courts to assess the validity and reliability of the technological evidence.

Implementing Whole of Case Visibility aims to prevent miscarriages of justice by establishing clear accountability for algorithmic errors or biases. By requiring complete documentation of the AI's involvement, stakeholders can identify where systems failed to provide adequate scrutiny or where human operators relied improperly on automated matches. This approach supports the integrity of legal proceedings by ensuring that technological assistance does not obscure the factual basis of decisions affecting fundamental rights.
