# Public Policy

## Overview
**Public Policy** refers to the system of laws, regulatory measures, courses of action, and funding priorities concerning a given topic promulgated by a governmental entity or its representatives. In the context of [[concepts/health|healthcare]], it bridges [[entities/clinical-practice|clinical practice]] with systemic organization, influencing how services are delivered, funded, and evaluated.

## Key Research & Developments

### Patient Activation Measure (PAM)
Recent literature integrates public policy frameworks with [[concepts/personalised-care|patient-centered care]] models, specifically focusing on activation metrics.

- **Source Integration**: [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Hibbard---Development-of-the-Patient-Activation|Hibbard - Development of the Patient Activation]]
- **Core Findings**:
  - Development of the **[[concepts/emotional-engagement|Patient Activation]] Measure (PAM)** to conceptualize and measure patient activation stages.
  - Focus on **chronic** disease management and patient engagement in **[[concepts/health-care|health care]]** systems.
  - Authors: Judith H. Hibbard, [[entities/jean-stockard|Jean Stockard]], [[entities/eldon-r-mahoney|Eldon R. Mahoney]], [[entities/martin-tusler|Martin Tusler]], [[entities/lo-sasso|Lo Sasso]].
  - Methodology includes **[[concepts/survey|Survey]] Research** and **Literature Review**.
  - Published in 2002 by Ey ([[concepts/consulting|Consulting]] Company context); available via Blackwell Synergy.
- **Policy Implications**:
  - Provides a standardized metric for evaluating patient readiness to manage their own health, relevant for policy-driven quality improvement initiatives.
  - Links individual **activation** levels to broader **health care** outcomes, informing reimbursement and care delivery models.
