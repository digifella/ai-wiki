# PFAS

**Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances** (PFAS) are a large, diverse group of synthetic chemicals that have been used in industry and consumer products worldwide since the 1940s. Known as "[[concepts/forever-chemicals|forever chemicals]]" due to their extreme persistence, they resist degradation and accumulate in the environment and human bodies.

## Key Characteristics
- **Persistence:** Virtually indestructible in the natural environment; do not break down easily.
- **Ubiquity:** Found in soil, water, air, and living organisms globally.
- **Applications:** Historically used in non-stick cookware, stain-resistant fabrics, firefighting foams, and food packaging.

## Health & Environmental Impact
- **Bioaccumulation:** Accumulate in humans and wildlife over time.
- **Toxicity:** Linked to various health issues including cancer, liver damage, and immune system suppression.
- **Water Contamination:** Major concern for drinking water supplies due to leaching from industrial sites and landfills.

## Recent Research & Strategies
- **Removal Technologies:** Focus on advanced filtration and chemical breakdown methods to mitigate contamination [[lab-notes/2026-09-17-Toxic-PFAS-in-Water-Contamination-Health-Risks-and-Remov|Toxic PFAS in Water: Contamination, Health Risks, and Removal Strategies]].
- **Regulatory Trends:** Increasing scrutiny and potential bans on specific PFAS compounds.
