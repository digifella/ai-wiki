# Subject Isolation

[[concepts/foregroundbackground-separation|Subject isolation]] is a post-processing technique used in digital photography to separate a subject from its background through refined mask [[concepts/edge-refinement|edge refinement]]. The primary tool for this work is the [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] functionality available in [[entities/adobe-lightroom|Adobe Lightroom]] and [[concepts/camera-raw|Camera Raw]], which allows photographers to apply selective [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] with precise [[concepts/power|control]] over where edits occur within an image.

## Mask Edge Refinement

The core of subject isolation involves using mask edge refinement tools to create clean, accurate boundaries around a subject. Rather than relying on broad, blanket adjustments across an entire image, photographers can use these tools to isolate specific areas for localized edits. This precision helps ensure that adjustments to one part of the image—such as [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]], [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], or color—do not unintentionally affect other areas.

## Application and Results

Subject isolation is particularly useful for enhancing image sharpness and detail. By isolating the subject and applying sharpening selectively, photographers can achieve tack-sharp results on their main focal point while controlling how sharpening is applied elsewhere in the frame. This technique works with any camera and helps overcome limitations of in-camera focusing or initial capture conditions.
