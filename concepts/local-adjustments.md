# Local Adjustments

Local adjustments refer to edits applied to specific regions of a photograph rather than to the entire image. This technique allows photographers to selectively enhance or modify particular areas—such as brightening a subject's face, adjusting color in a specific region, or correcting exposure in isolated zones—while leaving the rest of the image unchanged. Local adjustments are fundamental to non-destructive digital editing workflows, as they preserve the original image data while enabling targeted refinements.

## Implementation and Tools

Modern editing software utilizes vector-based masks, gradient filters, and radial brushes to define the boundaries of these adjustments. Advanced implementations, such as Adobe Lightroom’s intersect masking feature, enable precise control by allowing users to combine multiple mask criteria. This capability ensures that edits are applied only to pixels that satisfy all selected conditions, facilitating complex selections like isolating a subject against a busy background.

## Workflow Integration

These tools are typically integrated into the post-processing pipeline after global adjustments. By applying local changes, editors can correct exposure imbalances, enhance texture details, or shift color balance in specific parts of the frame without affecting the overall tonal balance. This selective approach is essential for professional retouching, where maintaining natural lighting and realistic depth requires nuanced control over individual elements within the composition.
