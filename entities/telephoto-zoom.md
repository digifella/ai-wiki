# Telephoto Zoom

Telephoto zoom refers to the use of longer focal lengths in panoramic photography, extending the traditional panorama technique beyond wide-angle composition. While panoramas are conventionally created with wide-angle lenses to capture expansive landscapes, telephoto lenses provide an alternative approach that emphasizes magnification and detailed framing rather than breadth of view.

## Characteristics and Applications

When telephoto lenses are used for panoramic stitching, the resulting images exhibit increased magnification and compressed perspective. This approach allows photographers to create panoramic compositions that focus on distant subjects or isolate specific elements within a scene rather than capturing the full expanse of the landscape. The compressed perspective characteristic of telephoto lenses can create distinctive visual qualities in panoramic images, emphasizing layers and depth relationships within the scene.

## Distinction from Wide-Angle Panoramas

The telephoto panorama approach differs fundamentally from conventional wide-angle panoramic photography in both technique and visual outcome. Rather than assembling multiple images to create a wider field of view, telephoto stitching typically aims to increase magnification or create artistic compositions that benefit from the optical properties of longer focal lengths. This technique expands the creative possibilities of panoramic photography beyond its traditional use case of capturing broad vistas.
