# Lean

Lean is a project management and business methodology focused on maximizing customer value while minimizing waste. The approach originated from lean manufacturing principles developed at Toyota in the automotive industry during the mid-20th century, where it became foundational to the company's production system. Since then, Lean has been adapted across multiple sectors including software development, healthcare, and general organizational management.

## Core Principles

The methodology centers on continuous improvement and the elimination of inefficiencies in processes. Key concepts include identifying value from the customer's perspective, mapping the value stream to remove non-value-adding steps, establishing flow to ensure work moves smoothly, and implementing pull systems to produce only what is needed. This framework encourages organizations to optimize resources and reduce costs by systematically addressing sources of waste, such as overproduction, waiting, and defects.

## Application and Evolution

While initially rooted in industrial manufacturing, Lean has evolved into a broader strategic philosophy applicable to various industries. In software development, it often intersects with Agile methodologies to enhance delivery speed and quality. Healthcare organizations utilize Lean to improve patient outcomes and operational efficiency, while service industries apply its principles to streamline administrative workflows. The adaptability of Lean allows it to serve as a versatile tool for organizational transformation, emphasizing data-driven decision-making and employee empowerment to sustain long-term operational excellence.
