# Hypothesis Formation

Hypothesis formation is a fundamental stage in the scientific method where researchers propose testable explanations for natural phenomena. A hypothesis serves as a provisional answer to a research question, crafted prior to empirical investigation. To be scientifically valid, it must be falsifiable, meaning it is capable of being proven wrong through observation or experiment. This requirement ensures that the proposed explanation remains grounded in reality rather than remaining an unfalsifiable assertion.

Richard Feynman described this process as a cycle involving guessing, computing, and comparing the results against nature. In this framework, the "guess" represents the hypothesis, which is then subjected to rigorous calculation or prediction. The final step involves comparing these predictions with experimental data. If the comparison fails, the hypothesis is discarded or revised; if it succeeds, the hypothesis gains credibility, though it is never considered absolutely proven.

Effective hypotheses are grounded in existing knowledge while remaining specific enough to guide experimental design. They bridge the gap between abstract theory and observable reality by providing a clear, measurable outcome to test. This specificity allows researchers to isolate variables and determine whether the proposed explanation holds true under controlled conditions, thereby advancing scientific understanding through iterative refinement.
