# Thermal Contraction

**Thermal contraction** is the physical process where a material decreases in volume or [[concepts/density|density]] as its temperature drops. This phenomenon is a primary driver in the formation of columnar jointing in igneous rocks.

## Mechanism
- As molten rock (magma/lava) cools, it loses heat to the surrounding environment.
- The reduction in temperature [[concepts/causes|causes]] the atomic bonds to tighten, leading to a macroscopic reduction in volume.
- This volume reduction generates tensile [[concepts/stress|stress]] within the cooling mass.
- When the [[concepts/tensile-stress|tensile stress]] exceeds the rock's [[concepts/tensile-strength|tensile strength]], fractures propagate to relieve the stress.
- The fractures typically propagate perpendicular to the cooling surface, resulting in polygonal (often hexagonal) column structures.

## Case Study: Basalt Column Formation
The formation of basalt columns is a classic example of thermal contraction in action.

- **Process**: Hot basaltic [[concepts/lava-flows|lava flows]] cool from the top and bottom surfaces inward.
- **Contraction**: As the lava cools, it undergoes significant thermal contraction.
- **Cracking**: The contraction induces stress that leads to [[concepts/hexagonal-cracking|hexagonal cracking]] patterns, creating regular polygonal columns.
- **Visual Evidence**: This process is clearly demonstrated in geological formations found in Iceland and [[entities/united-kingdom|Northern Ireland]].
