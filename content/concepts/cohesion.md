# Cohesion

Cohesion is the fundamental property or force that binds particles, elements, or parts of a substance together into a unified, stable [[concepts/structure|structure]]. In physical systems, cohesion operates through various mechanisms—from molecular attraction and chemical bonding to gravitational forces—enabling materials to maintain their form and [[concepts/integrity|integrity]] rather than dispersing into separate components. The degree of cohesion present in a substance directly influences its [[concepts/material-properties|physical properties]], including tensile strength, viscosity, and structural stability.

## Physical Manifestations

Cohesion manifests differently across material states. In liquids, cohesive forces between molecules create surface tension and enable liquids to maintain volume while adapting to container shapes. In solids, stronger cohesive forces lock particles into fixed arrangements, providing rigidity and resistance to deformation. [[concepts/granular-materials|Granular materials]]—such as sand or powders—exhibit variable cohesion depending on particle size, shape, moisture content, and packing arrangement. The concept of [[concepts/angle-of-repose|angle of repose]], the steepest angle at which loose material remains stable before sliding, directly reflects the balance between gravitational force and the cohesive or frictional forces holding particles together.

## Philosophical and Conceptual Significance

Beyond physical chemistry, cohesion serves as a metaphorical and conceptual framework in [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]] and systems theory, describing the abstract forces that unify diverse elements—whether social groups, ideas, or organizational structures—into coherent wholes. In this broader sense, cohesion addresses fundamental questions about unity, integration, and what prevents systems from fragmenting into disorder.
