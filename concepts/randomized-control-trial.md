# Randomized Control Trial

A randomized controlled trial (RCT) is a scientific experimental design primarily used to establish causal relationships between an intervention and a specific outcome. The defining feature of this methodology is the random assignment of participants to either an intervention group or a control group. This randomization ensures that the groups are comparable at baseline across both known and unknown variables, thereby minimizing selection bias and confounding factors that could skew results.

In the context of health and wellbeing, the control group typically receives standard care, a placebo, or no treatment, serving as a critical baseline for comparison. By maintaining this structure, researchers can isolate the effect of the specific intervention being tested. This rigorous approach is widely regarded as the gold standard for evaluating the efficacy of medical treatments, psychological therapies, and public health initiatives.

Within the domains of mental health and digital health, RCTs are frequently employed to validate new therapeutic approaches and technological interventions. For instance, in the evaluation of the SES One Intervention, RCTs provide the empirical evidence necessary to determine whether digital tools effectively improve user wellbeing outcomes compared to traditional methods or no intervention at all.
