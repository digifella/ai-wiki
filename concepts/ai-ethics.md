# AI Ethics

AI [[concepts/ethics|ethics]] encompasses the principles and frameworks that guide the design, development, and deployment of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems. It addresses fundamental questions about how [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] should behave, what values they should reflect, and how they should interact with humans and society. The field draws on [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]], computer [[concepts/science|science]], law, and social sciences to establish standards and [[concepts/best-practices|best practices]] for responsible [[concepts/ai-development|AI development]].

## Core Principles

Key concerns in AI ethics include fairness, [[concepts/opacity|transparency]], [[concepts/accountability|accountability]], and safety. Fairness relates to ensuring [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] do not discriminate against individuals or groups based on protected characteristics. Transparency involves making AI [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] processes understandable to users and stakeholders. Accountability establishes clear responsibility for [[concepts/ai-system|AI system]] outcomes and harms. Safety focuses on preventing AI systems from causing unintended damage or being misused for harmful purposes.

## Practical Applications

AI ethics considerations influence multiple stages of system development, from data collection and [[concepts/training-process|model training]] through to deployment and ongoing monitoring. Organizations implementing AI systems must evaluate potential [[concepts/biases|biases]] in [[concepts/language-data|training data]], establish appropriate oversight [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]], and consider broader societal impacts. These practices help mitigate risks including algorithmic bias, [[concepts/privacy|privacy]] violations, and misalignment between AI system objectives and human values.

The field remains actively developing as AI capabilities advance and new applications emerge in [[concepts/health|healthcare]], criminal justice, employment, and other consequential domains where [[concepts/ethical-considerations|ethical considerations]] carry significant weight.
