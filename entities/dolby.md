# Dolby

**Dolby** is a prominent audio technology company known for developing noise reduction and audio compression techniques. While historically dominant in cinema and professional audio, its modern consumer-facing [[concepts/dolby-atmos]] technology has faced scrutiny regarding the authenticity of its immersive claims in home environments.

## Dolby Atmos

[[concepts/dolby-atmos]] is an [[concepts/object-based-audio|object-based audio]] technology that allows sound engineers to place and move specific sounds in three-dimensional space. Unlike traditional channel-based audio, Atmos treats audio as independent objects, enabling a more immersive experience when supported by compatible hardware.

### Critical Perspectives

Recent analysis suggests a significant disparity between the theoretical capabilities of Atmos and the widespread consumer experience.

- **Simulated vs. True Atmos**: Many home theater setups utilize simulated Atmos (upmixing) rather than true object-based audio, leading to a "fake" immersive experience [[lab-notes/2026-09-14-Dolby-Atmos-Deception-Object-Based-Audio-vs.-Widespread|Dolby Atmos Deception: Object-Based Audio vs. Widespread Simulated Home Theater Sound]].
- **Marketing vs. Reality**: Critics argue that the widespread adoption of Atmos in consumer devices often masks the lack of genuine hardware support, creating a perception of immersion that is largely algorithmic rather than physical.
- **Hardware Dependency**: True Atmos benefits require specific speaker configurations (including height channels) and decoding capabilities, which are often absent in standard soundbars and budget systems.
