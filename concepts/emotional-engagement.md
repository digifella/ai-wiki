# Emotional Engagement

Emotional engagement in [[concepts/health|healthcare]] refers to the psychological and affective [[concepts/connection|connection]] patients develop with their own health conditions, treatment plans, and the [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] formed with care providers. This concept recognizes that health outcomes are not determined by clinical factors alone, but also by the patient's internal emotional investment in their care journey. Strong emotional engagement has been associated with improved adherence to treatment, better self-management of chronic conditions, and higher satisfaction with [[concepts/health-care|healthcare]] experiences.

## Dimensions of Patient Engagement

Emotional engagement operates across several interconnected dimensions. It includes the patient's willingness to actively participate in health decisions, their emotional response to diagnosis and prognosis, and the quality of [[concepts/trust|trust]] established with providers.

### Measurement and Conceptualization

*   **Patient Activation Measure (PAM):** The concept is empirically grounded in the development of the Patient Activation Measure (PAM), a validated instrument designed to quantify a patient's knowledge, [[concepts/skills|skills]], and confidence in managing their own health.
*   **Foundational Research:** [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Hibbard---Development-of-the-Patient-Activation|Hibbard - Development of the Patient Activation]] (2002) outlines the conceptual framework for activation, categorizing patients into stages based on their readiness to engage.
*   **Key Determinants:** Activation levels are influenced by [[concepts/chronic-disease-management|chronic disease management]] needs, health literacy, and systemic support structures.
*   **Outcome Correlation:** Higher activation scores correlate with improved health outcomes, reduced healthcare costs, and greater efficiency in care delivery.
