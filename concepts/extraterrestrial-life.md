# Extraterrestrial Life

Extraterrestrial life refers to hypothetical life forms that may exist beyond [[entities/earth|Earth]], ranging from simple microorganisms to intelligent civilizations. The scientific investigation of this possibility emerged as a formal discipline in the mid-20th century, driven by advances in [[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]], [[concepts/deep-space-exploration|space exploration]], and biology. Despite extensive searches and decades of [[concepts/scientific-method|scientific inquiry]], no confirmed evidence of extraterrestrial life has been discovered to date.

## Scientific Approaches

The search for extraterrestrial life has taken several methodological forms. Astronomers examine the spectral signatures of exoplanet atmospheres for biosignatures, such as oxygen or methane imbalances that might indicate biological activity. Simultaneously, astrobiologists study [[concepts/extremophiles|extremophiles]] on Earth to understand the potential limits of life and identify suitable environments for [[concepts/habitability|habitability]] elsewhere in the solar system, such as [[concepts/mars|Mars]] or the subsurface oceans of Europa and Enceladus.

## Historical and Cultural Context

The concept has long occupied a place in human [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]] and theology, often influencing cultural narratives about humanity's place in the cosmos. In the 20th century, figures like [[entities/carl-sagan|Carl Sagan]] played a pivotal role in popularizing the scientific [[concepts/seti|search for extraterrestrial intelligence]] (SETI) and framing the discussion within a rigorous [[entities/tomasz-janowski|academic]] framework. This shift helped transition the topic from speculative fiction to a legitimate field of interdisciplinary research involving [[concepts/physics|physics]], chemistry, and biology.
