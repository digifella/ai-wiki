# Cdc

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) is a U.S. federal [[concepts/health|health]] agency operating under the Department of Health and Human Services. Among its many [[concepts/population-health|public health]] functions, the CDC produces educational content addressing [[concepts/climate-change|climate change]] and its effects on human health, with particular [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to [[concepts/vulnerable-populations|vulnerable populations]].

## Climate Change and Public Health

The CDC develops resources that connect climate-related environmental changes to direct health impacts affecting American communities. This work includes identifying how shifting [[concepts/weather-patterns|weather patterns]], temperature extremes, and other climate-driven changes influence disease transmission, air quality, water safety, and heat-related illness. The agency emphasizes how these health risks are distributed unevenly across populations.

## Vulnerable Populations Focus

The CDC's climate and health messaging highlights disparities in [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] and susceptibility to climate impacts. Vulnerable groups—including low-income communities, elderly populations, people with chronic conditions, and communities with limited [[concepts/healthcare-access|access to healthcare]]—face elevated risk from climate-related health threats. The agency's educational materials aim to inform both public health professionals and the general public about these inequities and adaptation strategies.
