# Shaken Vs Stirred Technique

The choice between shaking and [[concepts/stirring-technique|stirring]] a martini affects both the drink's temperature and [[concepts/texture|texture]]. Shaking with ice cools the cocktail rapidly while introducing small air bubbles and dilution from melting ice. Stirring, performed in a mixing glass, chills the drink more gently while achieving finer dilution and a smoother mouthfeel. Most classic bartenders prefer stirring for a martini, as it maintains the drink's clarity and silky [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]]. The technique also influences how much ice water integrates into the final cocktail—shaking typically produces slightly more dilution.

## Cultural Association with James Bond

The fictional spy [[entities/james-bond|James Bond]] famously orders his martini "shaken, not stirred," a preference established early in Ian Fleming's novels and perpetuated throughout the film [[concepts/adaptations|adaptations]]. This choice became iconic in popular culture, often interpreted as a signature of Bond's character and [[concepts/style|style]]. However, the preference contradicts conventional [[concepts/martini-preparation|martini preparation]] in professional bartending, where stirring remains the standard method for achieving the preferred texture and presentation.

## Practical Differences

From a preparation standpoint, shaking requires a cocktail shaker, ice, and vigorous [[concepts/exercise|movement]], resulting in a colder drink served faster. Stirring demands a mixing glass and bar spoon, allowing the bartender to [[concepts/power|control]] dilution more precisely. The visual presentation also differs—shaken martinis typically appear slightly cloudy due to aeration, while stirred martinis remain crystal clear. These technical distinctions mean that Bond's preference yields a noticeably different drinking experience from the classical preparation method.
