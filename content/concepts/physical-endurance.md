# Physical Endurance

Physical endurance is the capacity to sustain physical effort over extended periods despite fatigue or discomfort. It involves both physiological systems—including cardiovascular function, muscular strength, and metabolic efficiency—and psychological factors that [[concepts/power|influence]] how the body responds to sustained [[concepts/stress|stress]]. The ability to persist through physical demands relies on the interplay between these systems rather than on either alone.

## Mental Toughness and Resilience

[[concepts/mental-toughness|Mental toughness]] plays a significant role in physical endurance, particularly in contexts involving [[concepts/chronic-pain|chronic pain]] or prolonged physical exertion. The psychological component of endurance includes the ability to manage discomfort, maintain [[concepts/motivation|motivation]], and regulate emotional [[concepts/responses|responses]] during difficult [[concepts/exercise|physical activity]]. [[concepts/resilience|Resilience]] mechanisms allow individuals to adapt to ongoing physical stress and recover from fatigue more effectively.

## Neurobiological Mechanisms

Brain-derived neurotrophic factor ([[concepts/bdnf|BDNF]]) is a protein that supports the survival of existing neurons and encourages growth of new neurons and synapses. Research suggests BDNF plays a role in supporting both physical performance and the psychological resilience needed to manage chronic pain or sustained physical challenge. Physical activity itself can stimulate BDNF production, creating a [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] mechanism that may enhance both endurance capacity and pain management [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]].
