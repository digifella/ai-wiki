# Tonal Music

Tonal music is a system of musical organization built around a central [[entities/pitch|pitch]] called the tonic or tonal center. All other pitches within a [[concepts/writing|composition]] relate hierarchically to this tonic, which functions as the point of harmonic rest and [[concepts/solution|resolution]]. A piece of tonal music typically operates within a specific key or scale—such as C major or A minor—where the [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] between pitches create patterns of tension and [[concepts/deployment|release]] that give the music structural [[concepts/coherence|coherence]] and forward direction.

## Historical Development

Tonal music emerged gradually during the Renaissance and became the dominant organizational system in Western classical music from the Baroque period (roughly 1600–1750) onward. This system replaced earlier modal frameworks and provided composers with a more flexible harmonic language. By the Classical era (1750–1820), tonal conventions were fully established, allowing composers like Mozart and Beethoven to explore sophisticated harmonic progressions while maintaining clear tonal centers.

## Key Characteristics

The foundation of tonal music rests on functional [[concepts/musical-harmony|harmony]], where chords serve specific roles in relation to the tonic. The dominant chord (built on the fifth scale degree) creates tension that naturally resolves to the tonic, while other chords support harmonic [[concepts/exercise|movement]] toward and away from the tonal center. Melodies, bass lines, and accompanying voices all reinforce the sense of tonality through their relationship to the key. This systematic approach allowed composers to create large-scale works with clear harmonic architecture.

## Modern Context

Tonal music remains central to many musical traditions, from popular music and jazz to film scores and contemporary classical composition. While twentieth-century composers developed alternative systems like atonality and twelve-[[concepts/tone|tone]] technique, tonality continues to be widely used and understood as a fundamental organizing principle in music.
