# Sonar

Sonar, an acronym for sound navigation and ranging, is a technique that uses sound propagation to navigate, communicate with, or detect objects on or under the surface of the water. The technology operates by transmitting acoustic signals through water, which reflect off objects and return to the source as echoes. By analyzing these returning signals, operators can determine the distance, direction, speed, and other characteristics of detected objects.

The system is generally categorized into two main types: active and passive sonar. Active sonar emits pulses of sound and listens for the echoes, making it effective for mapping the seafloor and locating specific objects. Passive sonar, conversely, does not emit any signals but instead listens for sounds generated by other objects, such as the noise from ship propellers or marine life, allowing for stealthy detection and identification.

Since its development in the early twentieth century, sonar has become fundamental to underwater exploration, naval operations, and marine research. It plays a critical role in submarine warfare, maritime safety, and the study of oceanography, enabling humans to perceive and interact with the underwater environment which is otherwise inaccessible to visual observation.
