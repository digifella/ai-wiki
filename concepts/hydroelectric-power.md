# Hydroelectric Power

**Hydroelectric power** is the conversion of energy from flowing water into electrical energy. It is a major source of renewable energy and relies on the potential energy of water stored in reservoirs or the kinetic energy of flowing rivers.

## Historical Context & Early Innovations

While large-scale hydroelectric dams are a 20th-century phenomenon, the foundational technology and early applications date back to the late 19th century.

- **First Domestic Application:** Cragside: William Armstrong's World-First [[concepts/victorian-smart-home|Victorian Smart Home]] Innovations represents the [[entities/earth|world]]'s first use of hydroelectric power in a private residence. Located in Northumberland, [[entities/united-kingdom|England]], it was installed by [[concepts/william-george-armstrong]] in the 1870s.
- **Technological Significance:** Armstrong's [[concepts/installation|installation]] at Cragside not only provided lighting but also powered hydraulic lifts and other domestic machinery, effectively creating the "world's first smart home."
- **Legacy:** This early [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] demonstrated the viability of hydroelectricity for decentralized, small-scale power generation long before the grid became ubiquitous.

## Key Components

1. **Dam/Reservoir:** Stores water to create potential energy.
2. **Penstock:** Channels water to the turbine.
3. **Turbine:** Converts the kinetic energy of water into mechanical energy.
4. **Generator:** Converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.
5. **Transformer:** Steps up voltage for transmission.

## Types of Hydroelectric Power

- **Impoundment:** The most common type, using a dam to store river water in a reservoir.
- **Diversion (Run-of-River):** Channels a portion of a river through a canal or penstock without a large reservoir.
- **Pumped [[entities/storage|Storage]]:** Uses excess electricity to pump water uphill, releasing it later to generate power during peak demand.
