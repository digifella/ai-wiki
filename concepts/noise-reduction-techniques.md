# Noise Reduction Techniques

Noise reduction is the process of removing unwanted noise from a signal or image, primarily used in [[concepts/smartphone-camera-apps|digital photography]] and [[concepts/audio-modality|audio]] [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] to improve [[concepts/camera-raw|signal-to-noise ratio]]. Key strategies span pre-capture [[concepts/preventive-care|prevention]] and post-processing correction.

## Causes and Characteristics
[[concepts/digital-image-noise|Digital noise]] manifests as grainy, discolored pixels, typically exacerbated by:
- High [[concepts/iso-settings|ISO settings]] increasing sensor sensitivity amplification.
- Long [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] times accumulating thermal noise.
- Low [[concepts/light|light]] conditions forcing higher gain.
- Small sensor sizes with higher photon shot noise.

## Prevention Strategies
Minimizing noise at the source is preferable to post-processing removal:
- Shoot in **RAW** format to retain maximum [[concepts/dynamic-range|dynamic range]] and bit depth.
- Use lower ISO settings and stabilize the camera (tripod/monopod) to allow for longer exposures without gain.
- Employ exposure-to-the-right (ETTR) techniques to maximize photon collection within highlight limits.
- Cool the sensor (in supported equipment) to reduce thermal noise during long exposures.

## Post-Processing Solutions
When noise is present, algorithmic and manual techniques are applied:
- **Luminance Noise Reduction**: Smooths grain while preserving edge detail, often at the cost of perceived [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]].
- **Chrominance Noise Reduction**: Targets colored speckles (purple/green spots), which are more objectionable than luminance noise.
- **AI-Based Denoising**: Utilizes [[concepts/artificial-intelligence-models|machine learning models]] to distinguish between [[concepts/texture|texture]] and noise, offering superior [[concepts/storing|retention]] of fine details compared to traditional smoothing [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]].
- **Multi-Frame Stacking**: Combines multiple exposures to average out random noise while reinforcing consistent signal.
