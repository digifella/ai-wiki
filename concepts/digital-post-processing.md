# Digital Post Processing

Digital post processing refers to the manipulation and enhancement of digital images or media files after their initial capture or creation. Rather than relying solely on in-camera settings or real-time parameters, post processing applies technical adjustments through software tools to improve, correct, or creatively alter the final output. This workflow has become standard practice across photography, videography, graphic design, and digital art, serving as a bridge between raw source material and finished product.

## Common Applications

Post processing encompasses a wide range of adjustments. Color correction addresses issues like white balance, exposure, and contrast to achieve accurate or intentional color reproduction. Retouching removes unwanted elements such as dust, blemishes, or distracting backgrounds. Sharpening and noise reduction enhance clarity and reduce digital artifacts. Advanced techniques include compositing, where multiple images are combined, and color grading, which establishes a consistent aesthetic or mood across a project through deliberate color palette control.

## Tools and Workflow

Professional post processing typically uses dedicated software such as Adobe Lightroom, Photoshop, DaVinci Resolve, or specialized tools depending on the medium. The workflow generally follows a logical sequence: organizing and reviewing source files, applying non-destructive adjustments when possible, performing localized corrections, and finalizing output for the intended medium. Many workflows employ non-destructive editing techniques that preserve the original file while storing adjustment instructions separately, allowing flexibility for future revisions.

Post processing has evolved from a specialized practice to an expected stage of digital media production, with industry standards and best practices varying by discipline and final output requirements.
