# Knowledge Graphs

Knowledge graphs are [[concepts/json-structuring|structured data]] representations that organize information as interconnected [[concepts/nodes|nodes]] and [[concepts/relationships|relationships]], forming a graph-like network. They enable systems to store both entities (concepts, people, [[entities/places|places]], objects) and the semantic relationships between them, creating a more nuanced understanding of domain knowledge than flat data structures.

## Role in RAG Systems

Knowledge graphs have become particularly valuable in [[concepts/answer-generation|retrieval-augmented generation]] (RAG) systems, where they enhance the quality and relevance of [[concepts/knowledge-bases|information retrieval]]. Rather than searching through [[concepts/unstructured-text|unstructured text]], [[concepts/contextualized-language-understanding|RAG systems]] can traverse [[concepts/knowledge-graph|knowledge graph]] structures to locate contextually related information, reduce hallucinations, and provide more accurate source attribution. This approach allows AI systems to reason across relationships and retrieve information with greater precision.

## AI Agent Reasoning

Knowledge graphs support AI [[concepts/reasoning-steps|agent reasoning]] by providing explicit semantic [[concepts/structure|structure]] that [[concepts/agents|agents]] can navigate and query. Agents can use knowledge graphs to understand domain relationships, perform logical [[concepts/inference|inference]], and maintain [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] across multiple queries or tasks. This [[concepts/structured-representation|structured representation]] is particularly useful for complex domains where understanding relationships between entities is critical to reaching correct conclusions.
