# Slope Stability
[[concepts/rock-stability|Slope stability]] refers to the capacity of soil and rock slopes to resist [[concepts/exercise|movement]] downslope under the influence of [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]]. It is crucial in understanding natural hazards such as landslides and for the design of engineered structures like roads, dams, and foundations.

## Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/angle-of-repose|Angle of Repose]]**: The maximum angle at which a granular material can be piled without sliding or collapsing.
- **[[concepts/cohesion|Cohesion]]**: A measure of how strongly soil particles stick together.
- **[[concepts/friction|Friction]] Angle (Phi)**: Determines the resistance of soil to movement along a slope.
- **Factor of Safety**: Ratio comparing the available resisting forces to those driving potential failure.

### Factors Influencing Slope Stability
- [[concepts/moisture-content|Moisture content]] and water pressure
- Vegetation cover and root reinforcement
- Human activities like construction or mining
