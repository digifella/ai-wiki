# Solid-Electrolyte Interphase

**Solid-Electrolyte Interphase (SEI)** is a passivation layer forming on the Anode (e.g., Graphite) during initial cycling of [[concepts/lithium-ion-battery]] systems. Generated via reduction of Electrolyte components, SEI is essential for stable operation.

## Properties
- **Selectivity:** Ionically conductive to Lithium ion; electronically insulating to prevent continuous solvent reduction.
- **Composition:** Heterogeneous mix of inorganic species ($Li_2CO_3$, $LiF$) and organic polymers derived from carbonate ethers.
- **Function:** Passivates anode surface, mitigating parasitic reactions while facilitating charge transfer.

## Evolution & Degradation Mechanisms
- **Thickening:** SEI undergoes slow, continuous growth during cycling and calendar aging, consuming active lithium inventory and electrolyte.
- **Impedance Increase:** Layer expansion elevates Internal resistance, degrading [[concepts/power|power]] density and Coulombic efficiency.
- **Capacity Fade:** Irreversible lithium sequestration within SEI [[concepts/structure|structure]] drives [[concepts/battery-degradation-rate|capacity loss]] over lifecycle.
- **Operational Impact:**
    - Real-world degradation metrics indicate EV batteries maintain structural SEI [[concepts/integrity|integrity]] longer than consumer electronics due to optimized [[concepts/thermal-regulation|thermal regulation]], reduced depth-of-discharge, and moderated C-rates [[lab-notes/2026-05-11-EV-Battery-Longevity-Actual-Degradation-Data-for-Buyers|EV Battery Longevity: Actual Degradation Data for Buyers]].
    - Longitudinal data refutes equivalence to smartphone battery decline patterns; EV cells demonstrate >80% capacity retention over extended usage, highlighting the [[concepts/robustness|robustness]] of SEI under managed operating envelopes versus high-[[concepts/stress|stress]] portable device conditions.
