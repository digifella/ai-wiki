# Automated Topic Outlining

Automated topic outlining refers to the computational generation of structured hierarchical outlines for research topics. Rather than relying on manual curation, these systems use algorithmic approaches to identify key subtopics, establish logical [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] between concepts, and organize information in a systematic way. The primary goal is to enable efficient exploration and synthesis of complex subjects [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] scale, reducing the manual effort required to comprehend and [[concepts/structure|structure]] large bodies of information.

## STORM and Agent-Based Research

[[entities/stanford-university|Stanford]]'s [[entities/storm|STORM]] (Synthesis of Topic Outline through Retrieval and Multi-perspective question generation) represents a significant approach to automated topic outlining. The system employs multiple [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] that conduct research from diverse perspectives, retrieve relevant information from web sources, and organize findings into comprehensive outlines. This multi-[[entities/agent|agent]] methodology aims to produce outlines that reflect different viewpoints and maintain verifiable connections to source material, addressing concerns about [[concepts/data-hallucination|hallucination]] and [[concepts/factual-accuracy|factual accuracy]] in automated research synthesis.

## Application and Process

Automated topic outlining systems typically operate by decomposing a query into subtopics, gathering information through retrieval mechanisms, and iteratively structuring content into hierarchical outlines. The process often involves question generation, perspective diversification, and knowledge [[concepts/consolidation|consolidation]] steps. These systems can accelerate the initial research [[concepts/phase|phase]] for complex topics, particularly in domains where comprehensive background understanding is needed before deeper investigation.
