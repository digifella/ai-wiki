# Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)

Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) is a financial metric that measures the mean annual growth rate of an investment or business metric over a specified period of time, assuming the profits are reinvested at the end of each year. It smooths out the effects of volatility in year-to-year returns to provide a single, consistent growth figure. CAGR is calculated by taking the ending value, dividing it by the beginning value, raising the result to the power of one divided by the number of years, and then subtracting one.

## Applications in Business Strategy

CAGR is widely used in business strategy and financial analysis to evaluate the performance of investments, product lines, and entire companies. Investors and analysts use CAGR to compare the relative performance of different investments or to assess whether a business is meeting its growth targets. Because it accounts for compounding effects over multiple years, CAGR provides a more meaningful measure of long-term performance than simple average annual growth rates.

## Limitations

While CAGR is a useful [[concepts/benchmark-testing|benchmarking]] tool, it has limitations. It assumes consistent growth patterns and does not account for volatility or risk involved in achieving that growth. Additionally, CAGR can be misleading when the beginning or ending values are exceptionally high or low, and it does not provide insight into the actual performance trajectory year-to-year.
