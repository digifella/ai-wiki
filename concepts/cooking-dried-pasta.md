# Cooking Dried Pasta

The fundamental method for [[concepts/dried-pasta-preparation|cooking dried pasta]] begins with bringing a large pot of salted water to a rolling boil. The standard ratio is approximately four parts water to one part pasta by weight, providing sufficient space for [[concepts/even-heat-distribution|even heat distribution]] and preventing the pasta from sticking together. Salt should be added to the water before the pasta enters, as this is when the starch structure most readily absorbs seasoning. The generous water volume maintains boiling temperature when pasta is added and allows starches to disperse into the cooking liquid rather than coating the noodles, which helps achieve a smoother [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]].

## Timing and Texture

Pasta is typically cooked until it reaches *[[concepts/al-dente-texture|al dente]]*, a state where the noodle is tender but still offers slight resistance when bitten. This usually occurs one to two minutes before the package [[concepts/instructions|instructions]] indicate the pasta is fully cooked. Removing the pasta at this stage ensures it retains [[concepts/payload-structure|structural integrity]] and prevents it from becoming mushy, especially since it [[entities/will|will]] continue to cook slightly when tossed with hot sauce.

## Sauce Integration

Optimizing [[concepts/flavor-integration|flavor integration]] involves reserving a portion of the starchy cooking water before draining. This liquid acts as an emulsifier when mixed with fat-based sauces, helping to bind the sauce to the pasta and create a cohesive, glossy coating. Draining the pasta completely and immediately tossing it with the sauce in a warm pan allows the noodles to absorb the flavors more effectively than if they were served separately or allowed to cool.
