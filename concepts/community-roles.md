# Community Roles

Community roles are the formal and informal positions and responsibilities that individuals occupy within community organizations and social structures. These roles define expectations for behavior, responsibilities, and contributions to collective goals. They function across diverse contexts including neighborhood associations, voluntary organizations, schools, health centers, religious institutions, and civic bodies. Community roles serve essential functions in organizing labor, distributing decision-making authority, and establishing accountability.

## Types of Community Roles

Community roles exist on a spectrum from highly formalized to entirely informal. Formal roles typically have explicit job descriptions, defined authority, and recognized credentials—such as elected officials, paid staff members, or board directors. Informal roles emerge organically through social recognition and trust, including mentors, advocates, organizers, and knowledge-keepers who may exercise significant influence without official appointment. Many roles blend both dimensions, with informal authority gradually becoming formalized through community recognition.

## Functions and Impact

Roles within communities create structure that enables coordination of activities, resource allocation, and decision-making processes. They establish clear accountability for specific tasks and help communities respond to members' needs more effectively. Through occupying roles, individuals gain status, agency, and a sense of belonging, while communities benefit from distributed expertise and sustained engagement. The effectiveness of community roles depends significantly on whether expectations align with individuals' capacities and whether role-holders receive adequate support and resources.
