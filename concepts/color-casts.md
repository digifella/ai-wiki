# Color Casts

Color casts are unwanted color tints that uniformly shift the overall color balance of a photograph away from neutral or intended tones. They occur when a single color—such as intense reds, blues, or greens—dominates an image, typically caused by the lighting environment where the photo was taken. [[concepts/digital-cameras|Digital cameras]] interpret [[concepts/light|light]] through white balance settings, which attempt to identify what should appear neutral in a scene. When actual lighting conditions deviate significantly from daylight, cameras may misinterpret the color information, resulting in a pervasive tint that obscures the true colors of the subject.

In concert photography, color casts are particularly prevalent due to the use of artificial stage lighting, which often employs saturated gels and dynamic color changes. These lighting setups create extreme deviations from standard daylight white balance presets, making automatic camera corrections ineffective. The resulting images often exhibit strong magenta, cyan, or yellow overlays that must be addressed to restore natural [[concepts/skin-tones|skin tones]] and accurate color representation.

Correcting these tints in [[concepts/lightroom-desktop|Adobe Lightroom Classic]] involves adjusting the white balance parameters to counteract the dominant hue. Photographers typically use the [[concepts/eyedropper-tool|eyedropper tool]] on a neutral gray area within the image or manually adjust the temperature and tint sliders to neutralize the cast. This process restores the image's [[concepts/color-accuracy|color fidelity]], ensuring that the subject is represented accurately despite the challenging lighting conditions present during capture.
