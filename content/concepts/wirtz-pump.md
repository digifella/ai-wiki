# Wirtz Pump Mechanics Overcoming Airlock and Hydrostatic Pressure Challenges

The Wirtz pump is a type of [[concepts/spiral-pump|spiral pump]] designed to lift water effectively. It stands out for its innovative mechanism that tackles common challenges like airlocks and [[concepts/hydrostatic-pressure|hydrostatic pressure]].

## Key Concepts
- **Airlocks:** Blockages caused by trapped air within the system, preventing fluid flow.
- **Hydrostatic Pressure:** The pressure exerted by a column of liquid at equilibrium due to [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]], affecting the pump's ability to lift water efficiently.

### Summary
The video delves into the scientific principles behind the Wirtz pump. [[entities/steve-mould|Steve Mould]] demonstrates that a small spiral-shaped Wirtz pump fails to lift water significantly higher than its own 20cm height. He attributes this initial malfunction to airlocks and [[concepts/hydrostatic-pressure|hydrostatic pressure]], which are critical factors in [[concepts/fluid-dynamics|fluid dynamics]].

### Detailed Mechanics
- **Overcoming Airlock Challenges:** The Wirtz pump's [[concepts/design|design]] includes features that prevent air from becoming trapped within the system, ensuring continuous flow.
- **Handling Hydrostatic Pressure:** By optimizing its geometry and operational [[concepts/parameters|parameters]], the pump can lift water beyond the constraints imposed by hydrostatic pressure.
