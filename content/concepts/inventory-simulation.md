# Inventory Simulation

Inventory simulation is a computational approach to modeling and optimizing inventory systems through algorithmic methods, [[concepts/iterative-refinement|iterative refinement]], and quantifiable performance metrics. Rather than relying solely on analytical formulas or static rules, this approach employs iterative algorithms that progressively improve solutions by [[concepts/testing|testing]] multiple configurations and [[concepts/learning|learning]] from outcomes. The method is particularly suited to complex inventory problems where demand patterns, supply constraints, and cost structures interact in non-linear ways.

The core methodology combines three interdependent elements: [[concepts/software-algorithms|algorithmic optimization]] techniques that explore the [[concepts/solution|solution]] space systematically, [[concepts/iterative-learning|iterative learning]] processes that incorporate [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] from each [[concepts/simulation|simulation]] cycle, and [[concepts/defined-metrics|defined metrics]] that measure system performance. These metrics typically include inventory carrying costs, stockout rates, order fulfillment times, and capital efficiency. By establishing clear measurement criteria, simulations can objectively compare different inventory [[concepts/policies|policies]] and identify configurations that balance competing objectives.

Inventory simulation finds practical application across supply chain management, retail operations, and manufacturing contexts. The approach enables organizations to test inventory policies under varied [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]]—such as demand fluctuations, lead time variations, or seasonal patterns—before implementation in live systems. This reduces operational risk and provides quantitative justification for inventory strategy changes. The iterative [[entities/nature|nature]] of the method allows continuous refinement as new data becomes available or operational conditions shift.
