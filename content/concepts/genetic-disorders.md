# Genetic Disorders

Genetic disorders are medical conditions caused by abnormalities in an organism's DNA. These conditions can result from mutations in single genes, variations across multiple genes, or chromosomal abnormalities. Some genetic disorders are inherited and passed from parents to offspring following predictable patterns, while others arise spontaneously from new mutations. The severity and expression of genetic disorders vary widely, ranging from minor conditions with little impact on [[concepts/health|health]] to serious or life-threatening illnesses.

## Classification and Examples

Genetic disorders are often categorized by inheritance pattern: autosomal dominant (requiring one mutated copy of a gene), autosomal recessive (requiring two mutated copies), and X-linked (associated with mutations on the X chromosome). Common examples include cystic fibrosis, sickle cell disease, hemophilia, and Huntington's disease. Some genetic conditions, such as certain cancers and heart diseases, involve both genetic and environmental factors in their development.

## Treatment and Research

Historically, genetic disorder management focused on symptom treatment and genetic [[concepts/offering-counseling-services|counseling]]. Modern approaches increasingly employ [[concepts/gene-editing|gene-editing]] technologies, most notably [[concepts/crispr|CRISPR]]-Cas9, which allows researchers to directly modify or correct genetic sequences. Scientists like [[entities/dr-feng-zhang|Feng Zhang]] have contributed to developing and refining these technologies, expanding their precision and potential [[concepts/software|applications]]. Beyond CRISPR, ongoing research explores other therapeutic approaches including gene therapy, RNA-based treatments, and successor gene-editing platforms designed to address limitations of earlier technologies.
