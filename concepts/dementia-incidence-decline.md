# Dementia Incidence Decline

## Overview
Evidence suggests a global reduction in the incidence of [[concepts/dementia]], particularly [[concepts/alzheimers-disease]]. This trend is attributed to improvements in [[concepts/cardiovascular-health|cardiovascular health]], education, and environmental factors, alongside emerging medical interventions.

## Key Drivers of Decline
*   **Cardiovascular Risk Management:** Better control of hypertension, diabetes, and cholesterol has significantly reduced vascular contributions to [[concepts/cognitive-impact|cognitive decline]].
*   **Educational Attainment:** Higher lifetime education levels build cognitive reserve, delaying symptom onset.
*   **Environmental & [[concepts/social-psychological-factors|Social Factors]]:** Improved air quality and social connectivity contribute to brain [[concepts/health|health]] [[concepts/preservation|preservation]].

## Recent Developments & Sources
*   Analysis of recent data indicates a "plummeting" trend in dementia cases, with scientists identifying specific causal links to lifestyle and medical advancements [[lab-notes/2026-10-12-Dementia-Plummeting-Causes-Prevention-and-Alzheimers-Tre|Dementia Plummeting: Causes, Prevention, and Alzheimer's Treatment Progress]].
*   **[[concepts/preventive-care|Prevention]] Strategies:** Focus on mid-life interventions, including [[concepts/diet|diet]], [[concepts/exercise|exercise]], and [[concepts/sound-perception|hearing]] loss management.
*   **Treatment Progress:** Advances in amyloid-targeting therapies and novel drug classes are shifting the paradigm from management to potential disease modification.
