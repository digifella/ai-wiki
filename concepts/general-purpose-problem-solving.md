# General Purpose Problem Solving

General purpose problem solving in artificial intelligence refers to the capacity of a system to address diverse tasks and domains without requiring task-specific optimization or retraining. This capability is particularly critical for small language models (SLMs), which are defined as models under 4GB in size. Unlike larger foundation models, SLMs operate under strict constraints regarding memory footprint and computational resources, necessitating efficient architectural designs and training methodologies to maintain broad utility.

## Benchmarking Efficiency

Benchmarking 4GB small language models to identify efficient general-purpose problem-solving champions involves evaluating performance across a wide array of standard datasets. These evaluations typically include reasoning tasks, code generation, and natural language understanding to ensure the models retain versatility despite their reduced parameter counts. The goal is to determine which architectures and training strategies yield the highest accuracy per unit of computational cost.

The selection of champions relies on metrics that balance raw performance with resource efficiency. Models are assessed not only on their ability to solve specific problems but also on their inference speed and memory usage during operation. This approach helps identify SLMs that can effectively replace larger models in edge computing scenarios or resource-constrained environments without significant degradation in problem-solving quality.
