# Cost-Benefit Analysis

**Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA)** is a systematic approach to estimating the strengths and weaknesses of alternatives. It is used to determine options that may provide the best approach to achieving benefits while preserving savings. In the context of [[concepts/artificial-intelligence]] and LLM selection, CBA evaluates [[concepts/ai-performance-evaluation|performance metrics]] against computational costs to determine the most efficient model for specific [[concepts/scenarios|use cases]].

## Key Components
- **Benefits:** Quantifiable improvements in [[concepts/output-quality|output quality]], [[concepts/speed|speed]], accuracy, and [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]].
- **Costs:** Direct API expenses, latency overhead, and operational complexity.
- **Trade-offs:** Balancing high-cost, [[entities/high-performance|high-performance]] models against lower-cost, adequate-performance alternatives.

## Application in AI Strategy
When conducting a CBA for AI tooling:
1. Define the specific use case (e.g., creative [[concepts/writing|writing]], data analysis, customer support).
2. Measure the baseline performance of current tools.
3. Calculate the marginal cost of switching to or integrating new models like [[entities/claude]] or GPT.
4. Weigh the marginal benefit in quality or speed against the increased cost.
