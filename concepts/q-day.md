# Q-Day

**[[concepts/slms|Definition]]**: The theoretical point in time when a quantum computer becomes sufficiently powerful to break the asymmetric [[concepts/cryptographic-standards|cryptographic standards]] currently securing global [[concepts/digital-infrastructure|digital infrastructure]], specifically RSA and Elliptic Curve [[concepts/cryptography|Cryptography]] (ECC).

## Current Threat Landscape
- **Timeline Acceleration**: The window for [[concepts/cryptographic-transition|cryptographic transition]] is shrinking; Q-Day is now anticipated as early as 2029.
- **Rapid Advancement**: Recent analysis indicates that [[concepts/quantum-computing|quantum computing]]'s ability to break existing [[concepts/encryption-standards|cryptographic standards]] is accelerating much faster than previously estimated, as detailed in [[concepts/quantum-computing|Quantum Computing]] Accelerates [[concepts/cryptography|Cryptography]] Threat: Q-Day Anticipated by 2029.
- **Primary [[concepts/vulnerability|Vulnerability]]**: The implementation of Shor's [[concepts/algorithm|Algorithm]] on sufficiently large-scale, error-corrected [[concepts/qubits|qubits]].
- **Policy Response**: The [[entities/us-government|US government]] has issued an [[concepts/executive-order|executive order]] mandating the transition to [[concepts/post-quantum-cryptography|Post-Quantum Cryptography]] (PQC) to mitigate risks associated with the evolving Q-Day threat [[lab-notes/2026-07-05-Post-Quantum-Cryptography-Transition-US-Executive-Order|Post-Quantum Cryptography Transition: US Executive Order and the Evolving Q-Day Threat]].
