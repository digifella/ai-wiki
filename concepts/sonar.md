# Sonar

Sonar, an acronym for sound navigation and ranging, is a technique that uses sound propagation to navigate, communicate with, or detect objects on or under the surface of the water. The technology operates by transmitting acoustic signals through water, which reflect off objects and return to the source as echoes. By analyzing these returning signals, operators can determine the distance, direction, speed, and other characteristics of detected objects.

The system is generally categorized into two main types: active and passive sonar. Active sonar emits pulses of sound and listens for the echoes that bounce back from objects, allowing for precise location and mapping. Passive sonar, in contrast, does not emit any signals; instead, it relies on listening to sounds generated by other objects, such as the noise from ship propellers or marine life, to detect and identify targets.

While primarily associated with underwater applications, the principles of sonar have influenced various other fields involving wave propagation and detection.
