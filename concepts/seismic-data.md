# Seismic Data

Seismic data consists of recordings of seismic waves propagating through Earth's interior, captured by global networks of seismometers. These instruments detect ground motion generated by natural events such as earthquakes and volcanic activity, as well as controlled sources like explosions. The raw data is converted into digital signals that record the amplitude, frequency, and arrival times of compressional (P) and shear (S) waves, providing a primary dataset for understanding the planet's internal dynamics.

Analysis of this data relies on the principles of wave propagation, reflection, and refraction. By examining how seismic waves travel through different depths and materials, scientists can infer the structure, composition, and physical state of Earth's layers, from the crust to the inner core. Variations in wave velocity and shadow zones have historically defined the boundaries between the mantle, outer core, and inner core, revealing distinct changes in density and phase.

Recent studies of seismic anomalies within the inner core have suggested complex structural variations that may indicate the presence of a new state of matter. These observations challenge traditional models of iron alloy behavior under extreme pressure and temperature conditions. Continued refinement of seismic tomography and waveform analysis is essential to resolve these discrepancies and accurately characterize the deep Earth's material properties.
