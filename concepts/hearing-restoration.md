# Hearing Restoration

**Hearing [[concepts/preservation|Restoration]]** refers to the medical and technological interventions aimed at recovering auditory function in individuals with hearing loss. This field encompasses surgical procedures, pharmacological treatments, and electronic devices that bypass damaged structures of the Ear to stimulate the [[concepts/auditory-nerve]] or Brain.

## Key Technologies

### Cochlear Implants
A [[concepts/cochlear-implant]] is a biomedical device that provides a sense of sound to a person who is profoundly deaf or severely hard-of-hearing. Unlike Hearing Aids, which amplify sound, cochlear implants bypass damaged portions of the Ear and directly stimulate the auditory nerve.

- **Mechanism**: Converts acoustic signals into electrical impulses that stimulate the [[concepts/auditory-nerve]].
- **Development History**: The evolution of the device has been complex, transitioning from early [[concepts/electrical-stimulation|electrical stimulation]] experiments to modern multi-channel systems. See [[lab-notes/2026-07-17-Cochlear-Implant-Development-From-Electrical-Stimulation|Cochlear Implant Development: From Electrical Stimulation to Hearing Restoration]] for detailed [[concepts/historical-context|historical context]].
- **Impact**: Currently enables hundreds of thousands of individuals worldwide to perceive sound, representing a significant milestone in Neurotechnology.

### Other Interventions
- **Hearing Aids**: Amplify sound for those with residual hearing.
- **Bone-Anchored Hearing Systems (BAHS)**: Bypass the outer and middle ear by conducting sound through the skull bone.
- **Pharmacological Restoration**: Emerging research into regenerating Hair Cells in the Cochlea.
