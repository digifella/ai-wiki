# Turkish Government

[[entities/republic-of-turkey|Turkey]] operates as a presidential republic with a unicameral legislature. The President serves as both head of state and head of government, wielding executive authority over the country's administration. The Grand National Assembly (Türkiye Büyük Millet Meclisi) functions as the sole legislative body, comprising 600 members responsible for enacting laws and representing the Turkish population. An independent judiciary forms the third branch, interpreting laws and administering justice according to the Turkish legal system.

## Administrative Structure

Turkey is divided into 81 provinces (il), each governed by a provincial administrator appointed by the central government. Local governance also includes municipalities and village administrations that handle regional affairs. This hierarchical structure allows the central government in Ankara to maintain administrative oversight while delegating certain functions to provincial and local authorities.

## Political System

The Turkish political system has undergone significant constitutional reforms, most notably in 2017, which strengthened presidential powers and abolished the office of Prime Minister. The country operates under a multi-party democracy, with elections held at regular intervals for the presidency and parliamentary representation. Political authority derives from the Turkish constitution, which establishes the fundamental principles of governance and citizen rights.
