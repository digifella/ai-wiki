# Governance Risk

[[concepts/governance|Governance]] Risk refers to the organizational and [[concepts/security|security]] challenges that arise from deploying [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] solutions without adequate oversight, control [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]], or formal governance frameworks. As [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] become increasingly autonomous and distributed across organizations, the lack of proper governance creates vulnerabilities in both [[concepts/cybersecurity|cybersecurity]] posture and operational [[concepts/compliance|compliance]]. This risk is particularly acute when AI implementations operate outside formal IT channels or bypass established approval processes.

## Shadow AI and Ungoverned Deployments

[[concepts/shadow-ai|Shadow AI]]—[[concepts/ai-tools|AI tools]] and systems deployed by business units without IT or security approval—represents a primary source of governance risk. These ungoverned solutions often lack security assessments, data handling controls, or integration with enterprise risk management systems. [[concepts/agentic-frameworks|Agentic frameworks]], which enable AI systems to operate autonomously across organizational systems, further amplify this risk by creating [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] processes that may lack human oversight or audit trails.

## Security and Compliance Implications

[[concepts/co-pilot-use|Ungoverned AI solutions]] introduce multiple security vulnerabilities, including uncontrolled data access, inadequate encryption, and [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] of sensitive information to third-party services. Organizations lose visibility into how AI systems process data, who has access to them, and what decisions they make. This creates compliance gaps with regulatory requirements around data [[concepts/secure|protection]], auditability, and [[concepts/responsible-ai-use|responsible AI use]]. The distributed nature of [[concepts/shadow-it|shadow AI]] makes it difficult to enforce consistent security standards across the organization.

## Mitigation Through Governance Frameworks

Effective governance requires establishing clear [[concepts/policies|policies]] for AI procurement, deployment, and monitoring. Organizations should implement centralized registries of [[entities/ai-tools|AI tools]], conduct security assessments before deployment, and maintain audit logs of system decisions and data usage. Governance frameworks should define [[concepts/roles-and-responsibilities|roles and responsibilities]], establish approval processes, and ensure alignment with broader [[concepts/internet-security|cybersecurity]] and compliance strategies.
