# Karl Marx

**Karl Marx** (1818–1883) was a German philosopher, political economist, historian, sociologist, journalist, and revolutionary socialist. His [[concepts/ideas|ideas]] have had a profound influence on modern intellectual history and politics.

## Core Theories
- **Historical Materialism**: Social development is driven by material conditions and economic relations rather than abstract ideas.
- **Class Struggle**: History is the history of class struggles between the oppressor and the oppressed (e.g., Bourgeoisie vs. Proletariat).
- **Surplus Value**: Profit is derived from the unpaid labor of [[entities/employees|workers]]; capitalism exploits this dynamic.
- **Alienation**: Under capitalism, workers are alienated from their labor, products, humanity, and each other.

## Key Works
- *The Communist Manifesto* (1848, with Friedrich Engels)
- *Das Kapital* (1867, Vol. 1)
- *The German Ideology* (unpublished in his lifetime)

## Legacy & Critique
Marx's theories formed the basis of Marxism, which later evolved into various political movements and states, most notably the Soviet Union. Interpretations of his work regarding the transition to [[concepts/moneyless-society|communism]] have varied significantly, leading to diverse implementations and critiques.

### Implementation Challenges in Early Soviet Theory
- The ideological [[concepts/ambition|drive]] toward a money-less society faced significant practical hurdles during the early years of the Soviet state.
- Attempts to abolish currency were rooted in Marxist ideals but resulted in economic disruption, necessitating policy reversals (e.g., the New Economic Policy).
