# Zipper Mechanics

The zipper evolved from the "[[entities/clasp-locker|clasp locker]]," an early fastening device patented in the late 19th century. The original design used a series of [[concepts/hooks-and-eyes|hooks and eyes]] that could be engaged sequentially, though it was cumbersome and prone to jamming. Subsequent improvements refined the mechanism, introducing [[concepts/interlocking-teeth|interlocking teeth]] that could be meshed together more reliably. These incremental innovations transformed the clasp locker into a practical fastening system suitable for everyday clothing and luggage.

## Engineering Principles

Modern [[concepts/buttons|zippers]] operate on a deceptively simple mechanical principle: two parallel rows of teeth that interlock when a slider is drawn along them. The slider contains two ramps that guide the teeth together on the downstroke and separate them on the upstroke. The precise geometry of the teeth, the alignment of the rows, and the angle of the ramps are critical to smooth operation and durability. Small variations in manufacturing tolerances can significantly affect performance, which is why industrial standardization became essential as zippers became ubiquitous.

## YKK and Modern Manufacturing

YKK (Yoshida Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha), founded in Japan in 1934, became the dominant global zipper manufacturer through innovations in production technology and [[concepts/quality-control|quality control]]. The company developed proprietary manufacturing processes that allowed for consistent, high-precision zipper production at scale. By the early 21st century, YKK controlled approximately 45% of the world's zipper market, making the "YKK" marking visible on countless garments and bags worldwide.
