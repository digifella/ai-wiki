# Buttons

A button is a fastening device consisting of a small object attached to fabric that passes through a corresponding buttonhole or [[concepts/loop|loop]] to [[concepts/secure|secure]] two pieces of material together. Buttons are among the oldest and most common closures used in clothing, functioning through a simple mechanical principle: the button's diameter is larger than the buttonhole, creating [[concepts/friction|friction]] and tension that holds the closure in place.

## Early History

Archaeological evidence indicates that buttons have been used across cultures for thousands of years. Early buttons served primarily decorative functions before becoming practical [[concepts/fastening-devices|fasteners]]. The widespread [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of buttons as functional closures accelerated during the Middle Ages in [[entities/europe|Europe]], where they gradually replaced pins and [[concepts/laces|laces]] as the primary method of securing garments. This shift reflected both technological advancement in manufacturing and changing fashions that favored fitted clothing.

## Materials and Construction

Buttons have historically been manufactured from numerous materials depending on availability and cost, including bone, horn, wood, [[concepts/cli|shell]], [[concepts/metal|metal]], and ceramic. The invention of plastic in the twentieth century made button production cheaper and more standardized. Button design involves two main [[concepts/attachment-theory|attachment]] methods: shank buttons, which have a stem on the reverse side for sewing, and sew-through buttons, which have two or four holes drilled directly through them. The choice of material and construction affects both durability and aesthetic [[concepts/presence|appearance]].

## Cultural and Economic Significance

The button industry became significant during [[concepts/industrialization|industrialization]], with [[concepts/mass-production|mass production]] making buttons affordable for common clothing. Buttons have carried cultural meaning across societies, serving as status symbols and [[concepts/indicators|indicators]] of craftsmanship in higher-quality garments. The standardization of button sizes and fastening methods helped establish modern clothing production as an industry.
