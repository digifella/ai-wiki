# Pomodoro Technique

The Pomodoro Technique is a time-management method developed by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s while he was a university student. The technique structures work into fixed intervals of focused activity, traditionally 25 minutes in length, separated by short breaks. Each interval is called a "pomodoro," a name derived from the tomato-shaped kitchen timer that Cirillo used while developing the system.

## How It Works

The basic cycle consists of choosing a task, setting a timer for 25 minutes of uninterrupted work, and then taking a short break of approximately 5 minutes. After completing four pomodoros, a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes is typically taken. The rigid time structure is intended to create a sense of urgency that helps maintain focus and reduce procrastination, while the regular breaks prevent mental fatigue and sustain overall productivity.

## Rationale and Application

The technique relies on the principle that frequent, structured intervals of work with built-in recovery periods improve concentration and task completion. By breaking work into manageable chunks, the method makes large or daunting projects feel more approachable. The approach has been adopted across various contexts, from academic study and software development to creative work and general task management, though the specific interval lengths can be adjusted to suit individual preferences and task requirements.
