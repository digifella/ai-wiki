# Conceptual Frameworks

A conceptual framework is a structured system of concepts, principles, and relationships that organizes understanding within a particular domain. Unlike simple collections of facts or definitions, conceptual frameworks establish the underlying logic that makes knowledge coherent and actionable. They function as mental models that enable practitioners to interpret new information, generate predictions, and solve problems within their field.

## Naming Versus Understanding

Physicist Richard Feynman emphasized a crucial distinction between knowing the name of something and actually understanding it. Knowing that a phenomenon is called by a particular name—or being able to recite its definition—does not necessarily mean one comprehends how it works or why it occurs. True understanding requires grasping the relationships between concepts, the mechanisms underlying observations, and the principles that connect different phenomena. This distinction is particularly important in physics, where multiple theoretical frameworks may describe the same phenomena using different conceptual structures.

## Equivalent Theories and Scientific Progress

Different conceptual frameworks can produce equivalent predictions while organizing knowledge in fundamentally different ways. Two theories may be mathematically equivalent yet require different mental models to work with effectively. The choice between equivalent frameworks affects not only how scientists think about problems but also which new questions become natural to ask and which experimental directions seem promising. Thus, conceptual frameworks shape the trajectory of scientific progress by determining which phenomena researchers are equipped to investigate next, even when the underlying mathematical descriptions are interchangeable.
