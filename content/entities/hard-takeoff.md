# Hard Takeoff

[[concepts/guiding-role|Hard takeoff]] is a theoretical scenario in [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] research describing a [[concepts/phase|phase]] of extremely rapid [[concepts/ai-development|AI development]] driven by [[concepts/ai-recursive-self-improvement|recursive self-improvement]]. In this model, an AI system reaches a capability threshold where it can meaningfully improve its own [[concepts/architecture|architecture]] and algorithms, leading to an exponential acceleration in intelligence gains that occurs over a compressed timeframe—potentially hours, days, or weeks rather than years.

The concept is central to certain discussions of existential risk in [[concepts/safe-ai-use|AI safety]], as proponents argue that a [[concepts/hard-takeoff|hard takeoff]] scenario would compress the timeline for AI development beyond human ability to intervene or adjust course. This contrasts with a "soft takeoff" model, where AI capability improvements occur more gradually alongside human progress.

[[entities/matthew-berman|Matthew Berman]], a technology YouTuber, released a video titled "Hard Takeoff has started" discussing [[concepts/hard-takeoff-phase|AI recursive self-improvement]] and the potential [[concepts/intelligence-explosion|intelligence explosion]]. The video examines whether current developments in [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]] and AI systems show early [[concepts/indicators|indicators]] of the hard takeoff scenario theorized by AI researchers and safety advocates.
