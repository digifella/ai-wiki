# CRISPR

CRISPR is a [[concepts/gene-editing|gene-editing]] technology that enables [[concepts/granular-control|precise modifications]] to DNA sequences. The system works by using a guide RNA to direct a protein called Cas9 to specific locations in the genome, where Cas9 cuts the DNA. This molecular mechanism is derived from bacterial immune systems, which naturally use CRISPR-like sequences to defend against viral infections. Scientists adapted this biological system for deliberate gene editing beginning in the early 2010s, and it has since become a foundational tool in molecular biology.

## Origins and Development

CRISPR technology emerged from decades of basic research into bacterial genetics. The system's potential for gene editing was recognized by researchers including Jennifer Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier, whose landmark 2012 publication demonstrated its application in [[concepts/eukaryotic-cells|eukaryotic cells]]. [[entities/feng-zhang|Dr. Feng Zhang]] and others contributed to early implementations and refinements. The relative simplicity and effectiveness of CRISPR compared to earlier gene-editing methods like TALENs and zinc finger nucleases made it rapidly accessible for widespread scientific use.

## Modern Applications and Integration with AI

Recent advancements highlight the convergence of CRISPR with [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] and high-[[concepts/solution|resolution]] sequencing technologies to decode complex biological systems.

- **AI-Driven Biological Decoding**: As discussed in [[lab-notes/2026-07-15-Decoding-Biologys-Language-AI-CRISPR-and-Single-Cell-Seq|Decoding Biology's Language: AI, CRISPR, and Single-Cell Sequencing for Disease]], Silvana Konermann emphasizes the role of AI in interpreting the "language" of biology. The human cell is described as wildly complex, requiring computational power to map interactions that traditional methods cannot fully resolve.
- **Single-Cell Sequencing Synergy**: CRISPR is increasingly paired with single-cell sequencing to observe gene function at unprecedented resolution. This combination allows researchers to track the effects of genetic edits on individual cells, providing granular data on disease [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] and cellular responses.
- **Disease Research**: The integration of these tools aims to tackle persistent [[concepts/health|health]] challenges by moving beyond bulk tissue analysis to understand heterogeneity within cell populations, potentially accelerating therapeutic development.
