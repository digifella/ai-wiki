# Volcanic Rock

**Volcanic rock** (or igneous rock) refers to extrusive igneous rock formed by the cooling and solidification of magma or lava at or very near the surface of a planet.

## Key Characteristics
- **[[concepts/texture-slider|Texture]]**: Typically fine-grained (aphanitic) due to rapid cooling, though porphyritic textures may occur if cooling rates vary.
- **[[concepts/writing|Composition]]**: Ranges from basalt (mafic) to rhyolite (felsic).
- **Structure**: Can exhibit vesicular textures (gas bubbles) or columnar jointing (contractural fracturing).

## Basalt Column Formation
Basalt columns are a specific structural [[concepts/presence|manifestation]] of volcanic rock cooling. They result from the physical processes of [[concepts/thermal-contraction|thermal contraction]].

- **Mechanism**: As basaltic [[concepts/lava-flows|lava flows]] cool, they undergo significant volume contraction.
- **Fracturing**: To relieve [[concepts/heat-stress|thermal stress]], the rock fractures into regular polygonal shapes, most commonly hexagons.
- **Geometry**: The hexagonal pattern is the most efficient way to pack space while minimizing surface area during contraction.
- **Visual Reference**: For a detailed breakdown of the cooling and contraction mechanics, see [[lab-notes/2026-10-06-Basalt-Column-Formation-Lava-Cooling-Contraction-and-Hex|Basalt Column Formation: Lava Cooling, Contraction, and Hexagonal Cracking]].
