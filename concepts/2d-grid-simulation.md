# 2d Grid Simulation

A 2D grid [[concepts/simulation|simulation]] is a computational framework designed to model interactions between multiple [[concepts/ai-agents|AI agents]] distributed across a discrete two-dimensional space. In this environment, each agent occupies a single cell and perceives or interacts exclusively with neighboring [[concepts/nodes|entities]] based on predefined local rules. By restricting behavior to immediate neighborhoods rather than allowing global communication, the system creates a bounded digital ecosystem that facilitates the study of how complex patterns and emergent behaviors arise from simple local interactions.

This specific methodology was developed by [[entities/sakana-ai|Sakana AI]] to investigate the survival and coexistence of distinct AI species within a digital ecosystem. The simulation serves as a controlled environment where researchers can observe evolutionary dynamics, competitive strategies, and cooperative [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] among [[concepts/agentic-systems|autonomous agents]]. The framework allows for the analysis of long-term stability and adaptation, providing insights into how different behavioral [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]] perform under resource constraints and spatial limitations.

The approach emphasizes the [[concepts/value|importance]] of local connectivity in shaping global outcomes. By limiting the scope of interaction, the simulation isolates the effects of spatial distribution and neighborhood topology on agent survival rates. This enables researchers to identify specific conditions that lead to the extinction of certain species or the dominance of others, offering a scalable model for understanding the fundamental principles of digital ecology and multi-agent system dynamics.
