# Chimaeras

Chimaeras are a diverse group of [[concepts/cartilaginous-fish|cartilaginous fish]] belonging to the subclass Chimaeromorphi, making them close relatives of sharks and [[concepts/rays|rays]]. Like their relatives, chimaeras possess skeletons made entirely of cartilage rather than bone. They have inhabited marine environments since at least the Jurassic period and represent one of the major surviving lineages of jawed vertebrates. Despite their evolutionary significance and an extensive [[concepts/fossil-evidence|fossil record]] spanning over 300 million years, chimaeras remain considerably less well-known than sharks, in part because most species inhabit deep waters where they are rarely observed.

## Physical Characteristics and Adaptations

Chimaeras are often referred to as [[concepts/ratfish|ratfish]] or ghost sharks due to their distinct [[concepts/presence|appearance]]. They typically possess a large, rounded head with a pointed snout, large eyes, and a long, whip-like tail. Unlike sharks, chimaeras have a single gill opening covered by a fleshy operculum on each side of the head. Their teeth are fused into grinding plates, which they use to crush the hard shells of crustaceans and mollusks, their primary [[concepts/diet|diet]]. Some species also exhibit bioluminescence, particularly in their tails, which may serve as a lure for prey or a means of communication in the dark depths of the ocean.

## Evolutionary History and Diversity

As one of the oldest surviving lineages of cartilaginous fish, chimaeras provide critical insights into the evolution of jawed vertebrates. Their [[concepts/evolutionary-lineage|lineage]] diverged from the common ancestor of sharks and rays hundreds of millions of years ago, allowing them to survive [[concepts/catastrophic-events|mass extinction events]] that wiped out many other groups. Today, they are classified into three extant families: Chimaeridae, Rhinochimaeridae, and Callorhinchidae. While their diversity is lower than that of sharks, chimaeras occupy a wide range of marine habitats, from continental shelves to the deep sea, demonstrating remarkable [[concepts/resilience|adaptability]] to varying environmental conditions.
