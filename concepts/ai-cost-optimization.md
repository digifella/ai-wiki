# AI Cost Optimization

[[concepts/cost-optimization|AI cost optimization]] refers to strategies and techniques for reducing the expenses associated with developing, deploying, and maintaining [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems. As [[concepts/ai-powered-applications|AI applications]] have become more widespread, the computational costs of training large models and running [[concepts/inference|inference]] at scale have grown significantly, making [[concepts/cost-efficient-solutions|cost efficiency]] a practical concern for organizations of all sizes.

## Local and Open-Source Approaches

One primary method for [[concepts/cost-optimization|cost optimization]] involves deploying [[concepts/reasoning-models|open-source models]] locally rather than relying exclusively on cloud-based or proprietary services. [[concepts/open-source|Open-source]] models eliminate [[concepts/licensing|licensing]] fees and allow organizations to run inference on their own hardware, reducing per-request costs. [[concepts/local-deployment|Local deployment]] also provides greater control over [[concepts/privacy|data privacy]] and latency.

## Agent Frameworks and Implementation

Implementing cost-effective AI often requires robust agent frameworks that can leverage local resources efficiently. The **[[concepts/agentic-ai|Hermes Agent]]** is a notable open-source, [[entities/hermes-agent|self-improving AI agent]] that exemplifies these principles. Key insights from recent implementations include:

*   **Setup and Optimization**: Comprehensive guides on configuring the Hermes Agent focus on minimizing resource overhead while maintaining performance.
*   **Local [[concepts/ai-application|AI Application]]**: Emphasis on running agents locally to avoid recurring API costs associated with proprietary models.
*   **[[concepts/self-improvement|Self-Improvement]]**: The agent's ability to self-improve reduces long-term maintenance and tuning expenses.
