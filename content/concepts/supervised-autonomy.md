# Supervised Autonomy

Supervised autonomy is a regulatory classification that permits autonomous vehicles to operate with human oversight rather than requiring constant manual [[concepts/power|control]]. Under this framework, the vehicle manages routine driving tasks—acceleration, braking, steering, and lane maintenance—while a human operator remains attentive and ready to intervene when needed. This model occupies a middle ground between fully manual driving and complete vehicle autonomy, allowing manufacturers to deploy advanced driver assistance systems [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] scale while maintaining legal [[concepts/accountability|accountability]] through human presence.

## European Regulatory Precedent

In April 2026, [[entities/tesla|Tesla]]'s [[concepts/full-self-driving-fsd|Full Self-Driving]] ([[entities/full-self-driving-fsd|FSD]]) system received approval for supervised autonomous operation in the Netherlands, marking a significant regulatory milestone in [[entities/europe|Europe]]. This approval established the first precedent for supervised autonomy under [[entities/european-union|EU]] frameworks and created a pathway for broader rollout across other EU member states. The Dutch approval demonstrated that European regulators were prepared to authorize advanced autonomous features when accompanied by adequate safety measures and human supervision protocols.

## Implementation and Oversight

The supervised autonomy framework typically requires drivers to maintain situational awareness and the ability to resume manual control within defined response times. Regulatory bodies establish specific conditions for operation—such as permitted road types, weather conditions, and traffic [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]]—and may mandate periodic human takeover to ensure driver attentiveness. Manufacturers must provide documentation of safety [[concepts/testing|testing]], failure modes, and driver [[concepts/training|training]] protocols before receiving approval in a jurisdiction.
