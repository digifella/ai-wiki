# Portrait Editing

Portrait editing encompasses a range of post-processing techniques used to enhance photographs of people. These adjustments address both technical issues—such as lighting, exposure, and focus—and aesthetic improvements to skin texture, facial features, and overall composition. Portrait editing is a standard practice in professional photography and remains accessible to photographers of varying skill levels through software like Adobe Photoshop.

## Common Techniques

Effective portrait editing typically involves several core approaches. Skin retouching removes blemishes and evens out tone while preserving natural texture. Dodge and burn techniques selectively lighten and darken areas to enhance facial structure and dimension. Color grading adjusts the overall color palette to create mood and consistency. Eye enhancement sharpens and brightens the eyes to draw focus, while adjusting lips and other facial features helps achieve a polished final image. Background adjustments, including blur or removal, help isolate the subject from distracting elements.

## Balancing Enhancement and Authenticity

Portrait editors must balance enhancement with maintaining a natural appearance. Over-processing can result in artificial-looking skin and loss of character. Modern portrait editing emphasizes subtle adjustments that improve technical quality without fundamentally altering the subject's appearance. The goal is typically to present subjects as polished versions of themselves rather than dramatically transformed representations.
