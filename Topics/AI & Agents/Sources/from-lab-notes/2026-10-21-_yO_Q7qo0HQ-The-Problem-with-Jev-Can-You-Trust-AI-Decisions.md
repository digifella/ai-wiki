## The Problem with Jev: Can You Trust AI Decisions?
**Clip title:** The Problem with Jev: Can You Trust AI Decisions?
**Author / channel:** IBM Technology
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_yO_Q7qo0HQ

### Summary
Here's a summary of the video:

**Main Topic:** 
The video discusses the role of trust in artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on a new approach called JEV (Judgment, Evaluation, and Validation), how it compares to Large Language Models (LLMs), and the importance of observability in cyber security.

**Key Points:**
* **JEV vs. LLM:** While LLMs generate words, JEV makes quick decisions, presenting potential automation benefits. However, both raise questions of trust and "zero trust" in cybersecurity.
* **Observability and Confidence:** Observability allows for reconstructing what happened in a system when things go wrong. Confidence is not proof, and high confidence in an AI model doesn't mean the decision is trustworthy.
* **Cyber Security Concerns:** The input and the model are part of the attack surface. Attackers can manipulate data or exploit weaknesses, making observability crucial to establish accountability and verify decisions.

**Conclusions & Takeaways:**
* **Verify then Trust:** AI should be treated as an untrusted component, requiring security controls around it. The speaker emphasizes that "confidence doesn't equal proof," and verification should precede trust.
* **Human in the Loop:** Keeping humans involved in the escalation process and independent verification of high-risk decisions is essential. 
* **Focus on "How" not "What":** Instead of just asking what AI decided, we should ask how we know what it decided, reinforcing the need for continuous verification and observability in AI-driven systems.
