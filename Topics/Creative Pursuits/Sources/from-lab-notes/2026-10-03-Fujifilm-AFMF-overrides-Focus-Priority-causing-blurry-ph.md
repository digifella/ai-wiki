## Fujifilm AF+MF overrides Focus Priority, causing blurry photos.
**Clip title:** Fujifilm Setting That Overrides Focus Priority
**[[entities/tasia-custode|Author]] / channel:** pal2tech
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vy6_PWCPiR0

### Summary
This video delves into two critical autofocus settings on [[entities/fujifilm|Fujifilm cameras]]: "Release/Focus Priority" and "AF+MF" (Auto Focus + [[concepts/manual-focus|Manual Focus]]), highlighting a subtle but important interaction between them that can lead to unexpected blurry photos. The main topic revolves around understanding how these settings behave, particularly when used in combination, and offering solutions to ensure sharp images.

The presenter first explains "Release/Focus Priority," found in the AF/MF menu, which offers two options for both [[concepts/single-autofocus|AF-S]] (single-shot autofocus) and AF-C ([[concepts/continuous-autofocus|continuous autofocus]]): "Focus" or "[[concepts/deployment|Release]]." When set to "Focus," the camera [[entities/will|will]] only fire the shutter if focus has been successfully acquired, preventing blurry shots. Conversely, "[[concepts/deployment|Release]]" prioritizes immediate shutter response, allowing the camera to take a photo even if it's not in focus. He demonstrates how "Focus" priority effectively stops the camera from taking an out-of-focus image until the subject is sharp.

The core problem arises when "Release/Focus Priority" is set to "Focus," but the "AF+MF" setting is simultaneously turned "ON." While "AF+MF" is designed to allow users to manually fine-tune focus immediately after autofocus has locked, the Fujifilm manual [[concepts/notes|notes]] that pictures can still be taken out of focus if "AF+MF" is enabled, regardless of the "Release/Focus Priority" setting. This means that if a photographer acquires focus on one subject, then quickly recomposes to another distant subject and presses the shutter without giving the lens enough time to re-focus, the camera [[entities/will|will]] still fire, resulting in a blurry image, despite "Focus" priority being selected. The camera essentially trusts the user's manual input and won't halt the shutter release.

To avoid this common pitfall, the video offers two main [[concepts/recommendations|recommendations]]. Firstly, photographers who primarily rely on the camera to ensure focus should keep "AF+MF" turned "OFF." This allows "Release/Focus Priority" to function as expected, preventing blurry shots when set to "Focus." For instances requiring manual [[concepts/fine-tuning|fine-tuning]] (like macro), "AF+MF" can be temporarily switched on. Secondly, for those who desire both the benefits of autofocus and manual override, setting the camera to full "Manual Focus" (M) mode and utilizing "back-button focus" is recommended. This approach allows the user to acquire focus with a separate button and then manually adjust the focus ring, effectively bypassing the conflicting [[concepts/open-source-philosophy|logic]] of "AF+MF" overriding "Focus" priority, thus giving ultimate control over [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]].
