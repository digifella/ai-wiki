# Sleep Hygiene

Sleep hygiene refers to the set of behavioral and environmental practices that support consistent, restorative sleep. These practices address both what people do during the day and evening, and the conditions they create in their sleeping environment. Good sleep hygiene recognizes that [[concepts/sleep-quality|sleep quality]] depends on [[concepts/habits|habits]] formed over time rather than isolated interventions.

## Key Behavioral Practices

Effective sleep hygiene typically includes maintaining a consistent sleep schedule, going to bed and waking at similar times each day. It involves limiting stimulating activities and screens in the hours before sleep, as blue light [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] can interfere with the body's natural circadian rhythm. Caffeine and alcohol consumption timing matters significantly—both can disrupt sleep quality despite initial sedative effects. Regular [[concepts/exercise|physical exercise]] during the day promotes better sleep, though vigorous activity close to bedtime may have the opposite effect.

## Environmental Factors

The physical sleeping environment plays an important role in sleep quality. A cool, dark, and quiet bedroom supports the body's natural sleep processes. Comfortable bedding and a mattress suited to individual needs contribute to uninterrupted rest. Minimizing noise and light disruptions, whether from external sources or devices, helps maintain sleep [[concepts/continuity|continuity]] throughout the night.

## Application and Effectiveness

Sleep hygiene practices are evidence-based approaches that can be implemented gradually and adjusted to individual circumstances. They form the foundation of sleep improvement strategies, often recommended before pharmaceutical interventions. Different practices may prove more or less effective for different people, making it useful to experiment with various approaches to identify what supports individual sleep patterns.
