# Los Angeles

Los Angeles is the second-largest city in the United States, located in Southern California along the Pacific Coast. With a population exceeding 3.9 million residents and a greater metropolitan area of nearly 13 million people, it is one of the world's most populous urban centers. The city serves as a major economic, cultural, and technological hub, with significant influence in entertainment, international trade, aerospace, and technology sectors.

## Seismic Setting and Geological Context

Los Angeles sits within a highly seismically active region defined by complex fault systems, including the San Andreas Fault and the Newport-Inglewood Fault. This geological configuration necessitates rigorous earthquake engineering standards for all new construction and the retrofitting of existing structures. The city's topography, characterized by steep hills and alluvial basins, further influences seismic wave propagation and amplification, requiring specialized geotechnical assessments for infrastructure stability.

## Critical Infrastructure Resilience

Given the high probability of significant seismic events, Los Angeles prioritizes the resilience of its critical infrastructure networks. This includes the reinforcement of water supply systems, transportation corridors, and energy grids to ensure continuity of essential services during and after earthquakes. Engineering protocols focus on minimizing structural failure and reducing secondary hazards, such as liquefaction and landslides, to protect both human life and economic assets within the metropolitan area.
