# Sustainable Development

Sustainable development is an approach to organizing economic, social, and environmental activities that aims to meet current human needs without depleting resources or degrading the natural and social systems that future generations will depend upon. The concept emerged as a formal framework during the 1980s, gaining widespread international recognition following the 1987 Brundtland Commission report, which defined it as development that "meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs."

## Core Framework

The sustainable development model rests on three interconnected dimensions: economic prosperity, social equity, and environmental protection. Rather than treating these as separate concerns, the framework emphasizes that long-term economic growth depends on social stability and healthy ecosystems. This integrative approach distinguishes sustainable development from earlier models that prioritized economic expansion with limited regard for environmental or social consequences.

## International Adoption

The concept gained formal endorsement through major international agreements and initiatives, including the 1992 Rio Earth Summit and the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), adopted in 2015. These frameworks have guided policy decisions across governments, corporations, and development institutions, though implementation varies significantly by region and sector. Sustainable development has become a reference point in discussions of climate change, resource management, poverty reduction, and infrastructure planning, despite ongoing debates about how to balance competing priorities and measure genuine progress.
