# Visual Arts

Visual arts encompasses creative practices that primarily communicate through visual media, including painting, sculpture, photography, printmaking, and digital [[concepts/art|art]]. These disciplines allow artists to explore complex [[concepts/ideas|ideas]], emotions, and experiences through form, color, composition, and spatial arrangement. Visual arts practice can serve both aesthetic and communicative purposes, often intersecting with social commentary, personal expression, and documentary practice.

## Dyslexia Visualization in Contemporary Practice

Contemporary visual artists have begun using artistic practice as a method to visualize and communicate experiences of neurodivergence. Artist [[entities/kim-percy|Kim Percy]]'s work explores dyslexia through [[concepts/visual-representation|visual representation]], creating artworks that attempt to [[entities/make|make]] invisible neurological experiences visible to viewers. This practice bridges personal experience with broader public understanding, using visual media to translate cognitive difference into accessible visual form. Percy's approach demonstrates how artistic practice can serve both as personal exploration and as a tool for disability representation and awareness.

The intersection of artistic practice and dyslexia visualization [[concepts/highlights|highlights]] the potential for visual arts to communicate experiences that may be difficult to articulate through [[concepts/text|text]] alone. By transforming abstract cognitive experiences into concrete visual forms, such work contributes to more nuanced public understanding of neurodivergent experiences while expanding the conceptual possibilities of what visual arts can address.
