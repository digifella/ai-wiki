# Cream Sauce

A cream sauce is a foundational cooking component that combines cream with other ingredients to add richness, body, and depth of flavor to dishes. The sauce typically achieves its characteristic [[concepts/texture|texture]] through the emulsification of fat and liquid, often thickened with a roux, reduction, or binding [[entities/agent|agent]]. Cream sauces are versatile and appear across numerous cuisines, from French classical preparations to contemporary cooking.

## Composition and Preparation

Cream sauces generally consist of heavy cream or crème fraîche combined with a flavorful base such as stock, wine, or pan drippings. A thickening agent—traditionally a beurre manié (kneaded butter and flour) or roux—creates the desired viscosity. Additional ingredients such as herbs, mustard, cheese, or aromatics are incorporated to develop complexity and tailor the sauce to specific dishes.

## Culinary Applications

Cream sauces accompany a wide [[concepts/range|range]] of proteins and vegetables. In classical French cuisine, they feature prominently in dishes prepared à [[entities/los-angeles|la]] Normandy, where regional ingredients like cider and seafood are combined with cream-based preparations. Cream sauces are particularly suited to poached or braised dishes, where their richness complements delicate proteins and helps bind flavors from the cooking liquid.
