# 135mm Prime

A 135mm prime lens is a [[concepts/prime-lens|fixed focal length lens]] commonly used in portrait and [[concepts/zoom-photography|telephoto photography]]. As a prime lens, it offers a single, non-variable [[concepts/focal-length|focal length]], which typically results in a wider maximum aperture and sharper optical performance compared to zoom lenses of equivalent [[concepts/range|range]]. The 135mm focal length falls into the short [[concepts/telephoto-lens|telephoto]] category, providing moderate subject magnification and background compression.

## Panorama Stitching Applications

Beyond traditional single-frame photography, 135mm [[entities/prime-lenses|prime lenses]] can be effectively used in [[concepts/panorama-stitching|panorama stitching]] workflows. By capturing multiple overlapping [[concepts/images|images]] with a longer focal length and combining them digitally, photographers can achieve wide-angle compositions that would otherwise require [[concepts/ultra-wide-lens|ultra-wide]] lenses. This technique leverages the [[concepts/lens-compression|lens compression]] characteristic of longer focal lengths—where the distance between foreground and background elements appears reduced—while still capturing expansive field-of-view results through stitching multiple frames together.

## Advantages in Stitching

Using a 135mm prime for panoramic work offers distinct advantages over conventional wide-angle approaches. The longer focal length provides greater [[concepts/power|control]] over perspective and depth [[concepts/relationships|relationships]], and the fixed aperture of a prime lens ensures consistent [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] across all frames in the stitch. This method allows photographers to create wide compositions while maintaining the optical properties and compression characteristics associated with telephoto lenses.
