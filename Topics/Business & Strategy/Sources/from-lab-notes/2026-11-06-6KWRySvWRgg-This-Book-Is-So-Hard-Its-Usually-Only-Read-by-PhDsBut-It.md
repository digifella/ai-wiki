### Summary
Here is a summary of the video:

**Main Topic:** 
The video explores the work of psychologist Jacob Cohen and his book *Statistical Power Analysis for the Behavioral Sciences*, presenting five core concepts to help viewers critically evaluate scientific research and claims.

**Key Points:**
* **First Principle:** Before trusting a research answer, ask whether the investigation was capable of finding one. Cohen introduced statistical power analysis to address the common flaw of experiments having low statistical power to detect real effects.
* **Second Principle:** Separate whether something exists from how much it matters. The speaker explains that statistically significant results do not necessarily mean the effect size is large, useful, or important.
* **Third Principle:** Decide what evidence you will need before collecting data. Researchers should determine effect size, sample size, and thresholds beforehand rather than data-mining after the fact.
* **Fourth Principle:** Use effect sizes as starting points and never as substitutes for judgment. Cohen established standardized effect sizes (such as Cohen's *d*), but cautioned against turning these conventions into mechanical dogmas.
* **Fifth Principle:** Resist the urge to treat standardized measures as dogma. The speaker highlights Cohen’s 1994 essay, "The Earth Is Round ($p < .05$)," which critiques the mechanical misuse of null hypothesis significance testing.

**Conclusions & Takeaways:**
According to the speaker, Cohen’s work revolutionized how researchers determine whether studies have discovered anything meaningful. The video concludes that viewers should apply a "Cohen-style audit" to claims in the news by evaluating sample sizes, practical significance in ordinary units, effect sizes, and alternative explanations, rather than relying blindly on published statistical significance.
