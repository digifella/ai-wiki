# Photoshop Technique

Focus stacking is a photographic technique used primarily in landscape photography to achieve greater depth of field and overall sharpness across an image. Rather than relying on a single exposure, the photographer takes multiple images with different focus points—typically focusing on the foreground, middle ground, and background separately. These images are then combined in post-processing software like Photoshop to create a final image where all elements appear sharp and in focus.

## The Process

The technique begins during the shooting phase, where the photographer uses manual focus to capture a series of photographs with incremental focus distances. Each image is focused at a different depth, ensuring that different planes within the scene are sharp in different frames. In post-processing, these images are loaded as layers in Photoshop, where layer masks and blending techniques are applied to selectively combine the sharp regions from each photograph. The result is a composite image with extended sharpness that would be difficult or impossible to achieve in a single shot.

## Practical Applications

Focus stacking is particularly valuable in landscape photography where maintaining sharpness from near foreground elements to distant mountains or horizons is desired. It is also commonly used in macro photography, product photography, and other situations where the camera's natural depth of field is insufficient. The technique requires careful camera setup, typically using a tripod to ensure consistent framing across all exposures, and precision in the post-processing workflow.
