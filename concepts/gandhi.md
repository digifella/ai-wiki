# Gandhi

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (1869–1948) was an Indian political and spiritual leader who pioneered nonviolent resistance as a method for achieving independence and social reform. Born in Gujarat, he studied law in England and practiced as a barrister in South Africa from 1893 to 1915. During his two decades in South Africa, Gandhi developed *satyagraha* (literally "truth-force"), a philosophy of nonviolent civil disobedience formulated in response to racial discrimination against Indian immigrants.

## Campaign for Indian Independence

Returning to India in 1915, Gandhi became the leading figure of the Indian independence movement against British colonial rule. He organized mass campaigns of civil disobedience, including boycotts of British goods, the Salt March of 1930, and non-cooperation movements. These tactics mobilized millions of Indians across class and religious lines. Gandhi's approach contrasted sharply with armed rebellion, instead emphasizing moral persuasion and the withdrawal of cooperation from the colonial system.

## Legacy and Philosophy

Gandhi's methods influenced subsequent independence and civil rights movements worldwide, most notably the civil rights movement in the United States led by Martin Luther King Jr. His philosophy combined elements of Hindu and Islamic thought with Western political theory, emphasizing self-reliance, communal harmony, and the inherent dignity of all people. He was assassinated in 1948 by a Hindu nationalist, shortly after Indian independence was achieved in 1947. His ideas continue to shape discussions of nonviolent resistance and social change.
