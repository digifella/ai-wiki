# Banana Architecture

Banana Architecture is an open-source design framework that applies distributed software development principles to hardware systems at miniaturized and nano scales. The approach emerged in the early 2010s from collaborative maker and research communities seeking to democratize access to small-scale mechanical and electronic system design. It adapts established open-source software practices—including version control, modular component organization, and collaborative documentation—to the domain of physical hardware design.

## Core Principles

The framework emphasizes modularity, allowing individual components to be designed, tested, and iterated independently while maintaining compatibility with larger systems. Documentation practices prioritize accessibility for non-specialist contributors, with design files, schematics, and assembly instructions made publicly available. The approach treats hardware designs as living projects subject to community feedback and incremental improvement, similar to collaborative software development workflows.

## Application and Impact

Banana Architecture has been applied primarily in fields requiring miniaturized systems, including biomedical devices, environmental sensors, and educational robotics platforms. By lowering barriers to hardware design participation, the framework has enabled distributed teams to contribute specialized knowledge without requiring access to expensive fabrication facilities or proprietary design tools. This has facilitated faster innovation cycles and broader experimentation with nano-scale and micro-scale technologies across academic and hobbyist communities.
