# Gene Editing

Gene editing encompasses techniques for making precise alterations to DNA sequences within living organisms. The field has experienced significant advancement over the past two decades, transforming from experimental laboratory methods into practical tools for research and medicine. Early approaches, including zinc finger nucleases and TALENs, demonstrated that targeted DNA modification was possible but remained limited by complexity, cost, and accessibility.

## CRISPR-Cas9 and Its Impact

CRISPR-Cas9 emerged as a transformative technology in the early 2010s, offering a simpler, more affordable, and more accessible method for gene editing than its predecessors. Derived from bacterial immune systems, CRISPR-Cas9 uses guide RNA to direct molecular scissors to specific DNA sequences, enabling researchers to make edits with relative precision. The technology rapidly became the dominant tool in molecular biology, enabling advances in basic research and laying groundwork for therapeutic applications in treating genetic diseases.

## Emerging Alternatives

Researchers at the Broad Institute and elsewhere have identified potential successors to CRISPR-Cas9 aimed at addressing limitations such as off-target effects and delivery challenges. These newer systems expand the toolkit available to scientists, offering different capabilities suited to particular applications. As gene editing technology continues to evolve, the field moves toward increasingly versatile and precise methods for modifying genetic material.
