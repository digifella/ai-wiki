# Layer Rotation

Layer rotation is the process of rotating a specific layer within a multi-layered structure while keeping other components fixed. The concept is most commonly associated with mechanical puzzles like the Rubik's Cube, where individual layers—rows, columns, or faces—can be turned independently. This fundamental mechanic allows solvers to manipulate the puzzle systematically and forms the basis of solution methodologies used across different puzzle variants.

## Mechanical Implementation

In physical puzzles, layer rotation is achieved through an internal mechanism that permits one section to move while the rest remains stationary. On a Rubik's Cube, a central axis system allows each face and its adjacent layer to rotate along three dimensional planes. The rotating layer carries its stickers or colored squares with it, while the fixed layers maintain their positions. This mechanical separation is essential to the puzzle's function, as it enables the scrambling and solving of the configuration.

## Application in Problem-Solving

Solvers use layer rotations as discrete moves in their attempts to organize the puzzle into a solved state. Each rotation is typically notated in standardized systems that record which layer moved and in which direction, allowing solutions to be documented and shared. Understanding how layers interact through rotation—particularly how one layer's movement affects adjacent pieces—is crucial for developing efficient solving techniques and memorizing solution algorithms.
