# Galaxy Redshift

**Galaxy redshift** is the displacement of spectral lines toward longer (redder) wavelengths in the [[concepts/light|light]] emitted by [[concepts/distant-galaxies|distant galaxies]], primarily interpreted as a consequence of the expansion of the universe [[concepts/cosmology]]. It serves as a primary tool for determining cosmic distances and ages via Hubble's Law.

## Mechanisms
*   **Cosmological Redshift**: Caused by the expansion of space itself during photon travel. Dominant at large distances.
*   **Doppler Redshift**: Caused by the peculiar velocity of the galaxy relative to the observer.
*   **Gravitational Redshift**: Negligible for galactic [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]], but relevant for compact objects.

## Measurement & Interpretation
*   **Spectral Lines**: Identified by shifting atomic signatures (e.g., Lyman-[[concepts/fine-structure-constant|alpha]], Balmer series).
*   **Redshift Parameter ($z$)**: Defined as $\Delta \lambda / \lambda_0$. High-$z$ objects correspond to earlier [[concepts/epochs|epochs]] in the universe.
*   **Photometric Redshift**: Estimated using broad-band colors when spectroscopy is unavailable; prone to degeneracy errors.

## Observational Challenges & Revisions
*   **Contamination by Foreground Objects**: High-redshift candidates can be misidentified if local objects mimic high-$z$ spectral energy distributions.
    *   See [[lab-notes/2026-05-25-JWSTs-Most-Distant-Galaxy-Mystery-Revealed-as-Milky-Way|JWST's "Most Distant Galaxy" Mystery: Revealed as Milky Way Objects]] for details on the reclassification of Capotauro and similar candidates.
*   **[[entities/jwst|JWST]] Findings (2026)**:
    *   Early JWST data suggested the existence of extremely high-redshift galaxies ($z > 10$).
    *   Subsequent spectroscopic analysis revealed that some "ultra-distant" candidates, such as Capotauro, were actually [[concepts/milky-way]] foreground objects (e.g., stellar systems or nebulae) misidentified due to photometric degeneracy.
    *   This highlights the necessity of spectroscopic confirmation for high-$z$ claims to distinguish between cosmological expansion and local kinematic/gravitational effects.

## Implications
*   **[[concepts/universe-age|Age of the Universe]]**: Redshift corrections are critical for dating the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]] and the formation of the first galaxies.
*   **[[concepts/the-composition-of-the-universe-extends-beyond-what-is-directly-observable|Dark Energy]]**: The evolution of redshift-distance [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] informs models of [[concepts/accelerating-universe|cosmic acceleration]].
*   **[[concepts/galaxy-formation-and-evolution|Galaxy Evolution]]**: Correct redshift assignment ensures accurate mapping of [[concepts/star-formation-history|star formation history]].
