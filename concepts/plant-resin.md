# Plant Resin

**Plant resin** is a complex mixture of organic compounds secreted by plants, primarily for defense against herbivores, pathogens, and environmental [[concepts/stress|stress]]. It is distinct from plant sap and plant gum, though often confused in common parlance. Over geological time, fossilized resin becomes [[concepts/amber]], a valuable source of paleobotanical and paleoentomological data.

## Key Characteristics
- **[[concepts/writing|Composition]]**: Primarily terpenes (monoterpenes, sesquiterpenes, diterpenes) and phenylpropanoids.
- **Function**:
  - Seals wounds to prevent infection.
  - Traps and kills herbivorous insects.
  - Reduces water loss.
- **Physical State**: Varies from liquid (fresh) to solid/glassy (fossilized/amber).

## Evolutionary Significance
Recent discoveries have shifted understanding of when and why plants began producing resin:
- **Oldest Amber Discovery**: New findings indicate resin production evolved earlier than previously thought, suggesting it was a critical early defense mechanism.
- **Redefining Defense Evolution**: Evidence suggests resin secretion was a key adaptation for early land plants to survive pathogen pressure and insect herbivory.
- **Paleontological Value**: Amber preserves soft tissues, providing a snapshot of ancient ecosystems.
