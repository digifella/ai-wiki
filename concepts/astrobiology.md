# Astrobiology

Astrobiology is an interdisciplinary scientific field that examines the conditions necessary for life to exist beyond [[entities/earth|Earth]] and investigates where such life might be found in the universe. The discipline combines methods and knowledge from biology, chemistry, [[concepts/geology|geology]], and [[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]] to address fundamental questions about life's origins, distribution, and the environmental requirements for [[concepts/habitability|habitability]]. Rather than engaging in speculation, astrobiology grounds its inquiries in empirical data, utilizing observations from telescopes, planetary probes, and laboratory experiments to define the boundaries of life as we know it.

A core component of the field is the study of [[concepts/extremophiles|extremophiles]], organisms that thrive in extreme environments on Earth such as deep-sea [[concepts/hydrothermal-vents|hydrothermal vents]], acidic lakes, and frozen tundras. These organisms serve as critical models for understanding how life might survive in the harsh conditions found on other celestial bodies, including [[concepts/mars|Mars]], Europa, and Enceladus. By analyzing the metabolic pathways and genetic [[concepts/adaptations|adaptations]] of extremophiles, researchers can identify potential biosignatures and assess the viability of extraterrestrial habitats.

The discipline also focuses on the [[concepts/abiogenesis|origin of life]], exploring how prebiotic chemistry evolved into biological systems. This involves investigating the chemical processes that led to the first self-replicating molecules and the subsequent development of [[concepts/cellular-life|cellular life]]. Astrobiologists study [[concepts/meteorites|meteorites]], comets, and interstellar dust to understand the delivery of organic compounds to early Earth and other planetary bodies, providing insights into whether the building blocks of life are common throughout the cosmos or unique to specific planetary environments.
