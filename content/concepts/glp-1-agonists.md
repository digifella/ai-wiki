# GLP-1 Agonists

**GLP-1 Agonists** are a class of medications that mimic the action of the hormone glucagon-like peptide-1, primarily used for the treatment of type 2 diabetes and obesity. They function by enhancing glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppressing glucagon release, slowing gastric emptying, and promoting satiety.

## Mechanism of Action
- **Insulin Secretion:** Stimulates pancreatic beta cells to release insulin in response to elevated blood glucose levels.
- **Glucagon Suppression:** Inhibits alpha cells from releasing glucagon, reducing hepatic glucose production.
- **Gastric Emptying:** Delays gastric emptying, leading to prolonged nutrient absorption and increased feelings of [[concepts/satiety|fullness]].
- **Central Appetite [[concepts/regulation|Regulation]]:** Acts on receptors in the hypothalamus to reduce food intake and caloric consumption.

## Clinical Applications
- **Type 2 Diabetes:** First-line or adjunctive therapy for glycemic [[concepts/power|control]].
- **Chronic [[concepts/weight-loss|Weight Management]]:** Approved for obesity treatment (BMI ≥30, or ≥27 with comorbidities) due significant [[concepts/weight-management|weight loss]] outcomes.

## Notable Agents
- **[[entities/ozempic|Semaglutide]]:** Ozempic (diabetes), Wegovy (obesity).
- **Tirzepatide:** Mounjaro (diabetes), Zepbound (obesity). *Note: Technically a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist.*
- **Liraglutide:** Victoza, Saxenda.
- **Dulaglutide:** Trulicity.

## Challenges & Considerations
- **Supply Constraints:** High global demand has led to periodic shortages.
- **Side Effects:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and potential risks of pancreatitis or gallbladder disease.
- **Long-term Efficacy:** Concerns regarding weight regain upon cessation of therapy.
- **Muscle Mass Loss:** Rapid weight loss may include [[concepts/lean|lean]] muscle mass reduction; resistance [[concepts/training|training]] is often recommended.
