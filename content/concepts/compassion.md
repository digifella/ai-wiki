# Compassion

[[concepts/love|Compassion]] is an emotional and cognitive response that arises when perceiving another person's suffering, characterized by both the recognition of their distress and a [[concepts/motivation|motivation]] to reduce or alleviate it. It differs from [[concepts/empathy|empathy]], which is primarily the capacity to understand and share another's emotional state; compassion adds a directional component—the impulse to act or help. While empathy can be experienced passively, compassion inherently includes concern and a desire for positive change in the other person's condition.

## Relationship to Wellbeing

Compassion functions as both an interpersonal and intrapersonal phenomenon relevant to health and [[concepts/health|wellbeing]]. Experiencing compassion toward others has been associated with improved [[concepts/mental-health|mental health]] outcomes, including reduced [[concepts/anxiety|anxiety]] and [[concepts/depression|depression]], while cultivating self-compassion—extending the same understanding to oneself—supports psychological [[concepts/resilience|resilience]] and adaptive [[concepts/stress-management|coping]]. The practice of compassion-based interventions, such as loving-kindness meditation, has shown measurable effects on [[concepts/stress|stress]] reduction and emotional [[concepts/regulation|regulation]].

## Development and Expression

Compassion appears to have both innate and learned components. While some capacity for compassionate response emerges early in human development, compassion can be strengthened through deliberate practice, cultural values, and [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] to others' experiences. It operates across different [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]], from intimate one-to-one [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] to broader social and global contexts, and serves as a foundation for ethical behavior and cooperative social structures.
