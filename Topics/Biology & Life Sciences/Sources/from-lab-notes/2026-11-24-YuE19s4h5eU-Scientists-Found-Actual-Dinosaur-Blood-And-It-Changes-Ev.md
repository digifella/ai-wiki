## Scientists Found Actual Dinosaur Blood (And It Changes Everything)

### Summary
The video discusses recent discoveries in paleontology regarding the preservation of soft biological tissues in dinosaur fossils. Traditionally, it was believed that soft tissues like blood, skin, and muscles decay after an animal dies, leaving behind only mineralized rock in the shape of bones. However, recent studies challenge this view. For instance, a study on the fossilized leg bone of an *Aratasaurus museunacionali*, a 115-million-year-old theropod dinosaur, found chemical evidence of actual dinosaur blood preserved within the fossil's vascular canals. Researchers detected heme molecules, which are typically found in hemoglobin.

The preservation of these heme molecules is attributed to the environment where the dinosaur was buried. The dinosaur was rapidly buried by a microbial mat, which is made of thin layers of microorganisms that often create low-oxygen environments. This lack of oxygen prevented normal bacterial decay. Subsequently, the red blood cells underwent a mineralization process involving a manganese oxide mineral called hausmannite, forming a microscopic time capsule that stabilized and shielded the biological structures and organic molecules for millions of years.

Further supporting the preservation of soft tissues in fossils, a study from the University of Liverpool and UCLA analyzed a 66-million-year-old *Edmontosaurus* fossil and confirmed the remnants of collagen, a structural protein found in skin and bones, as well as hydroxyproline, a specific amino acid. These findings challenge the assumption that organic materials in ancient fossils are merely modern contamination or bacterial slime. Additionally, studies on dinosaur eggs have found amino acids trapped inside calcite crystals, further suggesting that biological remnants can be preserved in fossils over millions of years.
