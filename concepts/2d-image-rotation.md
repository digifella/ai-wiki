# 2d Image Rotation

2D image rotation refers to the process of rotating flat, two-dimensional images around a central point or axis. Traditionally, this has involved simple directional turns at fixed angles (90°, 180°, 270°) or free rotation within a plane. These operations preserve the image's fundamental two-dimensional character while changing its orientation relative to the viewer. Standard rotation tools have been a basic feature of [[concepts/image-processing-software|image editing software]] for decades, relying on mathematical transformations to reposition pixels without altering their intrinsic properties.

## Traditional Methods

Conventional image rotation tools work by pivoting pixels around a central coordinate, often utilizing interpolation [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]] to fill gaps created by the rotation. This method maintains the image's planar [[concepts/honesty|integrity]], meaning the object remains flat and does not gain depth or perspective changes. While effective for correcting orientation or adjusting [[concepts/writing|composition]], traditional rotation cannot simulate how an object would appear if it were physically turned in three-dimensional space.

## AI-Driven 3D Manipulation

Recent advancements in [[concepts/generative-ai|generative AI]] have blurred the lines between 2D rotation and 3D manipulation. Tools such as [[concepts/beta-version|Photoshop Beta]]'s AI [[concepts/photoshop-tool|Rotate Object feature]] allow users to rotate selected elements within a 2D [[concepts/canvas|canvas]] while automatically generating the necessary perspective, lighting, and occlusion data to simulate a 3D environment. This capability enables the realistic reorientation of objects that would otherwise require complex manual [[concepts/digital-compositing|compositing]] or [[concepts/3d-modeling|3D modeling]], effectively allowing for the [[concepts/2d-to-3d-transformation|3D manipulation of 2D images]] within a standard raster workflow.
