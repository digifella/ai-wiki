# Universe Structure

Universe [[concepts/structure|structure]] refers to the large-scale arrangement and composition of matter and energy in the cosmos. The [[concepts/observable-universe|observable universe]] exhibits a hierarchical structure, from individual galaxies organized into clusters and superclusters, to vast filaments of matter separated by cosmic voids. This large-scale structure formed through gravitational processes acting on primordial density fluctuations in the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]].

## Dark Matter

A significant portion of the universe's mass consists of [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]], a form of matter that does not emit, absorb, or reflect [[concepts/light|electromagnetic radiation]] and therefore remains invisible to direct observation. Dark matter's presence is inferred through its [[concepts/gravitational-effects|gravitational effects]] on visible matter, radiation, and large-scale cosmic structures. [[concepts/wimp-particles|Weakly Interacting Massive Particles]] (WIMPs) are among the leading theoretical candidates for dark matter particles.

The detection of gamma-ray signals consistent with dark matter [[concepts/particle-annihilation|WIMP annihilation]] in the Milky Way represents an important observational development. These signals, detected through gamma-ray [[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]], suggest that WIMPs may annihilate when they collide with one another, particularly in regions of high dark matter density such as the [[concepts/galactic-center|galactic center]]. Such detections contribute to understanding both the [[entities/nature|nature]] of dark matter and the mass [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] within [[concepts/milky-way|our galaxy]].
