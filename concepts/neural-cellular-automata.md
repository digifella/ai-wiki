# Neural Cellular Automata

Neural Cellular Automata (NCA) are computational systems that merge the discrete, grid-based structure of cellular automata with the adaptive learning capabilities of neural networks. In this framework, each cell on a grid maintains an internal state that evolves over time based on local interactions with its neighbors. Unlike traditional cellular automata, which rely on static, hand-coded transition rules, NCA cells update their states using parameters learned by a neural network. This allows the system to exhibit dynamic, context-dependent behavior rather than following a fixed logical sequence.

The architecture enables complex global patterns and emergent behaviors to arise from simple local rules, facilitating the simulation of self-organization and regeneration. By training the underlying neural network on specific objectives, researchers can guide the population of cells to form stable structures or perform tasks such as shape formation and damage repair. The system's ability to adapt its internal states allows it to maintain functionality even when parts of the grid are altered or destroyed, mimicking biological resilience.

Recent applications, such as those by Sakana AI, utilize NCA to create digital ecosystems that simulate the survival and coexistence of AI species. These simulations explore how different "species" of cellular agents interact, compete, and coexist within a shared environment. This approach provides a platform for studying evolutionary dynamics and cooperative behaviors in artificial life, offering insights into how decentralized systems can achieve robust and adaptive outcomes without centralized control.
