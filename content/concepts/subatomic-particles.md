# Subatomic Particles

Subatomic particles are the fundamental constituents of matter and radiation that exist at [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] smaller than atoms. These particles include electrons, [[concepts/protons|protons]], neutrons, and numerous other exotic particles discovered through [[concepts/particle-physics-experiments|high-energy physics experiments]]. The [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|Standard Model of particle physics]] provides the [[concepts/theory|theoretical framework]] for understanding how these particles interact and combine to form the [[concepts/observable-universe|observable universe]].

## The Muon and Precision Measurements

The muon is a subatomic particle similar to an electron but approximately 207 times more massive. It is produced naturally when [[concepts/cosmic-rays|cosmic rays]] collide with Earth's atmosphere and can also be created in particle accelerator experiments. The muon's properties, particularly its magnetic moment, have become a critical subject of study in precision [[concepts/physics|physics]] experiments. The [[concepts/muon-g-2-experiment|Muon g-2 experiment]] at Fermilab measures the [[concepts/anomalous-magnetic-moment|anomalous magnetic moment]] of the muon—a value that describes how the particle behaves in a magnetic field. This measurement is sensitive enough to detect potential deviations from the Standard Model's predictions, making it a valuable tool for identifying new physics beyond current theoretical understanding. Results from ongoing experiments provide increasingly precise measurements that can either confirm existing theory or hint at undiscovered particles and forces.
