# Meat Texture

The texture of ground meat during cooking is primarily determined by the behavior of its proteins and the retention of moisture. As the meat is heated, proteins denature and contract, a process that can squeeze out internal water and result in a dense, dry final product. The structural integrity of the meat is therefore heavily dependent on how well the cooking process manages this protein contraction and subsequent moisture loss.

Surface condition plays a critical role in the quality of the crust formed during cooking. Excess moisture on the surface inhibits the Maillard reaction, which is responsible for browning and flavor development. Consequently, techniques that remove surface water or alter the protein structure to hold more water are often employed to improve the final texture and appearance of the cooked meat.

The addition of baking soda is a common technique to improve ground meat texture, juiciness, and browning. By raising the pH level of the meat, baking soda prevents proteins from bonding too tightly during heating. This allows the meat to retain more moisture and promotes faster browning, resulting in a more tender and flavorful dish.
