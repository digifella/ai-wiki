# Philosophy Of Peace

[[concepts/philosophy|Philosophy]] of peace is the systematic inquiry into the nature, value, and conditions of peace as both a social state and an ethical ideal. Rather than treating peace simply as the absence of war or violence, the discipline examines it as a multifaceted concept intertwined with justice, social [[concepts/musical-harmony|harmony]], and human flourishing. Peace philosophy draws on methods and insights from [[concepts/ethics|ethics]], political philosophy, metaphysics, and epistemology to address fundamental questions about the possibility, desirability, and means of achieving peace at individual, social, and global levels.

## Conceptual Foundations

Central to peace philosophy is the distinction between negative peace—the mere cessation of direct violence—and positive peace, which involves the removal of structural inequalities and injustices that prevent human dignity and flourishing. This distinction reflects disagreement about whether peace requires only the absence of [[concepts/conflict|conflict]] or actively demands the establishment of just social conditions. Philosophers also examine whether peace is a natural human state or one that must be deliberately constructed and maintained through institutional and cultural frameworks.

## Approaches to Non-Violence

Non-violence occupies a significant place in peace philosophy, particularly through the influence of figures such as [[concepts/gandhi|Mahatma Gandhi]] and [[entities/martin-luther-king-jr|Martin Luther King Jr]]., who articulated non-violent resistance as both a practical strategy and a philosophical stance rooted in [[concepts/respect|respect]] for human life. Peace philosophy explores the ethical foundations of non-violence, its relationship to pacifism, and debates about whether non-violent approaches are universally applicable or contextually dependent. These inquiries extend to questions about the moral legitimacy of violence in self-defense or [[entities/pursuitunimelbeduau|pursuit]] of justice.

## Peace and Justice

A central tension in peace philosophy involves the relationship between peace and justice. Some approaches prioritize peace as a prerequisite for addressing injustices, while others argue that genuine peace requires confronting and resolving underlying injustices. This debate shapes contemporary discussions on [[concepts/ses-family|conflict resolution]], transitional justice, and the possibility of reconciliation following violence or oppression.
