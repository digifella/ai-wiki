# Carbon Dioxide Levels

Carbon dioxide levels in [[concepts/earths-atmosphere|Earth's atmosphere]] have fluctuated significantly throughout geological history. These variations result from numerous natural processes, including volcanic outgassing, chemical weathering of rocks, photosynthesis by plants and marine organisms, and decomposition of organic matter. Over millions of years, these processes have created cycles of higher and lower atmospheric CO₂ concentrations that correlate with major [[concepts/climate-change|climate shifts]] and changes in ocean chemistry.

## Carboniferous and Permian Periods

During the Carboniferous and Permian periods (approximately 359 to 252 million years ago), atmospheric CO₂ levels were substantially different from today. The Carboniferous period experienced relatively high oxygen levels and variable CO₂ concentrations, conditions that supported the vast swamp forests whose remains formed major coal deposits. By the Permian period, CO₂ levels fluctuated considerably, influencing global climate patterns and ocean conditions during the time when the greatest diversity of fossil fuel precursors accumulated in sedimentary basins.

## Modern Context

The relationship between atmospheric CO₂ levels and fossil fuel formation is indirect but significant. The organic material that became coal, oil, and natural gas accumulated during periods with specific atmospheric compositions. Understanding these historical CO₂ variations provides context for interpreting how past climate conditions enabled the accumulation and preservation of the organic matter that would eventually transform into the fossil fuels used today.
