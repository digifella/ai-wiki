# Qanat (Qanāt)

A qanat is an ancient underground channel system used for transporting water from a source area to populated areas, typically in arid regions. The term "qanat" comes from the Old Persian word "kanat," meaning "channel." Originating in [[concepts/iran|Iran]] around 300 BC, these systems were crucial for irrigation and drinking water supply.

## Historical Significance

- [[concepts/qanats|Qanats]] played a vital role in transforming [[concepts/desert-landscapes|desert landscapes]] into fertile agricultural zones.
- They allowed civilizations to thrive where surface runoff would otherwise be insufficient due to evaporation and soil saturation issues.

## Modern Relevance

### Iran's Water Crisis
[[entities/iran|Iran]] [[concepts/faces|faces]] a severe and long-standing water [[concepts/crisis|crisis]], stemming from its inherently [[concepts/arid-geography|arid geography]] and low average annual precipitation, ranking 158th globally. Historically, much of the country's central lands were uninhabitable without sophisticated [[concepts/water-management|water management]]. The nation's water problem is compounded by the fact that 75% of its limited rainfall occurs on only 25% of the land.

- **Ancient [[concepts/qanat-management|Qanat Management]]**: Historical methods of qanat construction and maintenance ensured sustainable use of underground aquifers.
- **20th Century Decline**: With [[concepts/industrialization|industrialization]] and modern water infrastructure, traditional qanat systems began to fall into disuse. This shift led to over-extraction of groundwater and depletion of non-renewable resources.
