# Superficial Results

Superficial Results describes a common limitation in user interactions with [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|Large Language Models]] (LLMs), where overly simplistic prompts produce shallow, generic, or unhelpful outputs. According to photographer and [[entities/video-creator|content creator]] [[entities/kevin-patrick-robbins|Kevin Patrick Robbins]], this phenomenon occurs because most users fail to provide sufficient context, specificity, or direction when formulating their requests to AI systems.

## The Role of Prompt Quality

The depth and usefulness of LLM [[concepts/responses|responses]] correlate directly with the quality and detail of the input prompts. When users rely on basic, underdeveloped queries, the [[concepts/models|models]] lack the necessary information to generate nuanced, contextually appropriate, or professionally valuable results. This creates a [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] [[concepts/loop|loop]] where inadequate [[concepts/prompting|prompting]] leads to inadequate outputs, potentially causing users to underestimate the [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] of LLM technology itself.

## Implications for Practical Use

For professional [[concepts/software|applications]]—particularly in fields like advertising, photography, and editorial work—superficial results from LLMs can limit their utility for complex, domain-specific tasks. Robbins advocates for more deliberate and structured [[concepts/prompt-based-modeling|prompt engineering]] as a means to extract deeper, more useful information from these systems, suggesting that improved prompting techniques represent an accessible path to better outcomes without requiring changes to the underlying technology.
