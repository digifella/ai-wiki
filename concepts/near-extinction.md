# Near Extinction

**Definition:** A state in which a population, species, or civilization has dwindled to critically low numbers or functional capacity, facing an imminent [[concepts/probability|probability]] of total collapse or cessation. In the context of human civilization, it refers to [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]] where [[concepts/earths-population|global population]] viability, technological [[concepts/continuity|continuity]], or biospheric support systems are compromised to the point of non-recovery.

## Characteristics
- **Threshold Dynamics:** Distinct from gradual decline; involves crossing critical tipping points in [[concepts/biodiversity]], Climate Stability, or Global Infrastructure.
- **Irreversibility:** Once specific ecological or societal [[concepts/systems|feedback loops]] trigger, recovery becomes statistically improbable without external intervention (if possible).
- **Cascading Failure:** Collapse of one essential subsystem (e.g., [[concepts/supply-chains|supply chains]], pollination networks) accelerates the decline of others.

## Key Drivers & Factors
- **Anthropogenic Pressures:** Resource depletion, pollution, and habitat destruction leading to Sixth Mass Extinction.
- **Systemic Fragility:** Over-reliance on complex, interdependent global systems vulnerable to Black Swan Events.
- **Existential Risks:** High-magnitude, low-probability events such as Nuclear War, Pandemics, or [[concepts/ai-safety]] failures.
