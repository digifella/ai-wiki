# Shutter Speed

**Shutter speed** is a fundamental camera setting that controls the duration the camera's sensor is exposed to light. It directly influences motion blur, image sharpness, and exposure.

## Core Concepts
- **Definition**: The length of time the shutter is open, measured in seconds or fractions of a second (e.g., 1/1000s, 1s).
- **Exposure Triangle**: Works in conjunction with [[concepts/aperture]] and ISO to determine proper exposure.
- **Motion Control**:
  - Fast shutter speeds (e.g., 1/500s+) freeze motion.
  - Slow shutter speeds (e.g., 1/30s or slower) create motion blur.

## Strategic Usage in Professional Workflows
While manual mode offers full control, professional photographers often prefer strategic semi-automatic modes (like Aperture Priority or Shutter Priority) for efficiency and consistency.

- **Efficiency vs. Control**: As noted in recent analyses, mastering manual settings is important, but 95% of professionals utilize semi-automatic modes to maintain workflow speed without sacrificing critical creative control [[lab-notes/2026-09-13-Why-Professional-Photographers-Prefer-Strategic-Semi-Aut|Why Professional Photographers Prefer Strategic Semi-Automatic Camera Modes]].
- **Contextual Adaptation**: Semi-automatic modes allow photographers to prioritize either depth of field (via aperture) or motion freezing (via shutter speed) while the camera handles the other variable, reducing cognitive load during fast-paced shoots.
- **Consistency**: Helps maintain consistent exposure across a series of shots where lighting conditions change slightly but the creative intent (e.g., frozen action) remains constant.
