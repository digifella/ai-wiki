# Abiogenesis

**Abiogenesis** is the natural process by which life arises from non-living matter, such as simple organic compounds. It is distinct from evolution, which explains how life changes over time once it exists.

## Key Theories and Models

*   **[[concepts/primordial-soup|Primordial Soup]] Hypothesis**: The traditional view that life began in a warm, dilute solution of organic molecules in early Earth's oceans, energized by lightning or UV radiation.
*   **Hydrothermal Vent Hypothesis**: Proposes that life originated in deep-sea vents, where mineral-rich, alkaline fluids provided energy and catalytic surfaces for chemical reactions.
*   **Dual Origins Hypothesis**: Recent evidence suggests life may not have a single linear origin but rather two distinct beginnings in hydrothermal environments [[lab-notes/2026-08-28-Rethinking-LUCA-Evidence-for-Dual-Origins-of-Life-in-Hyd|Rethinking LUCA: Evidence for Dual Origins of Life in Hydrothermal Vents]].

## Recent Developments

*   **Rethinking [[concepts/luca|LUCA]]**: New studies challenge the concept of a single [[concepts/last-universal-common-ancestor|Last Universal Common Ancestor]] (LUCA), pointing toward [[concepts/dual-origins-of-life|dual-origins-of-life]] in [[concepts/hydrothermal-vents|hydrothermal vents]] [[Rethinking LUCA: Evidence for Dual Origins of Life in Hydrothermal Vents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zFk-8_0dXYo)].
*   **Evidence for Dual Origins**: Scientists have found geological and chemical evidence suggesting that early Earth's hydrothermal systems supported two separate lines of prebiotic chemistry that may have later merged or coexisted.
*   **Mineral Catalysis**: Emphasis on the role of iron-sulfur clusters and other minerals in vent systems as catalysts for early metabolic pathways.
