# Sky Masking

Sky masking is a selective editing technique in Adobe Lightroom that isolates the sky portion of an image to apply targeted adjustments without affecting other elements. By creating a mask around the sky, photographers can modify exposure, contrast, saturation, and color independently from the foreground and middle ground. This approach is particularly valuable in landscape photography, where skies often require different treatment than the land below to achieve balanced, visually compelling results.

## Challenges with Foreground Elements

A common difficulty in sky masking occurs when tree branches or other foreground objects intersect with the sky. Standard automatic selection tools may struggle to distinguish between the fine details of leaves and the background sky, resulting in incomplete masks or artifacts along the edges. This issue is exacerbated in images with high contrast or complex textures, where the algorithm may either include unwanted foreground pixels or exclude parts of the sky that should be adjusted.

## Mitigation Strategies

To address these challenges, photographers often employ manual refinement tools within the masking interface. Using the brush or range selector allows for precise adjustments to the mask edges, ensuring that intricate branch structures are excluded from the sky selection. Additionally, adjusting the feathering settings can help blend the mask more naturally, reducing harsh lines where the sky meets the foreground. In cases where automatic detection fails significantly, creating a custom mask from scratch may be necessary to achieve accurate isolation.
