# Decommissioning

**Decommissioning** refers to the formal process of taking a system, asset, or entity out of active service. In the context of advanced [[concepts/robotics|robotics]] and AI, this process often involves critical safety protocols, [[concepts/data-cleaning|data sanitization]], and physical destruction or recycling of hardware to prevent misuse or malfunction.

## Key Aspects
- **Safety & [[concepts/security|Security]]**: Ensuring no residual data or operational capabilities remain that could pose a threat.
- **Regulatory [[concepts/compliance|Compliance]]**: Adhering to industry standards for electronic waste and [[concepts/ai-governance|AI governance]].
- **Public Perception**: High-profile decommissioning events can significantly influence public [[concepts/trust|trust]] and policy.

## Notable Events

### Figure Robotics F.02 Termination
In a highly publicized incident, [[entities/figure-robotics|Figure Robotics]] executed the decommissioning of its [[entities/f02|F.02]] [[concepts/humanoid-robots|humanoid robots]]. The event was notable for its theatrical presentation, deliberately mimicking the iconic molten [[concepts/metal|metal]] destruction scene from *Terminator 2: Judgment Day*.

- **Event Details**: The destruction was performed live and verified as real, not CGI, emphasizing the finality of the hardware's end-of-life.
- **Public Reaction**: The event generated significant media coverage, with commentators like [[concepts/dr-know-it-all-knows-it-all|Dr. Know-it-all Knows it all]] highlighting the symbolic weight of the act [[lab-notes/2026-10-03-Figure-Robotics-F.02-Humanoid-Robot-Terminator-Style-Dec|Figure Robotics F.02 Humanoid Robot Terminator-Style Decommissioning Event]].
- **Implications**: This event set a precedent for how robotics companies might handle the retirement of advanced [[concepts/ai-hardware|AI hardware]], blending [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] with public relations spectacle.
