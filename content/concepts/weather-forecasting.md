# Weather Forecasting

Weather forecasting is the application of [[concepts/science|science]] and technology to predict atmospheric conditions over a specified area and time period. Modern forecasting combines [[concepts/empirical-evidence|observational data]] from satellites, radar, weather stations, and other instruments with mathematical [[concepts/models|models]] that simulate atmospheric behavior. Forecast [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]] depends on [[concepts/data-conceptsintegrityintegrity|data quality]], model sophistication, and [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]], with typical useful prediction horizons extending from hours to about two weeks ahead.

## Computational Approaches

Traditional weather forecasting relies on numerical weather prediction (NWP), which solves differential equations describing [[concepts/fluid-dynamics|fluid dynamics]] and thermodynamics. Recent developments have introduced machine [[concepts/learning|learning]] and [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] methods as complementary or alternative approaches. These include [[concepts/answer-generation|retrieval-augmented generation]] (RAG) systems, which incorporate relevant historical [[concepts/weather-patterns|weather patterns]] and observational data to inform predictions, and agent-based workflows that coordinate multiple [[concepts/custom-models|specialized models]] or data sources to improve forecast quality and efficiency.

The [[concepts/integration|integration]] of AI-driven methods with classical [[concepts/physics|physics]]-based models represents an emerging direction in operational forecasting. These hybrid approaches can reduce computational costs while maintaining or improving accuracy, particularly for specific forecast variables or lead times where [[concepts/machine-learning|machine learning]] demonstrates advantage.
