# Dark Matter

**Dark matter** is a hypothetical form of matter that is thought to account for approximately 85% of the matter in the universe and about 27% of its total mass–energy [[concepts/density|density]]. Its presence is inferred from [[concepts/gravitational-effects|gravitational effects]] on visible matter, radiation, and the large-scale structure of the universe.

## Key Properties
- **Non-luminous:** Does not emit, absorb, or reflect [[concepts/light|light]], making it invisible to electromagnetic observations.
- **Gravitational Influence:** Interacts with normal matter primarily through [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]].
- **[[concepts/writing|Composition]]:** Likely consists of [[concepts/wimp-particles|weakly interacting massive particles]] ([[concepts/wimp|WIMPs]]) or axions, though the exact nature remains unknown.

## Detection Methods
- **Gravitational Lensing:** Observing how dark matter bends light from distant objects, revealing its distribution.
- **[[concepts/milky-way|Milky Way]] Rotation [[concepts/curves|Curves]]:** Analyzing the rotational [[concepts/velocity|velocity]] of [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]] and gas to infer mass distribution. Recent data challenges standard models, suggesting a [[concepts/tensile-stress|tension]] between observed rotation curves and predictions from both dark matter [[concepts/halos|halos]] and Modified Newtonian Dynamics ([[concepts/modified-newtonian-dynamics-mond|MOND]]). See [[lab-notes/2026-10-12-Milky-Way-Rotation-Data-Challenges-Dark-Matter-and-Modif|Milky Way Rotation Data Challenges Dark Matter and Modified Gravity]] for detailed analysis of this discrepancy.
