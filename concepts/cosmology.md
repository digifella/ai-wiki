# Cosmology

Cosmology is the scientific study of the origin, structure, and evolution of the universe as a whole. It seeks to answer fundamental questions about how the universe began, what it is composed of, how it has evolved over billions of years, and what its ultimate fate may be. By combining observations from astronomy with theoretical physics, cosmologists develop models and frameworks to understand the cosmos on the largest possible scales.

## Observational Foundations

Modern cosmology relies heavily on observational data gathered through telescopes and other instruments. Observations of distant galaxies, the cosmic microwave background radiation, and the expansion of the universe provide crucial evidence for cosmological theories. The measurement of light from distant objects allows astronomers to look back in time and study the universe's history, while precise measurements of cosmic distances and velocities constrain our understanding of its large-scale structure.

## Theoretical Framework

The Big Bang theory serves as the dominant cosmological model, describing the universe's evolution from an extremely hot, dense initial state approximately 13.8 billion years ago. Cosmology also incorporates concepts such as inflation, dark matter, and dark energy to explain observations that challenge simpler models. These theoretical frameworks are constantly refined as new observational data becomes available, allowing for increasingly precise descriptions of cosmic history and structure.
