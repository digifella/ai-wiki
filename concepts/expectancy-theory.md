# Expectancy Theory

**Expectancy [[concepts/theory|Theory]]**, proposed by Victor Vroom, posits that [[concepts/motivation|motivation]] is a calculated decision based on the likelihood of effort leading to performance and performance leading to desired rewards.

## Core Components

Motivation ($M$) is a function of three variables:

1.  **Expectancy ($E \rightarrow P$)**: The belief that increased effort [[entities/will|will]] lead to increased performance.
    *   Influenced by [[concepts/skills|skills]], resources, support, and [[concepts/clear-goals|clear goals]].
2.  **Instrumentality ($P \rightarrow O$)**: The belief that successful performance will be rewarded.
    *   Influenced by [[concepts/trust|trust]] in management and [[concepts/clarity-slider|clarity]] of reward structures.
3.  **Valence ($V$)**: The value the individual [[entities/places|places]] on the reward.
    *   Subjective; varies by individual preferences and needs.

$$ Motivation = Expectancy \times Instrumentality \times Valence $$

*Note: If any factor is [[concepts/concept-of-nothingness|zero]], total motivation is [[concepts/zero|zero]].*

## Related Models & Context

*   **Equity Theory**: Often paired with Expectancy Theory; focuses on fairness of inputs/outputs relative to others.
*   **Goal-Setting Theory**: Complements Expectancy Theory by defining specific performance targets.

## Team Effectiveness Implications

While Expectancy Theory focuses on individual motivation, its principles extend to [[concepts/group-dynamics|team dynamics]]. For effective teamwork, structural conditions must align to support individual expectations and instrumentality.

*   See related analysis on structural prerequisites for high-performing teams: [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Hackman-model-of-team-effectiveness-explained-Five-facto| Five factors for great teamwork — BiteSize Learning]]

## Key Takeaways

*   Motivation is rational and cognitive, not just emotional.
*   Managers must ensure [[entities/employees|employees]] believe effort leads to performance (Expectancy), performance leads to rewards (Instrumentality), and rewards are valued (Valence).
*   Personalize rewards to match individual valences.
