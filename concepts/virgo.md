# Virgo

Virgo is a gravitational wave detector located in Cascina, Italy, operated by the European Gravitational-Wave Observatory (EGO). As one of the primary instruments in the global network of gravitational wave observatories alongside LIGO's facilities in the United States, Virgo uses laser interferometry to detect ripples in spacetime caused by violent cosmic events. The detector became operational in 2007 and has been integral to confirming and localizing gravitational wave sources since the first detection in 2015.

## Technical Design

Virgo employs a Michelson interferometer with 3-kilometer long arms arranged in an L-shape. The facility utilizes high-power lasers and ultra-precise mirrors to measure minute changes in distance between test masses, allowing it to detect distortions in spacetime on the scale of a fraction of a proton's diameter. The design incorporates advanced isolation systems to minimize seismic and thermal noise, ensuring the sensitivity required to observe distant astrophysical phenomena.

## Scientific Contributions

By operating in coincidence with LIGO detectors, Virgo enables triangulation of gravitational wave sources, significantly improving the accuracy of sky localization for events such as binary black hole and neutron star mergers. This collaborative approach has been crucial for multi-messenger astronomy, allowing optical and radio telescopes to identify electromagnetic counterparts to gravitational wave signals. Recent analyses involving Virgo data have also contributed to constraints on the population of sub-solar mass primordial black holes, providing evidence for their potential detection via gravitational wave observations.
