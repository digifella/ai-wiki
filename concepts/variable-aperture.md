# Variable Aperture

**Variable [[concepts/aperture|aperture]]** refers to camera systems capable of mechanically adjusting the size of the lens opening (f-stop) to control [[concepts/light|light]] intake and depth of field. In modern [[entities/mobile-photography|mobile photography]], this technology bridges the gap between computational photography and optical [[concepts/physics|physics]], allowing for improved [[concepts/low-light-performance|low-light performance]] and optical [[concepts/lens-blur|bokeh]] without relying solely on software [[concepts/simulation|simulation]].

## Key Concepts
- **Optical Control**: Unlike fixed-aperture sensors, variable aperture allows physical adjustment of the f-number (e.g., f/1.2 to f/4.0), enabling true optical depth-of-field changes.
- **Low-Light Performance**: Wider apertures (lower f-numbers) gather more light, reducing noise and [[concepts/shutter-speed|exposure time]] in dark environments.
- **Computational Integration**: Modern devices often blend variable aperture data with computational photography [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]] to optimize [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]] and [[concepts/dynamic-range|dynamic range]].
