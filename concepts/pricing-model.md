# Pricing Model

A [[concepts/pricing|pricing]] model defines the structure and strategy a company uses to set prices for its products or services. In the context of [[concepts/ai-platforms|AI services]], pricing models determine how customers are charged for access to models and their capabilities, typically based on factors such as usage volume, feature access, or subscription tier.

## Core Functions

Pricing models serve to balance multiple objectives: generating sufficient revenue for the provider, maintaining [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]] for target customers, and reflecting the actual resource costs of [[concepts/service-delivery|service delivery]]. The choice of [[concepts/pricing-structure|pricing structure]] directly influences customer acquisition, [[concepts/storing|retention]], and the overall [[concepts/market-competitiveness|competitiveness]] of a service offering. Different models suit different market segments and [[concepts/scenarios|use cases]].

## Common Approaches

Common pricing models include per-[[concepts/usage|usage]]-based, subscription-based, and tiered structures. Recent evaluations of competing [[concepts/ai-agent-platforms|AI agent platforms]] provide critical context for these models:

*   **Competitive Landscape Analysis**: Comparative performance data across major [[concepts/ai-agents|AI agents]] is essential for validating cost-benefit analyses in [[concepts/business-strategy|business strategy]].
*   **[[concepts/ai-performance-evaluation|Performance Metrics]]**: Assessments of [[concepts/model-performance|model performance]] across specific business jobs help determine the [[concepts/business-model|value proposition]] of different [[concepts/pricing-tiers|pricing tiers]].
*   **Platform Evaluation**: Detailed comparisons of capabilities such as [[concepts/visual-understanding|visual understanding]] and [[concepts/workflow-automation|task execution]] efficiency inform [[concepts/ai-pricing|AI pricing]] strategies.
