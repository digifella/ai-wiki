A muon is an elementary particle similar to the electron, with a negative electric charge and spin-1/2. It is a lepton belonging to the second generation of leptons and is unstable, decaying into electrons and neutrinos with a mean lifetime of 2.2 μs. [[concepts/muons|Muons]] are produced naturally in cosmic ray interactions and in particle accelerators.

## Fermilab Muon g-2 Experiment
A [[concepts/accuracy|precision]] [[concepts/scientific-experiment|experiment]] at Fermilab measuring the muon's [[concepts/anomalous-magnetic-moment|anomalous magnetic moment]] (g-2), with key aspects:
- Conducted in a building-sized facility using a 14.9 m diameter superconducting magnet ring
- Tests predictions of the [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|Standard Model]] of [[concepts/particle-physics|Particle Physics]] by measuring the muon's precession rate in a magnetic field
- Recent results (2023) showed a 4.2σ discrepancy with [[concepts/standard-model|Standard Model]] predictions, suggesting potential new [[concepts/physics|physics]]
- Upcoming 2025 results [[entities/will|will]] provide higher precision to confirm or refute deviations
