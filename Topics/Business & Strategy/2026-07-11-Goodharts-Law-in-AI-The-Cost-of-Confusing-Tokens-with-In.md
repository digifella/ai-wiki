## Goodhart's Law in AI: The Cost of Confusing Tokens with Intelligence
**Clip title:** AI Just Changed What Intelligence Means
**[[entities/tasia-custode|Author]] / channel:** Dr. Know-it-all Knows it all
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iX-3oJwyLi4

### Summary
The video discusses what it terms a "billion-dollar mistake" within the [[concepts/ai-industry|AI industry]], where companies have focused excessively on generating and consuming AI [[concepts/tokens|tokens]], rather than the actual intelligence or productive outcomes these [[concepts/tokens|tokens]] facilitate. The [[entities/speaker|speaker]] argues that this focus on "tokenmaxxing"—maximizing token usage and spending—is a misdirection, akin to measuring electricity consumption (kilowatt-hours) instead of the practical work it powers, like cooling or lighting. This misguided strategy has led to massive budget overruns for companies like Uber, forcing them to impose spending caps and reassess their approach to AI.

The core of the problem, according to the video, lies in confusing the unit of measurement (tokens) with the economic good being measured (intelligence). This phenomenon is explained through **[[concepts/perverse-incentives|Goodhart's Law]]**, which states that "when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." Companies incentivized [[entities/employees|employees]] to consume more tokens, inadvertently creating an artificial demand for the metric itself, rather than driving valuable outputs. The real value companies seek isn't tokens, but the intelligence and productive work these [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] can generate, such as [[concepts/writing|writing]] software, drafting legal briefs, or creating [[concepts/marketing-campaigns|marketing campaigns]].

Furthermore, the video introduces **Jevons paradox**, which posits that technological improvements increasing [[concepts/model-efficiency|resource efficiency]] often lead to an *increase* in total consumption of that resource, not a decrease. As AI tokens become dramatically cheaper to produce, the demand for AI intelligence doesn't fall; instead, it explodes as new, previously uneconomical applications become viable. This commoditization of [[concepts/advanced-intelligence|advanced intelligence]] is a significant [[concepts/phase|phase]] transition, moving from an "artisan" model (human brains requiring decades of training) to an industrialized one, much like electricity or manufacturing changed after the [[concepts/industrial-revolution|Industrial Revolution]]. [[concepts/unsloth-optimization|NVIDIA]]'s shift towards promoting "AI factories" that produce intelligence, rather than just selling GPUs, exemplifies this understanding, focusing on the infrastructure for generating these commoditized "labor units."

In conclusion, the perceived "collapse of the [[concepts/token-economy|token economy]]" is not a failure but a crucial step in the maturation of the [[concepts/ai-industry|AI industry]]. The future of AI [[entities/will|will]] not be defined by who builds the smartest models, but by who can effectively identify and deliver tangible business outcomes and "labor units" powered by increasingly abundant and affordable intelligence. The industry is transitioning from a "mainframe" era, where AI is accessed centrally, towards a more diffused and integrated future, where the real winners [[entities/will|will]] be those who can [[concepts/harness|harness]] commoditized intelligence to solve real-[[entities/earth|world]] problems and create new value chains.
