# Villa of the Papyri

The **Villa of the Papyri** is a Roman villa complex in Herculaneum, [[entities/italy|Italy]], famously preserved by the eruption of [[concepts/mount-vesuvius]] in 79 AD. It is renowned for its extensive collection of [[concepts/herculaneum-papyri]], a library of over 1,800 [[concepts/carbonized-papyrus|carbonized papyrus]] scrolls.

## The Herculaneum Papyri

The scrolls, likely belonging to the villa's owner (traditionally identified as Lucius Calpurnius Piso Caesoninus), contain primarily Epicurean philosophical texts. Due to carbonization, the scrolls were historically difficult to read without physical damage.

### Recent Developments in Decipherment

Significant advancements in digital humanities and [[concepts/artificial-intelligence]] have transformed the study of these texts.

*   **[[concepts/virtual-unrolling|Virtual Unrolling]]:** New AI techniques allow for the non-invasive "virtual unrolling" of carbonized scrolls, revealing text previously inaccessible to traditional physical methods.
*   **Breakthroughs:** Recent reports indicate that [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] are successfully deciphering complete scrolls, marking a major shift in classical scholarship.
