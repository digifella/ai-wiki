# pH Modification

pH modification is a cooking technique that involves adjusting the alkalinity of [[concepts/ground-meat|ground meat]] to improve its culinary properties. The most common method uses [[concepts/baking-soda|baking soda]] (sodium bicarbonate), a mild alkaline agent, which raises the pH of the meat's surface and interior. This chemical change affects how the meat's proteins behave during cooking, producing noticeable improvements in the final dish.

## Effects on Texture and Moisture

Raising the pH of ground meat [[concepts/causes|causes]] its proteins to denature differently than they would at the meat's natural pH. This altered protein structure allows the meat to retain more moisture during cooking, resulting in a juicier final product. The [[concepts/texture|texture]] becomes noticeably more tender, even in [[concepts/lean|lean]] ground meat varieties that might otherwise cook dry. This effect is particularly valuable for ground meats used in dishes where moisture [[concepts/storing|retention]] is important.
