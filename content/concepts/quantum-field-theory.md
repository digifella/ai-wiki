# Quantum Field Theory (QFT)

Quantum field [[concepts/theory|theory]] is a theoretical framework that combines classical field theory, special relativity, and [[concepts/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]]. It serves as the foundation for the [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|Standard Model]] of [[concepts/particle-physics|particle physics]].

### Summary
- QFT provides a mathematical description of particles and their interactions using fields.
- Theories within QFT include electrodynamics (QED), chromodynamics (QCD), and the Higgs mechanism.

### Key Concepts
- **Field [[concepts/parameter-reduction|Quantization]]**: Process where quantum properties are assigned to classical fields.
- **[[concepts/feynman|Feynman]] [[concepts/diagrams|Diagrams]]**: Graphical representations of particle interactions.
- **Renormalization**: Technique for removing infinities in QFT calculations.

### Historical Development
- Quantum field theory emerged from the work of [[entities/paul|Paul]] Dirac and [[entities/richard-feynman|Richard Feynman]], among others, during the mid-20th century.
- It has evolved to incorporate insights from [[concepts/string-theory|string theory]] and [[concepts/mathematics|mathematical physics]], offering a more comprehensive understanding of fundamental forces.

### Recent Experimental Tests
- The **[[concepts/muon|Muon]] g-2 experiment** [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] Fermilab measured the muon's [[concepts/anomalous-magnetic-moment|anomalous magnetic moment]], revealing a 4.2σ discrepancy with [[concepts/standard-model|Standard Model]] predictions, indicating potential new [[concepts/physics|physics]] beyond the Standard Model.
