Limited resources refers to the fundamental constraint that organizations face when allocating finite quantities of capital, labor, time, materials, and other business inputs. This scarcity is inherent to all business environments and represents the core economic reality that drives strategic decision-making. Because every organization has boundaries on what it can spend, build, or deploy, choices about resource allocation are never optional—they are a necessity of operation.

## Strategic Implications

Resource constraints force organizations to make deliberate tradeoffs between competing priorities. When capital is limited, a company cannot simultaneously pursue all possible projects or expand into all available markets. Similarly, finite labor requires prioritization of which functions or initiatives receive staffing first. These constraints are not temporary obstacles but permanent features of organizational life that shape competitive strategy, product development, and operational planning.

## Common Resource Types

Organizations typically manage constraints across multiple resource categories. Financial capital determines what investments and operations are feasible. Human resources limit the scope and speed of work execution. Time affects both product development cycles and market response capabilities. Physical materials and infrastructure impose constraints on production capacity and geographic reach. Each of these limitations interacts with others, creating complex allocation decisions.

The recognition of limited resources is foundational to business strategy, pushing organizations to develop methodologies for prioritization, efficiency, and optimal allocation that define their competitive approach.
