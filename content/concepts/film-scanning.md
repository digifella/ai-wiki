# Film Scanning

Film scanning is the process of converting physical film negatives or transparencies into digital image files. [[concepts/high-quality-images|Scanning film at home]] offers photographers and enthusiasts an alternative to commercial lab services, providing cost savings and greater control over the final digital output. The practice has become increasingly accessible as scanning technology has improved and prices have become more affordable for consumer-level equipment.

## Equipment and Methods

[[concepts/camera-scanning|Home film scanning]] typically involves using a dedicated film scanner or, in some cases, a [[concepts/flatbed-scanner|flatbed scanner]] with appropriate film holders. Dedicated film scanners are designed specifically for 35mm film and offer higher optical resolution and better color [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]] than general-[[concepts/motivation|purpose]] alternatives. The choice of equipment depends on factors such as budget, desired output quality, and the volume of film to be scanned.

## Benefits and Considerations

Scanning film at home eliminates ongoing lab fees and allows users to adjust color correction, [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], and other [[concepts/parameters|parameters]] during the scanning process rather than being limited to lab settings. However, the initial investment in scanning equipment and the [[concepts/learning|learning]] curve for proper technique and [[concepts/software|software]] use should be considered. [[entities/storage|Storage]] and [[concepts/organization|organization]] of digital files also becomes an important workflow consideration for those scanning large quantities of film.
