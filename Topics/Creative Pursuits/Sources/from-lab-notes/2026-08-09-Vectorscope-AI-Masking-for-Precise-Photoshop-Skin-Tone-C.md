## Vectorscope & AI Masking for Precise Photoshop Skin Tone Correction
**Clip title:** The Vectorscope Trick for PERFECT Skin Tones in Photoshop
**Author / channel:** Photoshop Training Channel
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oZAC8R6zL8A

### Summary
This video provides a comprehensive tutorial on utilizing the new Vectorscope feature in [[entities/adobe-photoshop|Adobe Photoshop]]'s [[concepts/camera-raw-filter|Camera Raw Filter]] to achieve perfect, natural-looking skin tones. The main topic revolves around understanding and applying this [[concepts/color-analysis|color analysis]] display to make precise adjustments to hue and saturation, specifically for human skin. The speaker emphasizes that the Vectorscope makes the complex process of color correction more intuitive and accurate.

The tutorial begins by demonstrating how to access the Vectorscope within the Camera Raw Filter, revealing its circular display that maps color information in an image. Key points discussed include that the angle of color clusters from the center represents hue, while their distance from the center signifies saturation. The Vectorscope only reflects hue and saturation, not contrast, highlights, or shadows, although adjusting these light parameters can indirectly affect saturation. The video highlights the Vectorscope's critical "Skin Tone Indicator" line, a reference guide for where natural skin tones should fall, as all skin tones share a similar hue regardless of individual differences in saturation and brightness.

A significant challenge in skin tone correction is making targeted adjustments without affecting the entire image. The video introduces an effective solution: using Photoshop's AI-powered masking tools. By navigating to the masking panel and specifically selecting components like "Facial Skin," "Body Skin," "Eyebrows," and "Lips," users can create a mask that isolates only the skin areas. This crucial step allows the Vectorscope to then display color information *solely* for the selected skin, enabling highly precise, localized color manipulation.

Once the skin is masked, the video details how to adjust its color. By using the "Point Color" eyedropper to sample a skin tone, users can then utilize the Hue, Saturation, Luminance, and Variance sliders to align the skin tone cluster with the Vectorscope's Skin Tone Indicator line. The Hue slider is used to shift the skin tone horizontally towards the indicator line, while the Saturation slider adjusts its intensity (typically kept below the midpoint for naturalness). Luminance controls brightness, and Variance helps unify or diversify the range of colors within the selected skin area.

In conclusion, the Vectorscope, especially when combined with Photoshop's masking capabilities, offers a powerful and visually guided method for achieving accurate and natural skin tones. By understanding how hue, saturation, and luminance are represented and by isolating skin areas for targeted adjustments, users can effectively correct color casts and ensure that skin appears balanced and realistic in their images. The video demonstrates a before-and-after comparison, showcasing a significantly improved image with natural-looking skin tones, reinforcing the efficacy of this technique.

#### Description
Photoshop's new Vectorscope has a hidden Skin Tone Line that tells you exactly when skin looks natural.

The Vectorscope is a new color-analysis display in the Camera Raw Filter. It maps every color in your image onto a circle, where the angle around the center is the Hue and the distance from the center is the Saturation. One glance tells you what colors your image is made of.

The real payoff is the skin tone line. It is a reference for where natural skin should fall, no matter the subject's ethnicity, because skin tones vary in saturation and brightness but not in hue. 

In this tutorial, you'll mask the skin, read where it lands on the Vectorscope, and push it onto that line using Point Color to achieve natural skin tones in Photoshop.

This is for anyone whose skin tones come out too yellow, too red, or oversaturated and can't tell why.

In this video, you'll learn:
- How to turn on the Vectorscope in Camera Raw
- How to read Hue and Saturation on the Vectorscope
- How to show the skin tone line indicator
- How to mask and target skin tones using People masking
- How to fix skin tones with Point Color
- How to adjust Hue, Saturation, Luminance, and Variance for natural skin
- How to unify skin tones across a portrait
