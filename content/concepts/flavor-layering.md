# Flavor Layering

Flavor layering is a fundamental cooking technique that involves building depth and complexity in a dish by adding ingredients and seasonings in strategic stages rather than all [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] once. This method allows each component to develop its own character while contributing to a cohesive whole, resulting in more nuanced and satisfying flavors.

## Application in Chef Neel Kajale's North Indian Skillet Chicken Curry

[[entities/neel-kajale|Chef Neel Kajale]] demonstrates flavor layering principles in his [[concepts/chicken-curry|North Indian skillet chicken curry]] recipe. The technique involves sequentially introducing aromatics, spices, and proteins at different points during cooking, allowing the heat and time to transform each layer. By tempering whole spices first, building a base with onions and tomatoes, and then adding the chicken and finishing elements, Kajale creates a curry with balanced complexity rather than one-dimensional heat or spice.

This approach is characteristic of traditional North Indian cooking, where spice blends and cooking stages are carefully orchestrated. The layered method ensures that ground spices bloom in hot oil, aromatics caramelize properly, and proteins absorb flavors throughout the cooking process. The result is a dish where individual spice [[concepts/notes|notes]] remain discernible while working together as a unified flavor profile.
