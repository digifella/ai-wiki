# Vermicelli Cooking

Vermicelli cooking encompasses techniques for preparing thin, delicate noodles used throughout Asian cuisines. These noodles are typically made from wheat or rice flour mixed with water, then extruded into strands considerably thinner than spaghetti. The delicate structure of vermicelli requires specific cooking approaches to prevent overcooking or breaking the noodles while achieving the desired texture.

## Basic Preparation

Vermicelli noodles cook quickly due to their thin diameter, typically requiring only 4–8 minutes in boiling water. Some varieties, particularly rice vermicelli, can be softened by soaking in hot water rather than boiling, which gives the cook greater control over final texture. Draining and rinsing cooked vermicelli with cold water stops the cooking process and prevents the noodles from clumping together.

## Singapore Fried Vermicelli

Singapore fried vermicelli is a popular stir-fried noodle dish that combines cooked vermicelli with vegetables, proteins, and curry seasoning. According to food historian Flo Lum, despite its name, Singapore fried vermicelli originated in Hong Kong during the mid-twentieth century, developed by Chinese cooks adapting fried noodle techniques to the thinner vermicelli strand. The dish is prepared by stir-frying pre-cooked vermicelli over high heat with aromatics, protein, and vegetables, then finishing with curry powder and seasonings to create a cohesive, lightly spiced noodle dish.
