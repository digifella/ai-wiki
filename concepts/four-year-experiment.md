# Four Year Experiment

A four-year [[concepts/scientific-experiment|experiment]] is an extended research, development, or manufacturing project characterized by sustained iterative work over a multi-year timeline. Such projects require repeated cycles of testing, refinement, and validation before achieving [[concepts/commercial-viability|commercial viability]] or [[concepts/proof|proof]] of concept. The extended duration allows teams to thoroughly test assumptions, identify unforeseen technical or [[concepts/operational-challenges|operational challenges]], and incrementally improve processes or products through accumulated [[concepts/learning|learning]] rather than rushing to market with incomplete solutions.

## Business Applications

In the context of business strategy, this approach is often employed to address significant safety or quality concerns that cannot be resolved through [[concepts/rapid-prototyping|rapid prototyping]]. A notable example is the project initiated by [[entities/destin-sandlin|Destin Sandlin]] of the [[entities/youtube|YouTube]] channel *[[entities/smarter-every-day|Smarter Every Day]]*. Motivated by his parents' background as [[entities/union-autoworkers|union autoworkers]] and the known dangers of wire bristle brushes, Sandlin undertook a four-year effort to manufacture an American-made grill brush that eliminated the risk of detached bristles.

This long-term [[concepts/software-sprint|development cycle]] allowed for the rigorous [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] required to create a durable, safe alternative to traditional wire brushes. By prioritizing iterative improvement over [[concepts/speed|speed]], the project demonstrated how extended timelines can facilitate the creation of products that meet high [[concepts/product-safety|safety standards]] and ethical manufacturing criteria, ultimately proving that complex physical products require substantial time to reach a viable commercial state.
