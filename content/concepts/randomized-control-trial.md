# Randomized Control Trial

A randomized controlled trial (RCT) is a research methodology in which participants are randomly assigned to either a treatment group or a [[concepts/power|control]] group. This random assignment helps ensure that differences in outcomes between groups can be attributed to the intervention being tested rather than to pre-existing differences between participants. RCTs are considered the gold standard for evaluating the effectiveness of interventions across medical, psychological, and [[concepts/health|health]]-related fields.

## Application in Digital Health Research

RCTs are particularly valuable in [[concepts/digital-health|digital health]] and [[concepts/mental-health|mental health]] research, where they can be used to evaluate the efficacy of apps, online interventions, and digital therapeutic tools. The [[concepts/jungian-grief-model|SES One Intervention]], developed [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] the University of Copenhagen, represents an example of digital mental health research that employs rigorous trial methodology. This intervention includes interactive modules addressing [[concepts/conflict|conflict]] resolution and other mental health topics, delivered through a user-friendly digital platform.

## Methodological Considerations

In digital health contexts, RCTs must account for unique challenges such as user engagement patterns, adherence to digital interventions, and the [[concepts/digital-paradox|digital paradox]]—the observation that while people express interest in digital health solutions, actual usage rates may not match these intentions. Proper randomization and control group [[concepts/design|design]] help researchers distinguish between the effects of the intervention itself and other factors influencing outcomes in digital mental health studies.
