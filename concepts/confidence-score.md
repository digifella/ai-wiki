# Confidence Score

A confidence score is a numerical metric that quantifies the certainty or reliability of outputs generated by Large Language Models (LLMs). Typically expressed as a value between 0 and 1 or as a percentage, these scores represent the model's self-assessed likelihood that its response is accurate and factually grounded. By making uncertainty explicit, confidence scores help developers and end-users identify when an LLM may be producing hallucinations—plausible but factually incorrect information that the model generates with high certainty.

In the context of AI agents, confidence scores serve as a critical control mechanism for mitigating errors in automated workflows. When an agent relies on an LLM to retrieve information or make decisions, the confidence score allows the system to evaluate the quality of the generated content before proceeding. If the score falls below a predefined threshold, the agent can trigger fallback mechanisms, such as requesting human review, re-running the query with different parameters, or abstaining from action to prevent the propagation of incorrect data.

The utility of confidence scores is significantly enhanced when combined with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and structured prompt engineering. In RAG systems, confidence metrics can be derived from the relevance of retrieved documents to the query, providing a more objective measure of accuracy than the model's internal logits alone. Similarly, prompt engineering techniques that encourage step-by-step reasoning or self-verification can improve the calibration of these scores, ensuring that the numerical output more accurately reflects the true probability of correctness.

Despite their utility, confidence scores have limitations and are not infallible indicators of truth. LLMs can exhibit overconfidence, assigning high scores to incorrect answers, particularly in domains with sparse training data or complex logical reasoning tasks. Consequently, confidence scores should be treated as probabilistic estimates rather than definitive guarantees. Effective AI agent design requires integrating these scores with other validation methods, such as cross-referencing external knowledge bases or using ensemble models, to maintain system reliability and trustworthiness.
