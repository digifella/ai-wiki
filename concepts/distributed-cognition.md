# Distributed Cognition

**Distributed [[concepts/cognition|Cognition]]** is a [[concepts/theory|theoretical framework]] positing that [[concepts/human-cognition|cognitive processes]] are not confined to individual minds but are distributed across individuals, tools, artifacts, and the environment. It emphasizes the interdependence of internal (neural) and external (sociotechnical) resources in [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] and knowledge processing.

## Core Principles

- **System-Level Processing**: Cognition is an emergent property of the interaction between agents and their context.
- **Externalization**: Information is offloaded into the environment (e.g., [[concepts/notes|notes]], [[concepts/diagrams|diagrams]], [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]]) to reduce [[concepts/cognitive-load|cognitive load]] and enable [[concepts/complex-reasoning|complex reasoning]].
- **Representation Transformation**: Cognition involves the continuous transformation of representations across different media and agents within the system.

## Empirical Evidence and Applications

- **[[concepts/group-dynamics|Team Dynamics]] as Cognitive Systems**: [[concepts/team-effectiveness|Team effectiveness]] relies on the efficient distribution of cognitive tasks and shared mental models.
