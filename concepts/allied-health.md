# Allied Health

[[entities/allied-health|Allied health]] refers to a diverse group of [[concepts/health|health]] professions that support and work alongside doctors and nurses in delivering [[concepts/roles-in-community-settings|patient care]]. These professions include physiotherapy, occupational therapy, speech pathology, podiatry, [[concepts/psychology|psychology]], dietetics, audiology, and numerous other specialties. [[concepts/ahps|Allied health professionals]] focus on [[concepts/preventive-care|prevention]], diagnosis, treatment, and rehabilitation across physical, mental, and [[concepts/social-well-being|social health]] domains. They typically comprise a significant portion of the [[concepts/health-care|healthcare]] workforce in most developed health systems.

## Scope and Practice

[[concepts/allied-health-professionals|Allied health professionals]] work in varied settings including hospitals, community clinics, private practice, residential care facilities, and educational institutions. ## Digital Health Integration

The sector is increasingly adopting [[concepts/digital-health|digital health]] technologies, including [[concepts/wearable-devices|wearable devices]] with [[concepts/embedded-sensors|embedded sensors]] and [[concepts/EMR|EMRs]]/EHRs. These tools aim to enhance care [[concepts/coordination|coordination]], safety, and [[concepts/efficient-operation|operational efficiency]].

Recent analysis of sector-specific digital challenges identifies critical readiness gaps:
- [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-allied-health-digital-readiness-issues-paper|allied-health-digital-readiness-issues-paper]] highlights specific barriers regarding provider access to national [[concepts/electronic-health-records|digital health records]] (e.g., [[entities/my-health-record|My Health Record]]).
- Consumer uptake of these digital records remains a significant variable affecting overall system efficacy and [[concepts/data-completeness-checking|data completeness]].
