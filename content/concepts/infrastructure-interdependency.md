# Infrastructure Interdependency

Infrastructure interdependency refers to the interconnected [[entities/nature|nature]] of modern systems where failures in one sector can cascade across others, amplifying impacts beyond the initial point of failure. This concept is particularly relevant to critical infrastructure like [[concepts/power|power]] [[concepts/grids|grids]], [[concepts/water-management-systems|water systems]], and telecommunications networks, which depend on each other for operation while simultaneously serving as dependencies for one another. Understanding these [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] is essential for identifying systemic vulnerabilities and designing more resilient systems.

## The 2021 Texas Power Grid Outage

The February 2021 [[concepts/electricity-grid|power grid]] failure in [[entities/texas|Texas]] demonstrated infrastructure interdependency in practice. [[concepts/winter-weather|Winter weather]] caused generation capacity to drop across multiple sources simultaneously—natural gas production facilities froze, wind turbines iced over, and coal plants experienced fuel supply disruptions. The cascading failures were not isolated to electricity generation; the power shortage then affected natural gas [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] systems, which required electricity to operate compressors and heating systems, creating [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] [[concepts/loops|loops]] that prolonged the crisis. [[entities/ercot|ERCOT]]'s ([[concepts/ercot-electric-reliability-council-of-texas|Electric Reliability Council of Texas]]) response involved controlled [[concepts/power-outages|blackouts]] to prevent grid-wide collapse, but the interdependent nature of the infrastructure meant that even "controlled" outages affected water treatment, heating systems, and emergency services that relied on continuous power.

## Systemic Implications

The Texas case illustrates that infrastructure failures rarely remain contained within a single sector. When power systems fail, natural gas pipelines may freeze without heating; when water systems lose power, sanitation and fire suppression [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] degrade; when communications networks lose backup power, emergency response coordination breaks down. These interdependencies mean that planning and investment decisions in one infrastructure domain have consequences across others, requiring coordinated [[concepts/resilience|resilience]] strategies rather than sector-by-sector approaches.
