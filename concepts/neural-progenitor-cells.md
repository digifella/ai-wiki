# Neural Progenitor Cells

**Neural progenitor cells ([[concepts/npcs|NPCs]])** are the self-renewing, multipotent cells that give rise to the neurons and glial cells of the central-nervous-system. Historically, the prevailing model posited that the human brain developed from a single, unified pool of NPCs.

## Recent Discoveries: Dual Embryonic Origins

Recent research challenges the traditional monolithic view of brain development, suggesting a more complex [[concepts/evolutionary-lineage|evolutionary history]].

- **Dual Origin Hypothesis:** Groundbreaking work by [[entities/stanford|Stanford]] developmental [[concepts/biology-researchers|biologists]] indicates that the human brain may have emerged from two separate embryonic pools rather than a single unified one [[lab-notes/2026-10-12-Dual-Embryonic-Origins-of-Human-Brain-Development|Dual Embryonic Origins of Human Brain Development]].
- **Implications for NPC Behavior:** This discovery suggests that distinct populations of neural progenitor cells may have different developmental trajectories, proliferation rates, and differentiation potentials depending on their embryonic origin.
- **Evolutionary Context:** The "two organs" [[concepts/theory|theory]] implies that the human brain is a composite structure, potentially explaining unique aspects of [[concepts/human-cognition|human cognition]] and susceptibility to certain neurodevelopmental disorders.
