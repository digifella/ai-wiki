# Ultra Wide Lens

An ultra wide lens is a camera lens characterized by an exceptionally broad field of view, typically exceeding 100 degrees. These lenses utilize short focal lengths, generally ranging from 14mm to 24mm on full-frame sensors, with proportionally shorter focal lengths required for crop-sensor formats to achieve equivalent angles of view. The primary function of this optical design is to capture a substantially larger portion of a scene than standard lenses, making them essential tools for landscape, architectural, and environmental photography where spatial context is critical.

## Visual Characteristics

Ultra wide lenses produce distinctive visual properties defined by their optical geometry. They exhibit significant perspective distortion, causing objects near the edges of the frame to appear stretched or curved, a phenomenon often referred to as barrel distortion. This characteristic exaggerates the sense of depth and scale, allowing foreground elements to appear dramatically larger while background elements recede rapidly. Consequently, these lenses are frequently employed to create dynamic compositions that emphasize the vastness of a setting or the proximity of a subject.

## Applications and Techniques

Due to their expansive coverage, ultra wide lenses are integral to panoramic photography and image stitching techniques. Photographers often use them to capture high-resolution images that would be impossible with standard focal lengths, particularly in tight interior spaces or sweeping natural vistas. While they offer unique creative possibilities, their use requires careful attention to composition to manage distortion and ensure that key subjects remain within the usable central area of the frame.
