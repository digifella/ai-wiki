# Precipitation Patterns

[[concepts/iran|Iran]] [[concepts/faces|faces]] a severe and long-standing water crisis due to its inherently [[concepts/arid-geography|arid geography]] and low average annual precipitation, ranking 158th globally. Historically, much of the country's central lands were uninhabitable without sophisticated [[concepts/water-management|water management]] techniques such as [[concepts/qanats|qanats]] (underground irrigation tunnels). The nation's water problem is compounded by the fact that 75% of its limited rainfall occurs on only 25% of its land area.

- **Ancient [[concepts/qanat|Qanat]] Management:** [[concepts/qanats|Qanats]] have been a cornerstone in managing scarce water resources for centuries, capturing and channeling groundwater to agricultural lands. These systems are crucial in transforming [[concepts/desert-landscapes|arid landscapes]] into productive farmland.
- **20th Century Decline:** The 20th century saw a significant decline in the maintenance of [[concepts/qanat-management|qanat systems]] due to modernization efforts and shifts towards more energy-intensive water extraction methods, such as deep wells powered by diesel pumps. This shift has exacerbated Iran's water crisis, leading to over-extraction of groundwater reserves.
- **Geographical [[concepts/distribution|Distribution]]:** Only 25% of Iran’s land receives 75% of its precipitation, making water [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] extremely uneven and challenging for agricultural and residential areas that rely heavily on qanat systems.
