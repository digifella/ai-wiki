# Feng Shui

Feng Shui is a Chinese philosophical system for organizing physical spaces to promote harmony and wellbeing. Developed over more than two thousand years, it is based on the belief that the arrangement of environments affects the flow of chi (vital life energy) and consequently influences human fortune, health, and prosperity. The name literally translates to "wind-water," referencing natural forces historically understood to shape landscapes and their habitability.

## Core Principles

Feng Shui operates on several foundational concepts drawn from Chinese philosophy. The system emphasizes balance between opposing forces (yin and yang), the five elements (wood, fire, earth, metal, and water), and the directional associations of the compass. Practitioners analyze how these elements interact within a space and how they relate to the person occupying it. The goal is to arrange furniture, structures, and design elements in ways that optimize the beneficial flow of chi while minimizing blockages or negative influences.

## Practice and Application

Feng Shui practitioners traditionally conduct assessments of buildings and spaces, making recommendations for modifications to layout, color, materials, and décor. Applications range from residential homes and offices to the planning of entire cities and gardens. While rooted in ancient Chinese cosmology and metaphysics, Feng Shui has become increasingly popular in Western countries, where practitioners sometimes adapt its principles to contemporary interior design and architecture. The system remains influential in East Asian cultures and continues to inform both traditional practices and modern design thinking.
