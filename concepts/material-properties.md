# Material Properties

Material properties are the observable and measurable characteristics that describe how a substance responds to physical stimuli and behaves under various conditions. These properties emerge directly from the atomic and [[concepts/molecular-structure|molecular structure]] of materials and determine their suitability for specific applications. Understanding material properties is essential across [[concepts/physics|physics]], materials chemistry, [[entities/national-academies|engineering]], and manufacturing, as they govern how materials perform in real-[[entities/earth|world]] situations.

## Physical Properties

Physical properties describe how a material behaves without changing its chemical [[concepts/writing|composition]]. Thermal properties include melting point, thermal conductivity, and heat capacity, which determine how a material responds to temperature changes. Mechanical properties such as hardness, elasticity, tensile strength, and ductility describe how materials deform under [[concepts/stress|stress]]. Electrical and optical properties characterize how materials conduct electricity and interact with [[concepts/light|light]]. [[concepts/density|Density]] and porosity define the material's mass distribution and internal structure.

## Chemical Properties

Chemical properties determine how a material reacts with other substances or changes under specific environmental conditions. These include corrosion resistance, oxidation behavior, and reactivity with acids, [[concepts/number-systems|bases]], or other chemicals. Chemical stability affects a material's longevity and performance in different chemical environments.

## Applications and Testing

Material properties are measured and characterized through standardized testing procedures such as tensile testing, hardness measurements, and thermal analysis. The choice of material for any application depends on selecting substances whose properties align with the required performance specifications. For instance, aerospace applications demand materials with high strength-to-weight ratios, while electrical applications require materials with specific conductivity characteristics.
