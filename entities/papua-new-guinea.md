# Papua New Guinea

Papua New Guinea is a country occupying the eastern half of the island of New Guinea in Oceania, sharing the island with Indonesia. Located in the southwestern Pacific Ocean, the nation is characterized by mountainous terrain, tropical rainforests, and significant geographical diversity. Papua New Guinea gained independence from Australia in 1975 and is a member of the Commonwealth. The country is known for its linguistic and cultural diversity, with hundreds of distinct languages spoken across its population.

## Kuru and Medical Significance

Papua New Guinea holds particular importance in medical and anthropological history due to the emergence of kuru disease among the Fore people in the Eastern Highlands. Kuru, a fatal neurodegenerative disease, was studied extensively in the mid-twentieth century and provided crucial evidence for the protein-only hypothesis of infectious disease. Research into kuru's transmission and mechanism contributed significantly to the understanding of prion diseases and earned researcher Carleton Gajdusek the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1976. The disease's association with certain cultural practices among the Fore community also became a landmark case in medical anthropology.
