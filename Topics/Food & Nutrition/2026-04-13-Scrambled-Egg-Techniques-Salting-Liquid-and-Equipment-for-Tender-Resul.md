## Scrambled Egg Techniques: Salting, Liquid, and Equipment for Tender Results
**Clip title:** The Only Scrambled Eggs Recipe You'll Ever Need | Techniquely with Lan Lam
**Author / channel:** [[entities/america|America]]'s Test Kitchen
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=keGd9CXUHco

### Summary
This video from Cook's Illustrated, presented by [[entities/lan-lam|Lan Lam]], demystifies the [[concepts/art|art]] of making scrambled eggs, emphasizing that while seemingly simple, technique is key to achieving your desired [[concepts/texture|texture]] and flavor. The core message is that by understanding a few basic [[concepts/scientific-principles|scientific principles]] and utilizing the right tools, anyone can master perfectly cooked scrambled eggs, customized to their preference.

The [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] begins with the eggs themselves, recommending two to three eggs per person, depending on whether they are the main dish or part of a larger meal. A crucial step highlighted is salting the eggs early (about 1/8 to 1/4 teaspoon for five eggs) and allowing them to sit for 5-10 minutes. This early salting ensures better flavor distribution and, more importantly, unwinds the egg proteins, leading to a more tender finished product that sets more quickly. Additionally, a tablespoon of liquid, such as milk, cream, or even water, is added to further dilute the egg proteins, preventing them from bonding too tightly and resulting in softer, more tender eggs. The eggs are then beaten with a fork (for small batches) or whisk until the yolks and [[concepts/whites|whites]] are fully incorporated.

For cooking, the video stresses the importance of using the right equipment. An 8-inch non-stick skillet is ideal for five eggs, as a smaller pan provides better control over the cooking process. Butter is not just for flavor; its water content acts as a [heat indicator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/heat_indicator). The pan is heated on [[entities/medium|medium]] until the butter begins to bubble gently and evenly across the surface. Once achieved, the heat is increased to medium-high, and the beaten eggs are poured in. Constant, slow stirring with a rubber spatula is key. Slow stirring allows the eggs to heat and set more thoroughly, resulting in a denser, more custardy texture, while quicker stirring yields softer scrambles.

Finally, once no runny egg is visible, the heat is turned off, any large clumps are broken up, and the pan is covered with a lid for about a minute and a half. This "coasting" period uses [residual heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/residual_heat) to finish cooking the eggs without overcooking them on the direct heat, which could squeeze out moisture and lead to a rubbery texture. The video concludes by showcasing various garnishes and toppings, such as chili crisp, pesto, everything bagel seasoning, furikake, and harissa, encouraging viewers to experiment and find their favorite combinations for perfectly fluffy and tender scrambled eggs.
