# Industrial Revolution

Transformative period (c. 1760–1840) characterized by transition from agrarian/handicraft economies to machine-driven manufacturing, factory systems, and steam power. Originated in [[entities/uk|Great Britain]], initiating global shifts in production, [[concepts/urbanization]], and class structure under Capitalism.

## Key Developments
- **Technological [[concepts/innovation|Innovation]]:** Widespread [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of Steam [[concepts/engine|Engine]], Spinning Jenny, and power loom; decoupled production from geographical constraints of water power.
- **Industrial Sectors:** Textile Industry mechanization led initial wave; subsequent expansion into iron, coal, and transportation (railways).
- **Energy Transition:** Shift from biomass/water to fossil fuels (coal), enabling sustained output increases and heavy industry.

## Socio-Economic Impact
- **Labor Restructuring:** Collapse of cottage industries; concentration of labor in factories; [[concepts/emergent-behavior|emergence]] of wage-dependent Proletariat and asset-holding Bourgeoisie.
- **[[concepts/quality-of-life|Standard of Living]]:** Early [[concepts/phase|phase]] marked by harsh working conditions, child labor, and urban squalor; long-term trajectory showed rising [[concepts/productivity|productivity]], GDP growth, and eventual consumer good [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]].
- **[[concepts/geopolitics|Geopolitics]]:** British economic dominance expanded via industrial output and naval power; triggered competitive [[concepts/industrialization|industrialization]] across [[entities/europe|Europe]] and North [[entities/america|America]].

## Resistance and Labor Unrest
- Mechanization disrupted traditional livelihoods, eroded wage stability, and threatened skilled autonomy, provoking organized resistance framed as defense of economic rights rather than opposition to progress.
- **Luddite [[concepts/exercise|Movement]]:** Modern usage often mislabels participants as technophobes; [[concepts/historical-context|historical context]] reveals sophisticated [[concepts/socio-economic-grievances|socio-economic grievances]] targeting specific labor abuses.
- Resistance motivations included:
  - [[concepts/secure|Protection]] of skilled craftsmanship and informal employment contracts undermined by mechanization.
  - Opposition to wage suppression and piece-rate exploitation enabled by machine integration.
  - Demands for political recognition and [[concepts/preservation|restoration]] of [[entities/meta-fair-lab|fair]] compensation structures threatened by factory owners.
  - Actions focused on property destruction targeting livelihood threats, not abstract rejection of technology.
