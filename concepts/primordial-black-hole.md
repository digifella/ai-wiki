# Primordial Black Hole

Primordial [[concepts/black-holes|black holes]] (PBHs) are hypothetical black holes that would have formed in the extremely [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]], within fractions of a second after the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]], from [[concepts/density|density]] fluctuations in the primordial plasma rather than from [[concepts/gravitational-collapse|stellar collapse]]. Unlike stellar black holes, which form when massive [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]] reach the end of their lives, PBHs could span an enormous range of masses—from microscopic objects to supermassive bodies—depending on the initial conditions and density variations present in the early cosmos.

## Formation and Theoretical Basis

PBHs would form when regions of sufficiently high density in the [[concepts/primordial-universe|early universe]] collapsed gravitationally before the universe expanded enough to prevent their formation. The conditions necessary for this process were present only during an extremely brief window in cosmic history. Because PBHs would not be constrained by the mass limits of [[concepts/stellar-evolution|stellar evolution]], they represent a distinct category of black holes with potentially unique properties and distributions.

## Observational Evidence and Detection

For decades, PBHs remained purely theoretical. Recent gravitational wave detections, particularly from the LIGO-[[concepts/virgo|Virgo]] collaboration, have revealed merging compact objects with masses in the range of tens of solar masses—lighter than typical stellar black holes but heavier than [[concepts/neutron-stars|neutron stars]]. These observations have renewed serious consideration of PBHs as a possible explanation for some of these events, though neutron star mergers and other formation channels remain under investigation as alternative sources.

## Current Status

While PBHs remain unproven, they remain an active area of theoretical and observational research. They have also been proposed as a potential component of [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]], though constraints from various observations limit the allowed mass ranges and abundances of any PBH population in the universe.
