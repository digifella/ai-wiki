# Temperature Adjustment

Temperature adjustment refers to the process of correcting or modifying the color temperature of digital images, typically to compensate for lighting conditions or achieve a desired aesthetic. In photography and image editing, temperature is measured on the Kelvin scale, with warmer (more orange/red) tones at lower values and cooler (bluer) tones at higher values. This adjustment is one of the fundamental color correction tasks in post-processing workflows, allowing editors to neutralize unwanted color casts or intentionally shift the mood of an image.

## Standard Implementation

Traditionally, this process is managed through White Balance controls in RAW converters or dedicated Temperature/Tint sliders in software like Adobe Lightroom and Photoshop. These tools allow for precise mathematical manipulation of the color balance, enabling users to match the image to a specific light source or create artistic contrast. The adjustment often works in tandem with saturation and vibrance controls to ensure that color shifts do not result in unnatural skin tones or loss of detail in highlights and shadows.

## Adobe Photoshop 2025 Update

In the 2025 release of Adobe Photoshop, the interface for these adjustments has been consolidated into a new Color and Vibrance tool. This update, detailed in tutorials by Blake Rudis, streamlines the workflow by integrating temperature control with vibrance management. The tool aims to provide a more intuitive approach to balancing color intensity and warmth simultaneously, reducing the need for multiple adjustment layers and offering a unified method for achieving accurate color representation in digital creative pursuits.
