# Crystalline Structure

Crystalline structure refers to the organized, repeating arrangement of atoms or molecules in a solid material. In crystalline solids, atoms are positioned in a highly ordered, geometric pattern that extends throughout the material, creating defined [[concepts/faces|faces]], edges, and angles. This orderly arrangement contrasts with amorphous solids, where atoms lack long-[[concepts/range|range]] order. The specific crystal [[concepts/structure|structure]] of a material directly influences its [[concepts/material-properties|physical properties]], including hardness, melting point, transparency, and electrical conductivity.

## Formation and Classification

Crystals form when atoms arrange themselves into their lowest energy state, a process that occurs during cooling, evaporation, or precipitation from [[concepts/solution|solution]]. Crystal structures are classified into several systems based on their geometry: cubic, tetragonal, orthorhombic, hexagonal, trigonal, monoclinic, and triclinic. Each system has characteristic symmetries and lattice [[concepts/parameters|parameters]]. The atoms within a crystal are held in place by chemical bonds, and the strength and type of these bonds [[concepts/power|influence]] the material's overall mechanical and thermal properties.

## Practical Applications

Understanding crystalline structure is essential in materials [[concepts/science|science]], chemistry, and engineering. X-ray diffraction is the primary technique used to determine crystal structures, revealing atomic spacing and arrangement. Crystalline materials include metals, minerals, semiconductors, and salts. By manipulating crystalline structure through processes such as annealing, tempering, or doping, scientists and engineers can engineer materials with specific desired properties for [[concepts/software|applications]] ranging from electronics to construction to pharmaceuticals.
