# Crisis

**Crisis** refers to a time of intense difficulty, trouble, or danger that disrupts normal operations and poses a threat to organizational or individual stability. In organizational [[concepts/psychology|psychology]], crises are characterized by uncertainty, high stakes, and the need for rapid [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]].

## Key Dimensions

- **Disruption**: Breaks in routine processes and expectations.
- **Uncertainty**: Lack of information regarding outcomes or duration.
- **Threat**: Potential for significant harm to reputation, assets, or [[concepts/well-being|well-being]].

## Psychological Impact on Employees

Crises trigger significant [[concepts/stress|psychological stress]], affecting [[concepts/employee-well-being|employee well-being]] and [[concepts/mental-health]]. The nature of the crisis (e.g., natural disaster, economic collapse, pandemic) dictates the specific trauma responses, but common outcomes include [[concepts/anxiety|anxiety]], burnout, and reduced cognitive performance.

## Mitigation Strategies: Leadership

[[concepts/leadership|Leadership]] [[concepts/style|style]] during crisis is a critical determinant of employee resilience.

- **[[concepts/inclusive-leadership|Inclusive Leadership]]**: Recent longitudinal evidence suggests that inclusive leadership practices are pivotal in maintaining [[concepts/psychological-well-being|psychological well-being]] during traumatic events. By fostering belonging, [[concepts/tone|voice]], and empowerment, leaders can buffer the negative impacts of crisis-induced stress.
