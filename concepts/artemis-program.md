# Artemis Program

The Artemis Program is [[concepts/nasa|NASA]]'s initiative to return humans to the Moon and establish sustained [[concepts/lunar-exploration|lunar exploration]]. Named after the twin sister of Apollo in [[concepts/greek-mythology|Greek mythology]], the program represents a continuation of lunar [[concepts/science|science]] and exploration that began with the Apollo missions of the 1960s and 1970s. Artemis aims to land astronauts on the lunar surface, with particular emphasis on the south polar region where water ice deposits have been detected.

## Program Components and Objectives

The program comprises multiple elements working in conjunction. The Space Launch System (SLS) serves as the primary launch vehicle, while the Orion spacecraft carries crew to lunar orbit. The Human Landing System (HLS) is designed to transport astronauts from orbit to the surface. Key mission phases include Artemis I (uncrewed test flight), Artemis II (crewed lunar orbit mission), and Artemis III (planned crewed lunar landing). Beyond initial landings, the program envisions establishing a sustained human presence through the proposed Lunar Gateway, an orbital outpost that would support extended exploration activities.

## Current Status and Challenges

The program has encountered significant technical and scheduling obstacles. Both the Space Launch System and Human Landing System have experienced development delays and cost overruns. As of recent assessments, the timeline for Artemis III has been pushed beyond its original target dates. These challenges reflect the technical complexity of developing new deep space exploration systems and the budgetary constraints facing large-scale government space projects.
