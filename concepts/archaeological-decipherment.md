# Archaeological Decipherment

**Archaeological decipherment** is the process of interpreting undeciphered or partially deciphered writing systems, inscriptions, or texts found in archaeological contexts. It combines linguistic analysis, [[concepts/historical-context|historical context]], and increasingly, [[concepts/calculation-methods|computational methods]] to unlock meaning from ancient materials.

## Key Methods & Technologies

*   **Traditional Philology:** Relies on comparative [[concepts/linguistics|linguistics]], paleography, and contextual historical knowledge.
*   **Computational Assistance:** Use of [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]] to identify patterns, predict missing characters, and analyze ink [[concepts/writing|composition]].
*   **[[concepts/virtual-unrolling|Virtual Unrolling]]:** Non-invasive imaging techniques to read scrolls without physical damage.

## Recent Breakthroughs: Herculaneum Papyri

The **[[concepts/herculaneum-papyri|Herculaneum Papyri]]**, a collection of over 1,800 [[concepts/carbonized-papyrus|carbonized papyrus]] scrolls discovered in the 18th century in the [[concepts/villa-of-the-papyri|Villa dei Papiri]], have long been a challenge due to their fragile, carbonized state.

*   **AI-Driven Virtual Unrolling:** Recent advances in [[entities/ai]] have enabled the virtual unrolling and decipherment of these scrolls without physical intervention.
*   **Significance:** This approach allows scholars to read text previously inaccessible due to the scrolls' fragility, potentially revealing new insights into Philodemus, Epicureanism, and [[concepts/ancient-greek]] literature.
