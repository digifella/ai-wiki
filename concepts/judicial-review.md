# Judicial Review

**Judicial review** is the process by which a court examines the lawfulness of decisions or actions made by public bodies, including government officials and tribunals. It ensures that administrative power is exercised within legal boundaries and adheres to principles of natural justice.

## Key Principles
- **Legality**: Decision-makers must act within the scope of their statutory authority.
- **Procedural Fairness**: Affected parties must be given a [[entities/meta-fair-lab|fair]] [[concepts/sound-perception|hearing]] and notice of the case against them.
- **Rationality**: Decisions must be based on logical [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] and relevant evidence.
- **Proportionality**: In some jurisdictions, the severity of the action must match the seriousness of the issue.

## Australian Context
In Australia, judicial review is primarily governed by the *Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977* (Cth) for federal matters, and similar state-based legislation. It focuses on the *process* of [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] rather than the *merits* of the decision.
