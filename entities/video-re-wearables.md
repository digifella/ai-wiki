# Video Re Wearables

Wearable devices equipped with embedded sensors are transforming clinical practice in allied health by enabling continuous, real-time monitoring of patient physiological data. These electronic instruments are designed to be worn directly on the body and collect metrics including heart rate, movement patterns, temperature, and other biometric indicators. Unlike consumer fitness applications, clinical-grade wearables are validated for medical accuracy and integrated into formal healthcare workflows.

In allied health professions, these technologies support physiotherapists and other practitioners in tracking patient progress and adherence to rehabilitation protocols. The continuous data stream allows for more precise adjustments to treatment plans based on objective evidence rather than periodic clinical assessments alone. This shift facilitates a more proactive approach to patient care, identifying potential issues before they become critical.

The integration of these devices into standard practice requires careful consideration of data privacy and interoperability with existing electronic health records. As the technology matures, the focus remains on ensuring that the collected data is clinically actionable and that the devices meet rigorous standards for reliability and safety in medical contexts.
