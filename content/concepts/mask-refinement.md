# Mask Refinement

Mask refinement refers to the [[concepts/iterative-refinement|iterative process]] of improving and adjusting [[concepts/layer-masks|layer masks]] in [[concepts/image-editing|image editing]] workflows, particularly within [[concepts/photoshop|Adobe Photoshop]]. This technique involves modifying mask properties after initial creation to achieve more precise selections, smoother transitions, and better [[concepts/power|control]] over which parts of an image are affected by layer [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]]. Refinement operations typically include feathering edges, adjusting mask [[concepts/opacity|opacity]], expanding or contracting selections, and [[concepts/fine-tuning|fine-tuning]] mask boundaries to better match intended subject matter.

## Workflow Integration

The concept emerged prominently in discussions surrounding [[concepts/camera-system-integration|Lightroom Classic V15]], where [[concepts/mask-creation|mask creation]] and adjustment [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] were expanded to provide more sophisticated control over localized edits. The refinement process acknowledges that initial mask creation rarely produces perfect results, and that subsequent adjustment steps are a normal and necessary part of achieving desired outcomes. This [[concepts/workflow|workflow]] [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]] has influenced how modern [[concepts/photo-editing|photo editing]] [[concepts/software|software]] presents mask tools to users, often positioning refinement options as integral to the [[concepts/masking|masking]] process rather than secondary corrections.

## Technical Aspects

Mask refinement typically involves several adjustable [[concepts/parameters|parameters]] including feather radius (which softens mask edges), density (controlling mask opacity), and edge detection algorithms that help distinguish between subject and background. These tools allow editors to recover details in transition areas, reduce hard edges that appear unnatural, and create seamless blends between masked and unmasked regions. The refinement approach represents a shift toward [[concepts/non-destructive-editing|non-destructive editing]] practices, where masks themselves remain editable rather than being permanently applied to underlying layers.
