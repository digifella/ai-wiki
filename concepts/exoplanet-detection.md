# Exoplanet Detection

The systematic identification and characterization of planets outside the Solar System. Key methodologies include the Transit Method, Radial [[concepts/velocity|Velocity]], and Gravitational Microlensing.

## Detection Methods

*   **Transit [[concepts/photometry|Photometry]]**: Measures periodic dips in stellar brightness as a planet passes in front of its host star.
*   **Radial Velocity**: Detects stellar wobble induced by planetary [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravitational pull]] via Doppler shifts.
*   **[[concepts/direct-imaging|Direct Imaging]]**: Captures actual [[concepts/light|light]] from the planet, requiring advanced coronagraphy or starshades.
*   **Gravitational Microlensing**: Exploits general relativity; a foreground object's gravity magnifies light from a background star, revealing companions.

## Recent Developments: Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope

The [[concepts/nancy-grace-roman-space-telescope]] (Roman) represents a significant advancement in [[concepts/survey|survey]] capabilities, particularly for microlensing events and wide-field imaging.

*   **Mission Status**: Following its simulated launch, the telescope has demonstrated initial operational [[concepts/success|success]].
*   **Projected Lifespan**: The mission is designed for a 22-year operational lifespan, allowing for extensive long-term monitoring of exoplanetary populations.
*   **Scientific Impact**: Roman is expected to drastically increase the [[concepts/catalog|catalog]] of known exoplanets, especially those in the habitable zones of distant [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]] and [[concepts/rogue-planets|free-floating planets]], complementing data from [[concepts/james-webb-space-telescope]] and TESS.
*   **Initial Results**: Early test images and mission [[concepts/software-updates|updates]] confirm the instrument's readiness for high-[[concepts/accuracy|precision]] photometry.
