# Seismic Data

Seismic data refers to recordings of seismic waves traveling through Earth's interior, captured by networks of seismometers placed across the planet's surface. These instruments detect vibrations caused by [[concepts/earthquakes|earthquakes]], volcanic activity, and controlled explosions, allowing scientists to map the [[concepts/structure|structure]] and composition of Earth's interior layers at depth and resolution impossible to achieve through direct observation.

## Applications in Earth Science

Seismic data has been instrumental in understanding [[concepts/earths-internal-structure|Earth's internal structure]], from the crust through the mantle to the core. By analyzing how seismic waves travel, refract, and reflect at different depths and densities, researchers have been able to characterize layer boundaries, identify composition changes, and detect anomalies in expected patterns. This information forms the basis for models of planetary structure and evolution.

## Recent Findings

Recent analysis of seismic data from [[concepts/inner-core|Earth's inner core]] has revealed unexpected wave behavior that does not match predictions based on known states of matter. These anomalies suggest that iron and other elements at the extreme pressures and temperatures of the inner core may exist in a previously unknown state of matter, with properties distinct from solid, liquid, or plasma states typically observed under terrestrial conditions. Further investigation of these seismic patterns may provide insights into the fundamental behavior of matter under extreme conditions.
