### Summary
The [[concepts/p-vs-np-problem|P vs NP problem]] stands as one of the most significant unsolved questions in [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] and computer science, carrying a [[concepts/million-dollar-prize|million-dollar prize]] for its [[concepts/solution|solution]]. It fundamentally asks whether problems whose solutions can be quickly *verified* (NP) can also be quickly *solved* (P). A definitive answer could revolutionize fields from medicine to [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]].

### Key Points
- The problem is one of the seven [[concepts/millennium-prize-problems|Millennium Prize Problems]], each carrying a $1 million prize.
- It questions the relationship between two sets of problems: P and NP.
  - **P**: Problems that can be solved in polynomial time by a deterministic [[concepts/turing-machine|Turing machine]].
  - **NP**: Problems whose solutions can be verified in polynomial time by a non-deterministic [[concepts/turing-machine|Turing machine]].
- Implications span across various fields including [[concepts/cryptography|cryptography]], algorithm [[concepts/design|design]], and [[concepts/theoretical-computer-science|theoretical computer science]].
- Historical context includes contributions from mathematicians like Stephen Cook and Richard Karp.
