# High Quality Images

High quality images in analog photography refers to the practice of digitizing film negatives and [[concepts/google-slides|slides]] at home while preserving maximum detail and tonal information from the original. Rather than relying on commercial scanning services, [[concepts/camera-scanning|home film scanning]] allows photographers to maintain control over the digitization process and achieve results tailored to their specific needs. The goal throughout the entire workflow—from initial capture through scanning and post-processing—is to retain as much visual information as possible from the film [[entities/medium|medium]].

## The Home Scanning Approach

Based on tutorials by [[entities/lotta-milde|Lotta Milde]] from [[entities/bh-photo|B&H Photo]], the process emphasizes careful handling of film to prevent damage and ensure accurate color reproduction. This method involves using dedicated film scanners or high-[[concepts/solution|resolution]] DSLR/mirrorless setups with macro lenses to capture the grain structure and [[concepts/dynamic-range|dynamic range]] inherent in analog media. Proper cleaning of the film surface is critical to avoid dust artifacts that are difficult to remove in post-processing.

## Workflow and Post-Processing

The digitization workflow typically begins with flatbed or dedicated film scanners that offer high bit-depth capabilities to capture subtle tonal gradations. For those using camera-based scanning, consistent lighting and precise [[concepts/blur-correction|focus stacking]] techniques are employed to maintain [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]] across the entire frame. Post-processing involves converting raw scan data to adjust exposure and [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], ensuring that the digital file accurately reflects the intent of the original negative while preserving highlight and shadow details.
