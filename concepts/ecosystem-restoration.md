# Ecosystem Restoration

**[[concepts/habitat-recovery|Ecosystem restoration]]** is the process of assisting the recovery of an ecosystem that has been degraded, damaged, or destroyed. It aims to restore ecological [[concepts/honesty|integrity]], [[concepts/biodiversity|biodiversity]], and ecosystem services, often moving beyond simple [[concepts/preservation|conservation]] to active intervention.

## Core Principles
- **Ecological [[concepts/integrity|Integrity]]**: Restoring native species [[concepts/writing|composition]] and structural complexity.
- **[[concepts/resilience|Resilience]]**: Enhancing the system's ability to withstand disturbances.
- **Context-Specificity**: Strategies must align with local biogeography and historical baselines.
- **Stakeholder Engagement**: Integrating indigenous knowledge and community needs.

## Strategies and Methods
- **Passive Restoration**: Removing stressors (e.g., fencing, pollution control) to allow natural regeneration.
- **Active Restoration**: Direct intervention such as replanting, soil amendment, or hydrological repair.
- **Rewilding**: Reintroducing [[concepts/apex-predation|keystone species]] and restoring trophic cascades to [[concepts/ambition|drive]] self-sustaining processes.
- **[[concepts/invasive-species|Invasive Species]] Management**: Eradication or control of non-native species that outcompete natives.

## Case Studies and Applications

### Banks Peninsula Rewilding: Hugh Wilson's Gorse-Based Approach
A notable deviation from conventional invasive species eradication is documented in [[lab-notes/2026-07-10-Banks-Peninsula-Rewilding-Hugh-Wilsons-Gorse-Based-Ecosy|Banks Peninsula Rewilding: Hugh Wilson's Gorse-Based Ecosystem Restoration]]. This case highlights:
- **[[concepts/mindset-shift|Paradigm Shift]]**: Botanist [[entities/hugh-wilson|Hugh Wilson]] challenged the standard practice of immediate removal of [[concepts/gorse]] (*Ulex europaeus*), a thorny invasive weed.
- **[[concepts/ecological-succession|Ecological Succession]]**: Utilized gorse as a nurse crop to stabilize [[concepts/degraded-farmland|degraded farmland]] soil and provide shelter for native seedling establishment.
- **Outcome**: Successful rewilding of large tracts of land on [[concepts/banks-peninsula|Banks Peninsula]], demonstrating that invasive species can sometimes serve as transitional infrastructure in restoration ecology rather than solely as targets for elimination.

## Challenges
- **Funding and Long-term Commitment**: Restoration projects often require decades to show full ecological maturity.
- **[[concepts/climate-change|Climate Change]]**: Shifting baselines make historical restoration targets potentially obsolete.
- **Social [[concepts/conflict|Conflict]]**: Balancing [[concepts/land-use|land use]] rights with ecological goals.
