# Cosmic Expansion

**Core Definition:** Metric expansion of space characterized by increasing proper distance between gravitationally unbound regions over time. Fundamental component of the [[concepts/big-bang]] [[concepts/cosmology|cosmology]] and Lambda-CDM Model.

## Dynamics & Drivers
- **Mechanism:** Expansion dynamics described by Friedmann Equations; current accelerated [[concepts/phase|phase]] attributed to [[concepts/the-composition-of-the-universe-extends-beyond-what-is-directly-observable|Dark Energy]] ($\Omega_\Lambda$).
- **Parameters:** Expansion rate quantified by the Hubble Constant ($H_0$); spatial geometry constrained by Curvature of the Universe measurements.
- **Observables:** Inferred via Redshift surveys, Type Ia [[concepts/supernova-standard-candles|Supernovae]], and [[concepts/cosmic-microwave-background|Cosmic Microwave Background]] anisotropies.

## Observable Universe Scale & Horizon
- **Age-Size Paradox:** The [[concepts/observable-universe|observable universe]] has a current proper radius of ~46 billion light-years, despite the universe being only ~13.8 billion years old. This discrepancy arises because space itself expanded while [[concepts/light|photons]] traveled from distant sources to the observer.
- **Particle Horizon:** The limit of causal [[entities/contact|contact]] is defined by the particle horizon, which grows faster than the [[concepts/speed|speed]] of light in comoving coordinates due to metric expansion.
- **Source Analysis:** Detailed explanation of this geometric effect is provided in [[lab-notes/2026-07-17-Observable-Universe-Expansion-Age-vs.-Current-Size-Expla|Observable Universe Expansion: Age vs. Current Size Explained]].
