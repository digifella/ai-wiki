# Human Landing System

The Human Landing System (HLS) is a critical spacecraft component within NASA's Artemis program, designed to transport astronauts from lunar orbit to the Moon's surface and return them to the Orion spacecraft. It functions as the final leg of the crewed lunar return architecture, operating in conjunction with the Space Launch System (SLS) rocket and the Orion spacecraft. This system enables the primary objective of the Artemis missions: establishing a sustainable human presence on the Moon.

Development of the HLS has faced significant schedule challenges, impacting the readiness for Artemis 3. The program initially relied on a single commercial provider, but NASA later expanded the contract to include multiple vendors to mitigate risk and ensure delivery timelines. These adjustments reflect the complexity of integrating new commercial technologies with existing government infrastructure to meet the ambitious goals of returning humans to the lunar surface.

The operational model for the HLS involves the Orion spacecraft carrying the crew to the vicinity of the Moon, where the HLS docks. The system then descends to the surface, allowing astronauts to conduct extravehicular activities before ascending back to lunar orbit for rendezvous with Orion. This architecture marks a shift from previous lunar programs by leveraging commercial partnerships for the landing phase, aiming to reduce costs and increase mission frequency for future exploration.
