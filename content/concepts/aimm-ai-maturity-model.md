# Aimm Ai Maturity Model

The Aimm (Ai Maturity Model) is a framework for evaluating and developing [[concepts/kai|personal AI infrastructure]], presented by [[entities/daniel-miessler|Daniel Miessler]]. The model provides a structured approach for individuals to systematically build and improve their use of [[concepts/ai-tools|AI tools]] and systems over time. Rather than treating AI [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] as an all-or-nothing transition, the framework conceptualizes AI capability development as a progression through defined maturity stages, allowing people to incrementally advance their relationship with [[concepts/ai-technologies|AI technologies]].

## Purpose and Application

The Aimm model is designed to help individuals assess their current level of [[concepts/ai-integration|AI integration]] and identify logical next steps for development. By establishing clear maturity levels, the framework enables practitioners to move beyond ad-hoc tool experimentation toward deliberate, staged capability building. This approach is particularly relevant for developing what Miessler describes as personal AI infrastructure—the systems, workflows, and practices through which individuals [[concepts/leverage-ai|leverage AI]] in their professional and personal lives.

## Maturity Progression

The model structures AI adoption as a journey rather than a destination, acknowledging that different individuals and organizations [[entities/will|will]] be [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] different stages of capability development. Each maturity level typically encompasses aspects such as tool familiarity, integration depth, [[concepts/workflow-optimization|workflow optimization]], and strategic application of AI across multiple domains. This staged approach allows practitioners to build sustainable AI practices incrementally rather than attempting comprehensive adoption all at once.
