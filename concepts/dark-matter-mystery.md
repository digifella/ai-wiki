# Dark Matter Mystery

The [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] mystery denotes a persistent discrepancy in observational [[concepts/cosmology|cosmology]] between the mass of visible [[concepts/baryonic-matter|baryonic matter]] and the total [[concepts/gravitational-mass|gravitational mass]] inferred from the dynamics of [[concepts/galaxy-clusters|galaxy clusters]]. Astronomers determine total mass through observations of galaxy rotation curves, [[concepts/velocity|velocity]] dispersions, and gravitational lensing effects. These measurements consistently indicate that the gravitational forces holding clusters together are significantly stronger than what can be accounted for by [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]], interstellar gas, and dust alone.

Historically, this mass deficit has been attributed to dark matter, a hypothetical form of matter that interacts gravitationally but does not emit, absorb, or reflect [[concepts/light|electromagnetic radiation]]. This paradigm posits that dark matter constitutes the majority of matter in the universe, providing the necessary gravitational scaffolding for large-scale structure formation. The [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|standard model]] of cosmology relies on this invisible mass component to explain the stability of galaxies and the clustering of galaxies on cosmic [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]].

Recent research has challenged the completeness of this explanation by highlighting underestimated baryonic matter within galaxy clusters. Advanced observational techniques suggest that previous models may have underestimated the amount of hot, diffuse gas and other baryonic components in the intracluster [[entities/medium|medium]]. If the mass of this visible matter is higher than previously calculated, the required amount of dark matter to explain the observed [[concepts/gravitational-effects|gravitational effects]] may be less than current theories predict, [[concepts/prompting|prompting]] a re-evaluation of the balance between baryonic and [[concepts/invisible-universe|non-baryonic components]] in cosmological models.
