# Physical World Modeling

Physical World Modeling is the approach of using mathematical and computational tools to represent real-world systems without necessarily requiring deep theoretical understanding of underlying principles. Rather than pursuing [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] as an abstract discipline, this perspective treats mathematical [[concepts/models|models]] as pragmatic instruments for prediction, [[concepts/simulation|simulation]], and [[concepts/power|control]]. The approach prioritizes functional utility—whether a model produces correct outputs—over conceptual comprehension of why the model works.

## The Mayan Example

[[concepts/feynman|Richard Feynman]] illustrated this distinction through the example of [[concepts/mayan-mathematics|Mayan mathematics]] and [[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]]. The Mayans developed highly accurate models for predicting celestial events and calendar cycles without possessing the [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|theoretical frameworks]] that underpin modern [[concepts/physics|physics]] or mathematics. Their methods worked because they were calibrated to observable patterns, yet the practitioners operated from a different conceptual foundation than contemporary scientists. This demonstrates that effective prediction does not require equivalent understanding of underlying mechanisms.

## Application to Modern Systems

This principle has contemporary relevance in simulation and [[concepts/robotics|robotics]], where computational models bridge the gap between [[concepts/simulation-based-training|simulated training]] environments and real-world [[concepts/deployment|deployment]] ([[concepts/sim2real|sim2real transfer]]). Systems trained on physical [[concepts/world-models|world models]] can operate effectively in [[concepts/complex-tasks|complex tasks]] even when the models contain approximations or when operators lack complete theoretical understanding of all system dynamics. The emphasis shifts from mathematical elegance to [[concepts/empirical-validation|empirical validation]] and practical performance.
