# Meat Preparation

Meat preparation refers to the techniques and methods used to cook various protein sources including poultry, pork, and fish. These methods transform raw meat into safe, palatable dishes through the application of heat and complementary cooking processes. The choice of [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] method depends on factors including the cut of meat, desired [[concepts/texture|texture]], flavor profile, and available equipment.

## Common Cooking Methods

[[concepts/bbq|Grilling]] involves cooking meat over direct or indirect heat, typically producing browned exteriors and variable doneness levels depending on [[concepts/temperature-management|heat management]]. Oven-cooking provides controlled, even heat [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] suitable for larger cuts and batch preparation. Poaching uses gentle simmering in liquid—such as cider-based broths—to produce tender, moist results. These methods can be combined with marinading, where meat is soaked in seasoned liquid before cooking to enhance flavor and tenderness.

## Technique Considerations

The juiciness and texture of cooked meat depend on several factors including marinade composition, cut selection, and cooking temperature. For example, poultry benefits from marinades that allow flavors to penetrate while maintaining moisture during cooking. Achieving uniform results, as with bacon cooked via oven methods or water-assisted techniques, requires [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to heat distribution and timing. Different proteins—chicken, salmon, and pork—respond variably to these methods and benefit from technique [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] suited to their composition and [[concepts/structure|structure]].
