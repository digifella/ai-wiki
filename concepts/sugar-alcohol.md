# Sugar Alcohol

**[[concepts/sugar|Sugar]] alcohols** (polyols) are a class of [[concepts/artificial-sweetener|sugar substitutes]] found in nature and used commercially as sweeteners. They are carbohydrates with a free aliphatic hydroxyl group functional group; they contain both aldehyde and alcohol functional groups. Common examples include [[entities/xylitol|Xylitol]], Sorbitol, Mannitol, and Erythritol.

## Properties & Metabolism
- **Sweetness**: Generally 40–100% as sweet as [[concepts/sucrose]].
- **Caloric Content**: Lower than sucrose (1.6–2.5 kcal/g vs 4 kcal/g).
- **[[concepts/yeast-fermentation|Metabolism]]**: Absorbed slowly and incompletely in the small intestine; not fermented by oral bacteria, reducing cavity risk.
- **Gastrointestinal Effects**: Poorly absorbed polyols draw water into the intestine via osmosis, potentially causing bloating, gas, and laxative effect in high doses.

## Health Implications
- **Dental [[concepts/health|Health]]**: Bacteria in plaque cannot easily convert sugar alcohols into acid, making them cariostatic (non-cavity-causing) and often found in sugar-free gum and mints.
- **Blood Sugar**: Minimal impact on blood glucose and insulin levels compared to [[concepts/sucrose]], making them suitable for Diabetes management in moderation.
