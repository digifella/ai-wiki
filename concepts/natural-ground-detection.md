# Natural Ground Detection

Natural Ground Detection is a post-processing technique used in Adobe Lightroom and Camera Raw to identify and isolate ground elements within landscape photographs. The method employs AI-powered masking tools that automatically recognize and separate terrestrial surfaces—such as soil, dirt, grass, and rock—from other image components like sky, water, and vegetation. This selective isolation enables photographers to apply targeted adjustments to ground areas without affecting the rest of the composition.

## Workflow and Application

The technique typically begins with activating the landscape or ground mask feature within Lightroom or Camera Raw's masking panel. The software analyzes the image to detect areas likely to be natural ground based on texture, color, and spatial positioning. Once the mask is generated, photographers can refine its boundaries manually if needed, then apply adjustments such as exposure, contrast, saturation, clarity, or temperature specifically to the isolated ground region. This allows for independent control over how terrestrial elements are rendered relative to the sky and other landscape features.

## Common Use Cases

Natural Ground Detection is particularly useful for landscape photographers seeking to enhance soil definition, recover shadow detail in foreground areas, or balance exposure between ground and sky. It streamlines workflows that might otherwise require manual selection tools or layer-based editing, reducing the time spent on precise masking while maintaining non-destructive adjustments within the raw processing environment.
