# Richard Feynman

**Richard Feynman** (1918–1988) was an American theoretical physicist and Nobel laureate known for his work in quantum electrodynamics, particle physics, and superfluidity. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential physicists of the 20th century.

## Key Contributions

- **[[concepts/feynmans-all-paths-interpretation|Path Integral Formulation]]**: Developed the [[concepts/path-integral-formulation]] of quantum mechanics, positing that a particle takes all possible paths between two points simultaneously. This framework revolutionized the understanding of quantum behavior and is foundational to modern quantum field theory.
- **Feynman Diagrams**: Invented Feynman diagrams, a pictorial representation scheme for the mathematical expressions governing the behavior of subatomic particles, simplifying complex calculations in quantum electrodynamics.
- **Quantum Electrodynamics (QED)**: Shared the 1965 Nobel Prize in Physics for his fundamental work in QED, resolving infinities in the theory and predicting precise values for electron magnetic moments.
- **Parton Model**: Contributed to the understanding of deep inelastic scattering, proposing the parton model which helped establish quantum chromodynamics (QCD).
