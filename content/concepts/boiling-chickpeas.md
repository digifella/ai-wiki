# Boiling Chickpeas

Boiling chickpeas is a foundational cooking technique for preparing dried chickpeas into a tender, edible form. The process begins with soaking dried chickpeas in water for at least 8 hours or overnight, which rehydrates them and reduces cooking time. After draining the soaked chickpeas, they are simmered in fresh water for 1–2 hours until tender, though cooking time varies depending on the chickpea variety and age. The goal is to achieve a consistent [[concepts/texture|texture]] throughout without splitting the skins.

## Nutritional Profile

Chickpeas are a legume high in plant-based protein and dietary fiber. A cooked cup of chickpeas (approximately 164 grams) provides roughly 15 grams of protein and 12 grams of fiber, along with essential minerals like iron, folate, and manganese. This nutrient density makes them a common ingredient in [[concepts/weight-loss|weight management]] diets, as both protein and fiber promote [[concepts/satiety|satiety]] and help regulate appetite by slowing digestion and maintaining stable blood sugar levels.

## Use in Salads

Boiled chickpeas are a versatile base for salads combining vegetables, herbs, and dressing. When incorporated into salad dishes with leafy greens, whole grains, or fresh vegetables, chickpeas increase the meal's nutritional completeness and satisfaction value. The firm texture of properly boiled chickpeas holds up well to dressing without becoming mushy, making them practical for meal [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] and [[entities/storage|storage]].
