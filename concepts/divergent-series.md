# Divergent Series

A series in [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] that does not converge to a finite limit. While standard summation yields infinity, advanced techniques allow for the assignment of finite values to these series, which is critical in [[concepts/quantum-field-theory]] and [[concepts/string-theory]].

## Key Concepts

- **Regularization**: The process of assigning finite values to divergent expressions.
- **[[concepts/analytic-continuation|Analytic Continuation]]**: Extending the domain of a given function. Crucial for defining the [[concepts/riemann-zeta-function]] $\zeta(s)$ beyond its original region of convergence.
- **Ramanujan Summation**: An alternative method for assigning sums to divergent series.

## The -1/12 Result

The sum of all positive integers $1 + 2 + 3 + \[[concepts/dots-agent|dots]]$ is classically divergent. However, through analytic continuation of the [[entities/bernhard-riemann|Riemann]] [[concepts/zeta-function|Zeta Function]] $\zeta(s)$, we find that $\zeta(-1) = -1/12$.

- This result is not a standard arithmetic sum but a value derived via [[Analytic Continuation: Deriving -1/12 for Quantum Physics Applications]].
- It appears in the Casimir Effect and the calculation of the Vibrational Modes of strings in bosonic string [[concepts/theory|theory]].
- The physical interpretation relies on the regularization of infinite [[concepts/type-ia-supernovae|vacuum energy]].
