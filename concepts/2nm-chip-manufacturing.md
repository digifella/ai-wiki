# 2nm Chip Manufacturing

2nm chip manufacturing refers to semiconductor production at the 2-nanometer process node, representing one of the most advanced tiers of integrated circuit fabrication. The "2nm" designation is a marketing convention established by the semiconductor industry rather than a precise physical measurement. It does not correspond directly to the actual dimensions of transistors or other physical features on the chip. Instead, process node names reflect historical naming practices that have persisted as transistor scaling has slowed in recent years.

## Technical Characteristics

At the 2nm node, the smallest physical features on a chip—including transistor gates, fins in FinFET designs, and metal interconnects—measure several nanometers in actual dimension, typically ranging from 5 to 40 nanometers depending on the specific feature type. The node represents an incremental advancement in transistor density, power efficiency, and performance compared to larger process nodes like 3nm and 5nm. Manufacturing at this scale requires extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography and other specialized techniques to achieve the necessary precision.

## Production and Development

As of the early 2020s, 2nm process technology was in early development or limited production by leading semiconductor manufacturers including TSMC, Samsung, and Intel. The extreme complexity and cost of establishing 2nm fabrication facilities make production highly concentrated among a small number of companies with the necessary capital, expertise, and infrastructure. Commercial availability of chips manufactured at the 2nm node remains limited, with production primarily directed toward high-performance applications such as advanced processors and specialized computing systems.
