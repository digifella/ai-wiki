# Ph Levels

pH is a logarithmic scale used to specify the acidity or alkalinity of a substance, ranging from 0 to 14. It measures the concentration of hydrogen ions in a solution, where a pH of 7 indicates neutrality. Values below 7 denote acidity, while values above 7 indicate alkalinity. Because the scale is logarithmic, each whole number change represents a tenfold difference in hydrogen ion concentration; for instance, a substance with a pH of 5 is ten times more acidic than one with a pH of 6 and one hundred times more acidic than a neutral substance.

## Measurement and Calculation

The pH value is determined by the negative base-10 logarithm of the hydrogen ion activity in a solution. In food science, this metric is critical for understanding preservation, texture, and flavor profiles. Acids such as citric acid in citrus fruits or lactic acid in fermented dairy lower the pH, creating environments that inhibit the growth of many pathogenic bacteria. Conversely, alkaline foods, such as certain leafy greens or mineral waters, possess higher pH values.

## Relevance in Food Nutrition

In the context of food and nutrition, pH levels influence both the safety and the nutritional quality of consumables. Foods with a pH below 4.6 are generally considered high-acid and are less susceptible to botulism, allowing for safer home canning without pressure processing. Additionally, the acidity of food affects the bioavailability of certain minerals and vitamins; for example, acidic environments can enhance the absorption of iron and calcium. Understanding pH helps in food processing, as it dictates the effectiveness of preservatives and the stability of enzymes during storage and preparation.
