# Philosophy

Philosophy is the systematic study of fundamental questions about existence, knowledge, values, and [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]]. The term derives from ancient Greek, meaning "love of wisdom." Philosophers employ conceptual analysis and logical argumentation to examine foundational issues that cut across disciplines and everyday experience. Rather than relying on empirical observation alone, philosophy uses rational inquiry to clarify concepts, identify assumptions, and construct coherent frameworks for understanding reality.

## Core Areas of Inquiry

The discipline traditionally divides into several major branches. Metaphysics investigates the [[entities/nature|nature]] of reality and existence itself. Epistemology examines how knowledge is acquired and what makes beliefs justified. [[concepts/ethics|Ethics]] explores questions of right action and the good life. Logic studies the principles of valid reasoning. Aesthetics considers the nature of beauty and art, while political philosophy addresses justice and [[concepts/governance|governance]]. These domains frequently overlap, as answers in one area often influence conclusions in others.

## Method and Practice

Philosophical method emphasizes careful argumentation, conceptual precision, and critical examination of assumptions. Philosophers construct arguments to support their positions while identifying potential objections and counterarguments. This dialogical approach—[[concepts/testing|testing]] ideas against opposing views—has been central to philosophy since Socrates. Rather than seeking final answers, philosophy often clarifies what questions mean, why they matter, and what [[concepts/responses|responses]] seem most defensible given available evidence and reasoning.

Philosophy remains distinct from empirical sciences while engaging with their findings and assumptions. Philosophers may ask not just what the world contains, but what existence itself means. Philosophy's value lies partly in its willingness to question fundamental premises that other disciplines take as starting points.
