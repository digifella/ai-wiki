# Lunar Flyby

A lunar flyby is a crewed spaceflight trajectory that brings a spacecraft and its crew near the Moon without entering lunar orbit or landing on the surface. This mission profile allows astronauts to travel beyond Earth orbit, conduct scientific observations, and test spacecraft systems in the deep space environment while returning to Earth on a predetermined trajectory. Lunar flybys serve as intermediate stepping stones in human spaceflight programs, providing critical data on human endurance and vehicle performance in deep space conditions.

The Artemis II mission represents the first crewed lunar flyby in decades, utilizing the Orion spacecraft to execute this complex maneuver. The mission involves specific operational procedures designed to navigate the spacecraft through the translunar injection phase, perform the lunar swing-by, and execute the trans-Earth injection burn. These operations are essential for validating the life support, navigation, and communication systems required for future Artemis missions that will involve lunar orbit and surface operations.

By bypassing lunar orbit, the flyby trajectory reduces mission complexity and fuel requirements compared to orbital insertion, while still exposing the crew and hardware to the radiation and thermal extremes of deep space. This approach allows for the verification of critical technologies and human factors in a realistic deep space environment before committing to the more resource-intensive lunar orbit rendezvous and landing phases of subsequent Artemis missions.
