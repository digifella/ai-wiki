# Uptime

Uptime refers to the amount of time a system, service, or application remains operational and accessible without interruption. In the context of personal AI assistants like [[concepts/local-ai-assistants|Clawdbot]] ([[concepts/automated-information-pipelines|OpenClaw]]), uptime is a critical performance metric that determines how reliably the system can be used for daily tasks and workflows. High uptime ensures consistent availability, while downtime—periods when the system is unavailable—can disrupt productivity and user workflows.

## Measurement and Standards

Uptime is typically expressed as a percentage over a specific period, such as monthly or yearly. Common targets include 99% uptime (allowing ~7 hours of downtime per month) up to 99.99% uptime ("four nines"), which permits only minutes of downtime annually. For personal AI assistants [[concepts/running|running]] on local systems or cloud infrastructure, uptime depends on factors including [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]], [[concepts/software|software]] stability, network connectivity, and maintenance schedules.

## Factors Affecting Uptime

Several elements influence the uptime of an [[entities/ai-assistant|AI assistant]] platform. System maintenance, software updates, and bug fixes may require scheduled downtime. Unplanned outages can result from hardware failures, network issues, or software errors. For locally-hosted solutions like OpenClaw, uptime is heavily influenced by the host machine's stability and the user's network reliability. For cloud-based deployments, the service provider's infrastructure redundancy and failover mechanisms become primary determinants.
