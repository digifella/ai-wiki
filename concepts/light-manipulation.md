# Light Manipulation

Light manipulation in portrait photography refers to techniques for controlling and adjusting how light appears in images during both capture and post-processing. In digital post-processing, photographers use software tools to selectively brighten, darken, or redirect light across different areas of a portrait to enhance visual impact and correct or refine the original lighting conditions.

## Common Techniques

Standard light manipulation methods include dodging and burning, where specific areas are selectively lightened or darkened to draw attention or create dimension. Curves and levels adjustments allow photographers to modify the tonal range across an image or in targeted regions. Graduated filters and radial masks enable localized adjustments that mimic natural lighting effects or correct uneven illumination. These tools help balance facial highlights, reduce shadows under eyes or chin, and create separation between the subject and background.

## Practical Applications

Light manipulation serves both corrective and creative purposes. Corrective applications address problems from the original shoot, such as unflattering shadows, overexposed skin, or backlit situations where the subject appears too dark relative to the background. Creative applications use light adjustments to enhance mood, emphasize facial features, or direct viewer attention. Subtle adjustments typically produce more natural results than aggressive modifications, with experienced photographers using these techniques to enhance rather than fundamentally alter the subject's appearance.
