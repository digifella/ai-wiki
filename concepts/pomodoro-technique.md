# Pomodoro Technique

The Pomodoro Technique is a time-management method developed by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s while he was a university student. The technique structures work into fixed intervals of focused activity, traditionally 25 minutes in length, separated by short breaks. Each interval is called a "pomodoro," a name derived from the tomato-shaped kitchen timer that Cirillo used while developing the system.

The basic cycle consists of choosing a task, setting a timer for 25 minutes of uninterrupted work, and then taking a short break of approximately 5 minutes. After completing four consecutive pomodoros, the user takes a longer break, typically lasting 15 to 30 minutes. This structure is designed to maintain mental agility over long periods of work by encouraging regular rest and reducing the cognitive load associated with prolonged concentration.

The method emphasizes the importance of tracking progress and managing distractions. By breaking work into manageable chunks, users can better estimate the effort required for tasks and maintain a sense of accomplishment. The technique is widely used in personal productivity, software development, and academic settings to improve focus and reduce burnout.
