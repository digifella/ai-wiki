# Meatball Texture

**Meatball [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]]** refers to the structural [[concepts/honesty|integrity]], mouthfeel, and binding characteristics of emulsified or ground meat products, primarily driven by the extraction and gelation of Myosin. Unlike Burger patties, which rely on minimal handling to preserve fat distribution, meatballs require significant [[concepts/mechanical-agitation|mechanical agitation]] to develop a cohesive protein network.

## Key Mechanisms

- **Protein Extraction**: Mechanical force (mixing/kneading) denatures Myosin, allowing it to form a viscous gel that binds fat and water.
- **Fat Emulsion**: Proper [[concepts/texture|texture]] depends on maintaining an emulsion where fat droplets are suspended within the myosin gel matrix. Over-mixing can break this emulsion, leading to greasiness; under-mixing results in crumbly texture.
- **Thermal Set**: The gel structure solidifies upon heating, determining the final bite resistance and juiciness.
