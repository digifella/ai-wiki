# Vertical Integration

Vertical integration in semiconductor manufacturing describes a business model where a single entity controls multiple stages of the production lifecycle, ranging from initial chip design and fabrication to final assembly and deployment. This approach consolidates operations that are traditionally distributed among specialized third-party firms, such as independent design houses, foundries, and packaging providers. By internalizing these phases, manufacturers aim to reduce supply chain dependencies and enhance quality control.

In the context of advanced computing, this strategy is exemplified by the proposed 2nm AI chip vertical integration for the Tesla-SpaceX Terafab. The initiative seeks to merge hardware design, semiconductor fabrication, and system-level deployment under one organizational umbrella. This consolidation is intended to accelerate the development cycle for high-performance computing hardware while mitigating risks associated with external supply chain disruptions.

The model contrasts with the traditional fabless-foundry split, where companies design chips but rely on external partners for manufacturing. By controlling the entire process, the entity can optimize the interface between architectural design and physical fabrication, potentially improving performance efficiency and reducing time-to-market for specialized AI accelerators. This structure supports the infrastructure requirements for large-scale autonomous systems and aerospace applications.
