# Peer-Reviewed Research

Peer-reviewed research refers to scholarly work that has been evaluated by experts in the same field before publication. This process ensures validity, quality, and significance, serving as a cornerstone for [[concepts/scientific-method]] and [[concepts/evidence-based-decision-making|evidence-based practice]].

## Characteristics
- **Expert Evaluation**: Manuscripts are assessed by independent reviewers for methodology, [[concepts/open-source-philosophy|logic]], and contribution.
- **[[concepts/quality-control|Quality Control]]**: Filters out flawed studies, reducing the propagation of errors.
- **Credibility**: Assigned high Information Credibility tiers (e.g., Tier 5) due to rigorous scrutiny.
- **Standardization**: Adheres to discipline-specific ethical and procedural norms.

## Integration with Specific Methodologies
- **[[concepts/grounded-theory|Grounded Theory]]**: A qualitative methodology often subjected to [[concepts/document-review|peer review]] to ensure [[concepts/data-saturation|theoretical saturation]] and rigor.
