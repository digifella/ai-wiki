# AI Development

AI development encompasses the research, engineering, and [[concepts/deployment|deployment]] processes required to create and maintain [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems. The field combines theoretical research in machine [[concepts/learning|learning]] with practical engineering to produce AI systems capable of performing specific tasks. This includes work across multiple layers: developing foundational algorithms, [[concepts/training|training]] [[concepts/neural-networks|neural networks]] on large datasets, [[concepts/fine-tuning|fine-tuning]] models for particular [[concepts/software|applications]], and integrating these systems into production environments.

## Core Technical Components

The foundation of modern AI development rests on machine learning algorithms and [[concepts/deep-learning-models|neural network architectures]]. Transformer-based models have become the dominant [[concepts/architecture|architecture]] for [[concepts/nlp|natural language processing]] and other sequential tasks since their introduction in 2017. Training these models involves processing vast amounts of data, optimizing billions of [[concepts/parameters|parameters]], and iteratively improving performance through techniques like supervised learning and reinforcement learning from human [[concepts/feedback|feedback]].

## From Research to Deployment

Contemporary AI development requires coordination between researchers who advance algorithmic understanding and engineers who implement systems at scale. This includes infrastructure for processing large datasets, distributed computing frameworks for parallel training, and monitoring systems for deployed models. Organizations developing AI systems must also address practical concerns including computational costs, model [[concepts/interpretability|interpretability]], and real-world performance validation before moving systems from experimental settings into production use.
