# Digital Surveillance

**Digital surveillance** is the monitoring of individuals' actions, communications, and behaviors using digital technologies. In the context of [[concepts/population-health|public health]] crises, it raises significant tensions between collective safety and individual [[concepts/privacy]] rights.

## Key Developments & Case Studies

*   **Pandemic-Era Tracking**: The deployment of [[concepts/contact-tracing]] applications during the [[concepts/covid-19|COVID-19]] Pandemic exemplifies the rapid expansion of state-led digital monitoring infrastructure.
    *   See specific legal analysis in: [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Bradford---COVID-19-contact-tracing-apps|Bradford - COVID-19 contact tracing apps]]
    *   This work highlights regulatory frameworks such as [[concepts/gdpr]], [[concepts/hipaa]], and [[concepts/ccpa]] in balancing [[concepts/health|health]] data utility with [[concepts/secure|protection]].

## Regulatory & Ethical Frameworks

*   **[[concepts/internet-security|Data Protection]] Laws**: [[concepts/compliance|Compliance]] varies by jurisdiction but generally relies on principles from the [[concepts/oecd|OECD]] [[concepts/privacy-principles|Privacy Principles]].
*   **Legal Scholarship**: [[entities/tomasz-janowski|Academic]] discourse (e.g., Aboy, Liddell, Bradford) focuses on the legitimacy of surveillance measures under existing privacy statutes.
