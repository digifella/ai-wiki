# Vertical Panorama

Vertical panorama is a photography technique that combines multiple overlapping images taken in a vertical sequence to create an elongated composition. Unlike traditional panoramas that extend horizontally across a wide angle, vertical panoramas extend the frame vertically, allowing photographers to capture subjects and scenes that exceed the vertical field of view of a single exposure. This approach is particularly useful for photographing tall buildings, waterfalls, cliff faces, trees, and other subjects where vertical extent is the primary compositional challenge.

## Technique and Process

The process involves positioning the camera vertically and taking a series of overlapping photographs from bottom to top (or top to bottom), ensuring sufficient overlap between consecutive frames—typically 30-50%—for proper alignment during post-processing. A tripod with a level head helps maintain consistent framing and reduces perspective distortion. The images are then combined using panorama stitching software, which automatically aligns the overlapping areas and blends them into a single cohesive image.

## Applications and Considerations

Vertical panoramas are especially effective for architectural photography, landscape features with significant height, and interior spaces with tall ceilings. The technique can produce unusual aspect ratios that emphasize verticality and can convey scale and grandeur more effectively than standard crops. However, successful stitching requires careful exposure consistency across frames and minimal subject movement, making it less practical for dynamic scenes with changing lighting or moving elements.
