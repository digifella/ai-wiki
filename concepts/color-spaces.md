# Color Spaces

Color spaces are mathematical models that represent colors as tuples of numbers, enabling consistent color reproduction across different devices and media. They define how colors are organized and measured, serving as essential frameworks for digital imaging, printing, and display technologies. By converting color information into standardized numerical values, color spaces allow designers, photographers, and compositors to work with predictable results regardless of the specific hardware being used.

## Common Color Models

RGB (Red, Green, Blue) is the primary color space for digital displays and screens, representing colors through combinations of red, green, and blue light. CMYK (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Key/Black) is used primarily in print production, where colors are created through subtractive color mixing. LAB color space describes colors in a way that approximates human perception, with separate channels for luminance and color information. HSV (Hue, Saturation, Value) and HSL (Hue, Saturation, Lightness) organize color information in ways that often feel more intuitive to artists and designers.

## Practical Applications

Understanding color spaces is crucial for maintaining color accuracy across different stages of creative work. When moving images between applications, devices, or from screen to print, the choice of color space affects how colors are interpreted and reproduced. Color management systems use profiles to translate colors accurately between different color spaces, ensuring that the intended colors are preserved as work moves through various stages of production.
