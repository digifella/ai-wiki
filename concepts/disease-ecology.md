# Disease Ecology

**Disease ecology** is the study of disease dynamics in relation to natural ecosystems and the interactions among hosts, vectors, pathogens, and environmental contexts. It integrates Ecology, [[concepts/epidemiology]], and [[concepts/evolutionary-biology|evolutionary biology]] to understand how ecological changes—such as habitat fragmentation, [[concepts/climate-change|climate change]], or [[concepts/biodiversity|biodiversity]] loss—influence the [[concepts/emergent-behavior|emergence]], transmission, and prevalence of infectious diseases.

## Core Principles
- **Host-Pathogen Dynamics**: Focuses on the triad of host susceptibility, pathogen virulence, and environmental reservoirs.
- **Environmental Drivers**: Abiotic factors (temperature, humidity) and biotic factors (species richness, community [[concepts/writing|composition]]) modulate disease risk.
- **Spillover Events**: Examines [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] by which pathogens jump from animal reservoirs to humans Zoonosis.

## Historical Context & Societal Impact
Disease ecology provides the [[concepts/framework-for-understanding|framework for understanding]] why certain populations are more vulnerable to pandemics, particularly when ecological shifts disrupt traditional barriers between wildlife and human settlements.

- **[[concepts/near-extinction|Civilizational Collapse]]**: Historical analysis demonstrates that pandemics can decimate civilizations, often exacerbated by ecological vulnerabilities and lack of herd [[concepts/immune-system-function|immunity]]. See [[lab-notes/2026-06-15-Report-on-Civilizations-Decimated-by-Historical-Pandemic|Report on Civilizations Decimated by Historical Pandemics]].
- **Ecological Determinants**: The severity of historical pandemics often correlates with population [[concepts/density|density]], trade networks, and environmental changes that facilitated [[concepts/disease-outbreaks|pathogen spread]].

## Key Concepts
- Biodiversity Dilution Effect: Higher biodiversity can reduce disease transmission rates by introducing incompetent hosts.
- One [[concepts/health|Health]]: An interdisciplinary approach recognizing that the health of people is connected to the health of animals and the environment.
- Vector-Borne Diseases: Pathogens transmitted by biological vectors such as mosquitoes or ticks, heavily influenced by ecological niches.
