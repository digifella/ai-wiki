# Composite Imaging

Composite imaging is a photographic technique in which elements from multiple similar photographs are combined to create a single, improved final image. Rather than relying on a single [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], photographers capture several nearly identical shots and then blend them together, selecting the best details from each frame. This approach is particularly valuable in situations where perfect conditions are difficult to achieve in a single shot, such as group portraits where individuals may blink at different moments, or scenes where lighting or focus varies slightly between frames.

## Common Applications

The most frequent application of composite imaging is in [[concepts/portrait-photography|portrait photography]], especially for group shots. When photographing multiple people, the [[concepts/probability|probability]] that everyone [[entities/will|will]] have their eyes open, be smiling, and be properly positioned simultaneously decreases with group size. By taking several photographs in quick [[concepts/ecological-succession|succession]] and combining them, photographers can ensure that each person appears at their best in the final image.

Composite imaging is also used in landscape and architectural photography to improve focus across an entire scene, a technique known as [[concepts/blur-correction|focus stacking]]. In nature photography, it helps overcome challenges like unpredictable lighting, wind-moved elements, or wildlife that shifts between frames. Additionally, the technique is employed in scientific and [[concepts/medical-imaging-technology|medical imaging]] to enhance [[concepts/clarity-slider|clarity]] and detail.

## Technical Process

Modern composite imaging relies on digital tools and software that allow photographers to layer images and selectively blend or mask portions of each frame. The process requires careful alignment of the source photographs to ensure [[concepts/hidden-engineering|seamless integration]]. While the technique was pioneered in darkrooms through manual printing methods, digital technology has made the process more precise and accessible to photographers at various [[concepts/skill|skill]] levels.
