# User Centric Data Control

User Centric Data Control is a framework designed to address structural imbalances in personal data flows between individuals and organizations. It grants users meaningful oversight and decision-making authority over their data throughout its entire lifecycle, ranging from initial collection through processing, storage, and eventual deletion or retention. This approach fundamentally aims to reduce informational asymmetry, a condition where organizations possess significantly more knowledge about data usage than the individuals whose data is being processed.

The concept operates within the tools, platforms, and infrastructure domain by advocating for technical architectures that prioritize user agency. Rather than relying solely on legal compliance or passive privacy policies, it emphasizes the implementation of granular controls that allow individuals to understand and manage how their information is utilized. This involves creating interfaces and backend systems that make data permissions transparent and actionable, shifting the power dynamic from centralized corporate control to distributed user consent.

Key aspects of this framework include the standardization of data portability and the right to erasure. By ensuring that data can be easily transferred between services and permanently removed upon request, the framework seeks to prevent vendor lock-in and reduce the long-term risks associated with data hoarding. These mechanisms are intended to foster a more equitable digital ecosystem where individuals retain ownership and accountability over their personal information, thereby mitigating the risks of unauthorized surveillance and exploitation.
