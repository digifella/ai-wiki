# Moon Exploration

[[concepts/lunar-exploration|Moon exploration]] encompasses the scientific investigation and human exploration of Earth's natural satellite through robotic missions, [[concepts/crewed-missions|crewed missions]], and ground-based research [[concepts/software|programs]]. These efforts aim to understand the Moon's geological composition, history, potential resource availability, and its role in Earth's evolution. Major space agencies, including NASA and international partners, have developed systematic programs to study the lunar surface, subsurface, and environment.

## Historical Context and Early Missions

The exploration of the Moon began in earnest during the 1960s Space Race, culminating in the Apollo program's human landings between 1969 and 1972. Following this period, robotic missions from various nations continued investigation of the lunar surface, gathering data on [[concepts/geology|geology]], mineralogy, and the presence of water ice. These missions established foundational knowledge about the Moon's characteristics and potential for sustained human activity.

## Contemporary Programs

Current lunar exploration efforts focus on establishing sustainable human presence and conducting advanced scientific research. NASA's [[concepts/artemis-program|Artemis program]] represents a major initiative to return humans to the Moon, with subsequent missions designed to support extended lunar operations and [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] for deep [[concepts/space-exploration|space exploration]]. These missions integrate advanced [[concepts/spacecraft-systems|spacecraft systems]], life support technologies, and operational protocols developed through iterative [[concepts/testing|testing]] and [[concepts/simulation|simulation]] programs to ensure mission success and crew safety.
