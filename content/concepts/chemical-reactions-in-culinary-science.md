# Chemical Reactions In Culinary Science

Chemical reactions in culinary [[concepts/science|science]] refer to the transformation of food ingredients through chemical processes during [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] and cooking. These reactions alter the molecular [[concepts/structure|structure]] of ingredients, changing flavor, [[concepts/texture|texture]], color, and nutritional properties. Understanding these processes allows cooks and food scientists to predict and [[concepts/power|control]] outcomes, from the browning of bread to the setting of custards. The discipline bridges experimental chemistry and practical cooking technique.

## Key Reaction Types

Maillard reactions occur when proteins and reducing sugars are heated together, producing brown colors and complex flavors in foods like seared meat and toasted bread. Caramelization involves the heating of sugars until they break down into new compounds, creating bitter and sweet flavor [[concepts/notes|notes]]. Protein denaturation happens when heat, acid, or mechanical action breaks down protein structures, changing texture in eggs, fish, and meat. Emulsification uses chemical [[concepts/agents|agents]] or mechanical action to blend normally incompatible substances like oil and water, as in mayonnaise and hollandaise sauce.

## Practical Applications

Bakers rely on chemical reactions between acidic ingredients, [[concepts/number-systems|bases]] like [[concepts/baking-soda|baking soda]], and heat to create leavening in cakes and quick breads. Fermentation involves microbial and enzymatic reactions that develop flavor and preserve foods such as bread, yogurt, and pickled vegetables. Gelation occurs when proteins or polysaccharides form networks that trap liquid, creating the set structure of jams, aspics, and custards. Controlling variables such as temperature, pH, and ingredient ratios allows predictable manipulation of these reactions in recipe development and food production.
