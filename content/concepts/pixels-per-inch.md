# Pixels Per Inch

Pixels Per Inch (PPI) is a measurement of [[concepts/pixel-density|pixel density]] that expresses how many pixels are contained within one linear inch of a display or digital image. It is calculated by dividing the total number of pixels along one dimension of a screen by its physical size in inches along that same dimension. Higher PPI values indicate greater pixel density and typically result in sharper, more detailed visual [[concepts/output|output]] [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] normal viewing distances.

## Common Applications

PPI is widely used in specifications for digital displays, including smartphones, tablets, monitors, and printers. Manufacturers use PPI to communicate display quality to consumers, as higher densities generally improve [[concepts/sharpness|image clarity]] and reduce the visibility of individual pixels. The term is also relevant in digital photography and image scanning, where it describes the resolution at which an image has been captured or rendered.

## Practical Considerations

The perceptible benefit of increased PPI varies depending on viewing distance and individual eyesight. For smartphones and handheld devices typically held close to the face, PPI values above 300 are often considered sufficient to eliminate visible pixelation. For larger displays like desktop monitors or televisions viewed from greater distances, lower PPI values may be adequate. Device manufacturers must balance pixel density improvements against considerations such as [[concepts/power|power]] consumption, manufacturing costs, and practical rendering [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]].
