# Evidence-Based Methods

**Evidence-Based Methods** refer to frameworks for integrating the best available research evidence, clinical [[concepts/expertise|expertise]], and patient/stakeholder values into [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] processes. In high-[[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] organizations like [[entities/nasa]], these methods are critical for mitigating uncertainty and ensuring safety.

## Key Principles
- **[[concepts/hierarchy|Hierarchy]] of Evidence:** Prioritizes systematic reviews and meta-analyses over anecdotal reports.
- **Risk [[concepts/quantification|Quantification]]:** Uses statistical modeling to predict adverse outcomes.
- **Iterative Review:** Continuous updating of protocols based on new data.

## Applications in Spaceflight Health
[[concepts/space-exploration|Space missions]] present unique constraints requiring rigorous risk-management. Key focus areas include:

- **[[concepts/in-flight-medical-conditions|In-Flight Medical Conditions]]:**
	- Assessment of performance decrements due to [[concepts/health|health]] events.
	- Evaluation of adverse health outcome probabilities during long-duration missions.
	- Reference: [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Review-of-NASAs-Evidence-Reports-on-Human-Health-Risks-2|Review of NASA's Evidence Reports on Human Health Risks: 2017 Letter Report (2018)]]
- **Systematic Reviews:**
	- Utilization of [[entities/national-academies|National Academies]] reviews to validate [[concepts/health-risk|health risk]] models.
	- Integration of 2017/2018 letter reports into current safety protocols.
