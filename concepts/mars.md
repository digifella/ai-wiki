# Mars

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun in Earth's solar system, orbiting between Earth and the asteroid belt. As a terrestrial planet, it possesses a thin atmosphere composed primarily of carbon dioxide, with surface temperatures ranging from approximately −125 °C at the poles to 20 °C at the equator during summer. The planet's distinctive reddish appearance results from iron oxide present in its surface rocks and dust. Mars has a diameter roughly half that of Earth and a mass approximately one-tenth that of Earth.

## Physical Characteristics

Mars orbits the Sun at an average distance of approximately 228 million kilometers, completing one orbit every 687 Earth days. The planet has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos. Its surface features include the largest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons, and a massive canyon system, Valles Marineris. Evidence indicates that Mars once possessed liquid water on its surface, including ancient riverbeds and possible lake basins, though the planet lost most of its magnetic field billions of years ago, allowing solar wind to gradually strip away much of its atmosphere.

## Scientific Interest

Mars remains a primary focus of planetary science and astrobiology research. The planet's geology and potential subsurface environments have motivated extensive robotic exploration to assess whether conditions could have supported microbial life in the past or might support extremophile organisms in present-day subsurface habitats. Numerous spacecraft and rovers have conducted detailed surveys of the Martian surface and atmosphere. Additionally, Mars features prominently in long-term human spaceflight planning, with multiple space agencies and private organizations developing technologies for eventual crewed missions.
