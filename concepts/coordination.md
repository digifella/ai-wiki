# Coordination

**Coordination** refers to the process of organizing multiple elements or parties to work together effectively towards a common goal. In [[concepts/health-care|healthcare]], it specifically denotes the management of care activities across providers, settings, and time to achieve safe and effective care outcomes.

## Key Dimensions

*   **Information [[concepts/flow|Flow]]**: Ensuring accurate, timely, and relevant data is shared among stakeholders.
*   **Task Allocation**: Aligning responsibilities to avoid duplication or gaps in service.
*   **Decision Synchronization**: Harmonizing clinical judgments and administrative actions.

## Role of Digital Health Systems

[[concepts/electronic-health-records|Electronic Medical Records]] ([[concepts/emr]]) and Electronic [[concepts/health|Health]] Records (EHR) serve as primary infrastructure for facilitating care coordination.

*   **[[concepts/allied-health|Allied Health]] Integration**:
    *   EMRs/EHRs provide the most significant benefit to [[entities/allied-health]] professionals by centralizing [[concepts/patient-information]].
    *   Enhanced access to comprehensive records reduces safety risks and improves [[concepts/efficiency-principles|workflow efficiency]].
    *   See [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-The-Biggest-Benefit-of-EMRs-EHRs-for-Allied-Health|The Biggest Benefit of EMRs EHRs for Allied Health]] for detailed analysis on this specific utility.

*   **Interoperability**:
    *   Standardized data formats enable seamless exchange between disparate systems, reducing fragmentation in care delivery.

## Challenges

*   **Silos**: Persistent data fragmentation between specialty-specific systems.
*   **Usability**: Poor [[concepts/user-interface|interface design]] can hinder rapid [[concepts/knowledge-bases|information retrieval]] during critical coordination moments.
