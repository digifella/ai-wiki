# Quality Gates

Quality gates are structured checkpoints designed to evaluate the [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]], clarity, and completeness of information before it is accepted or communicated further. In business strategy and [[concepts/knowledge-work|knowledge work]], quality gates function as systematic barriers that prevent flawed, ambiguous, or incomplete outputs from advancing in a process. Rather than allowing information to pass through unchallenged, quality gates require explicit verification against defined criteria.

The application of quality gates typically involves conducting a [[concepts/diagnostic-audit|rigorous critique]] of existing [[concepts/responses|responses]] or deliverables. This critique examines multiple dimensions: factual correctness, logical coherence, clarity of expression, completeness relative to the scope, potential bias, and practical feasibility. By systematically identifying weaknesses in these areas, organizations and individuals can catch problems early and propose meaningful improvements before information reaches its intended audience.

Quality gates are particularly valuable in contexts where accuracy matters significantly—such as strategic decision-making, [[concepts/technical-documentation|technical documentation]], or analysis that informs major business choices. They serve as a [[concepts/quality-assurance|quality assurance]] mechanism that raises the overall standard of work by making critique a formalized step rather than an optional afterthought. The discipline of regular quality gate review can improve both immediate outputs and longer-term capability, as individuals develop stronger [[concepts/habits|habits]] of self-assessment and [[concepts/critical-thinking|critical thinking]].
