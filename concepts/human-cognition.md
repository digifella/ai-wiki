# Human Cognition

[[concepts/human-intelligence|Human cognition]] refers to the mental processes through which humans acquire, process, and apply knowledge. These processes include perception, [[concepts/attention|attention]], [[concepts/memory|memory]], [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]], [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]], and language comprehension. [[concepts/cognition|Cognition]] operates through both conscious deliberation and unconscious automatic processes, enabling humans to navigate complex environments, learn from [[concepts/experience|experience]], and adapt behavior accordingly.

## Core Cognitive Processes

Perception allows humans to interpret sensory information from their surroundings. [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|Attention mechanisms]] filter relevant stimuli from overwhelming sensory input, directing mental resources toward specific tasks or objects. Memory systems encode, store, and retrieve information across different timescales—from immediate [[concepts/short-term-memory|working memory]] lasting seconds to long-term memory spanning years. These foundational processes support higher-order functions like reasoning and problem-solving, which enable humans to analyze situations, draw conclusions, and develop solutions.

## Distinction from Machine Intelligence

Human cognition differs fundamentally from artificial or [[concepts/machine-intelligence|machine intelligence]] in several ways. Humans learn efficiently from limited examples and can transfer knowledge across disparate domains, whereas machines typically require [[entities/big-data|large datasets]] and [[concepts/neural-network-fine-tuning|task-specific training]]. Human cognition incorporates emotion, intuition, and embodied experience—physical interaction with the [[entities/earth|world]] influences how humans think and learn. Additionally, humans can generate novel [[concepts/ideas|ideas]], ask new questions, and adapt to genuinely unprecedented situations in ways that remain difficult for computational systems to replicate.

Human cognition remains one of biology's most complex phenomena, integrating neural [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] across multiple brain regions to produce the flexible, adaptive intelligence characteristic of human behavior and thought.
