# Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND)

Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND) is an alternative [[concepts/theory|theoretical framework]] proposed to explain the large-scale gravitational behavior of galaxies and [[concepts/galaxy-clusters|galaxy clusters]] without invoking [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]]. Rather than assuming the existence of invisible matter, MOND proposes that Newton's laws of gravitation require modification at extremely low accelerations—specifically, in the regime where gravitational acceleration falls below approximately 10^−10 m/s². The theory was first formulated by Mordehai Milgrom in 1983.

## Motivation and Problem Context

Astronomical observations consistently show that galaxies and galaxy clusters rotate and move in ways that cannot be accounted for by the visible matter alone—stars, gas, and dust. Standard [[concepts/physics|physics]] predicts these systems should fly apart due to insufficient gravitational force. Conventional [[concepts/astrophysics|astrophysics]] resolves this discrepancy by proposing dark matter, a hypothetical form of matter that does not interact electromagnetically but contributes substantially to [[concepts/gravitational-effects|gravitational effects]]. MOND offers an alternative: instead of adding invisible matter, it adjusts the gravitational law itself at large [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]].

## Core Principles

Under MOND, when acceleration falls below a critical threshold, gravitational effects become stronger relative to Newtonian predictions. This modification naturally produces the observed flat rotation curves of galaxies and explains the dynamics of galaxy clusters using only observable [[concepts/baryonic-matter|baryonic matter]]. MOND successfully accounts for numerous galactic phenomena without requiring dark matter, though it remains controversial and faces challenges when applied to cosmological scales and certain astrophysical systems.
