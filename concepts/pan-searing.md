# Pan-Searing

**Pan-searing** is a cooking method that uses high heat to quickly brown the surface of food, creating a flavorful crust via the Maillard reaction while attempting to retain internal moisture. Success depends heavily on managing surface moisture and heat distribution.

## Key Principles
- **Surface Dryness**: Moisture inhibits browning by lowering the pan temperature to the boiling point of water (100°C/212°F). Food must be patted dry before contact with the pan.
- **High Heat**: Requires sufficient thermal mass to instantly vaporize residual surface moisture and initiate browning.
- **Space**: Overcrowding causes steaming rather than searing.

## Advanced Techniques: Mushroom Browning
Mushrooms are particularly prone to becoming pale and watery due to high water content and porous structure. Applying specific moisture control methods can optimize results.

- **Japanese Chef's Moisture Control Method**: A refined technique focusing on managing water release to ensure deep browning and plump texture [[lab-notes/2026-09-13-Optimal-Mushroom-Browning-Japanese-Chefs-Moisture-Contro|Optimal Mushroom Browning: Japanese Chef's Moisture Control Method]].
- **Avoid Premature Oil**: Adding oil before the mushrooms have released and evaporated their initial moisture can lead to steaming and pale results.
- **Goal**: Achieve golden-brown, juicy mushrooms without the common pitfall of becoming soggy.
