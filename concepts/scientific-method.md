# Scientific Method

The scientific method is a systematic approach to investigating natural phenomena and developing reliable knowledge about the world. Rather than a rigid set of steps, it functions as a flexible framework that emphasizes observation, hypothesis formation, experimentation, and validation. Scientists across all disciplines employ this method to minimize personal bias and ensure that conclusions are supported by empirical evidence rather than speculation or assumption.

## Core Elements

The process typically begins with careful observation of the natural world, which leads to the formulation of a testable hypothesis. This hypothesis serves as a tentative explanation for the observed phenomenon, guiding the design of controlled experiments. Through rigorous experimentation, data is collected and analyzed to determine whether the evidence supports or refutes the initial hypothesis.

## Iterative Nature and Validation

Scientific inquiry is inherently iterative; results from one study often prompt new questions or refinements to existing theories. Peer review and replication by independent researchers are critical components of validation, ensuring that findings are robust and reproducible. This continuous cycle of testing and refinement allows scientific knowledge to evolve, correcting errors and expanding understanding over time.
