# Silicon

Silicon is a chemical element with atomic number 14, belonging to the carbon group of the periodic table. It is the second most abundant element in Earth's crust after oxygen, typically found in silicate minerals and silicon dioxide (quartz). As a metalloid, silicon exhibits properties intermediate between metals and non-metals, making it essential to both natural [[concepts/geology|geology]] and industrial [[concepts/software|applications]].

## Industrial and Technological Significance

Silicon's primary technological importance derives from its semiconducting properties, which form the foundation of modern electronics and computing. When doped with other elements, silicon can conduct electricity in controlled ways, enabling the creation of transistors, diodes, and integrated circuits. This capability has made silicon the material of choice for microprocessor manufacturing and continues to [[concepts/ambition|drive]] advances in computing technology.

## Other Applications

Beyond semiconductors, silicon has broad industrial utility. It is used in the production of glass, ceramics, and concrete; in steel manufacturing as an alloying [[entities/agent|agent]]; and in the pharmaceutical and cosmetic industries. Solar cells based on crystalline silicon remain among the most cost-effective methods for converting solar energy into electricity, supporting the growing renewable energy sector.
