# 2026 04 11 Ritter Island 1888 Volcanic Flank Collapse And Mega Tsunami

## The 1888 Event

On 13 March 1888, Ritter Island, a volcanic island located in the Bismarck Sea north of Papua New Guinea, experienced a catastrophic flank collapse. The collapse removed a large portion of the island's structure, with an estimated volume of rock and debris entering the ocean. This sudden displacement of material directly generated a tsunami that propagated across the surrounding waters of the western Pacific Ocean.

## Tsunami Generation and Impact

The mega tsunami produced by the flank collapse reached considerable heights in nearby areas and was recorded at various island stations throughout the region. The event demonstrated the hazard posed by unstable volcanic flanks in oceanic settings, where rapid underwater or subaerial collapses can generate waves far more efficiently than conventional submarine earthquakes. The tsunami affected populated areas in the immediate vicinity of the island and serves as a well-documented historical example of volcano-tsunami coupling.

## Geological Significance

The Ritter Island collapse remains one of the most significant recorded instances of a major volcanic flank failure in the historical period. The event contributed substantially to scientific understanding of volcanic instability, tsunami generation mechanisms, and hazard assessment in volcanically active regions. The island's post-collapse configuration continues to be of geological interest as an example of structural failure in a tropical marine volcanic setting.
