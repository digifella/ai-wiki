# Optimization Goals

Optimization goals in the context of [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]] refer to the objectives that guide [[concepts/knowledge-acquisition|model development]] and [[concepts/training|training]]. As observed by [[entities/dave-plummer|Dave Plummer]] in mid-2025, [[concepts/llm-conceptsalgorithmic-optimizationoptimization-techniques|LLM optimization]] involves balancing multiple competing priorities: achieving [[entities/high-performance|high performance]] on benchmarks, reducing [[concepts/inference|inference]] costs, improving response [[concepts/speed|speed]], and maintaining alignment with user expectations. Different model providers prioritize these goals differently based on their business [[concepts/models|models]] and intended [[concepts/scenarios|use cases]].

## Current Industry Focus

Plummer's assessment of the four leading LLM implementations reveals that optimization goals have shifted from pure capability expansion toward practical efficiency and [[concepts/user-experience-design|user experience]]. Models are increasingly optimized for latency and cost-effectiveness rather than simply maximizing [[concepts/performance-data-gathering|performance metrics]]. This reflects market pressures where [[concepts/deployment|production deployment]] requires models that balance capability with computational feasibility and operating expenses.

The tension between various optimization objectives shapes visible differences in [[concepts/model-behavior|model behavior]] and performance characteristics. Some models are optimized aggressively for speed, others for [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]] on specific tasks, and still others for reduced infrastructure requirements. These choices reflect fundamental trade-offs that cannot be fully resolved simultaneously, making the selection of optimization goals a critical strategic decision for model developers.
