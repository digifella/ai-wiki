# Purpose-Built Jet

A **Purpose-Built Jet** refers to an aircraft designed from the outset to utilize jet propulsion, rather than being a conversion of a piston-[[concepts/engine|engine]] design. This category encompasses both military interceptors and commercial airliners, marking a distinct era in [[concepts/aviation-history]] characterized by high-[[concepts/speed|speed]] flight and pressurized cabins.

## Key Characteristics
- **[[concepts/minimalist-design|Design Philosophy]]:** Optimized [[concepts/fluid-dynamics|aerodynamics]] for high subsonic or supersonic speeds; thin wings; swept-back configurations.
- **Propulsion:** Reliance on Jet Engine technology (turbojet or turbofan) for [[concepts/engine-thrust|thrust]].
- **Operational Profile:** Higher cruise speeds, greater operational ceilings, and reduced vibration compared to propeller-driven aircraft.

## Historical Context & Examples
The transition to purpose-built jets occurred rapidly in the late 1940s and 1950s. While the De Havilland Comet and Boeing 707 defined commercial [[concepts/success|success]], several ambitious projects failed to reach production, illustrating the high risks of early jet development.

### The Avro C102 Jetliner
A notable example of a cancelled purpose-built jet program is the [[entities/avro-c102-jetliner|Avro C102 Jetliner]]. Developed by [[concepts/avro-canada]], this project represented a significant but unrealized potential in Canadian aerospace history.

- **Project Status:** Cancelled before reaching production, often overshadowed by the more famous [[entities/avro-arrow|Avro Arrow]] cancellation.
- **Significance:** Represents a "tragic and largely forgotten chapter" in Canadian aerospace, highlighting the fragility of national industrial ambitions in the jet age.
