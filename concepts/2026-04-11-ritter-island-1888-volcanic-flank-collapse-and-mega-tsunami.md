# 2026 04 11 Ritter Island 1888 Volcanic Flank Collapse And Mega Tsunami

On 13 March 1888, Ritter Island, a volcanic island located in the Bismarck Sea north of Papua New Guinea, experienced a catastrophic structural failure. Approximately 7 cubic kilometers of rock and volcanic material detached from the volcano's edifice and collapsed into the ocean in a single event. This incident is recognized as one of the largest documented volcanic flank collapses in recorded history, driven by a combination of volcanic activity and gravitational instability.

The massive displacement of material generated a significant tsunami that affected coastal communities throughout the Bismarck Sea and parts of the Pacific Ocean. The wave caused widespread destruction and loss of life in nearby settlements, particularly on the coast of New Britain and the northern coast of Papua New Guinea. Historical records indicate that the tsunami waves reached heights of up to 15 meters in some areas, resulting in severe damage to infrastructure and agriculture.

Geological studies have since identified the deposits from this event, confirming the scale of the collapse and its impact on the regional landscape. The event remains a key case study in understanding the hazards associated with volcanic flank instability and the subsequent generation of megatsunamis in island arcs.
