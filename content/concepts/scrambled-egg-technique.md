# Scrambled Egg Technique

Scrambled eggs are a fundamental cooking technique that relies on three primary variables: [[concepts/salt-timing|salt timing]], liquid ratios, and equipment selection. Each of these factors significantly influences the final [[concepts/texture|texture]] and quality of the dish. Mastering these elements produces consistently tender, creamy results rather than the rubbery or watery outcomes that often result from inconsistent approaches.

## Salting and Liquid Ratios

The timing of salt addition affects how the eggs cook and their final texture. Salt dissolves into any liquid present in the eggs, which impacts protein [[concepts/structure|structure]] during heating. The ratio of added liquid—whether cream, milk, or water—to eggs determines moisture retention and creaminess. Finding the correct balance prevents the eggs from becoming either too dry or excessively wet.

## Equipment Selection

The choice of cooking vessel and utensils affects heat [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] and [[concepts/power|control]]. Different materials conduct heat [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] different rates, which changes how quickly the eggs cook and how easily they can be stirred. Low to [[entities/medium|medium]] heat is typically preferred to allow gradual, even cooking that maintains tenderness throughout the process.
