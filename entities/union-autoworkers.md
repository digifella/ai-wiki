# Union Autoworkers

Union autoworkers are members of labor unions within the automotive manufacturing industry, primarily represented by the United Auto Workers (UAW) in North America. These workers organize collectively to negotiate with manufacturers on wages, benefits, workplace safety, and job security through formal collective bargaining agreements. The unionization of autoworkers emerged in the early 20th century as a response to demanding factory conditions, long working hours, and low compensation in manufacturing plants.

## Organization and Representation

The UAW, founded in 1935, became the largest union representing autoworkers across the United States, Canada, and Mexico. Union autoworkers are employed by major automotive manufacturers and their suppliers, with representation extending to assembly plants, parts factories, and related facilities. Through elected representatives and stewards, union members participate in contract negotiations, grievance procedures, and workplace governance.

## Key Areas of Advocacy

Union autoworkers have historically advocated for standardized wage scales, pension plans, health insurance coverage, and safe working conditions. Collective bargaining agreements typically establish wage progression, overtime compensation, and dispute resolution mechanisms. Job security concerns, including protection against layoffs and plant closures, have remained central to union negotiations, particularly during periods of industry restructuring or economic downturns.
