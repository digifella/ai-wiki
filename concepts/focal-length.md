# Focal Length

Focal length is an optical measurement, expressed in millimeters, that defines the distance between a camera's lens and its image sensor when the lens is focused at infinity. This value fundamentally determines how a lens captures and frames a scene, influencing both the magnification of subjects and the perspective of the resulting image. Common focal lengths span a wide spectrum, ranging from 14mm or shorter for ultra-wide-angle lenses to 600mm or longer for telephoto lenses.

## Classification and Field of View

A 50mm lens is traditionally classified as standard because its field of view closely approximates that of the human eye, providing a natural perspective without significant distortion. Lenses with shorter focal lengths are categorized as wide-angle, offering a broader field of view that captures more of the scene but may introduce barrel distortion. Conversely, longer focal lengths are designated as telephoto, narrowing the field of view to magnify distant subjects and compress spatial relationships between foreground and background elements.

## Relevance to Panoramic Photography

In the context of panoramic photography, focal length plays a critical role in determining the final composition and stitching quality. Shorter focal lengths allow for a wider capture area, reducing the number of individual frames required to cover a scene, which can minimize alignment errors during post-processing. However, using very wide lenses may introduce geometric distortions that complicate the seamless merging of images. Photographers often select focal lengths that balance coverage with minimal distortion to ensure accurate representation of the panoramic view.
