# Numerical Analysis

Numerical analysis is a branch of [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] and computational science that develops and studies algorithms for solving [[concepts/mathematical-problems|mathematical problems]] using approximate computational methods. Rather than seeking exact analytical solutions, numerical analysis focuses on techniques that can be efficiently implemented on computers to obtain numerical approximations to problems that may be difficult or impossible to solve symbolically. This field is essential for practical [[concepts/software|applications]] across science and engineering where real-world phenomena are modeled by complex mathematical equations.

## Core Methods and Applications

The fundamental techniques of numerical analysis include methods for solving linear and nonlinear equations, interpolation and approximation, numerical differentiation and integration, and solving ordinary and partial differential equations. These algorithms must balance [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]], [[concepts/computational-efficiency|computational efficiency]], and stability—accounting for how errors propagate through calculations. Numerical analysis is widely applied in [[concepts/physics|physics]] simulations, [[concepts/fluid-dynamics|fluid dynamics]], structural engineering, [[concepts/financial-modeling|financial modeling]], and climate prediction, where it provides the computational framework for transforming theoretical mathematics into practical solutions.

## Error Analysis and Algorithm Development

A central concern in numerical analysis is understanding and controlling errors that arise from discretization, [[concepts/rounding|rounding]], and approximation. Researchers in this field develop convergence proofs, establish error bounds, and optimize algorithms to minimize computational cost while maintaining acceptable accuracy. The choice of numerical method depends on problem characteristics such as smoothness, dimensionality, and required precision, making algorithm selection and analysis crucial to successful computation.
