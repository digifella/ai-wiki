# Data Transfer Speed

Data transfer [[concepts/speed|speed]] refers to the rate at which [[concepts/digital-information|digital information]] moves through a cable or [[concepts/connection|connection]], typically measured in gigabits per second (Gbps). For [[concepts/usb-20|USB-C cables]], this specification determines how quickly files, video streams, and other data can be transmitted between devices. The actual speed achieved in practice depends on both the cable's capabilities and the devices it connects, as the transfer rate is limited by the slower component in the chain.

## USB-C Speed Standards

USB-C cables support several distinct speed standards that have evolved over time. Early implementations adhered to the USB 2.0 standard, offering speeds up to 480 Mbps, which was sufficient for charging and basic peripheral connectivity but inadequate for high-[[concepts/network-speed|bandwidth]] tasks. The introduction of USB 3.0 and subsequent revisions increased the baseline to 5 Gbps, enabling faster file transfers and improved video output capabilities.

Subsequent [[concepts/software-updates|updates]] to the USB specification have significantly expanded these limits. USB 3.1 Gen 2 doubled the speed to 10 Gbps, while USB 3.2 and USB4 technologies further increased throughput to 20 Gbps and 40 Gbps, respectively. These higher speeds are essential for supporting high-[[concepts/solution|resolution]] video streams, rapid [[concepts/external-storage|external storage]] access, and Thunderbolt compatibility, which allows for daisy-chaining multiple [[entities/high-performance|high-performance]] devices through a single [[entities/usb-c-port|USB-C port]].
