# Interlocking Teeth

Interlocking teeth are the paired metal or plastic components that form the core mechanical element of a zipper. Positioned on opposite edges of two zipper tapes, these teeth are engineered to mesh together when a slider moves along the track. The teeth alternate between left and right sides in a precise sequence, allowing them to interlock in a continuous, overlapping pattern. This interlocking creates the secure closure that makes zippers functional for fastening clothing, bags, and other items.

## Mechanical Design and Materials

The geometry of interlocking teeth varies significantly depending on the intended application and material composition. In traditional metal zippers, the teeth are typically individual links riveted to the tape, designed with specific contours to ensure smooth engagement and disengagement. Modern plastic coil zippers utilize a continuous monofilament coil that is stitched to the tape, offering flexibility and resistance to corrosion. The precise alignment of these components is critical; any deviation in tooth shape or spacing can prevent the slider from properly forcing the elements together, leading to zipper failure.

The engineering of these teeth relies on the principle of mechanical interference, where the physical dimensions of the opposing teeth are slightly larger than the gap they occupy when separated. As the slider advances, it forces the teeth into a confined space, causing them to deform slightly and lock into place. This design allows for a balance between tensile strength and ease of operation, ensuring that the closure remains secure under tension while remaining operable by hand or machine.
