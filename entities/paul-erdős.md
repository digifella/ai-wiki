# Paul Erdős

**[[entities/paul|Paul]] [[concepts/erdős|Erdős]]** (1913–1996) was a prolific Hungarian mathematician renowned for his collaborative approach, having co-authored more papers than any other mathematician in history. He made significant contributions to Combinatorics, [[concepts/number-theory]], Graph [[concepts/theory|Theory]], and [[concepts/probability|Probability Theory]].

## Overview

*   **Lifestyle:** Known for his nomadic lifestyle, traveling constantly to collaborate with colleagues, a habit that earned him the nickname "the last true gentleman of [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]]."
*   **The Erdős Number:** A measure of the "collaborative distance" between a mathematician and Erdős, based on co-authorship of mathematical papers.
*   **[[concepts/problem-solving|Problem Solving]]:** Famous for posing problems and offering monetary rewards for their solutions. He maintained an encyclopedic knowledge of [[concepts/unsolved-problems|unsolved problems]] across various mathematical fields.

## Key Contributions

*   **Erdős–Kac Theorem:** Establishes a [[concepts/bell-curve|normal distribution]] for the number of distinct [[concepts/prime-lens|prime]] factors of a random integer.
*   **Ramsey Theory:** Pioneered the field, which studies conditions under which order must appear in large structures.
*   **Probabilistic Method:** A foundational technique in combinatorics for proving the existence of a certain mathematical object by proving that a randomly chosen object has the property with nonzero probability.
*   **Erdős–Rényi Graph Model:** A mathematical model used to generate random graphs, fundamental in the study of Random Graphs.
