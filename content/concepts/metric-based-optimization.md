# Metric Based Optimization

Metric Based Optimization refers to the systematic improvement of [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] through automated, continuous [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] cycles that operate at speeds and frequencies beyond human capability. Rather than relying on manual tuning or periodic updates, this approach establishes quantifiable performance metrics and uses high-frequency [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] loops to incrementally refine agent behavior. The process typically involves [[concepts/running|running]] numerous experimental variations of an agent, measuring their performance against [[concepts/defined-metrics|defined metrics]], and automatically propagating improvements across subsequent iterations.

## The Iteration Cycle

The core mechanism involves setting a computational budget—either in time or resources—and leveraging it to execute many [[concepts/training|training]] or evaluation cycles automatically. Each cycle produces measurable results that inform the next iteration, creating a compounding improvement effect. This approach can operate continuously, including during periods when human oversight is unavailable, allowing [[concepts/agents|agents]] to accumulate improvements across many more experiments than would be practical through manual intervention.

## Practical Application

In practice, metric based optimization enables AI agents to explore [[concepts/solution|solution]] spaces more thoroughly than human-directed development allows. By establishing clear success metrics upfront, the system can autonomously test variations, measure outcomes, and implement changes without requiring human decision-making between cycles. This makes it particularly effective for optimization problems where the relationship between [[concepts/parameters|parameters]] and performance is complex or non-obvious.
