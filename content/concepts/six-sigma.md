# Six Sigma

Six Sigma is a data-driven project management and quality improvement methodology designed to reduce defects and variation in business processes. The name refers to a statistical measure: six standard deviations from the mean, representing a defect rate of 3.4 per million opportunities—an extremely high level of quality. Originally developed by Motorola in the 1980s, Six Sigma has since been adopted across manufacturing, [[concepts/health|healthcare]], finance, and service industries as a structured approach to continuous improvement.

## Core approach

The methodology relies on rigorous measurement and statistical analysis to identify process inefficiencies and eliminate their root causes. Six Sigma projects typically follow a phased framework known as DMAIC: Define the problem, Measure current performance, Analyze data, Improve the process, and [[concepts/power|Control]] future performance. Practitioners are trained and certified [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] different levels—yellow belt, green belt, and black belt—with each level reflecting increasing expertise and project responsibility.

## Application and outcomes

Organizations implementing Six Sigma aim to reduce costs, improve customer satisfaction, and enhance [[concepts/cost|operational efficiency]] by systematically lowering error rates and process variation. The methodology emphasizes customer focus, evidence-based [[concepts/decision-making|decision making]], and cross-functional collaboration. While Six Sigma has proven effective for many organizations, its resource intensity and statistical focus [[entities/make|make]] it better suited to large enterprises with complex manufacturing or transactional processes than to smaller operations or highly creative work.
