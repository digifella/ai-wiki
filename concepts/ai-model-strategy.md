# AI Model Strategy

**Definition:** The comprehensive plan governing how an organization develops, trains, fine-tunes, and deploys [[concepts/artificial-intelligence-models|artificial intelligence models]]. This encompasses decisions on architecture selection, data sourcing, [[concepts/licensing|licensing]] (open vs. closed), [[concepts/compute|compute]] resource allocation, and alignment with broader product or research goals.

## Key Strategic Axes

*   **Licensing & Distribution:** Balancing proprietary control against community [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] via [[concepts/open-source]] initiatives.
*   **[[concepts/efficient-operation|Compute Efficiency]]:** Optimizing training costs and [[concepts/inference|inference]] latency.
*   **[[concepts/specialization|Specialization]] vs. [[concepts/abstraction|Generalization]]:** Deciding between foundational [[concepts/base-models|base models]] and domain-specific fine-tunes.
*   **Ecosystem Integration:** Ensuring compatibility with existing hardware pipelines and software frameworks.

## Recent Developments & Case Studies

*   **Shift to Open-Source Foundations:** Leading hardware manufacturers are increasingly leveraging [[concepts/reasoning-models|open-source models]] to stimulate ecosystem growth and validate hardware capabilities. See [[lab-notes/2026-06-06-NVIDIAs-Nemotron-3-Ultra-Open-Source-AI-Model-Strategy|NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Ultra: Open-Source AI Model Strategy]].
