# Night Photography

Night photography encompasses the techniques and equipment used to capture [[concepts/images|images]] in low-light conditions, typically after sunset or in dimly lit environments. This creative pursuit requires understanding how cameras handle reduced light, including [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] to [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] time, sensor sensitivity (ISO), and aperture settings. Successful night photography depends on controlling these variables to produce clear, well-exposed images while managing challenges like noise and motion blur.

## Equipment and Technology

Modern smartphone cameras have significantly expanded access to night photography. Apple's [[entities/iphone-13-pro|iPhone 13 Pro]] and later devices include computational photography features designed to improve low-light capture. Adobe has released a dedicated [[concepts/camera-app|Camera App]] for Lightroom on these devices, providing photographers with advanced controls specifically optimized for night shooting [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]]. Such tools allow photographers to adjust settings in real-time and process images with professional-grade editing capabilities.

## Techniques and Applications

Night photography encompasses various subjects and approaches, from landscape and architectural photography under moonlight or city lights to long-exposure techniques that capture light trails from moving vehicles. Photographers working in low-light conditions typically rely on tripods to stabilize their cameras during longer exposures. Post-processing plays an important role in night photography, as adjusting [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], [[concepts/shadows|shadows]], and noise reduction can significantly improve images captured in challenging lighting conditions.
