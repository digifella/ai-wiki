# Social Well Being

Social well-being refers to the conditions and factors within societies that enable people to maintain and improve their [[concepts/health|health]]. It encompasses the social [[concepts/determinants-of-health|determinants of health]]—including income, education, employment, housing, food [[concepts/security|security]], and [[concepts/healthcare-access|access to healthcare]]—that shape individual and [[concepts/population-health|community health]] outcomes. Rather than focusing solely on individual behaviors or medical interventions, social well-being recognizes that population health is fundamentally influenced by the social, economic, and environmental contexts in which people live.

## Public Health Dimensions

[[concepts/public-health|Public health]] approaches to social well-being examine how social structures and institutions affect disease patterns, health equity, and community [[concepts/resilience|resilience]]. This includes understanding how factors like poverty, discrimination, social isolation, and community [[concepts/cohesion|cohesion]] [[concepts/power|influence]] health disparities across different populations. Strong social networks, civic participation, and community trust have been consistently associated with better health outcomes and lower mortality rates.

## Social Determinants of Health

The [[concepts/social-determinants-of-health|social determinants of health]] are the conditions in which people are born, grow, work, and age. These include access to quality education, stable employment, affordable housing, nutritious food, and safe neighborhoods. Research shows that these factors often have greater impact on health outcomes than individual medical care or genetic factors. Addressing social well-being therefore requires coordinated action across multiple sectors beyond healthcare, including education, [[concepts/urban-planning|urban planning]], labor policy, and economic development.
