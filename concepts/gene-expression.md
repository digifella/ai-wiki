# Gene Expression

**Gene expression** is the process by which information from a gene is used to synthesize a functional gene product, typically proteins or [[concepts/rna]] molecules. This process involves two major stages: transcription and translation.

## Core Mechanisms

*   **Transcription**: DNA sequence is copied into mRNA by RNA polymerase.
*   **Translation**: mRNA is decoded by ribosomes to produce a specific amino acid sequence.
*   **[[concepts/regulation|Regulation]]**: Expression levels are controlled by promoters, enhancers, transcription factors, and epigenetic modifications (e.g., DNA methylation, histone modification).

## Mitochondrial Gene Expression

[[concepts/cpu|Mitochondria]] possess their own distinct gene expression machinery, separate from the nuclear genome.

*   **Semi-autonomous Nature**: Mitochondria contain their own DNA (mtDNA) and ribosomes, allowing for localized protein synthesis.
*   **Central Processing Role**: Recent research challenges the view of mitochondria as mere "powerhouses," suggesting they act as sophisticated [[concepts/central-processing-units|central processing units]] or "pilots" that regulate entire cellular functions [[lab-notes/2026-10-11-Mitochondria-Beyond-Powerhouses-Running-the-Body-Quantum|Mitochondria: Beyond Powerhouses, Running the Body, Quantum Effects, and Magnetism]].
*   **[[concepts/quantum-phenomena|Quantum Effects]]**: Emerging theories propose that [[concepts/qubit-reliability|quantum coherence]] may play a role in mitochondrial efficiency and signaling, potentially influencing how energy states affect gene regulation.
*   **Magnetism**: There is growing interest in how magnetic fields might interact with [[concepts/mitochondrial-function|mitochondrial function]] and gene expression pathways.
*   **Integration**: Mitochondrial gene expression is tightly coupled with nuclear gene expression via retrograde signaling to maintain cellular homeostasis.
