# Advance Fee Scam

**Advance Fee Scam** (also known as **419 [[concepts/fraud|fraud]]**, **[[concepts/nigerian-prince-scam|Nigerian Prince scam]]**, or **prepayment fraud**) is a type of fraud in which the victim is promised a large sum of money in return for a small advance payment. The scammer typically claims to need the advance fee to [[concepts/deployment|release]] the larger sum, often citing legal fees, taxes, or bribes. Once the fee is paid, the scammer disappears or invents further obstacles requiring additional payments.

## Core Mechanics
- **Promise of High Reward**: Victims are lured by the prospect of inheriting wealth, winning a lottery, or facilitating a large financial transaction.
- **Urgency and Secrecy**: Scammers create a sense of urgency and request confidentiality to prevent victims from verifying claims.
- **Incremental Fees**: Initial requests for small fees escalate into larger demands as the scam progresses.
- **Identity Fabrication**: Use of fake identities, forged documents, and impersonation of officials or wealthy individuals.

## Historical Evolution
The structure of the advance fee scam has remained consistent despite changes in [[entities/medium|medium]] and [[concepts/storytelling|narrative]].

- **[[concepts/spanish-prisoner-swindle|Spanish Prisoner Swindle]]**: The earliest recorded [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] dates to the late 18th century. It involved letters from supposed prisoners in Spanish jails requesting funds for legal fees or bribes to [[concepts/secure|secure]] release, promising a share of their hidden wealth in return.
- **Modern Iterations**: The narrative evolved to fit geopolitical contexts and technological capabilities.
	- **Nigerian Prince**: The most famous modern variant, emerging in the late 20th century, involving fictitious Nigerian officials or royalty seeking to transfer funds out of the country.
	- **Digital Adaptation**: With the rise of the internet, scams shifted from postal mail to [[entities/email|email]], and later to social media and cryptocurrency platforms.
- **Source Analysis**: Detailed [[concepts/historical-context|historical context]] on the transition from the Spanish Prisoner to the Nigerian Prince is available in [[lab-notes/2026-07-13-Evolution-of-Advance-Fee-Scams-From-Spanish-Prisoner-to|Evolution of Advance Fee Scams: From Spanish Prisoner to Nigerian Prince]].

## Common Variants
- **Romance Scams**: Building emotional [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] online to solicit money for emergencies or travel.
- **Lottery/Prize Scams**: Notifying victims of non-existent wins requiring upfront taxes or fees.
- **Business Email Compromise (BEC)**: Impersonating executives to authorize fraudulent wire transfers.
- **Inheritance Scams**: Claiming to be lawyers or heirs managing an estate that requires upfront legal costs.

## Prevention and Mitigation
- **[[concepts/verification|Verification]]**: Independently verify the identity of the sender and the legitimacy of the claim.
- **No Upfront Payments**: Legitimate transactions do not require advance fees for release of funds.
- **Skepticism of Urgency**: Be wary of requests for immediate action or secrecy.
- **[[concepts/cryptography|Secure Communication]]**: Avoid sharing personal or financial information via unsecured channels.
