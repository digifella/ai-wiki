# Autism

Autism is a neurodevelopmental condition that affects how individuals process information, communicate, and interact socially. Rather than a disorder to be cured, autism is increasingly understood as a neurological difference that shapes how a person experiences and engages with the [[entities/earth|world]]. The condition emerges during early development and persists throughout a person's lifetime, though the way autism manifests and impacts daily functioning varies considerably between individuals.

## Characteristics and presentation

Autistic individuals commonly [[concepts/experience|experience]] differences in social communication, including challenges with interpreting social cues, nonverbal communication, and maintaining reciprocal conversations. Many also display restricted or repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or sensory experiences. These might include focused interests pursued intensely, preference for routine, or heightened sensitivity to sensory stimuli such as sound or [[concepts/light|light]]. However, autism presents differently across individuals—some autistic people are nonspeaking or have significant support needs, while others develop typical or advanced language [[concepts/skills|skills]] and live independently.

## Spectrum and support needs

Autism exists on a spectrum, a framework reflecting the wide variation in how autistic traits combine and affect individuals. Clinicians often assess support needs on a three-level scale, from minimal support to requiring very substantial assistance with daily functioning. Two autistic individuals may share the same diagnostic label while having markedly different abilities and challenges. Some autistic traits—such as [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to detail, [[concepts/thematic-analysis|pattern recognition]], or sustained focus—can represent strengths in particular contexts.

## Understanding and diagnosis

Autism typically becomes identifiable in early childhood, though diagnosis can occur at any age. It is diagnosed through behavioral assessment and observation rather than biological testing, though researchers continue investigating genetic and neurological correlates. Contemporary approaches emphasize acceptance and support rather than normalization, recognizing that autistic individuals often thrive when their neurological differences are accommodated rather than suppressed.
