# Quantum Advantage

Quantum advantage, also known as quantum supremacy, refers to the point [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] which a quantum computer can solve a specific problem faster than the best classical computers available. This threshold represents a significant milestone in [[concepts/quantum-computing|quantum computing]] development, demonstrating that quantum systems can outperform traditional silicon-based machines in practical [[concepts/software|applications]]. The concept gained prominence following [[concepts/google-search|Google]]'s 2019 announcement that their quantum processor had achieved this milestone, though the practical implications of their specific result remained debated among researchers.

## Reality Versus Expectations

The gap between theoretical quantum advantage and commercially viable quantum computing remains substantial. Early achievements in quantum advantage often involved artificial benchmark problems specifically designed to favor quantum systems, rather than real-world applications with immediate economic value. As classical computers have continued to improve and quantum error correction challenges persist, some previously celebrated quantum advantages have become less dramatic when accounting for the total time and resources required to solve problems on quantum [[concepts/hardware|hardware]].

## Current Landscape

Modern quantum computing research has shifted focus from achieving quantum advantage in abstract terms toward developing fault-tolerant quantum computers capable of solving problems in optimization, [[concepts/simulation|simulation]], and [[concepts/cryptography|cryptography]] with genuine practical benefit. Progress depends on reducing error rates, increasing qubit counts, and improving coherence times—engineering challenges that require sustained investment beyond the initial excitement of early experimental achievements. The timeline for quantum computing delivering transformative advantages in industry remains uncertain, with most experts acknowledging it remains years away from widespread commercial application.
