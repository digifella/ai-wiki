# Bar Lacing

Bar lacing is a method of fastening shoes in which the lace runs horizontally across the shoe from one eyelet to the opposite eyelet, creating a series of parallel bars across the instep. This technique differs from cross-lacing, the predominant lacing method, where the lace passes diagonally across the shoe's surface. In bar lacing, the horizontal segments run straight across at each eyelet level, with vertical segments connecting successive rows along the sides of the shoe.

## Historical Development and Distribution

Bar lacing has appeared sporadically in shoe designs throughout history, though its prevalence has been limited compared to cross-lacing. The technique has been documented in various footwear traditions and has seen renewed interest in modern athletic shoe design, particularly among [[concepts/running|running]] shoe manufacturers and enthusiasts seeking alternatives to conventional lacing patterns.

## Functional Considerations

Advocates of bar lacing suggest the method offers ergonomic advantages for runners, particularly in distributing pressure more evenly across the foot and reducing pressure points that can develop with cross-lacing. The horizontal pattern is said to provide a more uniform fit across the instep. However, comparative studies on the [[concepts/health|health]] benefits of bar lacing versus cross-lacing remain limited, and the technique's practical advantages continue to be debated within running and footwear communities.
