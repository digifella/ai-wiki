# AI Assisted Editing

AI-assisted editing refers to automated features in creative [[concepts/software|software]] that use machine [[concepts/learning|learning]] to streamline repetitive and time-consuming tasks in editing workflows. These tools analyze content and user patterns to suggest or automatically execute common operations, reducing manual labor while preserving user control over final creative decisions. The technology has become increasingly integrated into professional creative applications, particularly in image editing, video production, and [[concepts/photo-management|photo management]] software used by designers, photographers, and content creators.

## Common Applications

Typical AI-assisted editing features include automatic layer naming based on content analysis, intelligent object selection and [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]], background removal, color correction suggestions, and [[concepts/metadata|metadata]] tagging. In [[concepts/video-editing|video editing]], these systems can assist with scene detection, audio synchronization, and subtitle generation. Photo management applications use AI to organize [[concepts/images|images]] by subject, location, or quality, and to suggest batch edits across similar photos. These capabilities aim to handle routine or predictable aspects of creative work, allowing editors to focus on higher-level creative decisions.

## Design Considerations

AI-assisted editing tools are designed to operate alongside rather than replace human judgment. Most implementations include transparency about what [[concepts/automation|automation]] has been applied and allow users to review, modify, or reject suggestions before finalizing edits. The effectiveness of these tools depends on [[concepts/training-data|training data]] quality and the specificity of the editing context, meaning [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]] can vary depending on the type of content and the particular task being automated.
