# Architectural Reconstruction

**Architectural reconstruction** refers to the process of recreating, restoring, or interpreting historical or unfinished structures based on available evidence, including original plans, fragments, and modern [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] techniques. This concept bridges [[concepts/historical-preservation|historical preservation]], digital modeling, and structural engineering.

## Key Principles
- **Evidence-Based Interpretation**: Utilizing archaeological data, original sketches, and material analysis to guide restoration.
- **Technological Integration**: Employing modern tools such as BIM (Building Information Modeling) and [[concepts/3d-printing|3D printing]] to fill gaps in historical records.
- **Philosophical Approaches**: Balancing between *anastylosis* (reusing original materials) and *conjectural reconstruction* (using modern materials for missing parts).

## Case Study: Sagrada Família
The ongoing construction of the [[concepts/sagrada-família]] in Barcelona exemplifies long-term architectural reconstruction and evolution.

- **Gaudí's [[concepts/computer-vision|Vision]]**: [[entities/antoni-gaudí|Antoni Gaudí]]'s original design was heavily influenced by organic architecture and natural forms, utilizing complex geometric principles.
- **Historical Disruption**: The project faced significant delays due to the [[concepts/spanish-civil-war]], which resulted in the destruction of Gaudí's original models and plans.
- **Modern Reconstruction**: Post-war reconstruction relied on fragmented evidence, photogrammetry, and computational geometry to interpret Gaudí's intent.
- **Timeline**: The project spans over 144 years, highlighting the challenges of reconstructing visionary architecture across generations.
