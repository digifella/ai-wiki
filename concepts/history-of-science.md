# History Of Science

The history of [[concepts/science|science]] encompasses the [[concepts/scientific-advancement|development of scientific knowledge]], methodologies, and institutional practices across cultures and centuries. Science as a systematic approach to understanding the natural [[entities/earth|world]] emerged gradually, building on observations and [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] practices from [[concepts/urban-planning|ancient civilizations]]. Early contributions came from diverse traditions—including [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] and [[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]] in ancient Mesopotamia and Egypt, [[concepts/physics|natural philosophy]] in Greece, and [[concepts/mathematics|mathematical innovations]] in medieval Islamic societies. These foundational developments established key tools and [[concepts/conceptual-frameworks|conceptual frameworks]] that would later form the basis of modern [[concepts/scientific-method|scientific inquiry]].

## The Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment

The sixteenth and seventeenth centuries marked a transformative period in which empirical observation and mathematical description became central to scientific practice. Figures such as [[concepts/galileo|Galileo]], Kepler, and Newton developed new methods for testing hypotheses and articulating natural laws, particularly in physics and astronomy. This period, often called the Scientific [[concepts/revolution|Revolution]], established the experimental method as a cornerstone of [[concepts/scientific-discovery|scientific inquiry]] and challenged prevailing theological and philosophical worldviews about the natural order.

## Institutionalization and Specialization

From the eighteenth century onward, science became increasingly institutionalized through the establishment of scientific societies, universities, and laboratories. The professionalization of science created distinct disciplines—chemistry, biology, [[concepts/geology|geology]], and others—each with specialized [[concepts/terminology|terminology]], methods, and communities of practitioners. This [[concepts/specialization|specialization]] enabled more focused investigation of natural phenomena while also creating new frameworks for how knowledge is validated, shared, and built upon across generations of scientists.

## Modern Science and Continued Evolution

Contemporary science continues to evolve through technological advances, [[concepts/interdisciplinary-collaboration|interdisciplinary collaboration]], and the [[concepts/emergent-behavior|emergence]] of new fields such as molecular biology, [[concepts/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]], and computer science. The history of science remains an active area of study, examining not only what scientific discoveries were made but how cultural, economic, and institutional factors have shaped the development and application of scientific knowledge.
