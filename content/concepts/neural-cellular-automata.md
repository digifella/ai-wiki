# Neural Cellular Automata

Neural Cellular Automata (NCA) are computational systems that combine cellular automaton principles with [[concepts/neural-networks|neural networks]] to model complex adaptive behavior. In these systems, discrete grid cells update their states based on local interactions with neighboring cells, guided by learned [[concepts/neural-network|neural network]] rules rather than fixed algorithms. This approach enables the emergence of sophisticated patterns and behaviors from simple local rules, making it useful for simulating distributed systems and self-organizing phenomena.

## Application in AI Digital Ecosystems

[[entities/sakana-ai|Sakana AI]] has applied Neural Cellular Automata to create [[concepts/2d-grid-simulation|digital ecosystems]] that simulate the survival and coexistence of multiple [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] or "species." In these simulations, individual AI agents interact within a shared environment, competing for resources and adapting to local conditions through learned behaviors. The neural components allow agents to optimize their strategies in response to environmental pressures and the presence of other agents, producing emergent ecosystem dynamics that reflect principles of biological evolution and [[concepts/ecological-balance|ecological balance]] without explicit programming of those behaviors.

These digital ecosystems serve as experimental environments for understanding how [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems can coexist, compete, and cooperate [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] scale. By studying the resulting patterns of survival, extinction, and [[concepts/specialization|specialization]], researchers can gain insights into multi-[[entities/agent|agent]] [[concepts/learning|learning]], resource allocation, and the conditions under which diverse AI populations remain stable or collapse.
