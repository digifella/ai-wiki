# Starship

A starship is a spacecraft engineered for extended missions beyond Earth orbit, typically designed to support human crews or advanced payloads across interplanetary or deep-space distances. Unlike conventional spacecraft optimized for short-duration missions or orbital operations, starships incorporate systems for life support, radiation shielding, propulsion efficiency, and autonomous operation over months or years of transit. The engineering requirements scale significantly with mission duration and distance, necessitating advances in materials science, power generation, and consumable recycling.

## Current Development Context

Contemporary [[concepts/starship-development|starship development]] [[concepts/software|programs]], including NASA's Artemis initiative and related [[entities/orion|Orion spacecraft]] systems, represent incremental steps toward long-duration [[concepts/space-exploration|space exploration]] [[concepts/architecture|architecture]]. These systems prioritize crew safety, mission [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]], and modular design principles that enable staged capability advancement. Near-term applications focus on lunar missions and cislunar operations as testbeds for technologies required for more distant objectives such as Mars exploration.

## Technical Considerations

Starships must address multiple engineering challenges simultaneously: achieving sufficient delta-v for trajectory changes, maintaining structural [[concepts/integrity|integrity]] across thermal and radiation environments, managing power systems across long operational periods, and ensuring redundancy in critical life-support and navigation systems. The economic feasibility of starship operations depends on reusability, propellant efficiency, and in-space logistics support infrastructure. As space-based operations expand, starship designs increasingly accommodate integration with orbital staging facilities and resource utilization systems.
