# Wildfires

Wildfires are uncontrolled fires that spread rapidly across vegetation, forests, grasslands, and other natural landscapes. They occur naturally in many ecosystems and are driven by a combination of factors including weather conditions (heat, low humidity, wind), available fuel (dry vegetation), and ignition sources (lightning, human activity). The frequency, intensity, and geographic range of wildfires have been documented to increase in many regions, particularly during periods of drought and elevated temperatures.

The impacts of wildfires extend beyond the immediate destruction of vegetation and structures. As fires burn, they release large quantities of particulate matter, smoke, and gases into the atmosphere, which degrades air quality across affected regions and sometimes extends hundreds of miles downwind. This air pollution poses direct [[concepts/health|health]] risks to populations, particularly for individuals with respiratory conditions, children, and elderly people. Communities near active wildfires or in smoke-affected areas experience increased rates of respiratory symptoms and hospital visits during fire seasons.

Wildfires also have broader effects on earth systems. They alter local and regional [[concepts/weather-patterns|weather patterns]], affect carbon cycling through the release of stored carbon into the atmosphere, and can impact water quality through ash and runoff. Recovery of burned landscapes varies by ecosystem type, with some areas regenerating naturally while others require decades to restore.
