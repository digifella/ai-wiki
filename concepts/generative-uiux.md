# Generative UI/UX

Generative UI/UX refers to design methodologies and tools that use artificial intelligence to automate the creation of user interfaces and user experiences. Rather than manually designing individual screens or components, designers leverage AI-powered systems to generate interface elements, design tokens, and interaction patterns based on textual specifications, design principles, or conversational inputs. This approach accelerates the design process by allowing AI to synthesize layouts, components, and interactions from high-level descriptions or design systems.

## Key Applications

Tools like Google Stitch exemplify this approach by enabling designers to create conversational interfaces through AI assistance. These systems can generate design variations, suggest component compositions, and produce code-ready outputs based on natural language prompts or design constraints. The technology bridges the gap between design intent and implementation by automating repetitive design tasks and generating multiple design options for evaluation.

## Current Scope and Limitations

Generative UI/UX is still an emerging practice, with adoption primarily among teams exploring AI-assisted workflows. While the technology can accelerate certain design phases—particularly prototyping and variant generation—it typically requires human oversight to ensure usability, accessibility, and brand coherence. The approach works best when combined with established design systems and human judgment rather than as a fully autonomous design solution.
