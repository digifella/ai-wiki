# Tectonic Plate Shifts

[[concepts/tectonic-plate|Tectonic plate]] shifts refer to the large-scale movements of [[entities/earth|Earth]]'s lithospheric plates, which constantly reshape the planet's crust through processes spanning millions of years. These movements occur along plate boundaries where continental and oceanic plates interact, creating zones of deformation, volcanism, and seismic activity. The rates of plate motion vary from millimeters to centimeters per year, with faster movements typically occurring at mid-ocean ridges and slower movements at [[concepts/tectonic-collision|continental collision]] zones.

## Historical Context and Deep Time Evidence

Recent findings challenge previous timelines for the onset of plate tectonics:
*   [[lab-notes/2026-05-28-Earliest-evidence-of-plate-tectonics|Earliest evidence of plate tectonics]] suggests motion began as early as 3.48 billion years ago, based on [[concepts/magnetic-traces|magnetic traces]] in ancient crust.
*   This early activity likely influenced Earth's atmospheric [[concepts/writing|composition]], [[concepts/protective-magnetic-field|protective magnetic field]], and the flourishing of life.
