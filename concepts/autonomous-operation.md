# Autonomous Operation

**Autonomous Operation** refers to the capability of systems, particularly [[concepts/ai-agent]]s, to execute tasks, make decisions, and adapt to environments with minimal or no human intervention. This paradigm shifts from [[concepts/action-based-automation|reactive automation]] to proactive, [[concepts/goal-oriented-behavior|goal-oriented behavior]] driven by [[concepts/agentic-ai]] architectures.

## Core Components

Recent frameworks, such as those defined by IBM, identify five key terms essential to understanding the architecture of [[concepts/system-autonomy|autonomous systems]]:

- **Planning**: The ability of an agent to decompose complex goals into actionable steps.
- **[[concepts/acting|Tool Use]]**: Integration with [[concepts/third-party-apis|external APIs]], code interpreters, or databases to perform actions beyond native model capabilities.
- **Memory**: Short-term and long-term [[concepts/storing|retention]] of context, previous interactions, and learned patterns.
- **Reflection**: Self-evaluation of outputs and processes to correct errors or optimize performance.
- **[[concepts/agent-collaboration|Multi-Agent Collaboration]]**: [[concepts/coordination|Coordination]] between [[concepts/specialized-sub-agents|specialized agents]] to solve complex, multi-faceted problems.
