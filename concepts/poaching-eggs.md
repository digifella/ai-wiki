# Poaching Eggs

Poaching is a culinary technique in which eggs are gently simmered in water without their shells. The process typically continues until the egg whites are fully set while the yolk remains soft and runny. This method is valued for producing eggs with a delicate texture and is commonly served on toast, salads, or as a component of dishes such as eggs Benedict.

A specific variation of this technique involves pre-soaking eggs in vinegar before the cooking process begins. Demonstrated by cooking educator PapaMau, this method aims to influence the structural integrity of the egg white. The acidic environment helps the proteins in the white coagulate more rapidly and firmly, which can reduce the likelihood of the white dispersing into the poaching water. This results in a neater, more compact poached egg with less "feathering" around the edges compared to traditional methods.
