# Strategic Depth

**Strategic depth** refers to the geographical space and resources available to a defending force, allowing it to absorb initial attacks, disperse forces, and regroup without immediate collapse. It is a critical component of Defense in Depth and Grand Strategy, enabling a nation to trade space for time and attrition.

## Core Principles

- **Spatial Buffer**: The physical distance between the front line and critical industrial, political, or logistical centers.
- **Resource Dispersion**: The ability to spread out [[concepts/supply-chains|supply chains]] and command structures to mitigate the impact of [[concepts/accuracy|precision]] strikes.
- **Attrition Capacity**: Using vast territory to exhaust an aggressor’s momentum and logistical capabilities before decisive engagement.

## Application in Modern Conflict

In contemporary hybrid warfare, strategic depth is not merely geographical but also logistical and informational. It involves the [[concepts/resilience|resilience]] of infrastructure and the ability to maintain operational [[concepts/continuity|continuity]] under sustained pressure.
