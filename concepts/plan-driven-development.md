# Plan Driven Development

Plan-driven development, often referred to as the waterfall methodology, is a software engineering approach that emphasizes comprehensive upfront planning and the sequential execution of distinct project phases. This linear progression structures development into clearly defined stages, typically including requirements gathering, system design, implementation, testing, and deployment. A core tenet of this methodology is that each phase must be substantially completed and verified before the next one begins, ensuring that the foundation for subsequent work is stable and well-understood.

The primary objective of plan-driven development is to establish predictability and control over the software development lifecycle. By requiring detailed specifications and extensive documentation early in the process, the approach aims to minimize ambiguity and reduce the risk of scope creep. This rigorous documentation serves as a contract between stakeholders and developers, providing a clear roadmap for implementation and facilitating easier maintenance and future updates based on established records.

While effective for projects with stable and well-understood requirements, this methodology is often criticized for its rigidity in the face of changing needs. Because changes to requirements are difficult and costly to implement once the design phase has concluded, plan-driven development is generally less suitable for projects where requirements are expected to evolve frequently. Consequently, it is most commonly applied in domains where safety, regulatory compliance, or strict budgetary constraints necessitate a highly controlled and predictable development environment.
