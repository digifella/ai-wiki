# Desalination Technology

[[concepts/desalination|Desalination]] is a process that removes dissolved salts and minerals from seawater or brackish water, producing freshwater suitable for human consumption, [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]], and industrial applications. As freshwater sources become increasingly strained by [[concepts/population-growth|population growth]], climate variability, and competing demands, desalination has become an important technology for water [[concepts/security|security]] in arid and coastal regions worldwide.

## Major Desalination Methods

The two primary desalination approaches are thermal and membrane-based processes. Thermal desalination, particularly Multi-Effect Distillation (MED) and Reverse Osmosis (RO), operate through different [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] but both require significant energy inputs. Reverse osmosis, the most widely deployed method globally, forces seawater through semipermeable membranes under high pressure to separate salt from freshwater. Thermal methods, including distillation, heat seawater to produce vapor that condenses into fresh water, leaving salts behind.

## Energy and Economic Considerations

A primary constraint on desalination expansion is its high [[concepts/energy-consumption|energy consumption]]. Modern [[concepts/desalination-plants|desalination plants]] typically require substantial electrical input, making them economically viable primarily in regions with access to affordable energy or where alternative freshwater sources are unavailable or depleted. The energy intensity also creates [[concepts/operational-costs|operational costs]] that can limit deployment in developing regions, though technological improvements and renewable energy integration are gradually reducing these barriers.

## Brine Disposal and Environmental Impacts

Desalination produces concentrated brine as a byproduct, which must be managed carefully to avoid environmental harm to marine ecosystems. Discharge of high-salinity brine back into coastal waters can alter local water chemistry and affect marine life. Proper brine management strategies, including deep-sea disposal, evaporation ponds, or industrial reuse, remain important considerations for sustainable desalination operations.
