# Windsurf

Windsurf is a water sport that combines elements of surfing and sailing, requiring practitioners to balance on a specialized board while manipulating an attached sail to harness wind power for propulsion. The sport emerged in the late 1960s as an evolution of traditional surfing, incorporating sailing mechanics to enable movement across various water conditions. Modern windsurfing boards typically measure between 2.4 and 3.5 meters in length, with the sail attached to a flexible mast and boom that allows the rider to adjust angle and trim.

## History and Development

Windsurfing was developed in California during the late 1960s, building on decades of experimentation with powered surfboards. The sport gained rapid popularity through the 1970s and 1980s as equipment design improved and communities of practitioners grew. Windsurfing achieved significant mainstream recognition when it became an Olympic discipline in 1984, legitimizing it as a competitive sport while also establishing its presence in recreational water sports globally.

## Sport and Recreation

Today, windsurfing exists as both a competitive discipline and a recreational activity. Competitive formats include wave sailing, freestyle, slalom, and course racing, each emphasizing different skills and equipment specifications. The sport requires balance, upper body strength, and understanding of wind and water conditions, making it accessible to practitioners of varying ability levels while offering progression pathways for serious athletes.
