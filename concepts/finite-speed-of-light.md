# Finite Speed of Light

The **finite [[concepts/speed|speed]] of light** ($c \approx 299,792,458$ m/s) is a fundamental constant in [[concepts/physics|physics]], representing the maximum speed at which all energy, matter, and information in the universe can travel. This limit is central to Special Relativity and General Relativity, dictating the causal structure of spacetime.

## Key Implications

- **Causality**: No signal or influence can propagate faster than $c$, ensuring that cause precedes effect in all reference frames.
- **Time Dilation & Length Contraction**: As objects approach $c$, time slows down and lengths contract relative to a stationary observer.
- **Mass-Energy Equivalence**: Expressed by $E=mc^2$, indicating that mass is a form of energy and that accelerating massive objects to $c$ requires infinite energy.
- **[[concepts/lookback-time|Lookback Time]]**: Observing distant objects means seeing them as they were in the past, due to the travel time of [[concepts/light|photons]].

## Cosmological Context

The finite speed of light interacts with the expansion of the universe to create apparent paradoxes regarding distance and age.

- **[[concepts/observable-universe|Observable Universe]] Paradox**: The universe is approximately 13.8 billion years old, yet the observable universe has a radius of ~46.5 billion light-years (diameter ~93 billion light-years). This discrepancy arises because space itself has expanded during the time light traveled to us.
