## Why you can't touch Stonehenge any more
**Clip title:** Why you can't touch Stonehenge any more
**Author / channel:** Tom Scott
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0V-4lQYNugA
**Credibility:** Commentary (tier 1 of 5). YouTube video from Tom Scott: not edited or peer reviewed, so its claims are the speaker's own. Summary written by Gemini.

### Summary
The video explores the history, construction, and modern-day preservation challenges of Stonehenge, featuring Tom Scott and Stonehenge curator Win. 

Key points discussed include:
- **Historical Context:** Stonehenge's timeline dates back to around the 26th century BC, with farming starting around 4,000 BC and the earliest phase of Stonehenge beginning 1,000 years later. The site has been famous and visited since medieval times.
- **Construction and Architecture:** Stonehenge features trilithons (two upright stones and a lintel), and the video explains the mortise and tenon joints used in their construction. The stones are carefully shaped and placed, and isotopes in cattle and pig teeth suggest people traveled from far distances, like Wales or Dartmoor, to reach the site.
- **Preservation Challenges:** The video highlights the delicate balance between public access and site conservation. Modern conservation efforts, including the use of concrete to stabilize stones, are discussed, along with the impact of large visitor numbers on the site.

The takeaway is that Stonehenge is a marvel of ancient engineering and social coordination, reflecting a society capable of organizing massive numbers of people without writing or advanced technology. However, preserving such a historic site in the modern era presents ongoing challenges, balancing public access with the need to protect the monument from further damage.
