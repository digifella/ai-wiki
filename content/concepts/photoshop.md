# Photoshop

The Photoshop brush tool is one of the most fundamental features in [[entities/adobe|Adobe]]'s [[concepts/image-editing|image editing]] [[concepts/software|software]], allowing users to paint, draw, and apply effects directly onto digital canvases. Understanding how to effectively use this tool is essential for digital artists, photo editors, and creative professionals working with raster [[concepts/images|images]].

## Basic Brush Settings

The brush tool offers numerous customizable [[concepts/parameters|parameters]] that affect how paint is applied to an image. Users can adjust [[concepts/brush-size|brush size]], hardness, [[concepts/opacity|opacity]], and [[concepts/flow|flow]] to achieve different visual effects. Hardness determines the softness of the brush edges, while opacity controls transparency; flow regulates how quickly paint is applied with each stroke. These settings can be modified through the tool options panel and are essential for achieving precise [[concepts/power|control]] over brushwork.

## Advanced Brush Techniques

Beyond basic painting, the brush tool integrates with Photoshop's layer and blending systems to enable more sophisticated effects. [[concepts/layer-styles|Blend modes]] allow brushstrokes to interact with underlying layers in different ways, such as multiplying colors or dodging [[concepts/highlights|highlights]]. Users can also take advantage of brush presets, which come pre-configured for specific purposes like [[concepts/texture|texture]] application, special effects, or natural media [[concepts/simulation|simulation]]. Custom brushes can be created and saved for repeated use across projects.
