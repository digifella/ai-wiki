# Grounded Theory

**Grounded [[concepts/theory|Theory]]** is a systematic methodology in [[concepts/qualitative-research]] involving the construction of theories through the collection and analysis of data. It is grounded in the data themselves rather than beginning with hypotheses.

## Core Principles
- **Inductive [[concepts/reasoning|Reasoning]]**: Theory emerges from data, not from pre-existing frameworks.
- **Theoretical Sampling**: Data collection is driven by emerging concepts to saturate categories.
- **Constant Comparative Method**: Continuous comparison of incidents with other incidents, categories with categories, and data with emerging theory.
- **[[concepts/coding|Coding]] Process**:
  - **Open Coding**: Fracturing data into discrete parts to examine and [[concepts/feynmans-three-step-scientific-method|compare]].
  - **Axial Coding**: Reassembling data through connecting categories and subcategories.
  - **Selective Coding**: Integrating and refining the theory to produce a cohesive whole.
- **Theoretical Sensitivity**: The [[entities/tomasz-janowski|researcher]]’s ability to recognize what is important in data, to be sensitive to it, and to know what [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] exist among data.

## Application in Health Data
Recent literature emphasizes the utility of Grounded Theory in deriving theory specifically from [[concepts/health|health]] data, allowing for the identification of patterns in patient experiences, [[concepts/clinical-delivery|healthcare delivery]], and [[concepts/medical-outcomes|clinical outcomes]] that may not be captured by quantitative metrics alone.
