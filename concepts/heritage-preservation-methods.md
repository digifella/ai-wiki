# Heritage Preservation Methods

[[concepts/preservation|Heritage preservation]] encompasses the strategies and techniques used to maintain, protect, and document culturally significant spaces, objects, and practices. These methods ensure that important historical elements remain accessible to future generations while respecting their original context and [[concepts/honesty|integrity]]. Preservation approaches vary depending on the type of [[concepts/cultural-heritage|heritage]] involved, ranging from architectural conservation to the documentation of intangible cultural practices.

## Physical Conservation

Physical conservation addresses the maintenance of tangible heritage materials and structures. This includes the restoration of architectural elements, flooring systems, and decorative surfaces. For example, heritage floors such as those made from Dunlop rubber represent specific periods of industrial and design history and require specialized care to prevent deterioration while maintaining their authentic character. Similarly, mosaic tiling—whether found in ancient structures or early modern buildings—demands careful cleaning, stabilization, and repair techniques that [[concepts/respect|respect]] the original craftsmanship and materials.

## Documentation and Context

Effective preservation extends beyond physical restoration to include systematic documentation of heritage sites and objects. Recording the [[concepts/historical-context|historical context]], construction methods, and previous interventions helps conservators make informed decisions about future care. This documentary approach ensures that knowledge about how heritage items were made, used, and modified over time is preserved alongside the physical artifacts themselves.

## Ethical Considerations

Heritage preservation requires balancing conservation goals with respect for cultural ownership and significance. Methods must be reversible where possible, avoiding irreversible changes that might limit future conservation options. The approach to preserving any heritage site or object considers not only its historical [[concepts/value|importance]] but also its ongoing relationship to communities and contemporary use.
