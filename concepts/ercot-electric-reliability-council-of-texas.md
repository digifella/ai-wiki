# Ercot Electric Reliability Council Of Texas

The Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) is the independent system operator responsible for managing the flow of electric power to more than 26 million Texas customers and nearly 90 percent of the state's electric load. Established in 1971, ERCOT operates as a non-profit organization that oversees the interconnected transmission grid serving central, eastern, and northern portions of the state. It functions as the sole entity coordinating the bulk electric system, ensuring that supply meets demand in real-time while maintaining reliability.

## Operational Scope and Grid Management

ERCOT manages the physical operation of the grid, balancing electricity supply and demand every second to prevent blackouts. This involves coordinating the generation of electricity from various sources, including natural gas, coal, nuclear, wind, and solar, and directing it through high-voltage transmission lines to substations. The organization does not own the transmission lines or power plants but regulates their operation to ensure safe and reliable service. Its jurisdiction covers most of Texas, excluding areas served by municipal utilities, electric cooperatives, and a few small regions near the borders with other states.

## Market Structure and Regulatory Framework

ERCOT operates a competitive wholesale electricity market where generators sell power and retail providers purchase it. This market design aims to promote efficiency and lower costs by allowing prices to fluctuate based on real-time supply and demand conditions. The organization is regulated by the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT), which oversees retail electricity providers and ensures compliance with state regulations. While ERCOT coordinates the grid, it does not regulate retail electricity prices or customer service issues, which are handled by the PUCT and local retail providers.

## Historical Context and Reliability Challenges

Since its inception, ERCOT has evolved to address the growing energy needs of Texas, particularly during periods of rapid population and economic growth. The grid's isolation from the rest of the North American power grid allows Texas to manage its own energy policies without federal oversight, a feature that has been both a point of pride and a subject of debate. Major weather events, such as the February 2021 winter storm, have highlighted vulnerabilities in the system's resilience, leading to ongoing discussions about infrastructure hardening and regulatory reforms to enhance reliability under extreme conditions.
