# Experimental Model

An experimental model refers to an early-stage or prototype version of an AI system made available for [[concepts/testing|testing]] and evaluation purposes. In the context of [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]], experimental models serve as a means for developers and researchers to access cutting-edge capabilities before they are formally released to the general public. These models typically come with access restrictions and are provided to qualified users for evaluation, [[concepts/feedback|feedback]], and integration testing.

## Gemini Diffusion

Gemini Diffusion is an experimental model developed by [[concepts/google-search|Google]] that was made available through a controlled access program. Users selected for the program received email notifications granting them access to the model's capabilities. Such experimental releases allow Google to gather [[concepts/user-feedback|user feedback]], identify edge cases, and refine the model's performance across diverse [[concepts/scenarios|use cases]] before broader [[concepts/deployment|deployment]].

## Purpose and Use

Experimental models like Gemini Diffusion serve multiple functions within the [[concepts/ai-development|AI development]] lifecycle. They enable early adopters to build [[concepts/software|applications]] and integrate new capabilities into their workflows, provide valuable data on real-world performance and user needs, and create a feedback loop between developers and the research community. Access to experimental models is typically restricted to specific individuals or organizations based on selection criteria set by the developing [[concepts/organization|organization]].
