# Adhesive Residue

**Adhesive residue** refers to the sticky, often tacky material left behind after the removal of labels, stickers, or tapes from surfaces. It is a common challenge in recycling and upcycling projects, particularly when preparing [[concepts/containerization-technology|containers]] for reuse.

## Common Substrates
Residue behaves differently depending on the [[entities/surface|surface]] material:
- **Glass**: Non-porous; residue can often be scraped or dissolved without damaging the surface.
- **Plastic**: Porous or sensitive to solvents; requires gentler methods to avoid clouding or melting.

## Effective Removal Methods
Based on recent practical demonstrations, the following methods are effective for removing labels and residue from glass and [[concepts/plastic-containers|plastic containers]] [[lab-notes/2026-10-04-Effective-Methods-for-Removing-Labels-and-Residue-from-G|Effective Methods for Removing Labels and Residue from Glass and Plastic]]:

- **Pre-soaking**: Submerge containers in warm, soapy water to loosen adhesive bonds before [[concepts/scraping|scraping]].
- **Mechanical Removal**: Use plastic scrapers or credit cards to gently [[entities/lift|lift]] labels without scratching glass or gouging plastic.
- **Solvent Application**: Apply household solvents (e.g., rubbing alcohol, vinegar, or oil-based products) to dissolve remaining tacky residue.
- **Surface Safety**: Test solvents on a small, inconspicuous area of plastic containers first to ensure no discoloration or damage occurs.
