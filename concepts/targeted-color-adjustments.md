# Targeted Color Adjustments

[[concepts/color-range-manipulation|Targeted color adjustments]] allow editors to modify specific hues within an image while leaving other [[concepts/color-ranges|color ranges]] unaffected. Unlike global corrections that apply uniform changes to all tones, this method isolates individual color channels—such as reds, oranges, greens, and blues—for independent manipulation. This selective approach is essential for precise corrections, such as refining [[concepts/skin-tones|skin tones]] without altering background foliage or enhancing sky saturation while preserving natural landscape colors.

## Color Mixer Tool

The primary interface for these adjustments in [[entities/lightroom-classic|Lightroom Classic]] is the [[concepts/color-mixer|Color Mixer]] panel, located within the HSL/Color/Split Toning module. This tool provides three distinct sub-panels: Hue, Saturation, and Luminance. Each sub-panel allows for the adjustment of specific color ranges by dragging sliders that correspond to the eight primary colors: Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Aqua, Blue, Purple, and Magenta.

Adjusting the Hue slider shifts a selected color toward its adjacent neighbors on the color wheel without changing its intensity or brightness. The Saturation slider controls the intensity of the selected [[concepts/color-range-control|color range]], allowing editors to make colors more vivid or muted. The Luminance slider alters the brightness of the specific color, which can be used to lighten dark skies or darken vibrant greens without affecting the overall [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] of the image.

## Practical Applications

These adjustments are particularly useful for correcting [[concepts/color-casts|color casts]] and enhancing specific elements of a photograph. For instance, an editor might reduce the saturation of oranges to soften skin tones in a [[concepts/portrait-photography|portrait]] while increasing the luminance of yellows to brighten a subject's clothing. In [[concepts/landscape-photography|landscape photography]], targeting blues can deepen the sky, while adjusting greens can restore natural foliage colors that may have appeared oversaturated during capture. This [[concepts/granular-control|granular control]] ensures that [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|color grading]] remains balanced and contextually appropriate.
