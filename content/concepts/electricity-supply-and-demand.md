# Electricity Supply And Demand

Electricity supply and demand refers to the balance between the amount of electrical [[concepts/power|power]] generated and distributed through a grid versus the amount consumed by end users. Maintaining this equilibrium is essential for grid stability and reliable [[concepts/service-delivery|service delivery]]. Supply must match demand in real-time, as electricity cannot be stored efficiently [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] scale and excessive imbalances can cause widespread outages or equipment damage.

## Grid Management and Reserve Capacity

Grid operators continuously forecast demand and coordinate generation resources to maintain sufficient supply. This includes keeping reserve capacity—extra generation available but not actively [[concepts/running|running]]—to handle unexpected demand spikes or supply disruptions. The challenge intensifies during peak usage periods or [[concepts/severe-weather-events|extreme weather]], when both demand surges and certain generation sources may become unavailable.

## The 2021 Texas Power Grid Failure

The February 2021 blackout in [[entities/texas|Texas]] demonstrated the consequences of supply-demand misalignment during crisis conditions. Record cold weather increased heating demand while simultaneously reducing [[concepts/output|output]] from wind turbines and causing other generation facilities to fail. The [[concepts/ercot-electric-reliability-council-of-texas|Electric Reliability Council of Texas]] ([[entities/ercot|ERCOT]]) implemented rolling [[concepts/power-outages|blackouts]] to prevent total grid collapse. The outage exposed gaps in winterization requirements, reserve planning, and coordination mechanisms among generators, highlighting how miscalibration between supply and demand can cascade into regional infrastructure failure.
