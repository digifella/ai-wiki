# Agile Workflow

Agile workflow is an iterative approach to development and task management that prioritizes adaptability and continuous feedback over rigid, predetermined plans. Rather than committing to fixed requirements at the outset, agile workflows break work into smaller, manageable increments—often called sprints or iterations—that can be reviewed, tested, and refined based on stakeholder input and evolving project needs. This approach acknowledges that requirements frequently change during development and that learning from early outputs is essential for delivering value.

In the context of tools and platforms, agile workflows are supported by infrastructure that facilitates rapid iteration and collaboration. This includes version control systems, automated testing pipelines, and continuous integration/continuous deployment (CI/CD) mechanisms. These technical foundations allow teams to maintain a comprehensive guide to their processes, ensuring that installation, configuration, and operational procedures are consistent and reproducible across iterations.

The integration of context engineering techniques further enhances the effectiveness of agile workflows. By structuring information and prompts effectively, teams can improve the accuracy and relevance of automated tools, such as AI-assisted coding assistants. This alignment between human intent and machine execution reduces friction in the development cycle, enabling faster feedback loops and more resilient project outcomes.
