# Particle Annihilation

Particle annihilation occurs when a particle and its corresponding antiparticle meet and convert entirely into energy, typically in the form of radiation. The process is governed by Einstein's mass-energy equivalence (E=mc²), where the combined mass of both particles is converted into [[concepts/light|photons]] or other particles. Annihilation is a fundamental process in [[concepts/granular-physics|particle physics]] and has been observed in laboratory settings for decades.

## Dark Matter WIMP Annihilation

WIMPs ([[concepts/wimp-particles|Weakly Interacting Massive Particles]]) are hypothetical [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] candidates that interact primarily through [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]] and the weak nuclear force. When two WIMPs collide, they can annihilate and produce detectable [[concepts/gamma-rays|gamma rays]] and other particles. This annihilation process is considered a promising method for indirectly detecting dark matter, as it would produce distinctive signatures observable by gamma-ray telescopes.

## Observational Evidence

In April 2026, researchers reported detection of a gamma-ray signal from the [[concepts/milky-way|Milky Way]] that is consistent with [[concepts/wimp-annihilation|WIMP annihilation]]. The signal's characteristics, including its energy [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] and spatial distribution across the [[concepts/galactic-center|galactic center]] region, match predictions for dark matter annihilation events. This detection provides observational support for the existence of WIMPs as dark matter candidates and represents a significant development in dark matter research.
