# Regulatory Approval

Regulatory approval is the formal [[concepts/authorization|authorization]] process through which autonomous vehicle (AV) systems receive official clearance from government agencies and regulatory bodies to operate in specific jurisdictions. This process typically involves comprehensive [[concepts/testing|testing]], safety validation, and [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] with local transportation laws before a system can be deployed commercially or at scale. Different regions maintain distinct regulatory frameworks, meaning an AV system approved in one market may require separate approval processes in others.

## Key Components

The approval process generally requires developers to demonstrate that their systems meet established [[concepts/product-safety|safety standards]], undergo real-world testing under regulatory supervision, and provide detailed documentation of their capabilities and limitations. Regulatory bodies assess factors including technical performance, [[concepts/cybersecurity|cybersecurity]] measures, liability frameworks, and the system's behavior in edge cases. Approval may be granted in phases, beginning with limited geographic or operational domains before expanding to broader [[concepts/deployment|deployment]].

## Regional Variation

Regulatory approaches vary significantly across jurisdictions. Some regions, such as certain European markets, have established frameworks allowing supervised deployment of advanced driver assistance systems with human oversight. Other markets maintain stricter requirements or are still developing formal approval pathways. This fragmented landscape means that companies seeking multi-market deployment must navigate different technical requirements, testing protocols, and approval timelines across regions.
