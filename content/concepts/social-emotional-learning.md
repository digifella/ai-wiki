# Social Emotional Learning

Social Emotional Learning (SEL) is an educational approach that integrates the development of emotional awareness, social [[concepts/skills|skills]], and interpersonal competencies into academic instruction. Rather than treating these capacities as separate from traditional [[concepts/learning|learning]], SEL recognizes that students' ability to understand and manage their emotions, relate to others, and make responsible decisions directly influences their [[concepts/academic-performance|academic performance]] and long-term well-being.

## Core Competencies

SEL typically encompasses five key competency areas: self-awareness (recognizing one's own emotions and strengths), self-management (regulating emotions and behavior), social awareness (understanding others' perspectives and experiences), relationship skills (building and maintaining positive connections), and responsible decision-making (making ethical choices in social contexts). These competencies develop alongside content knowledge throughout a student's educational journey.

## Implementation and Context

In educational settings, SEL is delivered through explicit instruction, classroom practices, school [[concepts/policies|policies]], and integration with academic curriculum. The approach has gained particular [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] in discussions around adolescent well-being, where recognition of emotional development during teenage years has highlighted the importance of structured social-emotional support. AI-powered tools and systems are increasingly being explored to personalize SEL delivery and track progress in these domains.
