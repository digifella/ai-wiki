# Dirac's Equation

**Dirac's equation** is a relativistic wave equation formulated by [[entities/paul|Paul]] Dirac in 1928. It describes spin-½ particles (such as electrons) consistent with both special relativity and [[concepts/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]], serving as the foundation for Quantum Electrodynamics.

## Mathematical Formulation
The equation is often written in covariant form:
$$ (i\[[entities/gamma|gamma]]^\mu \partial_\mu - m)\psi = 0 $$
Where $\gamma^\mu$ are the Dirac matrices, $\partial_\mu$ represents partial derivatives with [[concepts/respect|respect]] to spacetime coordinates, and $m$ is the particle mass.

## Key Physical Implications
- **Spin Intrinsicity:** Unlike the Schrödinger Equation, spin emerges naturally from the requirement of relativistic invariance rather than being added ad hoc.
- **[[concepts/antimatter|Antimatter]] [[concepts/user-attention-prediction|Prediction]]:** The existence of negative energy solutions led to the prediction of the [[concepts/positron]] (antimatter), confirmed experimentally in 1932.
- **Fine Structure Accuracy:** Successfully explains the fine structure splitting of hydrogen spectral lines, which depends on the strength of the [[concepts/electromagnetic-interaction|electromagnetic interaction]] defined by the [[concepts/fine-structure-constant]].
