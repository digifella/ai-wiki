# Safety Culture

**Safety Culture** is the [[concepts/cultural-ethos|shared values]], attitudes, perceptions, and patterns of behavior among organizational members committed to the [[concepts/secure|protection]] and [[concepts/well-being|well-being]] of people, processes, and systems. It determines how an organization responds to risk, encourages reporting, and prioritizes human factors over operational [[concepts/speed|speed]].

## Core Components

- **[[concepts/psychological-safety|Psychological Safety]]**: The foundational element where individuals feel safe to take interpersonal risks (e.g., admitting mistakes, asking questions) without fear of negative consequences. This enables [[concepts/open-communication|open communication]] and error reporting. See [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Psychological-Safety-Amy-C.-Edmondson|Psychological Safety – Amy C. Edmondson]] for detailed framework analysis.
- **Non-punitive Reporting**: Systems that encourage [[concepts/opacity|transparency]] by removing blame for honest errors, focusing instead on systemic fixes.
- **[[concepts/leadership|Leadership]] Commitment**: Visible prioritization of safety by leadership, ensuring resources and time are allocated to safety protocols over conflicting pressures.
- **Collective Responsibility**: A shift from individual [[concepts/accountability|accountability]] to shared ownership of safety outcomes across all hierarchical levels.
