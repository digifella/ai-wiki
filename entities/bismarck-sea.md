# Bismarck Sea

The Bismarck Sea is a marginal sea located in the southwestern Pacific Ocean, situated within the region of Oceania. It lies between the island of New Britain and the Bismarck Archipelago to the north, and the mainland of Papua New Guinea to the south. The body of water connects to the Solomon Sea in the east and is bounded by major landmasses including New Ireland and New Britain.

The sea derives its name from Otto von Bismarck, the German statesman instrumental in the unification of Germany. This nomenclature reflects the area's historical status as part of German New Guinea during the late 19th century, prior to its administration by Australia following World War I.

The region is geologically active and is notably associated with Ritter Island, a volcanic island located within the sea. On 13 March 1888, Ritter Island experienced a catastrophic flank collapse, which triggered a massive tsunami. This event remains one of the most significant volcanic disasters in recorded history for the region, highlighting the area's susceptibility to severe geological hazards.
