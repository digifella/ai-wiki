# Narrative Theory

Narrative [[concepts/theory|theory]] is an academic field concerned with understanding how stories function across different media, including literature, film, games, and digital platforms. It examines the fundamental structures, conventions, and techniques through which narratives are constructed and conveyed to audiences. By analyzing the components that [[entities/make|make]] stories coherent and meaningful—such as plot, character, point of view, and temporal [[concepts/structure|structure]]—narrative theory provides frameworks for understanding how meaning emerges from the arrangement of events and information.

## Application in Games and Entertainment

In games and interactive media, narrative theory addresses distinctive challenges posed by player agency and non-linear [[concepts/storytelling|storytelling]]. Unlike traditional narratives with predetermined sequences, games often require structures that accommodate player choice while maintaining narrative coherence. Narrative theorists studying games examine how branching storylines, [[concepts/world-building|world-building]], [[concepts/dialogue-systems|dialogue systems]], and environmental storytelling work together to create engaging experiences. This has led to specialized concepts such as emergent narrative—stories that arise unpredictably from player interaction with game systems—and the relationship between narrative and gameplay mechanics.

## Cultural and Interpretive Dimensions

Narrative theory recognizes that how stories are interpreted depends significantly on cultural context, audience expectations, and individual experience. The same narrative structure may communicate different meanings across cultures or historical periods. This interpretive dimension is central to understanding how narratives function in entertainment, where creators shape audience understanding through storytelling choices while audiences actively construct meaning based on their own frameworks and backgrounds. The study of narrative thus bridges formal structural analysis with the social and cultural dimensions of how stories matter to people.
