# Portrait Editing

Portrait editing encompasses a range of post-processing techniques used to enhance photographs of people. These adjustments address both technical issues—such as lighting, exposure, and focus—and aesthetic improvements to skin texture, facial features, and overall composition. Portrait editing is a standard practice in professional photography and remains accessible to photographers of varying skill levels through software like Adobe Photoshop.

Effective portrait editing typically involves several core approaches. Skin retouching removes blemishes and evens out tone while preserving natural texture to avoid an artificial appearance. Frequency separation is a common method for this, allowing editors to treat color and detail independently. Additionally, dodging and burning are employed to sculpt facial features by selectively lightening or darkening specific areas, thereby enhancing depth and dimensionality.

Color correction and contrast adjustments further refine the image by ensuring accurate skin tones and balanced exposure. Tools such as curves, levels, and selective color adjustments help correct white balance errors and improve the overall mood of the portrait. Final touches often include sharpening the eyes and hair to draw viewer attention to key focal points, completing the enhancement process.
