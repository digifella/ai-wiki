# Musical Composition

Musical composition is the [[concepts/art|art]] and process of creating original music, involving the organization of melodies, harmonies, rhythms, and instrumental or vocal elements into structured works. Composers draw on [[concepts/music-conceptstheorytheory|music theory]], their cultural context, and personal creativity to craft pieces ranging from simple songs to complex symphonies and operas.

## J.S. Bach's Foundational Influence

[[entities/js-bach|Johann Sebastian Bach]] (1685–1750) established compositional techniques and structural principles that continue to shape music creation across genres. His systematic exploration of [[concepts/counterpoint|counterpoint]], harmonic [[concepts/exercise|movement]], and fugal form provided a technical foundation that later composers built upon. Modern musicians in classical, jazz, and contemporary genres cite Bach's mathematical approach to music and his ability to balance complexity with emotional expression as central to their own work.

Bach's [[concepts/power|influence]] extends beyond technical instruction. His [[concepts/output|output]]—including the Well-Tempered Clavier, Brandenburg Concertos, and Mass in B minor—demonstrated how systematic compositional principles could yield works of profound artistic depth. This combination of rigor and expression became a model for understanding how constraints can enhance rather than limit creative possibility, making Bach's compositional [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]] relevant to contemporary music practices across multiple traditions.
