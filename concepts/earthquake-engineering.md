# Earthquake Engineering

Earthquake [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] is an applied discipline that integrates structural engineering, geotechnical engineering, and seismology to design and construct [[concepts/infrastructure|infrastructure]] capable of withstanding seismic forces. The field addresses the complex technical challenges posed by ground motion, which varies significantly based on earthquake magnitude, distance from the epicenter, local soil conditions, and underlying bedrock [[concepts/geology|geology]]. By analyzing these variables, engineers aim to minimize structural damage and protect human life during [[concepts/earthquakes|seismic events]].

The discipline emerged as a formal field of study following major destructive earthquakes in the early 20th century, particularly the 1906 [[entities/san-francisco|San Francisco]] earthquake. This event highlighted the inadequacy of existing construction practices and spurred the development of modern seismic codes and design methodologies. Over time, the focus has expanded from merely preventing collapse to ensuring functionality and [[concepts/resilience|resilience]], incorporating advanced materials and computational modeling to predict structural behavior under dynamic loads.

Modern earthquake engineering employs a range of strategies to mitigate risk, including [[concepts/base-isolation-systems|base isolation]], which decouples the structure from ground motion, and [[concepts/energy-dissipation|energy dissipation]] devices that absorb seismic energy. These techniques are applied to various types of infrastructure, such as high-rise buildings, bridges, and dams. The field continues to evolve with advancements in seismic hazard assessment and the increasing integration of performance-based [[concepts/design|design principles]] to enhance the safety and durability of the built environment.
