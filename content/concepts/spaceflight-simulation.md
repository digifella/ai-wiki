# Spaceflight Simulation

Spaceflight simulation refers to the computational and operational recreation of [[concepts/spacecraft-systems|spacecraft systems]] and crew procedures to evaluate mission performance and readiness. In the context of NASA's [[concepts/artemis-program|Artemis program]], simulations serve as critical validation tools for [[concepts/crew-operations|crew operations]] protocols, equipment [[concepts/responses|responses]], and emergency procedures before actual [[concepts/crewed-missions|crewed missions]]. These simulations integrate data from multiple spacecraft systems and allow mission planners to [[concepts/stress|stress]]-test procedures and train crews in realistic operational [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]].

## Artemis II Applications

Artemis II simulations have focused specifically on crew operations involving the [[entities/orion|Orion spacecraft]] and related systems. These simulations model nominal mission phases as well as contingency scenarios, providing engineers and flight controllers with detailed performance data. The simulated missions generate comprehensive reports that assess system [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]], crew response times, and the effectiveness of operational procedures. This data directly informs modifications to spacecraft design and procedural documentation prior to crewed flight.

[[concepts/simulation|Simulation]] results contribute to broader updates across the Artemis [[concepts/architecture|architecture]], including integration considerations with supporting vehicles and launch systems. The [[concepts/iterative-refinement|iterative process]] of simulation, analysis, and refinement represents a standard approach to managing risk in [[concepts/human-spaceflight|human spaceflight]] programs and ensures that crew safety and mission success criteria are met before launch.
