# Thermal Regulation

Thermal [[concepts/regulation|regulation]] refers to the methods organisms and man-made systems use to maintain a stable internal body temperature or environment despite changes in external conditions. This includes physiological [[concepts/responses|responses]] (e.g., sweating, shivering) for living beings and architectural [[concepts/design|design]] features (e.g., insulation, shading) for buildings and other structures.

### Key Concepts
- **Physiological Regulation:** The biological processes organisms employ to regulate their body temperatures.
- **Environmental Design:** Architectural techniques that help maintain temperature equilibrium in buildings and spaces.
- **Seasonal Adaptation:** Strategies used by both living beings and constructed environments to adjust to different seasons.

### Examples

- **[[concepts/closed-ecosystem|Biosphere 2]]:** An artificial ecological system designed to mimic Earth's environments, including thermal regulation mechanisms for maintaining stable temperatures.
- **Green Roofs:** Utilizes vegetation on rooftops to provide natural insulation against extreme heat or cold.
- **Passive Solar Design:** [[concepts/architecture|Architecture]] that uses sunlight to capture solar energy for heating space and water, minimizing the need for artificial heating and cooling.
