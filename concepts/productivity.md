# Productivity

Productivity measures the efficiency with which work is completed, calculated as the ratio of output produced to inputs consumed. In business contexts, it reflects how effectively individuals, teams, or organizations convert their effort, capital, and materials into completed work or economic value. The concept serves as a fundamental metric for assessing operational performance and identifying improvement opportunities.

## Measurement

Productivity metrics vary significantly depending on industry, role, and organizational goals. Labor productivity—output per worker or per hour worked—remains the most common measure in business. Other approaches include capital productivity, which measures output relative to invested capital, and total factor productivity, which accounts for multiple inputs simultaneously. The choice of metric depends on what the organization seeks to understand about its operations.

## Factors and Improvement

Productivity is influenced by numerous factors including employee skills, technology availability, process design, management practices, and working conditions. Organizations typically pursue productivity improvements through investment in better tools and systems, workforce training, process optimization, and organizational restructuring. However, productivity gains must be balanced against other considerations such as quality, employee wellbeing, and sustainability, as optimizing for productivity alone can generate unintended consequences.
