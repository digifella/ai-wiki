# Local Health Service Levels

Local [[entities/health-service|health service]] levels represent the hierarchical structure through which [[concepts/health|healthcare]] is organized and delivered within communities and regions. This organizational framework ensures that [[concepts/health-care|health services]] are distributed appropriately according to population needs, geographic [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]], and available resources. The levels typically progress from basic primary care facilities serving immediate communities to larger secondary and tertiary facilities that provide specialized care at district, regional, or national [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]].

## Primary and Secondary Levels

Primary health service levels include [[concepts/community-health|community health]] centers, clinics, and general practitioner offices that serve as the first point of [[entities/contact|contact]] for patients. These facilities handle routine care, preventive services, and basic treatments. Secondary health service levels, typically district or regional hospitals, provide more specialized medical services and manage cases referred from primary facilities. They serve broader populations and maintain greater clinical [[concepts/expertise|expertise]] and diagnostic capacity.

## Tertiary and Specialized Services

Tertiary health service levels consist of large regional and national hospitals that offer advanced specialized care, complex procedures, and medical research. These facilities handle the most severe cases and conditions requiring expert intervention. The relationship between these levels forms a referral pathway that helps ensure patients receive appropriate care at the right facility while optimizing resource use across the [[concepts/health-system|health system]].

The specific configuration of local health service levels varies by country and region, depending on factors including population [[concepts/density|density]], economic resources, disease burden, and existing healthcare infrastructure. Effective [[concepts/coordination|coordination]] between these levels supports [[concepts/continuity|continuity]] of care and strengthens overall health system performance.
