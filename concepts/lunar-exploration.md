# Lunar Exploration

Lunar exploration encompasses scientific and crewed missions designed to study the Moon and establish sustained human presence in lunar orbit and on the lunar surface. Modern programs build upon decades of robotic reconnaissance, including orbital mapping missions and lander deployments, as well as the Apollo program's achievements in the 1960s and 1970s. Contemporary initiatives integrate advanced spacecraft systems, updated operational procedures, and refined technologies developed since the last crewed lunar landing in 1972.

## Current Programs

NASA's Artemis program represents the primary contemporary effort to return humans to the Moon. Artemis II, an uncrewed test flight of the Space Launch System and Orion spacecraft, validates crewed spacecraft systems and operational procedures necessary for subsequent missions carrying astronauts. These missions serve as development and testing phases for technologies and protocols intended to support longer-duration lunar activities, including surface exploration and construction of supporting infrastructure in lunar orbit.

## Scientific Objectives

Lunar exploration missions collect data on lunar geology, composition, and subsurface characteristics. Robotic missions continue to provide high-resolution orbital imagery and conduct targeted surface studies. Scientific investigations focus on understanding the Moon's geological history, identifying water ice and other resources, and assessing environmental conditions relevant to sustained human activity. These findings inform both the technical requirements and site selection for future crewed operations.
