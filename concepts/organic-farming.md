# Organic Farming

**Organic farming** is a production system that sustains the [[concepts/health|health]] of soils, ecosystems, and people. It relies on ecological processes, [[concepts/biodiversity|biodiversity]], and cycles adapted to local conditions, rather than the use of synthetic inputs. Core principles include avoiding **synthetic fertilizers**, genetically modified organisms (GMOs), and industrial pesticides, favoring biological pest control and soil conservation techniques.

## Principles & Methods
- **Soil Health**: Prioritizes compost, manure, and crop rotation to maintain organic matter and microbiome diversity.
- **Biodiversity**: Encourages polycultures and habitat [[concepts/preservation|preservation]] to support natural pest predators.
- **Input Restrictions**: Prohibits most synthetic agrochemicals; allows only approved natural substances under strict regulations (e.g., OMRI standards).

## Challenges & Criticisms
- **Yield Gaps**: Often produces lower yields per hectare compared to conventional [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]], raising concerns about food [[concepts/security|security]] scalability.
- **Policy Implementation Risks**: Sudden, top-down mandates for full organic transition without adequate infrastructure or gradual adaptation can lead to severe agricultural crises. A prominent example is the [[lab-notes/2026-06-15-Sri-Lankas-Organic-Fertilizer-Policy-Debacle-Motivations|Sri Lanka's Organic Fertilizer Policy Debacle: Motivations, Implementation, and Impact]], where a 2021 nationwide ban on chemical fertilizer imports caused significant economic instability and crop failure.
- **Economic Volatility**: Transition periods often involve high costs for new inputs and labor-intensive practices before yield stability or premium [[concepts/pricing|pricing]] is achieved.

## Case Study: Sri Lanka (2021)
- In April 2021, the government banned all chemical fertilizer imports to promote health and sustainability.
- The abrupt implementation lacked sufficient support systems, leading to plummeting yields in key export crops like tea and rubber.
- This event highlights the critical need for gradual transitions and robust safety nets when shifting from conventional to [[concepts/organic-farming]] models on a national scale.
