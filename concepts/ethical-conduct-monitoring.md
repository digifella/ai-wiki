# Ethical Conduct Monitoring

Ethical Conduct Monitoring is the systematic oversight of artificial intelligence systems to ensure they operate reliably and in accordance with established ethical standards. This practice involves deploying specialized AI agents or monitoring frameworks that observe, evaluate, and report on the behavior of primary AI models during operation. The primary objective is to detect deviations from intended behavior, identify potential harms, and maintain accountability within complex computational environments.

The implementation of these monitoring systems often relies on secondary AI agents, such as Anthropic Observer Agents, which function independently of the primary model being monitored. These observer agents analyze input-output pairs, internal states, and interaction logs to assess compliance with predefined safety guidelines and ethical constraints. By providing real-time feedback or triggering intervention protocols, they aim to mitigate risks associated with hallucination, bias, or malicious usage before they escalate.

This domain intersects with broader efforts in AI alignment and safety engineering, focusing on the technical mechanisms required to verify that autonomous systems adhere to human values. While specific methodologies vary across organizations, the core function remains consistent: to create a verifiable layer of oversight that enhances the trustworthiness and predictability of AI deployments in critical applications.
