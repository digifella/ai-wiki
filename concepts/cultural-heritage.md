# Cultural Heritage

Cultural heritage encompasses the physical artifacts, practices, knowledge systems, and traditions that communities inherit from their ancestors and pass forward to future generations. It includes archaeological remains, historical structures, artistic works, oral traditions, and other tangible and intangible cultural expressions that shape collective identity and understanding of human history. The [[concepts/preservation|preservation]] and study of cultural heritage serves both scholarly and social purposes, contributing to historical knowledge while reinforcing community bonds and cultural [[concepts/continuity|continuity]].

## Tangible and Intangible Forms

Tangible cultural heritage consists of physical objects and structures—archaeological finds, buildings, monuments, and artworks—that can be directly examined and conserved. Intangible cultural heritage includes practices, oral traditions, music, crafts, and knowledge systems that exist through transmission and practice rather than physical form. Both categories are essential to understanding how societies have developed and how they maintain their identities across generations.

## Documentation and Public Participation

The systematic documentation and excavation of cultural heritage traditionally fell to professional archaeologists and historians. In recent years, some countries have expanded participation in [[concepts/artifact-discovery|artifact discovery]]. For example, the [[entities/danish-government|Danish government]] has engaged private [[concepts/metal|metal]] detectorists in systematic surveys of farm fields to locate and recover historical artifacts. This approach leverages [[concepts/community-interest|community interest]] in archaeology while generating data about material culture across broader geographic areas, though it operates within regulated frameworks to ensure proper documentation and preservation of finds.

## Contemporary Significance

Cultural heritage remains relevant to modern societies as a source of historical understanding, educational value, and cultural identity. The management of heritage sites, [[concepts/secure|protection]] of artifacts, and support for traditional practices reflect broader commitments to preserving human diversity and historical continuity in an era of rapid social change.
