# Human Affordance

Human [[concepts/affordances|Affordance]] addresses the mismatch between the operational [[concepts/speed|speed]] of [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] and the speed [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] which humans can meaningfully interact with them. While AI systems can process and execute tasks at rates orders of magnitude faster than [[concepts/human-cognition|human cognition]] and reaction time, this speed advantage often fails to translate into proportional improvements for human users. The concept suggests that the bottleneck lies not in AI capability but in the infrastructure mediating between humans and [[concepts/automations|automated systems]].

## Web Infrastructure and Human-Centered Design

Current [[concepts/web-infrastructure|web infrastructure]] was largely designed for human-paced interaction—load times measured in seconds, interfaces requiring deliberate clicks and navigation, and workflows built around sequential human [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]]. When [[concepts/ai-agents|AI agents]] operate within this same infrastructure, they become constrained to human timescales. Rebuilding this infrastructure with human affordance in mind means creating systems where the speed advantages of AI can be meaningfully captured and translated into better human outcomes, rather than simply making systems faster in ways humans cannot perceive or act upon.

The challenge is not simply acceleration but alignment—ensuring that as [[concepts/automation|automation]] speeds increase, the tools, interfaces, and decision frameworks humans rely on evolve in tandem. This requires rethinking how information is presented, how humans maintain meaningful [[concepts/power|control]], and how the benefits of rapid AI processing can be channeled into domains where human judgment and approval remain essential.
