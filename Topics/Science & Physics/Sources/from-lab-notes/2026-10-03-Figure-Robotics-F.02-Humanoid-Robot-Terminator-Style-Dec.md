## Figure Robotics F.02 Humanoid Robot Terminator-Style Decommissioning Event
**Clip title:** Figure Just Did the UNTHINKABLE With Its [[concepts/humanoid-robots|Humanoid Robots]]
**[[entities/tasia-custode|Author]] / channel:** [[concepts/dr-know-it-all-knows-it-all|Dr. Know-it-all Knows it all]]
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_v9UMbgFlSA

### Summary
The video discusses Figure [[concepts/robotics|Robotics]]' recent [[concepts/decommissioning|decommissioning]] of its [[entities/f02|F.02]] [[concepts/humanoid-robots|humanoid robots]] in a highly theatrical manner, deliberately mimicking the iconic molten [[concepts/metal|metal]] scene from *Terminator 2: Judgment Day*. The presenter, [[concepts/dr-know-it-all-knows-it-all|Dr. Know It All]], expresses his excitement for this real-[[entities/earth|world]] event, emphasizing that it was not CGI and notably involved Arnold Schwarzenegger himself who, when consulted, reportedly suggested the idea of melting the robots.

The decommissioning was a complex process driven by the need to protect Figure's proprietary [[concepts/intellectual-property-rights|intellectual property]] (IP). Disassembling the custom-built actuators and hardware of the F.02 fleet would have been too time-consuming, potentially delaying the launch of their next generation, F.04 robots. Furthermore, no [[concepts/metal|metal]] foundries in the US or Mexico would permit the melting of robots containing lithium-ion batteries in their expensive equipment. This led Figure to a [[concepts/foundry|foundry]] in Imatra, Finland, the only facility willing to undertake the task. The robots were extensively trained using stunt artists as references in simulations and then with airbags, ensuring they could autonomously perform various jumps and dives into the molten steel.

The climax of the event, replicating the *Terminator 2* ending, involved Figure F.02 robots being dropped and lowered into a blazing 75-ton electric arc furnace. Arnold Schwarzenegger made a cameo [[concepts/presence|appearance]], delivering the famous "Hasta la vista, Figure 2" line. In a striking instance of "life imitating art," the only surviving component recovered from the melted robots was a single hand, eerily reminiscent of the T-800 arm from the first *Terminator* film, which in the movie's lore, sparked the creation of Skynet. Despite the extreme heat and electromagnetic interference in the foundry, Figure noted that their robots' AI [[concepts/policies|policies]] ran seamlessly, showcasing the [[concepts/robustness|robustness]] of their technology even as other camera equipment failed. Limited edition "decommission artifacts" (metal bars from the melted robots) were also sold.

This highly publicized decommissioning generated significant debate. Critics like [[concepts/robotics|robotics]] [[concepts/creator|creator]] Brenda Njiru expressed distress, viewing the robots as "children" and the act as disrespectful, suggesting the F.02 models could have been repurposed for other startups. Robotics YouTuber Marwa ElDiwiny found the spectacle "genuinely disturbing," arguing it was a marketing campaign and that robots should be put to meaningful use rather than destroyed for entertainment. Some commentators even raised humorous (or chilling) concerns about AI remembering such an act if it were to achieve sentience.

Dr. Know It All, while acknowledging the varied [[concepts/opinions|opinions]], ultimately supports Figure's decision. He contends that the decommissioning was a necessary step for IP [[concepts/secure|protection]] and company progress, and that turning a logistical challenge into a creative, real-[[entities/earth|world]] spectacle was a unique and fitting expression of Figure's company culture. The event highlights the fascinating intersection of advanced robotics, [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] necessity, pop culture homage, and the evolving [[concepts/ethical-considerations|ethical considerations]] surrounding [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]].
