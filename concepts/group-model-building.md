# Group Model Building

Group Model Building (GMB) is a [[concepts/participatory-research|participatory research]] methodology that convenes stakeholders in structured group settings to develop shared understandings of complex social problems. Emerging from system dynamics research in the 1980s, the approach has been adapted across multiple fields including [[concepts/population-health|public health]], environmental management, and social policy. GMB operates on the principle that collaborative [[concepts/communication|dialogue]] among individuals with diverse perspectives and [[concepts/expertise|expertise]] can generate more comprehensive and nuanced problem representations than expert analysis alone.

In the context of history and anthropology, GMB serves as a specialized research space for [[concepts/2026-04-14-how-to-get-tack-sharp-photos-with-any-camera|focus groups]] within [[concepts/clearing-houses|clearing houses]] and contexts involving [[concepts/children-and-family-violence|children and family violence]]. This application allows researchers to map causal [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] and systemic factors influencing family dynamics through the direct input of community members and professionals. By facilitating collective sense-making, the method helps identify leverage points for intervention and policy development in sensitive social environments.

The process typically involves iterative cycles of group discussion, diagramming, and [[concepts/simulation-technology|computer simulation]] to test hypotheses about system behavior. Participants collaboratively construct causal [[concepts/loop|loop]] [[concepts/diagrams|diagrams]] that reflect their mental models of the issues at hand. These visual representations are then used to build stock and [[concepts/flow|flow]] models, enabling stakeholders to explore the long-term consequences of different policy choices or social interventions.
