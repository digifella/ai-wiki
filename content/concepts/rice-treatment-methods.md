# Rice Treatment Methods

Rice treatment is a fundamental step in preparing fried rice, as the [[concepts/texture|texture]] and [[concepts/moisture-content|moisture content]] of the rice directly affect the final dish. The primary goal of rice treatment is to achieve separate, non-clumpy grains that can absorb flavors and ingredients evenly. Raw rice contains surface starches that cause grains to stick together, so proper [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] begins with rinsing the rice to remove excess starch before cooking.

## Cooking and Cooling

The most critical aspect of rice treatment for fried rice is using properly cooked and cooled rice. Rice should be cooked to a fluffy, separate-grain [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] rather than soft or mushy. After cooking, the rice must be spread out and allowed to cool completely—ideally overnight in the refrigerator. This cooling period allows moisture to evaporate and starches to set, resulting in firmer grains that won't clump when stir-fried [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] high heat.

## Preparation Before Frying

Before adding rice to the hot wok or pan, breaking up any remaining clumps by hand or with a fork ensures even cooking and consistent texture throughout the finished dish. This treatment step is essential because cold, properly separated grains [[entities/will|will]] heat through quickly and develop [[concepts/light|light]] browning without becoming mushy. The drier texture also allows the rice to better absorb the flavors of oil, aromatics, and sauces added during the frying process.
