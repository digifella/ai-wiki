# Meat Tenderization

Meat tenderization refers to techniques that improve the [[concepts/texture|texture]], moisture retention, and cooking properties of meat. One effective and simple method involves using [[concepts/baking-soda|baking soda]], a common kitchen ingredient that chemically alters the surface of meat to enhance its [[concepts/structure|structure]] and cooking behavior.

## Baking Soda Method

When baking soda is applied to [[concepts/ground-meat|ground meat]], it raises the pH level of the meat's surface, breaking down proteins and allowing them to bond more effectively during cooking. This process improves the final texture by creating a more tender result while also increasing the meat's capacity to retain moisture, leading to juicier finished dishes. Additionally, the altered protein structure promotes better browning during cooking, contributing to improved flavor development through the [[concepts/maillard-reaction|Maillard reaction]].

## Application and Results

The baking soda approach is straightforward and works particularly well with ground meat preparations. The technique has been documented as effective across various cuisines and [[concepts/cooking-methods|cooking methods]], from grilled chicken to Korean-style dishes, making it a practical option for home cooks seeking to improve meat quality without specialized equipment or extended [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] times.
