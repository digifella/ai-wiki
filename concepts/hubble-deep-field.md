# Hubble Deep Field

The **Hubble Deep Field (HDF)** is a famous image captured by the Hubble Space Telescope in December 1995. It represents one of the deepest views into the universe, revealing thousands of galaxies in a tiny patch of sky with no known bright objects. The field was chosen near the north pole of the ecliptic to avoid obscuration by the [[concepts/milky-way|Milky Way]] or zodiacal [[concepts/light|light]].

## Significance & Impact
- **Galactic Census**: Provided a statistical sample of [[concepts/distant-galaxies|distant galaxies]], allowing astronomers to estimate the number of galaxies in the [[concepts/observable-universe|observable universe]] and their evolution over [[concepts/cosmic-time|cosmic time]].
- **High-Redshift Galaxies**: Revealed numerous galaxies at high redshifts ($z > 3$), offering insights into [[concepts/early-galaxy-formation|early galaxy formation]] and structure.
- **[[concepts/big-bang-formation-timescale|Cosmic Chronology]]**: Established a baseline for understanding how star formation rates evolved from the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]] to the present day.

## Integration with Star Formation History
The HDF data serves as a critical observational anchor for reconstructing the universe's stellar history, particularly in relation to:

- [[lab-notes/2026-06-12-The-Universes-Star-Formation-History-The-Madau-Plot-and|The Universe's Star Formation History: The Madau Plot and Cosmic Noon]]
	- **[[concepts/madau-plot|Madau Plot]]**: The HDF contributes data points to the Madau plot, which charts the cosmic star formation rate [[concepts/density|density]] (SFRD) over redshift.
	- **[[concepts/cosmic-noon|Cosmic Noon]]**: Observations from the HDF helped identify "Cosmic Noon" ($z \approx 2$), the period of peak star formation activity in the universe's history.
	- **Elemental Synthesis**: The data supports the [[concepts/storytelling|narrative]] that the elements constituting biological life were forged during these intense [[concepts/epochs|epochs]] of [[concepts/galaxy-formation-and-evolution|galaxy evolution]] observed by Hubble.

## Technical Details
- **[[concepts/exposure|Exposure]] Time**: ~10 hours (150 orbits)
- **Target Area**: 2.6 arcminutes square (~$1/30,000,000$ of the sky)
- **Filters**: Six different visible-to-infrared filters to capture color and redshift information.
