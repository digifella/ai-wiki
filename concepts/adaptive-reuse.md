# Adaptive Reuse

Adaptive reuse is the practice of converting existing buildings or structures to serve new purposes while retaining their original architectural and historical character. Rather than demolishing outdated or underutilized structures, this approach preserves built cultural heritage and directs resources toward maintaining culturally significant spaces. It allows cities to retain their architectural identity while meeting contemporary functional needs, serving as a key strategy in urban conservation.

The concept emphasizes resilience by extending the lifecycle of existing infrastructure, thereby reducing the environmental impact associated with new construction and demolition. This method supports sustainable development by repurposing materials and energy already embodied in the structure, aligning economic viability with environmental stewardship.

A prominent example of this practice is the preservation and resilience of the historic Banking Chamber at 333 Collins Street in Melbourne. The building demonstrates how significant historic interiors can be maintained and integrated into modern commercial use, ensuring that the cultural narrative of the site remains intact despite changes in occupancy and function.
