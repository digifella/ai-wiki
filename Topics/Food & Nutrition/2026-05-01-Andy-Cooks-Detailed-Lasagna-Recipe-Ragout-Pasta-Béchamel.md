## Andy Cooks' Detailed Lasagna Recipe: Ragout, Pasta, Béchamel
**Clip title:** Lasagna Better Than Your Mums
**Author / channel:** Andy Cooks
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-__qVqib9Pw

### Summary
The video provides a comprehensive, step-by-step guide to creating a lasagna that promises to be superior to restaurant versions, emphasizing meticulous [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] and quality ingredients. Chef Andy details the process, beginning with the foundational ragout (meat sauce). This starts with a finely diced *[soffritto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirepoix)* of carrots, onions, and celery, with a careful note on garlic usage inspired by Northern Italian traditions. A crucial step involves incorporating Parmesan rind into the ragout, which naturally enhances [umami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umami) flavors.

The ragout continues with browning a mixture of beef and high-fat pork mince (an 80/20 [[concepts/lean|lean]] to fat ratio is recommended). This browning is vital for achieving a deep [[concepts/maillard-reaction|Maillard reaction]], which contributes significantly to the meat's rich flavor profile. Excess fat is then removed before the previously diced vegetables are slowly sweated to develop their natural sugars, balancing the sauce without needing additional [[concepts/sugar|sugar]]. Red wine is reduced, followed by tomato passata, and the entire ragout is simmered for an extended period, ideally 2 to 6 hours, allowing the flavors to fully meld and intensify.

Next, Andy demonstrates making fresh *pasta verde* by incorporating blanched and blended spinach into the egg and double-[[concepts/concept-of-nothingness|zero]] flour dough, emphasizing proper kneading and resting the dough. The béchamel sauce is then prepared, focusing on a smooth [roux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roux) base, gradually adding milk to prevent lumps, and seasoning with salt and fresh nutmeg for a balanced flavor and thin-enough [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] for easy spreading.

The lasagna is then assembled in layers, starting with meat sauce at the bottom, followed by pasta sheets, béchamel, mozzarella, and Parmesan. This layering is repeated, culminating in a top layer of plain tomato passata, fresh basil, and cheeses to create an appealing, non-greasy crust. The baking process is two-fold: initially covered in foil at 180°C (355°F) for 35-40 minutes, then uncovered with additional cheese and basil for a final 10-12 minutes under a grill to achieve a bubbly, golden-brown finish. Crucially, the finished lasagna must rest for at least 10 minutes before serving to allow it to set properly.

The video concludes by demonstrating the dish's versatility as a make-ahead meal. Andy compares the freshly made lasagna to portions refrigerated and frozen for two days. He finds that while the ragout's flavor deepens and improves significantly with resting and reheating, the pasta's [[concepts/texture|texture]] does experience a slight decline, especially when frozen. However, he concludes that the overall convenience and enhanced flavor of the ragout make preparing multiple lasagnas in advance a worthwhile endeavor, providing a delicious and practical [[concepts/solution|solution]] for future meals.
