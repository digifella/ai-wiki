# Modified Conceptsmit Licensemit License

The MIT License is a permissive [[concepts/open-source|open-source]] [[concepts/software|software]] [[concepts/license|license]] that grants users broad freedoms to use, modify, and distribute software with minimal legal restrictions. Originally created by the [[concepts/massachusetts-institute-of-technology-entitiesmitmit|Massachusetts Institute of Technology]], the license has become one of the most widely adopted permissive licenses in open-source [[concepts/coding|software development]] due to its simplicity and flexibility.

## Core Permissions and Terms

The MIT License permits users to freely use, copy, modify, and distribute the licensed software, including for commercial purposes. The primary requirements are that users must include a copy of the original license and copyright notice in any [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] of the software or substantial portions of it. The license imposes no restrictions on how the software may be used or modified, making it particularly attractive for projects seeking maximum [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] and integration into other works.

## Comparison with Other Licenses

Unlike copyleft licenses such as the GPL, the MIT License does not require derivative works to be released under the same license terms. This means that software incorporating MIT-licensed code can be relicensed under different terms, including proprietary licenses. This permissiveness has made the MIT License a common choice for libraries and foundational software intended for broad reuse across diverse projects and commercial applications.
