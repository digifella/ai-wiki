# Rna

RNA (ribonucleic acid) is a nucleic acid molecule present in all living cells that plays critical roles in gene expression and protein synthesis. Like DNA, RNA is composed of nucleotides with a sugar-phosphate backbone and nitrogenous bases. However, RNA typically exists as a single-stranded molecule rather than the double helix structure of DNA, and contains the sugar ribose instead of deoxyribose. These structural differences reflect RNA's distinct biological functions compared to DNA.

## Structure and Function

RNA molecules are built from four types of nucleotides, each containing one of the nitrogenous bases adenine, cytosine, guanine, or uracil. The presence of uracil instead of thymine is a key chemical distinction from DNA. While DNA serves as the long-term storage of genetic information, RNA acts as a messenger and functional molecule, facilitating the translation of genetic codes into proteins.

## Evolution of Gene Editing Technologies

Recent advancements in biological sciences have focused on the evolution of CRISPR and gene editing technology. Dr. Feng Zhang at the Broad Institute has presented significant developments in this field, highlighting how RNA-based mechanisms are utilized to target specific genetic sequences. These technologies leverage the natural properties of RNA to enable precise modifications to the genome, marking a major shift in molecular biology and therapeutic applications.
