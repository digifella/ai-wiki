# Segmented Responsibility

Segmented Responsibility is a governance principle that mandates forensic-level transparency regarding the distribution of tasks, decisions, and outcomes across AI systems and human operators. It addresses a structural accountability gap inherent in complex AI projects, where responsibility is divided among multiple algorithmic components, human reviewers, and organizational units. This fragmentation often obscures the actual locus of decision-making, making it difficult to trace how specific inputs lead to final outputs.

The concept is closely associated with the Farah Jama Principle, which highlights the dangers of opaque decision-making processes in automated systems. When the contribution of each segment is not explicitly documented, organizations risk creating environments where no single party can be held accountable for errors or harms. This lack of clear attribution undermines trust and complicates legal and ethical oversight, as the causal chain between human intent and algorithmic action becomes indistinguishable.

To mitigate these risks, the principle requires rigorous documentation of the workflow segments involved in any AI-driven outcome. By mapping the precise role of each algorithmic module and human intervention point, organizations can establish a clear chain of custody for decisions. This approach ensures that accountability remains traceable and that responsibility is not diluted across the technical and organizational layers of the system.
