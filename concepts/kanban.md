# Kanban

Kanban is a project management methodology originating from [[concepts/low-cost-manufacturing|lean manufacturing]] practices developed by Toyota in the 1940s. The term derives from Japanese and translates to "signboard" or "billboard." While initially designed for manufacturing workflows, Kanban has been widely adopted for [[concepts/knowledge-work|knowledge work]] and [[concepts/coding|software development]]. The methodology centers on visualizing work items, limiting concurrent tasks, and optimizing workflow to enhance efficiency and team responsiveness.

## Core Principles

Kanban operates on several foundational principles. Work is made visible through a board divided into columns that represent the stages of the workflow, allowing teams to track the progress of items in real time. A key constraint is the implementation of [[concepts/draft|Work In Progress]] (WIP) limits, which restrict the number of tasks allowed in any given column to prevent bottlenecks and reduce context switching. This limitation forces the team to focus on completing current tasks before pulling new ones, thereby improving [[concepts/flow|flow]] and reducing cycle time.

## Evolution and Application

Originally a pull-based inventory control system, Kanban evolved into a broader management framework for managing knowledge work. It emphasizes continuous improvement and [[concepts/resilience|adaptability]] rather than rigid roles or fixed iterations. Teams use metrics such as lead time and throughput to analyze performance and identify areas for optimization. By focusing on the flow of work and managing constraints, Kanban enables organizations to respond more effectively to changing priorities while maintaining a sustainable pace of delivery.
