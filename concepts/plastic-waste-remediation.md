# Plastic Waste Remediation

**Plastic waste remediation** refers to the processes and technologies aimed at removing, degrading, or neutralizing [[concepts/plastic-pollution|plastic pollution]] from the environment. As humanity has produced 8.3 billion tons of plastic since the 1950s, biological and enzymatic approaches have emerged as critical solutions to mitigate this accumulation.

## Biological and Enzymatic Approaches

Recent research highlights the potential of living organisms and engineered enzymes to consume and break down plastic materials. Key insights include:

- **Biological Consumption**: Certain organisms are being studied for their ability to metabolize plastic waste, offering a sustainable alternative to mechanical recycling.
- **[[concepts/enzymatic-degradation|Enzymatic Degradation]]**: Enzymes are being developed to accelerate the breakdown of polymer chains, targeting specific types of plastics that are traditionally resistant to degradation.
- **Global Impact**: These [[concepts/biological-solutions|biological solutions]] address the urgent need to manage the vast quantities of plastic pollution, illustrated by the severe ecological consequences such as marine life entanglement and ingestion.
