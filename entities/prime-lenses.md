# Prime Lenses

Prime lenses are optical devices characterized by a fixed focal length, meaning they cannot zoom in or out. Common focal lengths include 35mm, 50mm, and 85mm. Because the perspective is static, photographers must physically move closer to or farther from their subject to adjust the framing, rather than manipulating the lens mechanics.

These lenses typically offer superior optical quality compared to zoom lenses of equivalent focal length. The optical design is optimized for a single focal length rather than a variable range, allowing manufacturers to reduce aberrations and improve sharpness. This fixed design often results in larger maximum apertures, which facilitate better performance in low-light conditions and allow for greater control over depth of field.

The use of prime lenses is particularly advantageous in specialized photographic techniques such as panorama stitching. By providing consistent perspective and high resolution across the frame, they enable photographers to capture detailed images that can be seamlessly merged. This approach allows for the creation of high-resolution panoramas that extend beyond the capabilities of standard wide-angle shots, offering greater flexibility in post-processing and final output quality.
