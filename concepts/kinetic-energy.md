# Kinetic Energy

**Kinetic energy** is the energy possessed by an object due to its motion. It is defined as the work needed to accelerate a body of a given mass from rest to its stated [[concepts/velocity|velocity]].

## Core Principles

- **Formula**: For non-relativistic speeds, $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$.
- **Relativistic Context**: At speeds approaching the [[concepts/finite-speed-of-light|speed of light]], kinetic energy contributes significantly to the system's total mass-energy.
- **Work-Energy Theorem**: The net work done on an object equals the change in its kinetic energy.

## Mass-Energy Equivalence

In [[concepts/high-energy-physics|high-energy physics]], the relationship between energy and mass becomes bidirectional. While nuclear fission and [[concepts/fusion-power|nuclear fusion]] convert mass into energy, particle accelerators can convert kinetic energy into new mass.

- **Inverse Process**: The creation of mass from energy is the inverse of the process seen in [[concepts/nuclear-weapons|atomic bombs]], where mass is converted into explosive energy.
- **Particle Creation**: High-velocity collisions allow kinetic energy to manifest as new particles, effectively "creating mass from energy."
- **Higgs Mechanism**: The discovery of the [[entities/higgs-boson|Higgs boson]] at [[entities/cern|CERN]] confirmed how particles acquire mass, a process deeply intertwined with energy interactions in the vacuum.

## Practical Applications & Research

- **Particle Accelerators**: Facilities like the [[concepts/large-hadron-collider|Large Hadron Collider]] (LHC) utilize immense kinetic energy to probe fundamental physics.
- **Recent Findings**: Research into how energy converts to mass has been pivotal in understanding the [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|Standard Model]].
