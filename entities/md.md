# Md

Md is an abbreviation commonly used in medical and healthcare contexts, most frequently standing for "Medical Doctor" or "Doctor of Medicine." It represents the primary professional degree awarded to physicians in many English-speaking countries, including the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom. The MD credential indicates that a healthcare professional has completed medical school and the necessary training to practice medicine.

## Educational Requirements

To obtain an MD degree, individuals must typically complete four years of medical school following an undergraduate degree. Admission to medical school generally requires completion of prerequisite coursework in biology, chemistry, physics, and other sciences, along with competitive scores on standardized medical entrance examinations such as the MCAT (Medical College Admission Test) in the United States. After earning the MD, graduates must complete residency training in their chosen specialty, which can last from three to seven years or longer depending on the field.

## Professional Practice

Physicians holding an MD degree are licensed to diagnose illnesses, prescribe medications, and perform medical procedures. Licensing requirements vary by country and jurisdiction but typically involve passing written and clinical examinations and meeting continuing education standards. The MD degree is distinct from other healthcare credentials such as DO (Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine) in the United States, though both qualifications lead to full medical licensure.
