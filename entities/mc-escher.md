# M.C. Escher

**Maurits Cornelis Escher** (1898–1972) was a Dutch graphic artist renowned for his mathematically inspired woodcuts, lithographs, and mezzotints. His work explores impossible constructions, [[concepts/mathematical-concepts|mathematical concepts]] such as tessellations, symmetry groups, infinity, and the nature of space-time. Escher had no formal training in [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] but collaborated with crystallographers and mathematicians to refine his understanding of geometric structures.

## Key Themes & Techniques
*   **Tessellation:** Interlocking tilings using birds, reptiles, fish, and demons Tessellation.
*   **Impossible Objects:** Structures that violate Euclidean geometry or [[concepts/fundamental-laws-of-physics|physical laws]], such as Waterfall, Relativity, and Ascending and Descending.
*   **Perspective & Infinity:** Distorted viewpoints and recursive [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]], notably in Circle Limit series.
*   **Duality:** Interchangeable positive and negative space.

## Major Works
*   *Day and Night* (1938)
*   *Drawing Hands* (1948)
*   *Waterfall* (1961)
*   *Relativity* (1953)
*   **The Print Gallery** (1956): A lithograph depicting a fish-eye view of a harbor scene viewed from a building corner, featuring a recursive [[concepts/loop|loop]] where the viewer looks at a print of the scene they are in.

## Mathematical Connections
Escher’s work has profound implications for Geometry, Topology, and [[concepts/zeta-function|Complex Analysis]]. While he approached these subjects intuitively, later mathematicians formalized the underlying structures.
