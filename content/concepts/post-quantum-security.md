# Post Quantum Security

[[concepts/mathematical-problems|Post-quantum security]] refers to cryptographic methods designed to resist attacks from both classical and [[entities/quantum-computing|quantum computers]]. As [[concepts/quantum-computing|quantum computing]] technology advances, it poses a theoretical threat to many widely-used encryption schemes that rely on the difficulty of factoring large numbers or computing discrete logarithms. [[concepts/post-quantum-cryptography|Post-quantum cryptography]] aims to maintain data confidentiality and [[concepts/integrity|integrity]] in an era when sufficiently powerful quantum computers may become available.

## Lattice-Based Cryptography

[[concepts/lattice-cryptography|Lattice cryptography]] is among the most promising post-quantum solutions currently under investigation. It is based on the mathematical difficulty of solving lattice problems, such as the shortest vector problem, which are believed to be resistant to both classical and quantum algorithms. Lattice-based schemes offer several advantages, including relatively efficient computation and strong theoretical [[concepts/security|security]] guarantees. These properties [[entities/make|make]] lattice cryptography a leading candidate for standardization and [[concepts/deployment|deployment]] in post-quantum cryptographic systems.

## Current Status

Government agencies and technology organizations, including [[concepts/open-standards|standards]] bodies, are actively evaluating and developing post-quantum [[concepts/cryptographic-algorithms|cryptographic algorithms]] to prepare for potential quantum threats. [[concepts/adoption|Adoption]] of post-quantum methods is expected to occur gradually alongside existing encryption infrastructure, with migration strategies being developed to protect sensitive data against future quantum [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]].
