# Community Health Nursing

Community health nursing is a specialized nursing practice that delivers [[concepts/health|healthcare]] services and promotes health within community settings rather than institutional environments. It focuses on populations and groups, addressing the health needs of entire communities through [[concepts/preventive-care|prevention]], education, and direct care services. [[concepts/population-health|Community health]] nurses work in diverse settings including public health departments, schools, workplaces, clinics, and homes, serving as a bridge between individuals and broader public health systems.

## Scope and Focus

Community health nurses engage in [[concepts/health-promotion|health promotion]], disease prevention, and health maintenance across all age groups and populations. Their practice encompasses health education, disease surveillance, epidemiological investigation, and advocacy for community health needs. Rather than treating individual acute illnesses, community health nurses emphasize population-level outcomes and address the social, economic, and environmental factors that influence health status.

## Practice Roles

Community health nurses assume various roles depending on community needs and organizational context. They function as educators, advocates, researchers, and program managers, working collaboratively with other healthcare professionals, community leaders, and residents. Their practice is grounded in understanding the [[concepts/determinants-of-health|determinants of health]]—including [[concepts/healthcare-access|access to healthcare]], education, income, housing, and social support—and developing interventions that address these underlying factors to improve [[concepts/social-well-being|community wellbeing]].
