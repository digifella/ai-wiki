# Adjuvant Therapy

**Adjuvant therapy** refers to additional treatment given after the primary treatment for cancer, aimed at lowering the risk of the cancer coming back. It is commonly used when there is a high risk of micrometastasis or residual disease despite successful primary intervention (e.g., surgery).

## Key Modalities
- **Chemotherapy**: Systemic treatment to kill rapidly dividing cells.
- **[[concepts/radiation-therapy|Radiation Therapy]]**: Localized treatment to destroy remaining cancer cells.
- **Immunotherapy**: Enhances the body's immune system to fight cancer.
- **Hormone Therapy**: Used for hormone-sensitive cancers (e.g., breast, prostate).
- **Targeted Therapy**: Attacks specific abnormalities in cancer cells.

## Recent Developments in Melanoma
Significant advancements in immunotherapy and [[concepts/personalized-medicine|personalized-medicine]] have reshaped adjuvant strategies for high-risk melanoma.

- **mRNA Vaccine Integration**: Personalized mRNA vaccines are being investigated to stimulate a robust [[concepts/immune-system-function|immune response]] against tumor-specific neoantigens.
- **[[entities/moderna|Moderna]] & [[entities/merck|Merck]] Collaboration**: Recent [[concepts/phase-3-trial|Phase 3 trial]] results for the combination of the personalized mRNA vaccine ([[entities/intismeran-autogene|Intismeran Autogene]]) and Keytruda ([[concepts/pembrolizumab|pembrolizumab]]) in [[concepts/resected-melanoma|resected melanoma]] have shown positive outcomes.
- **Clinical Impact**: This combination represents a potential shift from standard-of-care immunotherapy alone to a dual-[[concepts/modality|modality]] approach leveraging both checkpoint inhibition and personalized antigen presentation.
