# Feng Shui

Feng Shui is a traditional Chinese philosophical system concerned with the arrangement of physical spaces to promote [[concepts/musical-harmony|harmony]] and [[concepts/health|wellbeing]]. Developed over more than two thousand years, the practice is rooted in the belief that the orientation and organization of environments influence the [[concepts/flow|flow]] of *qi* (vital life energy), which in turn affects human fortune, health, and [[concepts/prosperity|prosperity]]. The term literally translates to "wind-water," referencing natural forces historically understood to shape landscapes and their [[concepts/habitability|habitability]].

The system relies on foundational concepts from Chinese [[concepts/cosmology|cosmology]], particularly the interplay of Yin and Yang and the Five Elements (Wood, Fire, [[entities/earth|Earth]], [[concepts/metal|Metal]], and Water). Practitioners analyze the relationship between these elements and the spatial layout of buildings or rooms to ensure balance. Key tools include the Bagua map, which divides space into eight areas corresponding to different life aspects, and the analysis of directional energies to optimize the flow of *qi* within a given environment.

Historically, Feng Shui was applied primarily to burial sites and imperial architecture to align human activity with cosmic patterns. Over time, its application expanded to residential and commercial spaces, influencing interior design, garden layout, and [[concepts/city-planning|urban planning]]. While often viewed through the lens of metaphysics, it also incorporates practical considerations such as sunlight, ventilation, and topography to create functional and aesthetically pleasing living conditions.
