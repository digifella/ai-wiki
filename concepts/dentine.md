# Dentine

Dentine is a calcified tissue that constitutes the bulk of tooth structure in vertebrates. It is situated between the outer enamel layer and the inner pulp chamber, serving to provide structural support and [[concepts/secure|protection]] to the tooth. The tissue is composed primarily of mineral crystals embedded within an organic matrix of collagen and other proteins.

In terms of [[concepts/material-properties|physical properties]], dentine occupies an intermediate position regarding hardness and mineralization. It is less mineralized than enamel but significantly more mineralized than bone. This specific [[concepts/writing|composition]] allows dentine to be harder than bone while retaining greater flexibility, a characteristic that helps absorb [[concepts/stress|stress]] and prevent tooth fracture during mastication.

The biological role of dentine extends beyond mere [[concepts/payload-structure|structural integrity]]. It acts as a protective barrier for the sensitive pulp tissue, which contains nerves and blood vessels. In many vertebrates, including sharks, dentine is a key component in the evolution of dental [[concepts/adaptations|adaptations]], contributing to the durability and function of teeth across various species during different geological periods.
