# Inventory Simulation

Inventory [[concepts/simulation|simulation]] is a computational methodology for modeling and analyzing [[concepts/inventory-management|inventory systems]] through [[concepts/algorithm-efficiency|algorithmic optimization]] and [[concepts/iterative-learning|iterative refinement]]. Rather than relying exclusively on analytical formulas or predetermined [[concepts/policies|policies]], this approach uses computational models to test multiple system configurations and decision rules under various conditions. The methodology incorporates defined [[concepts/ai-performance-evaluation|performance metrics]]—such as holding costs, stockout rates, and [[concepts/happiness|fulfillment]] times—to evaluate the effectiveness of different inventory strategies systematically.

## Core Process

The simulation process typically begins with the construction of a [[concepts/ai-avatar-creation|digital twin]] of the physical inventory environment, incorporating variables such as demand patterns, lead times, and supply chain constraints. Algorithms iteratively adjust parameters to identify optimal stock levels and reorder points, utilizing techniques like Monte Carlo simulation or discrete-event simulation to account for stochastic variability. This iterative learning allows researchers to observe system behavior over extended periods without the risks associated with real-[[entities/earth|world]] experimentation.

## Evaluation and Application

Performance is assessed by comparing simulated outcomes against established benchmarks to determine the [[concepts/robustness|robustness]] of specific inventory policies. By varying input conditions, analysts can stress-test systems against potential disruptions or demand spikes, providing insights into [[concepts/resilience|resilience]] and efficiency. This data-driven approach supports [[concepts/evidence-based-choice|evidence-based decision-making]] in supply chain management, enabling organizations to balance service levels with cost containment more effectively than traditional static models.
