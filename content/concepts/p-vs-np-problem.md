# P vs NP Problem Computational Complexity Implications and Historical Context

The [[concepts/a-video-titled-p-vs-np|P vs NP problem]] is one of the most significant unsolved questions in [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] and computer [[concepts/science|science]]. It addresses whether problems whose solutions can be quickly verified can also be quickly solved.

## Key Points
- **Definition:** The [[concepts/business-logistics|P vs NP problem]] asks if every language accepted by some nondeterministic algorithm in polynomial time is also accepted by a deterministic algorithm in polynomial time.
- **[[concepts/million-dollar-prize|Million Dollar Prize]]:** The Clay [[concepts/mathematics|Mathematics]] Institute offers $1 million for the [[concepts/solution|solution]] to this problem as part of the [[concepts/millennium-prize-problems|Millennium Prize Problems]].
- **Implications:** Solving [[concepts/complexity-classes|P vs NP]] could revolutionize fields such as [[concepts/cryptography|cryptography]], medicine, and [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]].

## Historical Context
The concept was first introduced in 1971 by Stephen Cook, who formulated the [[entities/notion|notion]] of [[concepts/np-complete|NP-completeness]]. Since then, it has become a central question in [[concepts/theoretical-computer-science|theoretical computer science]].
