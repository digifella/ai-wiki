# Emperor Ashoka

Emperor Ashoka (r. 268–232 BCE) was the third and most renowned emperor of the [[concepts/mauryan-empire|Mauryan Empire]] in [[concepts/ancient-india|ancient India]]. He inherited a vast but militaristic state built by his grandfather Chandragupta and father Bindusara. Early in his reign, Ashoka expanded the empire through conquest, most notably the [[concepts/kalinga-war|Kalinga War]] (circa 260 BCE), which brought significant territory under Mauryan control but resulted in substantial loss of life and civilian suffering.

## Buddhist Conversion and Reforms

The devastation of the Kalinga War prompted a dramatic transformation in Ashoka's outlook and [[concepts/governance|governance]]. He renounced aggressive expansion and converted to [[concepts/buddhism|Buddhism]], becoming one of history's most significant royal patrons of the religion. Rather than force conversion, Ashoka promoted Buddhist principles of non-violence, [[concepts/compassion|compassion]], and moral conduct throughout his empire and beyond, establishing it as a dominant religious and philosophical framework across much of Asia.

## Legacy and Influence

Ashoka's reign marked a shift from purely military imperial power toward governance based on moral and religious authority. He commissioned the construction of numerous stupas and monasteries, sponsored missionary missions to distant lands, and issued edicts inscribed on pillars and rocks across his empire to communicate ethical principles to his subjects. His model of Buddhist kingship influenced rulers throughout Asia for centuries and established precedents for the relationship between state authority and religious institutions that persisted long after the Mauryan Empire's decline.
