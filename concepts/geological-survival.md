# Geological Survival

**Geological Survival** refers to the [[concepts/data-persistence|persistence]] of biological, ecological, or structural systems across significant geological timescales, often characterized by [[concepts/resilience|resilience]] to tectonic shifts, climate oscillations, and [[concepts/catastrophic-events|mass extinction events]]. It emphasizes the interplay between Deep Time and adaptive stability.

## Core Principles

- **Temporal Resilience**: Systems that maintain functional [[concepts/honesty|integrity]] over millions of years despite environmental volatility.
- **Biotic [[concepts/continuity|Continuity]]**: [[concepts/preservation|Preservation]] of ancient lineages or ecosystem structures that predate major climatic shifts.
- **Geological Anchoring**: Dependence on stable substrates or unique geochemical conditions that buffer against surface-level disturbances.

## Case Studies & Examples

- **[[concepts/daintree-rainforest|Daintree Rainforest]]**: Represents a critical example of geological survival in tropical ecosystems.
	- Identified as the oldest living tropical rainforest, showcasing ancient [[concepts/biodiversity|biodiversity]] that has persisted through significant climatic changes.
	- Demonstrates how coastal-terrestrial interfaces can preserve prehistoric ecological structures.
