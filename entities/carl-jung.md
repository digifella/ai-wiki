# Carl Jung

Carl Jung (1875–1961) was a Swiss psychiatrist and psychoanalyst who founded analytical psychology, one of the major schools of depth psychology. He studied medicine at the University of Zurich and developed an early interest in psychiatric practice, eventually becoming a prominent figure in the psychoanalytic movement of the early twentieth century.

## Relationship with Freud

Jung collaborated extensively with Sigmund Freud beginning in the early 1900s, and Freud identified him as his intellectual successor. However, their professional relationship deteriorated during the 1910s as Jung developed significant theoretical disagreements with fundamental Freudian concepts. Jung rejected Freud's emphasis on sexuality as the primary driver of human psychology and proposed alternative models of the unconscious mind.

## Major Contributions

Jung developed several influential concepts that distinguished analytical psychology from Freudianism, including the theory of psychological types, the concept of the collective unconscious, and the process of individuation. He emphasized archetypes—universal symbols and character patterns present across human cultures—as fundamental structures within the collective unconscious. His work on dream analysis, complex psychology, and personality typology became foundational to modern psychology and influenced fields beyond psychiatry, including anthropology and literary criticism.
