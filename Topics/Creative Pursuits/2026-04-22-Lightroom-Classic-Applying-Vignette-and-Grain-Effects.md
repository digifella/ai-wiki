## Lightroom Classic: Applying Vignette and Grain Effects
**Clip title:** Adding Vignette and Grain Effects in Lightroom Classic
**Author / channel:** [[entities/julieanne-kost|Julieanne Kost]]
**URL:** https://youtu.be/XDqwMmrs-M8

### Summary
[[entities/julianne-kost|Julianne Kost]]'s video provides a comprehensive guide on applying vignette and grain effects to [[concepts/images|images]] within [[entities/lightroom-classic|Lightroom Classic]]. The [[concepts/tutorial|tutorial]] outlines three distinct methods for creating vignettes, each offering varying levels of control and application, followed by an explanation of how to add [[concepts/film-grain|film grain]] to photos. The main takeaway is the versatility [[concepts/lightroom|Lightroom]] offers in enhancing [[concepts/images|images]] with these stylistic effects.

The first two methods for vignettes are found in the [[concepts/lens-correction|Lens Correction]] and Effects panels. The Lens Correction panel's vignetting slider, initially designed to *remove* lens distortion, can also be used to add an "in-camera" vignette. However, this method is applied *before* cropping, meaning any subsequent cropping will make the vignette appear uneven. For a more dynamic and flexible approach, the Effects panel's "Post Crop Vignetting" is recommended. This feature applies the vignette *after* cropping and updates it if the crop is altered. It offers three styles: [Highlight Priority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highlight_priority) (recovers highlights but may shift colors in dark areas), [Color Priority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_priority) (minimizes color shifts but no highlight recovery), and Paint Overlay (similar to a [[concepts/black-and-white|black and white]] paint overlay, useful for high-key images). Sliders such as Amount, Midpoint, Roundness, Feather, and Highlights allow for precise [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] to the vignette's intensity, position, shape, softness, and highlight [[concepts/preservation|preservation]].

For even greater control and creative freedom, Lightroom Classic offers a third method using [[concepts/masking|masking]] tools, specifically the [Radial Gradient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radial_gradient). This technique enables the creation of off-center vignettes, providing precise control over the area affected. Users can drag out the gradient, reposition, resize, rotate, and adjust its feathering to control the fall-off. The 'Invert' option allows the vignette effect to be applied either inside or outside the gradient. This [[concepts/masking|masking]] approach also permits the combination of multiple radial gradients and other [[concepts/masking-tools|masking tools]], like brushes, to selectively include or exclude areas from the vignette effect, showcasing a highly customizable [[concepts/workflow|workflow]].

Beyond vignettes, the video also details how to add film grain effects, which are found within the same Effects panel. Users can zoom in to 100% to accurately visualize the impact of the grain. Three sliders—Amount, Size, and Roughness—control the intensity, scale, and [[concepts/texture|texture]] of the grain, respectively. Similar to vignettes, grain can also be applied to selective areas of an image using Lightroom's [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] tools, further extending creative possibilities. Ultimately, the video demonstrates that Lightroom Classic provides a robust set of tools for photographers to artfully apply both vignettes and grain, enhancing mood and guiding the viewer's eye.
