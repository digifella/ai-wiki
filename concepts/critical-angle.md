# Critical Angle

The **critical angle** is a concept in [[concepts/optics|optics]] that refers to the smallest angle of incidence at which [[concepts/light|light]], traveling from one [[entities/medium|medium]] into another with a higher refractive index, can be totally internally reflected. This angle depends on the indices of [[concepts/refraction|refraction]] of the two media involved.

### Key Points
- The critical angle is defined as the minimum angle of incidence for which [[concepts/total-internal-reflection|total internal reflection]] occurs.
- Beyond this angle, no light passes across the boundary and all light is reflected back into the first medium.
- It plays a crucial role in fiber optics and other applications requiring the confinement of light within materials.

## Additional Notes
- The concept can be extended to the study of angles such as the [[concepts/angle-of-repose|angle of repose]], where the critical angle refers to the maximum slope at which a pile of granular material [[entities/will|will]] naturally rest without sliding down.
- In [[concepts/solid-mechanics|solid mechanics]] and materials [[concepts/science|science]], understanding these angles is vital for applications ranging from grain [[entities/storage|storage]] silos to [[concepts/catastrophic-landslide|landslide]] [[concepts/preventive-care|prevention]].
