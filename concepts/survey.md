# Survey

A survey is a systematic method of collecting data from a sample population to gather information about trends, preferences, and behaviors. In the context of [[concepts/coding|software development]] and [[concepts/cryptography|cryptography]], surveys serve as empirical tools for understanding how developers adopt new tools, technologies, and practices across the industry.

## AI Tool Adoption in Development

Recent [[concepts/developer|developer]] surveys have examined [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] patterns of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] tools in software [[concepts/development-workflows|development workflows]]. These surveys track metrics such as the percentage of developers using [[concepts/ai-assisted-coding|AI-assisted coding]] tools, frequency of usage, and perceived impact on [[concepts/productivity|productivity]]. The data reveals varying adoption rates across different [[concepts/experience|experience]] levels, with patterns differing between individual developers, teams, and organizations. Survey methodologies typically include both quantitative metrics and qualitative [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] about integration challenges, [[concepts/trust|trust]] concerns, and practical benefits observed in day-to-day work.

## Tech Stack Preferences

Surveys also document shifts in technology stack choices among developers, capturing changes in programming language popularity, framework adoption, and infrastructure preferences. This data helps identify emerging technologies gaining traction and declining technologies losing mindshare within the [[entities/developer|developer]] community. Such surveys provide snapshots of industry trends at specific points in time and, when conducted periodically, reveal trajectories of [[concepts/technological-change|technological change]].

## Methodological Considerations

The [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] of developer surveys depends significantly on sampling methodology, response rates, and question design. Surveys reaching large audiences through platforms like Stack Overflow or [[entities/github|GitHub]] provide broad coverage, though they may have self-selection bias. Longitudinal surveys conducted by the same organization enable tracking of changes over time, while one-off surveys offer single-moment assessments useful for [[concepts/benchmark-testing|benchmarking]] against other data sources.
