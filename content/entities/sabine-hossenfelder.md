# Sabine Hossenfelder

Sabine Hossenfelder is a [[concepts/science|science]] communicator and physicist known for her accessible [[concepts/explanations|explanations]] of complex topics in [[concepts/theoretical-physics|theoretical physics]] and [[concepts/cosmology|cosmology]]. Her work spans multiple areas including [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]], galaxy cluster dynamics, nuclear physics, and emerging quantum technologies. She is recognized for translating technical scientific concepts into formats accessible to general audiences through [[concepts/writing|writing]], video content, and public engagement.

## Research and Communication Focus

Hossenfelder's communication addresses fundamental questions in physics with particular emphasis on distinguishing between established science and speculative [[concepts/ideas|ideas]]. She has examined challenges in dark matter [[concepts/theory|theory]], including how [[concepts/dark-matter-mystery|baryonic matter in galaxy clusters]] may be underestimated in current [[concepts/models|models]]. Her coverage extends to nuclear physics, including developments in understanding [[concepts/superheavy-nuclei-stability|superheavy nuclei stability]] and their implications for the periodic table. She has also analyzed [[concepts/quantum-computing|quantum computing]], critically examining both the technological potential and the inflated expectations surrounding the field.

## Approach to Science Communication

Her work is characterized by critical examination of hype versus substantive evidence in contemporary physics. Rather than uncritically promoting emerging technologies or [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|theoretical frameworks]], Hossenfelder emphasizes the importance of [[concepts/empirical-evidence|empirical evidence]] and realistic assessment of scientific limitations and uncertainties.
