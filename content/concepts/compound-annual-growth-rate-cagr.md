# Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)

Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) is a financial metric that measures the mean annual growth rate of an investment or business metric over a specified period of time, assuming profits are reinvested [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] the end of each year. Unlike simple year-over-year growth rates, CAGR smooths out the effects of volatility and fluctuations in returns to provide a single, standardized growth figure. This makes it particularly useful for comparing performance across different time periods or investments with varying levels of annual volatility.

## Calculation and Formula

CAGR is calculated using the formula: (Ending Value / Beginning Value)^(1 / Number of Years) − 1. For example, if an investment grows from $10,000 to $25,000 over five years, the CAGR would be approximately 25.1% per year. The metric assumes that earnings are reinvested each year and does not account for the timing or size of individual cash flows during the measurement period.

## Applications in Business Strategy

In business strategy, CAGR serves as a key performance indicator for evaluating company growth, revenue expansion, and market penetration over multi-year periods. Organizations use CAGR to set realistic growth targets, benchmark performance against competitors, and assess the effectiveness of strategic initiatives. It is commonly employed when analyzing historical growth trends, projecting future performance, and communicating investor results. CAGR is particularly valuable in mature markets where consistent, predictable growth rates are more meaningful than volatile annual figures.
