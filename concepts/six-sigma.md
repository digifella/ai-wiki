# Six Sigma

Six Sigma is a data-driven project management and quality improvement methodology designed to reduce defects and variation in business processes. The name derives from a statistical concept: six standard deviations from the mean, which in manufacturing corresponds to a defect rate of 3.4 per million opportunities. Originally developed by Motorola in the 1980s, Six Sigma was later popularized and refined by General Electric under Jack Welch's leadership. The methodology has since been adopted across manufacturing, healthcare, finance, and service industries.

## Core Approach

The methodology relies on a structured problem-solving framework known as DMAIC, which stands for Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, and Control. This cycle guides teams through identifying process problems, collecting data to understand current performance, determining root causes, implementing solutions, and establishing controls to sustain improvements. For new product or process development, the DMADV framework (Define, Measure, Analyze, Design, Verify) is often employed.

## Organizational Roles

Six Sigma utilizes a hierarchical structure of certified professionals to ensure consistent application of its tools and techniques. Green Belts typically handle smaller projects while maintaining their primary job functions, whereas Black Belts lead complex, cross-functional initiatives full-time. Master Black Belts serve as internal experts who mentor Black and Green Belts, manage the strategic deployment of the methodology, and ensure alignment with organizational goals.

## Industry Adoption

While initially rooted in manufacturing, Six Sigma has expanded into diverse sectors including healthcare, finance, and software development. In healthcare, it is used to reduce patient wait times and medical errors. In finance, it helps streamline transaction processing and risk management. The methodology’s emphasis on statistical analysis and continuous improvement allows organizations to standardize processes and enhance customer satisfaction across various operational contexts.
