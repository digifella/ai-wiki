### Summary
The video "What is intelligence?" provides a comprehensive exploration of [[concepts/human-intelligence|human intelligence]], challenging the narrow, conventional view that it is solely measured by IQ scores. It argues for a broader, more nuanced understanding, emphasizing that intelligence is a multifaceted construct rather than a singular, quantifiable entity. The central theme revolves around identifying various forms of intelligence, critiquing traditional assessment methods, and promoting the idea of intelligence as a dynamic and developable capacity.

A significant portion of the video is dedicated to Howard Gardner's [[concepts/theory|theory]] of [[concepts/multiple-intelligences|Multiple Intelligences]]. It outlines distinct types such as linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, bodily-kinesthetic, musical, interpersonal, intrapersonal, and naturalistic intelligences, suggesting that individuals possess unique strengths across these different areas. The video then critically examines IQ tests, explaining their origin with Alfred Binet to identify academic [[concepts/learning|learning]] needs. However, it [[concepts/highlights|highlights]] major limitations, arguing that IQ tests are often culturally biased, fail to measure crucial aspects like creativity, emotional intelligence, or practical [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] [[concepts/skills|skills]], and can be influenced by external factors, thus providing an incomplete picture of a person's overall cognitive capability.

Beyond academic prowess, the video discusses the profound importance of Emotional Intelligence (EQ), which encompasses the ability to understand and manage one's own emotions, as well as empathize with others, presenting it as a strong predictor of success in various life domains. Practical intelligence, or "common sense" and problem-solving [[concepts/skills|skills]] applied to real-world [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]], is also highlighted as a vital yet often overlooked form of intelligence. Crucially, the video introduces Carol Dweck's concept of fixed versus growth mindsets, asserting that intelligence is not a static trait but a malleable capacity that can be developed and enhanced through effort, [[concepts/learning|learning]], and perseverance, emphasizing the brain's plasticity and our potential for continuous intellectual growth.

In conclusion, the video strongly advocates for moving beyond a singular, score-based definition of intelligence. It encourages viewers to recognize and [[concepts/value|value]] the diverse forms of intelligence that exist, both in themselves and others. The main takeaway is that true intelligence encompasses a wide [[concepts/range|range]] of abilities, including emotional and practical skills, and that fostering a growth [[concepts/mindset|mindset]] is key to unlocking and continuously expanding one's potential throughout life, rather than being limited by a single metric like an IQ score.
