# Modified Newtonian Dynamics Mond

Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND) is a theoretical framework proposed in 1983 by physicist Mordehai Milgrom to explain gravitational phenomena at galactic and larger scales. The theory posits that Newton's laws of gravitation require modification in regimes of very low acceleration, specifically when the acceleration falls below a critical threshold denoted as $a_0$. This adjustment aims to account for the observed rotational speeds of galaxies without invoking the existence of dark matter, which is the standard explanation within the Lambda-CDM model.

The core premise of MOND is that gravity behaves differently in environments where accelerations are extremely weak, such as the outer regions of spiral galaxies. In these low-acceleration regimes, the gravitational force decays more slowly with distance than predicted by Newtonian mechanics. This modification allows the theory to predict flat rotation curves for galaxies based solely on their visible baryonic matter, effectively resolving the "missing mass" problem that typically necessitates the postulation of dark matter halos.

While MOND has achieved significant success in predicting the dynamics of individual galaxies and binary systems, it faces substantial challenges when applied to larger cosmological structures. The theory struggles to explain the dynamics of galaxy clusters and the cosmic microwave background radiation without introducing some form of unseen mass, often referred to as "dark neutrinos" or other relativistic extensions. Consequently, while MOND remains a viable alternative to dark matter for galactic scales, it has not yet provided a complete replacement for the Lambda-CDM model in describing the universe as a whole.
