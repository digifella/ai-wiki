# Semiconductor Supply Chain

The semiconductor supply chain encompasses the interconnected stages required to design, manufacture, and distribute integrated circuits. Historically, this supply chain has been characterized by significant specialization and geographic fragmentation, with different companies specializing in distinct phases—design, fabrication (foundries), packaging, testing, and distribution. This division of labor has enabled efficiency and expertise concentration, but has also created vulnerabilities through concentrated dependencies on specific suppliers and geographic regions.

In response to these vulnerabilities, certain technology firms have pursued vertical integration strategies to gain greater control over critical components. For instance, Tesla and SpaceX have implemented internal strategies to manage their hardware needs, including the development of custom silicon. This approach involves moving beyond traditional outsourcing models to directly influence the design and production of specialized chips, such as those intended for artificial intelligence applications.

The shift toward vertical integration represents a structural change in how major technology entities approach semiconductor procurement. By internalizing parts of the supply chain, companies like Tesla and SpaceX aim to reduce reliance on external foundries and mitigate risks associated with global supply chain disruptions. This strategy contrasts with the historical norm of highly fragmented, specialized roles across the industry.
