# Ercot Electric Reliability Council Of Texas

ERCOT (Electric Reliability Council of Texas) is the independent system operator (ISO) responsible for managing electricity transmission and ensuring reliable power distribution across most of Texas. Established in 1971, ERCOT operates as a non-profit organization overseeing the interconnected transmission grid that serves approximately 26 million customers in central, eastern, and northern portions of the state. The organization does not own generation facilities, transmission lines, or distribution infrastructure; instead, it coordinates the operations of these systems managed by utilities, generators, and other market participants.

## Operations and Market Structure

ERCOT manages real-time electricity markets and operates a day-ahead market where generators and retailers bid to supply power. The organization dispatches generating units to meet demand while maintaining system frequency and stability. It also maintains adequate reserve capacity to prevent outages during periods of high demand or unexpected generation failures. ERCOT's service area covers roughly 90 percent of Texas's electricity demand, with the remainder served by utilities outside its jurisdiction, including those in West Texas and along the eastern border.

## Notable Events and Challenges

ERCOT gained significant public attention during the February 2021 winter storm when widespread power outages affected millions of Texans, highlighting vulnerabilities in the state's power generation and winterization practices. The event prompted reviews of grid preparation and capacity planning. ERCOT continues to face challenges balancing rapid growth in electricity demand, integration of renewable energy sources like wind and solar, and infrastructure maintenance across its sprawling service territory.
