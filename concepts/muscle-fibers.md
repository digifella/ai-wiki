# Muscle Fibers

**Muscle fibers** (muscle cells) are the fundamental contractile units of Skeletal Muscle, Cardiac Muscle, and Smooth Muscle. They are specialized cells containing Myofibrils, which are composed of repeating units called Sarcomeres. The interaction between Actin (thin filaments) and Myosin (thick filaments) within the sarcomere drives contraction via the Sliding Filament [[concepts/theory|Theory]].

## Types of Muscle Fibers

### Skeletal Muscle Fibers
Skeletal muscle fibers are multinucleated, striated, and voluntary. They are generally categorized by metabolic profile and contraction [[concepts/speed|speed]]:

*   **Type I (Slow-Twitch Oxidative):** High mitochondrial [[concepts/density|density]], high myoglobin content, fatigue-resistant. Used for posture and [[concepts/physical-endurance|endurance]].
*   **Type IIa (Fast-Twitch Oxidative-Glycolytic):** Intermediate properties; can use both aerobic and anaerobic [[concepts/yeast-fermentation|metabolism]].
*   **Type IIx (Fast-Twitch Glycolytic):** Low mitochondrial density, high glycolytic capacity, rapid fatigue. Used for short bursts of power.

### Cardiac and Smooth Muscle
*   **Cardiac Muscle:** Striated, involuntary, branched fibers with intercalated discs.
*   **Smooth Muscle:** Non-striated, involuntary, spindle-shaped fibers found in hollow organs.

## Molecular Mechanism: Myosin and Actin

Contraction is powered by the hydrolysis of ATP by the myosin head. The cycle involves:
1.  **Cross-bridge formation:** Myosin binds to actin.
2.  **Power stroke:** Myosin pivots, pulling actin toward the center of the sarcomere.
3.  **Detachment:** ATP binds to myosin, breaking the cross-bridge.
4.  **Cocking:** ATP hydrolysis repositions the myosin head.

## Culinary Applications: Myosin Extraction

In food [[concepts/science|science]], particularly in [[concepts/ground-meat|ground meat]] preparations, the mechanical extraction of Myosin from muscle fibers is critical for [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]] and binding. This process, known as **[[concepts/myosin-development|myosin development]]**, involves breaking down the structural [[concepts/honesty|integrity]] of the muscle to [[concepts/deployment|release]] soluble proteins that form a gel network upon heating.

*   **Mechanism:** Physical agitation (mixing, pounding, or grinding) disrupts the Z-disc and M-line structures, allowing myosin to leach out. This protein network traps water and fat, preventing separation and providing elasticity.
*   **[[concepts/texture|Texture]] Control:**
    *   **Burgers:** Minimal [[concepts/uniform-crispness|myosin development]] is preferred to maintain a tender, crumbly texture. Over-mixing leads to a dense, rubbery [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]].
    *   **Sausages and Meatballs:** High [[concepts/universal-technique|myosin development]] is required to create a "snap" or cohesive bite. This is achieved through vigorous mixing, often aided by cold temperatures and salt, which solubilizes the myosin.
*   **Key Factors:** [[concepts/thermal-regulation|Temperature control]] (keeping meat cold prevents fat smearing and premature [[concepts/denaturation|protein denaturation]]) and salt concentration are essential for optimal extraction.
