## China's Reusable Rocket, Reflect Orbital, Asteroid Flybys, and Spaceflight Highlights
**Clip title:** China Lands Rocket, Reflect Orbital Declares War on The Night - Deep Space [[concepts/software-updates|Updates]], July 15th

### Summary
The video provides a comprehensive update on recent [[concepts/deep-space-exploration|deep space]] activities, starting with a flurry of rocket launches. [[entities/spacex|SpaceX]] conducted multiple [[entities/falcon-9|Falcon 9]] missions, primarily deploying Starlink constellations, with one notable launch carrying two "Fabships" from [[entities/beskar|Beskar]] for [[concepts/microgravity-manufacturing|microgravity manufacturing]] experiments. [[entities/china|China]] also performed several launches, including Long March 6A and 8A for communication satellites. A significant development was China's first successful booster recovery with a Long March 10B, utilizing a unique cable-catching system on a sea-based platform, showcasing a distinctive approach to reusability.

In [[concepts/human-spaceflight|human spaceflight]], the Soyuz MS-29 mission successfully launched from Kazakhstan, delivering three crew members, including [[concepts/nasa|NASA]] astronaut Anil Menon, to the International [[concepts/space-station|Space Station]]. This event was highlighted by [[concepts/nasa|NASA]] Administrator Bill Nelson's presence at the launch, signaling continued international cooperation and reinforcing the ISS's extended operational timeline to 2030. This period also saw the passing of Wally Funk, a pioneering aviator from the Mercury 13 program who finally achieved spaceflight aboard [[entities/blue-origin|Blue Origin]]'s New Shepard. In [[concepts/deep-space-exploration|deep space exploration]], China's Tianwen-2 spacecraft achieved a close flyby of asteroid 469219 Kamo'oalewa, capturing the smallest close-up image of an asteroid to date, while [[entities/japan|Japan]]'s Hayabusa2, on its extended mission, conducted a flyby of asteroid 98943 Torifune, revealing its [[entities/contact|contact]] binary structure.

The commercial space sector demonstrated notable progress. Blue Origin continued its recovery and reconstruction efforts at its orbital launch site, simultaneously advancing the manufacturing of its Mark 1 and Mark 2 lunar landers. Katalyst Space's Swift Rescue mission reported successful commissioning of its spacecraft in orbit, proceeding with its rendezvous plan. [[entities/india|India]]'s Skyroot is preparing for its inaugural orbital launch attempt with the Vikram-1 rocket, aiming to establish private sector orbital capabilities. Furthermore, the [[entities/us-government|US government]] broadened its [[concepts/export-control|National Security]] Space Launch program by incorporating Relativity Space and [[concepts/impulse|Impulse]] Space, with [[concepts/impulse|Impulse]] Space recognized for its specialized upper stages designed to enhance payload delivery to diverse orbits.

Technological advancements were also highlighted, with Rocket Lab performing a full-duration test fire of its Archimedes vacuum [[concepts/engine|engine]], and Astra showcasing its redesigned [[concepts/first-stage-engine|first-stage engine]]. [[entities/reflect-orbital|Reflect Orbital]] received FCC [[concepts/authorization|authorization]] for its Arendel-1 satellite, a large thin-film reflector intended to beam sunlight back to [[entities/earth|Earth]], offering potential applications in nighttime illumination for search and rescue or extending [[concepts/solar-energy|solar power]] generation. The video concludes by building anticipation for SpaceX's next integrated flight of [[concepts/starship|Starship]] and Super Heavy, citing recent successful static fire tests of Booster 20 as an indication of its readiness for another ambitious orbital launch attempt, underscoring the dynamic and rapidly evolving landscape of the global space industry.
