# Gobel

Gobel is a scholar known for critical work on the limitations of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] training methodologies and [[concepts/expert-systems|expert systems]]. His critique builds upon and challenges earlier [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|theoretical frameworks]] in the field of [[concepts/ai-development|AI development]] and machine [[concepts/learning|learning]].

## Critical Contributions

Gobel's work is notable for examining constraints in how models and expert systems can be trained and deployed. Working alongside scholars such as [[entities/chassy|Chassy]], Gobel has provided critique of earlier proposals—including those influenced by the [[entities/dreyfus-and-dreyfus|Dreyfus brothers]]' theoretical work—that underestimated fundamental limitations in expert [[concepts/system-capabilities|system capabilities]] and [[concepts/abstraction|generalization]].

His contributions form part of a broader scholarly conversation about the realistic boundaries of [[concepts/machine-learning-systems|machine learning systems]] and the gap between theoretical promise and practical performance in [[entities/ai|artificial intelligence]] applications.
