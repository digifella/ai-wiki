# Light

Light is electromagnetic radiation visible to the human eye, occupying wavelengths between approximately 380 and 700 nanometers in the electromagnetic spectrum. It represents a fundamental phenomenon in physics and plays a central role in understanding the universe, from the behavior of atoms to the structure of galaxies. Light travels at a constant speed of approximately 299,792 kilometers per second in a vacuum, a value denoted as *c* in physics equations.

## Wave-Particle Duality

Light exhibits wave-particle duality, behaving simultaneously as both a wave and a particle depending on the experimental context. As a wave, it demonstrates interference and diffraction patterns, characterized by frequency and wavelength. As a particle, it consists of discrete quanta of energy called photons, which have no mass but carry momentum. This dual nature is a cornerstone of quantum mechanics, explaining phenomena such as the photoelectric effect and the emission spectra of atoms.

## Interaction with Matter

When light encounters matter, it can be absorbed, reflected, refracted, or transmitted. The interaction depends on the material's properties and the light's wavelength. For instance, refraction occurs when light passes from one medium to another with a different density, changing its speed and direction. Reflection allows light to bounce off surfaces, enabling vision and the formation of images by mirrors and lenses. Absorption converts light energy into other forms, such as heat, which is critical in thermodynamics and photosynthesis.
