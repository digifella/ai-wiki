## Qwen 3.6 27B vs 35B Local AI Agents: Anki Translation Performance
**Clip title:** [[concepts/testing|Testing]] [[concepts/qwen-36-35b-a3b|Qwen 3.6 35B]] vs 27b Locally on a Project
**Author / channel:** Jarods Journey
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ykqIQ6TFohw

### Summary
The video provides a comparative test of two new Qwen 3.6 [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]] (LLMs), the 27B and 35B variants, using them as local [[concepts/ai-coding-agents|AI coding agents]]. The main objective is to evaluate their performance in adding a new feature—English translation of Japanese sentences—to an existing Anki flashcard add-on. The [[entities/speaker|speaker]] aims to determine if these [[concepts/reasoning-models|open-source models]] can serve as effective substitutes for commercial LLMs like [[concepts/gpt-4|GPT-4]] or [[concepts/claude-ai|Claude]] Opus in a practical development [[concepts/workflow|workflow]].

The testing methodology involved setting up separate [[concepts/git-branch-management|Git branches]] for each model (35B and 27B) within an "OpenCode" environment, which acts as an interface for the [[concepts/ai-agent|AI agent]]. Both Qwen models were launched locally using VLLM for optimized [[concepts/inference|inference]]. The speaker then used an `add-feature` skill in OpenCode to guide each LLM through the process of understanding the project, creating a feature plan, and implementing the translation functionality. This new feature was designed to automatically generate English translations for Japanese sentences and integrate them into the Anki flashcards.

During the evaluation, both Qwen models successfully implemented the desired translation feature, but with distinct characteristics. The Qwen 3.6-35B model was significantly faster in generating responses and code but exhibited a less "intuitive" interaction, [[concepts/prompting|prompting]] fewer [[concepts/clarifying-questions|clarifying questions]] and at times feeling more like a "[[concepts/codex|Codex]] model" that just implements. It initially encountered an HTTP 400 error during [[entities/api-calls|API calls]], which the speaker resolved by adjusting the API [[concepts/adoption|implementation]] to use standard [[concepts/python|Python]] libraries, thereby bypassing VLLM endpoint compatibility issues. The [[concepts/qwen-36-27b|Qwen 3.6-27B]] model, while approximately three times slower in its processing, proved to be "smarter" and more "intuitive." It asked more probing and relevant questions regarding the configuration [[concepts/structure|structure]] and potential overwrites, demonstrating a deeper understanding of the existing add-on's [[concepts/architecture|architecture]] and potential impacts. It also encountered an initial error, but similar to the 35B, it provided sufficient [[concepts/debugging|debugging]] steps for resolution.

In conclusion, both Qwen 3.6 models showcased impressive capabilities in assisting with coding tasks. The speaker chose the Qwen 3.6-27B model for his personal workflow due to its superior intelligence and proactive clarifying questions, prioritizing [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]] and alignment with his project's [[concepts/design|design]] over raw speed. He noted that while the 35B's speed could facilitate faster [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] for quick fixes, the 27B's thoughtful approach led to a more robust implementation from the start. The video highlights the rapid advancement of local LLMs, suggesting they are quickly catching up to the performance of closed-source models from just a few months prior, and emphasizes that quantized versions are available for users with less powerful [[concepts/hardware|hardware]].
