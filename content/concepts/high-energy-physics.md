# High Energy Physics

High energy physics is the branch of [[concepts/physics|physics]] concerned with understanding the fundamental constituents of matter and the forces that govern their interactions. By studying particle collisions [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] extremely high energy [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]]—typically measured in gigaelectronvolts (GeV) or teraelectronvolts (TeV)—physicists can probe the [[concepts/structure|structure]] of [[concepts/subatomic-particles|subatomic particles]] and test theoretical [[concepts/models|models]] of particle behavior. This field operates at the boundary between experiment and [[concepts/theory|theory]], with large-scale facilities like particle accelerators serving as tools to recreate conditions similar to those in the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]].

## Experimental Methods

High energy physics research relies on sophisticated experimental apparatus to detect and measure the products of particle collisions. Particle accelerators such as the [[entities/lhc|Large Hadron Collider]] (LHC) collide particles at relativistic speeds, generating the high energies needed to access new physics. Detector systems like those in the [[entities/cms-experiment|CMS experiment]] capture data from these collisions, allowing scientists to reconstruct the properties and trajectories of short-lived particles. Ground-based observatories such as [[concepts/lhaaso|LHAASO]] detect high-energy cosmic particles from space, providing complementary information about extreme physics occurring in distant astrophysical sources.

## Theoretical Framework

The [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|Standard Model of particle physics]] forms the theoretical foundation of high energy physics, describing three of the four fundamental forces and classifying known particles into quarks, leptons, and gauge bosons. Current research focuses on [[concepts/testing|testing]] the limits of this model and exploring physics beyond it—investigating questions about [[concepts/quark-substructure|quark substructure]], the [[entities/nature|nature]] of [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]], and the origin of fundamental symmetries. Emerging technologies like [[concepts/quantum-computing|quantum computing]] offer new computational approaches to solving complex problems in high energy [[concepts/theoretical-physics|physics theory]] and [[concepts/simulation|simulation]].
