# 256 QAM

256 Quadrature Amplitude Modulation (256 QAM) is a digital modulation technique used in radio frequency communications that encodes data by simultaneously varying both the amplitude and phase of a carrier signal. The modulation scheme produces 256 distinct signal states, with each state representing a unique 8-bit binary sequence. This allows 256 QAM to transmit 8 bits of information per symbol, making it an efficient encoding method for bandwidth-constrained communication systems.

## Technical Operation

In 256 QAM, the 256 possible states are arranged on a constellation diagram as a 16×16 grid in the complex plane, where each point represents a specific combination of amplitude and phase values. The transmitter maps incoming data bits into these constellation points, which are then modulated onto a carrier signal. At the receiver end, the demodulator identifies which constellation point was transmitted and recovers the original bit sequence. This approach balances spectral efficiency with the practical constraints of signal detection and noise tolerance.

## Application in Satellite Communications

256 QAM is employed in modern satellite communication systems, including SpaceX's Starlink v3 architecture, where efficient use of available bandwidth is critical. The modulation technique enables higher data transmission rates within the allocated frequency spectrum compared to lower-order modulation schemes. However, the denser constellation of 256 QAM makes it more susceptible to noise and signal degradation, requiring robust error correction and careful link budget management to maintain reliable communication.
