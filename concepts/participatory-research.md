# Participatory Research

**Participatory research** refers to a family of research approaches that actively involve those who [[entities/will|will]] be affected by the research in its processes and outcomes. It seeks to redress power imbalances between researchers and communities, emphasizing co-production of knowledge, mutual [[concepts/learning|learning]], and social change.

## Core Principles
- **Collaboration**: Equal partnership between researchers and community members/stakeholders.
- **Empowerment**: Enhancing the capacity of participants to influence decisions affecting their lives.
- **Relevance**: Ensuring research questions and methods address community-identified priorities.
- **Reciprocity**: Benefits [[concepts/flow|flow]] back to the community, not just the [[entities/tomasz-janowski|academic]] institution.

## Key Approaches
- Community-Based Participatory Research (CBPR)
- Action Research
- Co-Design

## Challenges
- **Power Dynamics**: Navigating institutional vs. community authority.
- **Resource Intensity**: Participatory methods often require more time and funding.
- **Tokenism**: Risk of superficial involvement without genuine [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] power for participants.
