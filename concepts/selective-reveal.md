# Selective Reveal

Selective Reveal is a digital image editing technique used to composite the optimal elements from multiple similar photographs into a single, improved final image. By stacking corresponding layers in software such as Adobe Photoshop, editors can isolate specific details from each source file. This process allows for the retention of the sharpest focus, most natural expressions, and best lighting conditions from individual shots while discarding unwanted artifacts or flaws present in any single frame.

The method is particularly valuable in group photography, where it addresses the common challenge of capturing perfect moments for every subject simultaneously. In such scenarios, individuals may blink, look away, or exhibit unflattering expressions at different times. By aligning multiple exposures of the same scene, an editor can manually mask or blend regions to ensure each person displays their best appearance in the final composite.

Implementation typically involves importing the source images as layers within a single document. The editor then uses layer masks to hide portions of upper layers, revealing the desired details from the layers beneath. This non-destructive workflow ensures that original data remains intact, allowing for precise adjustments to facial features, eyes, and background elements without degrading image quality.
