# Material Properties

Material properties are the observable and measurable characteristics that describe how a substance responds to physical stimuli and behaves under various conditions. These properties are fundamental to understanding matter in both [[concepts/physics|physics]] and materials chemistry, determining everything from how materials deform under [[concepts/stress|stress]] to how they conduct electricity or heat. Material properties emerge from the atomic and molecular [[concepts/structure|structure]] of a substance and can be predicted, measured, and modified through processing and composition changes.

## Physical Properties

Physical properties describe how a material responds to external forces and environmental conditions without altering its chemical composition. These include mechanical properties such as hardness, elasticity, and strength; thermal properties like conductivity and heat capacity; electrical properties including resistivity and conductivity; and optical properties such as transparency and reflectivity. Density, melting point, and [[concepts/phase|phase]] state are also classified as physical properties. These characteristics are essential for engineering [[concepts/software|applications]], as they determine whether a material is suitable for specific uses—for example, whether steel is appropriate for structural applications or whether copper is suitable for electrical wiring.

## Structure-Property Relationships

The relationship between a material's atomic structure and its observable properties is central to materials [[concepts/science|science]]. The arrangement of atoms, the types of chemical bonds present, crystal structure, and the presence of defects all directly [[concepts/power|influence]] how a material [[entities/will|will]] behave. Understanding these [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] allows scientists and engineers to [[concepts/design|design]] materials with specific desired properties or to predict how a material will perform in particular applications. This is especially important in fields like [[concepts/nanotechnology|nanotechnology]], where materials may exhibit novel properties due to their extremely small scale.
