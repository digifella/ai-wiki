# Leadership Theories

[[concepts/framework-for-understanding|Framework for understanding]] how individuals influence groups to achieve common goals. Major paradigms include Trait [[concepts/theory|Theory]], Behavioral Theory, Contingency Theory, and Transformational [[concepts/leadership|Leadership]].

## Core Concepts

- **Trait Theory**: Focuses on innate qualities of leaders (e.g., intelligence, self-confidence). Criticized for ignoring situational factors.
- **Behavioral Theory**: Shifts focus from "who leaders are" to "what leaders do." Key studies: Ohio State (Initiating Structure vs. Consideration) and University of Michigan (Task vs. Relationship-oriented).
- **Contingency Theory**: Leadership effectiveness depends on the match between [[concepts/style|style]] and situation. Includes Fiedler's Contingency Model and Hersey-Blanchard Situational Leadership.
- **Transformational Leadership**: Leaders inspire followers to exceed expectations through [[concepts/computer-vision|vision]], charisma, and intellectual stimulation. Contrasted with Transactional Leadership.
- **[[concepts/servant-leadership|Servant Leadership]]**: Leader’s primary role is to serve others. Emphasizes [[concepts/empathy|empathy]], listening, and stewardship.

- **[[concepts/team-effectiveness|Team Effectiveness]] & Servant Leadership**: Evidence suggests servant leadership positively correlates with [[concepts/team-performance|team performance]] by fostering [[concepts/trust|trust]], [[concepts/psychological-safety|psychological safety]], and [[concepts/cohesion|cohesion]].
