# Hackman

**J. [[concepts/feynman|Richard]] Hackman** is a prominent organizational psychologist known for his research on [[concepts/team-effectiveness|team effectiveness]]. His work contrasts with traditional leadership-centric models by emphasizing that team [[concepts/success|success]] is primarily determined by the structural design of the team rather than the [[concepts/leadership|leadership]] [[concepts/style|style]] alone.

## Core Framework

Hackman identifies five sufficient conditions for team effectiveness:
1. **Real Team**: The group must be a legitimate, bounded entity with a stable [[concepts/writing|composition]].
2. **Compelling Direction**: The team must have a clear, challenging goal that members are committed to achieving.
3. **Enabling Structure**: The team design (composition, work processes, norms) must support performance.
4. **Supportive Context**: The broader organizational environment must provide necessary resources, training, and evaluation systems.
5. **Expert Coaching**: Access to timely, relevant support to help the team overcome process pitfalls.
