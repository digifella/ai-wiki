# EU AI Act

The EU AI Act is the [[concepts/european-union|European Union]]'s comprehensive [[concepts/regulation|regulatory framework]] governing the development, deployment, and use of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems. Enacted to address growing concerns about [[concepts/ai-safety|AI safety]], [[concepts/opacity|transparency]], and [[concepts/accountability|accountability]], the legislation establishes a risk-based classification system that categorizes [[concepts/ai-powered-applications|AI applications]] according to their potential impact on individuals and society.

## Risk-Based Classification

The Act divides [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] into four risk tiers: prohibited, high-risk, limited-risk, and minimal-risk. Prohibited AI systems include those designed to manipulate behavior or exploit vulnerabilities. High-risk applications—such as those used in hiring, law enforcement, or critical infrastructure—face stringent requirements including impact assessments, documentation, and human oversight. Limited-risk systems require transparency disclosures, while minimal-risk applications face few restrictions.

## Key Requirements and Compliance

Organizations deploying high-risk AI systems must maintain detailed [[concepts/technical-documentation|technical documentation]], implement quality management systems, and conduct conformity assessments before market deployment. The framework also requires human oversight [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] and establishes obligations for [[concepts/data-integrity|data quality]], bias monitoring, and post-market surveillance. [[concepts/compliance|Compliance]] timelines vary by risk category, with most requirements phasing in over several years following the Act's entry into force.

## Global Implications

As the first major legal framework of its kind, the EU AI Act influences [[concepts/ai-governance|AI governance]] discussions globally and creates compliance obligations for any organization offering AI systems in the European market. The legislation reflects a precautionary approach to AI regulation, prioritizing [[concepts/risk-mitigation|risk mitigation]] and fundamental rights [[concepts/secure|protection]] alongside [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] considerations.
