# Selective Reveal

Selective Reveal is a [[concepts/photoshop-technique|Photoshop technique]] for combining the best elements from multiple similar photographs to create a single improved final image. The method is particularly useful in situations where it is difficult or impossible to capture a perfect shot in a single take, such as group photography where it is common for [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] least one person to have their eyes closed or an unflattering expression.

## Method

The technique involves layering multiple photos and selectively revealing portions of each layer to combine their strongest elements. By using [[concepts/layer-masks|layer masks]] and careful blending, a photographer can take the open eyes from one shot, a better smile from another, and any other improved details from additional frames, assembling them into a composite that looks natural and cohesive.

## Application and Benefits

Selective Reveal is especially valuable in group photography, wedding photography, and situations where getting everyone to look their best simultaneously is challenging. Rather than discarding photos because of minor flaws, the technique allows photographers to salvage and improve shots by drawing the best moments from multiple takes. This approach requires no specialized equipment beyond [[concepts/photoshop|Photoshop]] and can significantly increase the usable rate of photos from a [[concepts/session|session]].
