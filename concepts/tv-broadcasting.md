# TV Broadcasting

TV broadcasting refers to the transmission of television and film content to audiences through various distribution channels and platforms. Historically, broadcasting was centered on over-the-air transmission via radio frequencies, with content transmitted from centralized stations to household receivers equipped with antennas. This model dominated from the mid-20th century through the late 1990s and established the foundational infrastructure and regulatory frameworks that continue to shape the industry today.

## Traditional Broadcasting

Traditional broadcast television operated under a centralized model where networks controlled content scheduling and distribution. Viewers received programming on a fixed schedule determined by broadcasters, with advertising serving as the primary revenue model. Regulatory bodies in different countries—such as the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) in the [[entities/united-states|United States]]—established standards for technical transmission, content guidelines, and [[concepts/licensing|licensing]] requirements.

## Modern Distribution

The digital era fundamentally altered TV broadcasting through the [[concepts/emergent-behavior|emergence]] of cable, satellite, and internet-based platforms. Cable and satellite services expanded channel availability and introduced [[concepts/subscription-models|subscription models]] alongside [[concepts/ad-revenue|advertising revenue]]. More recently, [[concepts/streaming-services|streaming services]] have decentralized distribution, allowing viewers to access content on-demand rather than following broadcast schedules. Many traditional broadcasters now operate hybrid models combining linear television with [[concepts/movie-streaming|streaming platforms]] to reach diverse audiences across multiple devices.

## Current Landscape

Contemporary TV broadcasting encompasses both traditional networks and digital streaming providers, with audiences increasingly fragmenting across platforms. Content distribution now occurs simultaneously through multiple channels—live broadcasts, video-on-demand services, and social media platforms. The industry continues to adapt its business models and technical standards to accommodate changing viewing [[concepts/habits|habits]] and emerging technologies.
