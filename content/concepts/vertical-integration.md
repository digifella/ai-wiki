# Vertical Integration

Vertical [[concepts/integration|integration]] in semiconductor manufacturing refers to a single company controlling multiple stages of production, from chip [[concepts/design|design]] and fabrication to final assembly and [[concepts/deployment|deployment]]. This approach contrasts with the industry norm of specialized, separate companies handling different production phases.

## Tesla-SpaceX Terafab Context

The [[concepts/terafab|Tesla-SpaceX Terafab]] represents an effort to develop 2nm [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] chips through vertical integration. By consolidating design, manufacturing, and application within a unified corporate [[concepts/structure|structure]], the strategy aims to optimize production efficiency and reduce dependencies on external suppliers. This model allows for tighter coordination between chip specifications and end-use requirements in AI systems.

## Strategic Advantages and Considerations

Vertical integration in chip manufacturing can reduce costs and accelerate development cycles by eliminating intermediaries and improving communication between design and production teams. However, it requires substantial capital investment in fabrication facilities and expertise across multiple technical domains. The approach also concentrates operational risk, as disruptions [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] any production stage can impact the entire pipeline.
