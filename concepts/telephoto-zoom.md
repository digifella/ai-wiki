# Telephoto Zoom

Telephoto Zoom is a photographic technique that combines telephoto lenses with panoramic image stitching to create extended wide-angle compositions. Rather than relying on a camera's built-in panorama mode, the method involves capturing multiple overlapping frames using a telephoto lens, then digitally stitching the images together into a single panoramic photograph. This approach circumvents the limitations of standard smartphone panorama functions and produces results with greater detail and tonal consistency.

## Technical Process

The technique requires capturing a series of photographs with significant overlap to ensure seamless alignment during post-processing. By using a telephoto lens, the photographer maintains a consistent perspective and minimizes the distortion often associated with wide-angle lenses or the parallax errors common in traditional panoramic sweeps. The resulting composite image offers a higher resolution and more natural depth of field than standard smartphone panoramas, which often struggle with exposure consistency and edge alignment.

## Applications and Advantages

This method is particularly useful in landscape and architectural photography, where capturing vast scenes without sacrificing detail is essential. It allows for greater control over exposure and focus across the entire frame, avoiding the automatic exposure adjustments that can cause visible bands or shifts in brightness in automated panorama modes. Consequently, the final image exhibits superior tonal consistency and sharper details, making it a preferred choice for high-quality creative pursuits where standard wide-angle capabilities are insufficient.
