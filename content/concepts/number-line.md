# Number Line

A number line is a [[concepts/visual-representation|visual representation]] of numbers arranged along a straight, one-dimensional axis, typically with integers marked [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] regular intervals. It serves as a fundamental mathematical tool for understanding numerical order, distance, and [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] between values. Number lines can represent positive numbers, negative numbers, or both, depending on their [[concepts/motivation|purpose]].

## Application to Prime Numbers

Number lines are particularly useful for analyzing the [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] and patterns of [[concepts/prime-numbers|prime numbers]]. By plotting primes on a linear coordinate system, mathematicians can observe clustering patterns, gaps between consecutive primes, and other structural properties that might not be immediately apparent from numerical lists alone. This visualization helps in studying questions related to prime distribution, such as those connected to the [[concepts/riemann-hypothesis|Riemann Hypothesis]].

## Related Concepts

The study of [[concepts/prime-lens|prime]] distributions on number lines has led to investigations of what are sometimes called "[[concepts/awkward-primes|awkward primes]]"—primes with unusual spacing or positional properties relative to their neighbors. Understanding how primes are distributed along the number line remains a central problem in analytic [[concepts/number-theory|number theory]] and continues to generate mathematical research and interest.
