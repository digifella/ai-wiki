# Inventory Simulation

Inventory simulation is a computational methodology used to model and analyze inventory systems by applying algorithmic optimization and iterative refinement techniques. Unlike traditional approaches that rely exclusively on static analytical formulas or predetermined policies, this method utilizes dynamic computational models to evaluate multiple system configurations and decision rules. By simulating various operational conditions, it allows for a more nuanced understanding of how inventory levels fluctuate in response to stochastic demand and supply chain variability.

The core mechanism involves the iterative application of learning algorithms to refine decision parameters over time. This process enables the system to adapt to changing variables, such as lead times and demand patterns, which are often difficult to capture with closed-form mathematical solutions. The simulation runs through numerous iterations, comparing performance metrics across different scenarios to identify optimal stock levels and reorder points.

Key metrics used in this domain typically include total cost, service levels, and stockout frequency. These indicators are tracked throughout the simulation to assess the robustness of the inventory policy under uncertainty. The output provides a probabilistic view of system behavior rather than a single deterministic outcome, allowing researchers and practitioners to quantify risk and make more informed strategic decisions regarding supply chain resilience.
