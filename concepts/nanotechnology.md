# Nanotechnology

Nanotechnology is the engineering and manipulation of matter at the nanoscale, typically involving structures and devices between 1 and 100 nanometers in size. At this scale, materials exhibit different physical and chemical properties compared to their bulk counterparts due to increased surface area and quantum effects. These differences enable novel applications that would be impossible using conventional manufacturing approaches.

## Properties and Behavior

The distinctive behavior of nanoscale materials arises from the transition between bulk material properties and atomic-level interactions. As dimensions decrease, the ratio of surface atoms to interior atoms increases significantly, leading to enhanced reactivity and catalytic activity. Additionally, quantum confinement effects become prominent, altering electronic, optical, and magnetic properties. For instance, gold nanoparticles appear red or purple rather than yellow, and silicon becomes luminescent at the nanoscale. These unique characteristics allow for precise control over material functionality, enabling innovations in medicine, electronics, and materials science.

## Applications and Manufacturing

Nanotechnology employs various fabrication strategies, including top-down approaches like lithography and bottom-up methods such as self-assembly. Top-down techniques involve carving bulk materials into nanoscale structures, commonly used in semiconductor manufacturing for creating transistors and integrated circuits. Bottom-up approaches build structures atom by atom or molecule by molecule, often utilizing chemical synthesis or biological processes. These methods facilitate the creation of complex nanostructures with high precision, supporting advancements in fields ranging from targeted drug delivery systems to high-capacity energy storage devices.
