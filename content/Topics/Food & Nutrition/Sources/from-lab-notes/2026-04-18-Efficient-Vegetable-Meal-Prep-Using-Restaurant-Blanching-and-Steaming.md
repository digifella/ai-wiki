## Efficient Vegetable Meal Prep Using Restaurant Blanching and Steaming
**Clip title:** For Fast and Easy Meal Prep, Use This Restaurant Trick | Techniquely with Lan Lam
**Author / channel:** [[entities/america|America]]'s Test Kitchen
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ltnonDL_RkA

### [[concepts/summary|Summary]]
The video demonstrates restaurant techniques for efficiently preparing flavorful and perfectly textured vegetable side dishes. The main idea is that simple methods like blanching, a no-cook shortcut, and pan-steaming allow home cooks to achieve chef-quality vegetables quickly, whether for immediate consumption or as part of meal [[concepts/preparation|preparation]].

The first technique discussed is blanching, a method commonly used in professional kitchens. This involves quickly cooking trimmed and peeled vegetables in boiling water until they are just shy of being cooked through, then rapidly cooling them in an ice bath to stop the cooking process. Blanching concentrates the vegetables' flavor by reducing water content and partially breaks down cell walls, leading to a crisp-tender texture. Alternatively, for certain raw vegetables like cabbage, a "no-cook shortcut" can be employed: massaging the chopped vegetable with kosher salt. This process extracts moisture and tenderizes the cabbage, creating a texture similar to blanched vegetables, after which the excess salt is rinsed off. Both methods result in pre-prepped vegetables ready for quick reheating or dressing.

Another highly adaptable method is pan-steaming. This involves placing chopped vegetables (often sorted by size for even cooking) in a skillet with a small amount of water, covering it, and cooking until the water evaporates. This steaming action efficiently cooks the vegetables, allowing them to become tender while retaining their vibrant color. Once the water is gone, seasonings like butter, herbs, and lemon zest are added for flavor. The pan-steaming technique is versatile; the amount of water can be adjusted based on the vegetable's natural moisture or if it was pre-blanched, making it excellent for reheating prepped vegetables or cooking fresh ones in minutes.

For those engaging in meal preparation, blanching vegetables in bulk is presented as an efficient strategy. To avoid flavor transfer and maintain quality, it's recommended to blanch vegetables sequentially, starting with the palest and most mildly flavored, then moving to stronger, darker varieties. Using a generous amount of salted water helps maintain a rolling boil, speeding up the process and preventing over-salting. Once blanched and cooled, these vegetables can be stored and quickly reheated using the pan-steaming method, providing a consistent supply of delicious, perfectly cooked sides for various meals throughout the week. These techniques empower home cooks to elevate their vegetable dishes with minimal fuss and maximum flavor.
