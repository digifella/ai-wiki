# Black Death

The **Black Death** was a devastating global epidemic of bubonic plague that struck Eurasia and North Africa during the 14th and 16th centuries, considered one of the deadliest pandemics in human history. It resulted in the deaths of an estimated 75–200 million people in Eurasia alone, peaking between 1346 and 1353.

## Etiology and Pathogen
The primary causative [[entities/agent|agent]] was *[[concepts/bacterium-yersinia-pestis|Yersinia pestis]]*, a bacterium transmitted primarily by fleas infesting black rats (*Rattus rattus*). Transmission occurred through:
- Flea bites (bubonic form)
- Respiratory droplets (pneumonic form)
- Direct [[entities/contact|contact]] with infected bodily fluids (septicemic form)

## Origins and Spread
Originating in Central Asia (likely near the Tian Shan mountains), the disease spread along the Silk Road trade routes to the Black Sea coast, where Genoese ships carried it to [[entities/europe|Europe]] in 1347. Key vectors included:
- [[Trade Routes/Silk Road]]
- Maritime trade networks (Mediterranean ports)
- Urban density and poor sanitation in medieval cities

## Historical Impact
- **Demographic Collapse:** Reduced Europe's population by 30–60%, triggering labor shortages that disrupted the Feudalism system.
- **Socioeconomic Shifts:** Peasant revolts (e.g., the Peasants' Revolt of 1381) and rising wages due to labor [[concepts/limited-resources|scarcity]].
- **Cultural/Religious Response:** Rise in persecution of minorities (Jews, lepers) and the flagellant [[concepts/exercise|movement]]; shift in artistic themes toward *Danse Macabre*.
- **Scientific Legacy:** Early attempts [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] quarantine (Venice, 1377) and the eventual understanding of contagion.

## Theories on Trigger and Amplification
Traditional historiography emphasizes trade networks and rat populations as primary drivers. Recent research explores climatic and environmental catalysts:
- [[lab-notes/2026-05-19-2022-Study-Volcanic-Eruptions-Triggering-the-Black-Death|2022 Study: Volcanic Eruptions Triggering the-Black-Death]]: A 2022 study suggests [[concepts/volcanic-eruptions|volcanic eruptions]] preceding the pandemic may have triggered ecological changes (cooling, crop failure, rodent migration) that facilitated the spread of *Y. pestis*.
- Climate anomalies during the Medieval Warm Period transition may have destabilized ecosystems, increasing host-vector contact.
