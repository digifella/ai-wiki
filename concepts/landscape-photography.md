# Landscape Photography

Landscape photography is the practice of capturing expansive natural or built environments with emphasis on wide vistas, spatial depth, and environmental context. The discipline encompasses diverse subjects including mountains, coastlines, forests, deserts, and urban skylines. Photographers working in this genre employ composition techniques specifically designed to convey scale, atmospheric conditions, and the spatial relationships between foreground, middle ground, and background elements.

Traditional approaches rely on single exposures captured with wide-angle lenses to maximize the field of view within the camera's sensor limits. However, this method often struggles to capture the full dynamic range and resolution required for large-format prints or immersive digital displays. To overcome these physical constraints, practitioners increasingly utilize panoramic stitching techniques that combine multiple overlapping images into a single high-resolution composite.

Panoramic stitching allows for the capture of vistas that exceed the capabilities of standard smartphone panorama apps or single-frame sensors. By taking a series of individual shots with a standard lens and aligning them during post-processing, photographers can achieve significantly higher detail and greater control over exposure across the entire scene. This method preserves the integrity of individual exposures while creating a seamless, ultra-wide image that maintains sharpness and color accuracy across the entire horizon.

The technique requires precise camera movement, often using a tripod with a panoramic head to ensure consistent nodal points and prevent parallax errors. Software algorithms then stitch the images together, correcting for lens distortion and blending exposure differences. This approach is particularly valuable for capturing complex lighting conditions, such as sunrises or starry nights, where a single exposure would fail to capture both the bright highlights and dark shadows effectively.
