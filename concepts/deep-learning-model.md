# Deep Learning Model

A [[concepts/vanishing-gradient-problem|deep learning]] model is a type of [[concepts/neural-network|artificial neural network]] characterized by multiple layers of [[concepts/living-files|interconnected nodes]] that process data through successive transformations. Unlike shallow [[concepts/machine-learning|machine learning]] approaches, which often rely on manual feature extraction, deep learning architectures automatically learn hierarchical representations from raw input. Each layer in the network identifies increasingly abstract features, allowing the system to capture complex patterns and [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] within [[entities/big-data|large datasets]].

The architecture typically consists of an input layer, numerous hidden layers, and an output layer. In modern systems, these networks may contain dozens or even hundreds of layers, enabling them to model highly non-linear functions. The process involves propagating data forward through the network, where each [[entities/nodejs|node]] applies [[concepts/parameters|weights]] and [[concepts/biases|biases]] to its inputs, followed by [[concepts/activation-functions|activation functions]] that introduce non-linearity. This structure allows the model to build a layered understanding of the data, moving from simple edges and textures in early layers to complex objects or concepts in deeper layers.

Training these models relies on [[concepts/backpropagation|backpropagation]] and [[concepts/constrained-optimization|optimization algorithms]] to adjust the internal weights based on the error between predicted and actual outputs. This [[concepts/iterative-refinement|iterative process]] minimizes a loss function, gradually refining the model's ability to generalize from [[concepts/custom-dataset|training data]] to unseen examples. While computationally intensive, this approach has become foundational in various [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] applications, including [[concepts/computer-vision|computer vision]], [[concepts/language-processing|natural language processing]], and [[concepts/speech-recognition|speech recognition]], due to its superior performance in handling high-dimensional data.
