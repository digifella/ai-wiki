# Demography

Demography is the scientific study of human populations, focusing on their size, structure, and dynamics. It examines how populations change over time through the analysis of quantitative data on births, deaths, migration, and aging. By tracking these fundamental processes, demographers provide the statistical frameworks necessary to understand societal evolution and project future population trends.

## Key Components

The discipline relies on several key measurements to assess population health and stability. Birth rates and fertility patterns describe reproductive behavior across different groups, while mortality rates indicate the frequency of death within a population. Migration statistics track the movement of people across geographic boundaries, influencing both local and global population distributions. Age distribution, often visualized through population pyramids, reveals the proportion of dependents to working-age individuals, which is critical for planning healthcare, education, and pension systems.

## Applications in Health and Wellbeing

In the context of health and wellbeing, demography provides essential insights into disease prevalence, life expectancy, and healthcare resource allocation. Understanding demographic shifts allows public health officials to anticipate the needs of aging populations or growing youth cohorts. These data inform policy decisions regarding infectious disease control, maternal health initiatives, and the management of chronic conditions that vary by age and socioeconomic status. Consequently, demography serves as a foundational tool for evidence-based public health planning and intervention.
