# Color Masking

Color [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] is a selective editing technique that allows photographers and digital artists to apply color [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] to specific areas of an image while leaving other areas unaffected. Rather than adjusting an entire photograph uniformly, color masking creates boundaries that isolate particular [[concepts/color-ranges|color ranges]], tonal zones, or spatial regions, enabling precise [[concepts/power|control]] over how edits are applied.

## In Lightroom Classic

[[entities/lightroom-classic|Lightroom Classic]]'s AI-powered color [[concepts/masking-tools|masking tools]] streamline this process by automatically detecting and selecting colors based on user-defined [[concepts/parameters|parameters]]. Users can isolate adjustments to specific hues—such as correcting only the blue sky or enhancing [[concepts/skin-tones|skin tones]]—without manually drawing selections. The masking engine analyzes pixel values and creates intelligent boundaries, reducing the need for time-consuming manual selection work while maintaining natural-looking results with soft edge transitions.

## Applications

Color masking is commonly used for tasks such as correcting [[concepts/color-casts|color casts]] in specific image regions, adjusting saturation or luminance for particular colors, and creating localized [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] corrections. It proves especially useful in [[concepts/portrait-photography|portrait photography]] for skin [[concepts/tone|tone]] refinement, [[concepts/landscape-photography|landscape photography]] for selective sky or foliage enhancement, and product photography where different colored objects require different treatment.
