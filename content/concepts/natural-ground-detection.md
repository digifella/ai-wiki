# Natural Ground Detection

Natural Ground Detection is a post-processing technique used in Adobe Lightroom and [[concepts/camera-raw|Camera Raw]] workflows to identify and isolate ground elements within landscape photographs. The method leverages automated [[concepts/ai-powered-subject-selection|AI-powered masking]] tools designed specifically for [[concepts/landscape-photography|landscape photography]], allowing photographers to selectively adjust or enhance soil, dirt, grass, and other terrestrial surfaces independently from sky, vegetation, or other image components.

## Application in Workflow

The technique typically involves applying landscape-specific mask presets within Lightroom Classic or Camera Raw, which use [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] to recognize ground areas based on visual characteristics such as [[concepts/texture|texture]], color, and spatial positioning. Once isolated, these masked areas can be edited separately—adjusting [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], saturation, or [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]] without affecting other image elements. This selective editing approach helps maintain natural appearance while improving specific ground-related details.

## Connection to Image Sharpness

Natural Ground Detection intersects with landscape sharpness techniques, as isolated ground masks enable targeted sharpening that emphasizes soil texture and detail while preserving the appearance of other photograph elements. This targeted approach supports the broader goal of achieving tack-sharp landscape [[concepts/images|images]] across all focal planes and surface types.
