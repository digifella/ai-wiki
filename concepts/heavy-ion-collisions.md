# Heavy Ion Collisions

Heavy ion collisions are [[concepts/particle-physics-experiments|high-energy physics experiments]] in which heavy [[concepts/atomic-nuclei|atomic nuclei]] are accelerated to near-relativistic speeds and directed to collide head-on. These events take place at specialized particle accelerator facilities, most notably the [[concepts/large-hadron-collider|Large Hadron Collider]] (LHC) at [[entities/cern|CERN]] and the [[entities/rhic|Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider]] (RHIC) at [[entities/brookhaven-lab|Brookhaven National Laboratory]]. The experiments typically involve colliding nuclei of heavy elements such as lead or [[concepts/major-gold-deposit|gold]], with collision energies reaching several trillion electron volts.

## Scientific Goals

The primary [[concepts/purpose|objective]] of heavy ion collisions is to recreate the conditions of the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]], specifically the quark-gluon plasma (QGP). This state of matter existed microseconds after the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]], where quarks and gluons were not confined within hadrons. By analyzing the debris from these high-energy impacts, physicists study the strong force interactions and the [[concepts/phase|phase]] transitions of quantum chromodynamics (QCD).

## Experimental Significance

These collisions allow researchers to investigate fundamental [[concepts/quantum-phenomena|quantum phenomena]] and the properties of nuclear matter under extreme temperature and [[concepts/density|density]]. The data collected helps refine theoretical models of the strong interaction and provides insights into the behavior of [[concepts/vacuum-fluctuations|virtual particles]] and fundamental forces. Understanding these dynamics contributes to the broader framework of [[concepts/granular-physics|particle physics]] and the [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|standard model]] of [[concepts/cosmology|cosmology]].
