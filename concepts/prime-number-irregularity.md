# Prime Number Irregularity

Prime Number Irregularity is a geometric property observed when prime numbers are plotted as coordinate pairs on a two-dimensional plane. Rather than examining primes through traditional number-theoretic methods, this approach visualizes their distribution spatially, revealing patterns that emerge from their geometric arrangement. The concept explores how efficiently straight lines can cover or connect points representing prime coordinates, with particular focus on primes that resist alignment with existing linear patterns.

## Geometric Analysis and Line Coverage

When primes are plotted as (n, p_n) coordinates—where n is the index and p_n is the nth prime—or through other coordinate systems, certain primes align along or near straight lines while others deviate significantly. "Irregular" primes are those requiring minimal additional line coverage to represent the full set, meaning they avoid clustering around dominant linear trends. This geometric irregularity provides insight into the structural distribution of primes that complements analytical approaches.

## Popularization and Research

The concept gained wider recognition through Neil Sloane's discussion in a Numberphile video, bringing attention to this geometric perspective on prime structure. Sloane's work in integer sequences and combinatorial mathematics has frequently intersected with visual and geometric approaches to understanding fundamental mathematical objects. This popularization demonstrates how alternative representations of well-studied objects like primes can still reveal novel structural properties worthy of investigation.
