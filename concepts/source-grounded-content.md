# Source Grounded Content

Source-grounded content is material that derives directly from and remains verifiable through explicitly cited references. This approach anchors all claims, data, and arguments to identifiable sources rather than relying on speculation, synthesis without attribution, or unsubstantiated claims. By maintaining a transparent [[concepts/connection|connection]] between assertions and their origins, source-grounded content establishes credibility and allows readers to independently verify information.

## Core Characteristics

Source-grounded content requires several practices: clear identification of sources, accurate representation of what those sources actually say, and distinction between original claims and borrowed material. This differs from paraphrasing without attribution or presenting synthesized information without indicating where individual components originated. The approach is foundational to [[entities/tomasz-janowski|academic]] [[concepts/writing|writing]], journalism, and research where establishing [[concepts/trust|trust]] depends on readers being able to trace claims back to their origins.

## Disciplinary Applications

The practice is particularly valuable in research, journalism, and educational contexts. Academic researchers use [[concepts/citation-grounding|source grounding]] to build arguments within established scholarly conversations. Journalists rely on it to substantiate reporting and protect both credibility and legal standing. In educational settings, it teaches students to engage critically with information and understand how knowledge is constructed and validated within different fields.

Source-grounded content does not eliminate interpretation or argument—it simply requires that the factual foundations supporting those positions be made explicit and traceable. This [[concepts/opacity|transparency]] allows readers to evaluate both the quality of sources used and the validity of conclusions drawn from them.
