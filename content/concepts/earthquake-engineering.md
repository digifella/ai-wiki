# Earthquake Engineering

Earthquake engineering is an applied discipline that combines structural engineering, geotechnical engineering, and seismology to design, construct, and retrofit buildings and infrastructure capable of surviving [[concepts/earthquakes|seismic events]]. Engineers working in this field must account for complex ground motions, varying soil conditions, and the unpredictable [[entities/nature|nature]] of earthquake magnitude and frequency. The goal is to minimize loss of life, reduce economic damage, and maintain the functionality of critical infrastructure during and after seismic activity.

## Core Design Principles

Modern earthquake engineering employs several fundamental strategies to manage seismic forces. Structures are designed to distribute and absorb energy through mechanisms such as flexible foundations, damping systems, and reinforced materials. [[concepts/earthquake-bearings|Base isolation systems]], which decouple buildings from ground motion through bearings or other devices, represent one significant approach. Engineers also consider factors like soil liquefaction, [[concepts/slope-stability|slope stability]], and [[concepts/tsunami|tsunami]] generation when assessing site-specific hazards.

## Applications and Infrastructure

The discipline extends across diverse infrastructure types, from residential and commercial buildings to bridges, dams, hospitals, and utilities. Critical infrastructure facilities, such as power plants and emergency response centers, receive particular [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] due to their importance in post-earthquake recovery. Building codes and seismic [[concepts/open-standards|standards]], which vary by region according to local seismic hazard assessment, guide engineering practice and are regularly updated as knowledge advances through post-earthquake investigations and laboratory research.
