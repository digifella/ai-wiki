## Eric Kim's Perfect Dak Bulgogi: Quick Korean BBQ Chicken Recipe
**Clip title:** My Korean Mom Said This Dak Bulgogi Recipe Is Perfect | Eric Kim | NYT Cooking
**Author / channel:** NYT Cooking
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7k8Yf1UEJOs

### Summary
In this New York Times Cooking video, food columnist [[entities/eric-kim|Eric Kim]] shares his "mom-approved" recipe for [[concepts/dak-bulgogi|Dak Bulgogi]], a [[concepts/korean-bbq|Korean BBQ]] chicken dish. He describes it as a juicy, succulent, and gorgeously browned chicken that makes for a perfect [[concepts/weeknight-dinner|weeknight dinner]], recalling how his own mother's enthusiastic approval solidified the recipe. The video focuses on creating deeply flavorful chicken quickly, emphasizing a balance of sweet, savory, and fresh elements that come together in about 30 minutes.

The heart of Eric's Dak Bulgogi lies in its vibrant marinade. Key ingredients include scallions, ginger, and garlic – which are roughly chopped or smashed to gently infuse their flavors. To this, he adds [apple juice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_juice) for subtle sweetness and a beautiful caramel color, soy sauce for essential saltiness and [umami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umami), maple syrup as a perfect sweetener, and a touch of [sesame oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame_oil) for nutty richness. A unique addition is [soju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soju) (or sake), which Eric explains helps to tenderize the chicken and enhances the perception of other flavors through aroma. All these ingredients are combined with bite-sized pieces of boneless [chicken thighs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_thighs) in a Ziploc bag, ensuring maximum [[entities/contact|contact]] and flavor penetration, even with a short marinating time of just 10 minutes.

For [[concepts/preparation|preparation]], Eric demonstrates cutting the chicken thighs against the grain to ensure tenderness, trimming some but not all of the fat for added flavor. The chicken is then pan-fried in batches on a non-stick skillet over high heat with a bit of olive or canola oil. The crucial technique involves allowing the chicken to sear undisturbed for about a minute on the first side to achieve significant browning and a delicious crust, before [stir-frying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stir-frying) and continuing to cook until done (approximately 3-6 minutes total per batch). He stresses the importance of cleaning the pan between batches to prevent burnt [[concepts/classical-bits|bits]] from accumulating and ensures each piece is seasoned with salt and freshly cracked black pepper during the cooking process.

Finally, Eric plates the beautifully browned chicken alongside fresh lettuce and steamed rice. He highlights the non-optional step of a generous squeeze of fresh lemon juice over the cooked chicken, which he intentionally adds at the end for its bright, fresh flavor. For an enhanced eating experience, he suggests drizzling the lettuce with a tiny bit of sesame oil and a sprinkle of salt. Eric emphasizes that, much like the [banchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banchan) and condiments accompanying traditional Korean [[concepts/bbq|BBQ]], these small "extras" contribute significantly to the perfect final bite. He concludes by noting that this savory, nuanced dish represents a different, yet equally authentic, facet of Korean cuisine that often gets overlooked in favor of spicier red preparations.
