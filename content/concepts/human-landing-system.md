# Human Landing System

The Human Landing System (HLS) is a critical component of [[concepts/nasa|NASA]]'s [[concepts/artemis-program|Artemis program]], designed to transport astronauts from lunar orbit to the Moon's surface and back. As part of the broader Artemis [[concepts/architecture|architecture]], the HLS works in conjunction with the Space Launch System (SLS) and the [[entities/orion|Orion spacecraft]] to enable crewed lunar missions beyond the initial [[entities/artemis-ii|Artemis II]] flyby mission.

## Artemis 3 Development Challenges

Multiple technical and programmatic challenges affect the readiness of both the HLS and SLS for Artemis 3, currently targeted for the late 2020s. Development of the lander system has involved iterative [[concepts/design|design]] refinements and [[concepts/testing|testing]] protocols to ensure crew safety and mission [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]]. These challenges extend across propulsion systems, thermal management, landing guidance, and ascent stage operations, with each subsystem requiring extensive validation before crewed flight.

## Program Timeline and Status

The convergence of HLS and SLS development schedules presents ongoing constraints on mission readiness. While design maturation continues, program officials have assessed various [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]] for maintaining momentum toward lunar surface operations. The realistic availability of both systems for integrated Artemis 3 operations depends on sustained technical progress and resource allocation across multiple contractor teams and NASA centers.
