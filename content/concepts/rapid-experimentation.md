# Rapid Experimentation

Rapid experimentation refers to a methodological approach in AI and [[concepts/coding|software development]] that leverages [[concepts/automated-feedback-loops|automated feedback]] [[concepts/loops|loops]] to accelerate [[entities/agent|agent]] or system improvement. Rather than relying on manual [[concepts/testing|testing]] and human-directed [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] cycles, this approach employs continuous [[concepts/automated-software-testing|automated testing]] [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] high frequency, enabling [[concepts/agents|agents]] to explore [[concepts/solution|solution]] spaces and refine their behavior with minimal human intervention between cycles.

The core mechanism involves establishing closed-[[concepts/loop|loop]] systems where agents execute tasks, performance is measured against [[concepts/defined-metrics|defined metrics]], and results feed directly into the next iteration of optimization. This [[concepts/automation|automation]] removes traditional bottlenecks associated with human review and [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]], allowing for orders of magnitude more experiments.

- [[lab-notes/2026-05-17-Anthropic-Engineers-Claude-Prompting-Skills-Based-AI-Int|Anthropic Engineers' Claude Prompting: Skills-Based AI Interaction Principles]]: [[entities/anthropic-institute|Anthropic]] engineers demonstrate a shift from ad-hoc, one-off prompts to structured "[[concepts/skills|skills]]," encapsulating interaction patterns to enable more stable and repeatable optimization cycles.
- Skills-based [[concepts/prompting|prompting]] reduces manual intervention by treating prompt definitions as reusable system components, aligning with the goal of automated iteration and minimizing human bottlenecks in the [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] loop.
- This methodology supports [[concepts/continuous-learning|continuous learning]] by allowing agents to leverage modular [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] that evolve independently, facilitating higher-frequency experimentation without destabilizing core behaviors.
