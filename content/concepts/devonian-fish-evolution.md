# Devonian Fish Evolution

The [[concepts/devonian-period|Devonian Period]] (approximately 419 to 359 million years ago) represents a critical [[concepts/phase|phase]] in vertebrate evolution, often referred to as the "Age of Fishes." During this time, fish diversified into numerous forms and occupied a wide [[concepts/range|range]] of aquatic ecological niches. The period witnessed the radiation of jawed fish (gnathostomes), including early sharks, armored fish (placoderms), and the ancestors of modern bony fish. This diversification established the fundamental body plans and ecological roles that fish would maintain through subsequent geological periods.

## Major Fish Groups

Several distinct fish groups flourished during the Devonian. Placoderms, characterized by bony armor plating, dominated early Devonian ecosystems before declining toward the period's end. [[concepts/cartilaginous-fish|Cartilaginous fish]], including primitive sharks and relatives, became increasingly abundant and diversified. Sarcopterygians (lobe-finned fish) evolved during this period and would eventually give rise to tetrapods—the four-limbed vertebrates that colonized terrestrial environments. Ray-finned fish (Actinopterygii) also became more diverse, establishing the foundation for modern fish diversity.

## Evolutionary Significance

The Devonian fish fauna demonstrated increasing complexity in skeletal [[concepts/structure|structure]], feeding mechanisms, and body morphology. Many species developed specialized teeth and jaws adapted to different dietary niches. The period's end marked the Late Devonian extinction events, which significantly reduced fish diversity but did not eliminate the major lineages that would continue to evolve in subsequent periods. The anatomical innovations that emerged during Devonian fish evolution directly influenced the trajectory of vertebrate evolution on land.
