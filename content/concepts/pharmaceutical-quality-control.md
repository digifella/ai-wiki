# Pharmaceutical Quality Control

Pharmaceutical [[concepts/quality-control|quality control]] encompasses the systematic [[concepts/testing|testing]] and [[concepts/verification|verification]] processes that ensure drug products meet established [[concepts/open-standards|standards]] for safety, efficacy, and [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]]. These [[concepts/quality-assurance|quality assurance]] mechanisms are critical throughout manufacturing, as variations in drug formulation, purity, and stability can significantly impact therapeutic outcomes. The discipline combines chemistry, biology, and [[concepts/chemical-engineering|process engineering]] to identify and prevent defects before drugs reach patients.

## The Ritonavir Polymorph Crisis

The Ritonavir case illustrates a fundamental quality control challenge: polymorphism, the phenomenon where a single chemical compound can exist in multiple crystalline forms with different [[concepts/material-properties|physical properties]]. In the mid-1990s, [[entities/abbott-laboratories|Abbott Laboratories]] discovered that Ritonavir, an HIV protease inhibitor, had spontaneously converted from its original Form I polymorph to a more thermodynamically stable Form II during manufacturing and [[entities/storage|storage]]. Form II exhibited significantly lower bioavailability, rendering tablets ineffective [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] their standard dose. This discovery forced a temporary market withdrawal and complete reformulation of the drug, highlighting how inadequate characterization of crystalline structures during drug development can have severe consequences.

## Implications for Quality Assurance

The Ritonavir crisis fundamentally changed pharmaceutical quality control practices by emphasizing the need for comprehensive polymorph screening during early-stage drug development and the [[concepts/adoption|implementation]] of robust stability testing protocols. Modern quality control now routinely includes characterization of all possible crystalline forms, identification of conversion pathways, and validation of manufacturing conditions that favor stable forms. This case remains a seminal example in pharmaceutical education of how seemingly minor physical chemistry issues can escalate into major product failures without rigorous quality oversight.
