## AI Can Now Rewrite Neural Networks Directly - This Could Change Machine Learning Forever

### Summary
The video discusses the concept of "Weight-Space Programming," a finding from an MIT research blog highlighting that large language models (LLMs) can natively read, edit, and write neural network weights. Traditionally, neural networks start as a random set of connection strengths that are refined through training. However, the video asserts that LLMs can interpret and manipulate these complex numerical structures directly, without requiring traditional training processes.

The speaker demonstrates this capability by prompting GPT-6 Luna to write the weights for a tic-tac-toe policy network from scratch. By providing specific instructions and constraints, the model successfully generated a playable model file that outperformed baseline performance expectations. Additionally, the video shows how an LLM can analyze an existing model (such as Qwen3.5-0.8B) to identify and correct issues in its weights—like reasoning loops—boosting its math benchmark performance significantly with only minor edits.

The practical applications of this technique include repairing broken AI models, auditing models for hidden behaviors, and creating specialized neural networks without expensive retraining phases. This suggests a potential shift toward a new form of AI engineering, where developers can program specific internal behaviors directly in natural language rather than relying solely on iterative training cycles.
