# Gene Editing

Gene editing encompasses techniques for making precise alterations to DNA sequences within living organisms. The field has experienced significant advancement over the past two decades, transforming from experimental laboratory methods into practical tools for research [[entities/national-academies|and medicine]]. Early approaches, including zinc finger nucleases and TALENs, demonstrated that targeted DNA modification was possible but remained limited by complexity, cost, and [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]].

## CRISPR-Cas9 and Its Impact

CRISPR-Cas9 emerged as a transformative technology in the early 2010s, offering a simpler, more efficient, and cost-effective method for genome manipulation compared to its predecessors. By utilizing a guide RNA to direct the Cas9 enzyme to specific DNA locations, researchers could introduce double-strand breaks that cells repair via non-homologous end joining or homology-directed repair. This mechanism allowed for widespread [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] across biological disciplines, accelerating progress in genetic research, [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]], and therapeutic development.

## Post-CRISPR Innovations

Recent developments at the [[entities/broad-institute|Broad Institute]] and other research institutions have focused on addressing the limitations of CRISPR-Cas9, such as off-target effects and delivery challenges. Scientists have discovered and engineered potential successors, including base editors and [[concepts/prime-lens|prime]] editors, which enable precise single-nucleotide changes without inducing double-strand breaks. These newer technologies aim to provide greater [[concepts/accuracy|precision]] and safety, expanding the scope of editable genomic targets and paving the way for more refined clinical applications.
