# Linguistics: The Scientific Study Of Language

[[concepts/linguistics|Linguistics]] is the scientific discipline that examines human language in its structure, use, and historical development. As an empirical field, it applies systematic methods to understand how languages work, how they change over time, and how they function in [[concepts/human-intelligence|human cognition]] and society. Linguists study both the universal properties shared across all human languages and the specific features that distinguish individual languages from one another.

## Core Areas of Study

The field encompasses several interconnected subfields. Phonetics and phonology investigate the sounds of language and how they are organized into meaningful units. Syntax examines the rules that govern sentence structure, while semantics explores how meaning is conveyed through language. Morphology studies the structure of words and how they are formed from smaller meaningful units. Pragmatics analyzes how context shapes language use and interpretation.

Historical and comparative linguistics trace how languages evolve and relate to one another, reconstructing aspects of ancient languages and tracking linguistic change across centuries. Sociolinguistics examines how language varies across different social groups and contexts, investigating dialect, register, and the [[concepts/social-psychological-factors|social factors]] that influence speech. Psycholinguistics explores the [[concepts/human-cognition|cognitive processes]] underlying language comprehension and production in the human [[concepts/the-mind|mind]].

## Methods and Applications

Linguists employ diverse methodologies, from formal analysis of [[concepts/language-grammar|language structure]] to field research and corpus studies of naturally occurring language use. Modern linguistics makes extensive use of [[concepts/calculation-methods|computational methods]] and data analysis. The discipline's findings have applications in language education, speech technology, translation, clinical treatment of language disorders, and understanding human [[concepts/cognition|cognition]] more broadly.
