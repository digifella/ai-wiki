# Artemis Program

The [[concepts/space-exploration|Artemis Program]] is [[concepts/nasa|NASA]]'s initiative to return humans to the Moon and establish sustained [[concepts/lunar-exploration|lunar exploration]]. Named after the twin sister of Apollo in [[concepts/greek-mythology|Greek mythology]], the program represents a continuation of lunar [[concepts/science|science]] and exploration that began with the Apollo missions of the 1960s and 1970s. Artemis aims to land astronauts on the lunar surface, with particular emphasis on the south polar region where water ice deposits have been detected.

## Program Components and Objectives

The program relies on several key technological developments, primarily the Space Launch System (SLS) heavy-[[entities/lift|lift]] rocket and the [[concepts/orion-systems|Orion spacecraft]] for [[concepts/deep-space-exploration|deep space]] transit. A critical component is the [[concepts/human-landing-system|Human Landing System]] (HLS), which is responsible for transporting astronauts from lunar orbit to the surface. NASA has contracted multiple commercial providers to develop these landers, aiming to ensure redundancy and sustainability for long-term missions.

## Current Status and Challenges

The program is currently navigating significant technical and logistical hurdles, particularly concerning the readiness of the Human Landing System. Delays in HLS development have impacted the timeline for the Artemis III mission, which is designated to be the first crewed [[concepts/lunar-landing|lunar landing]] in over fifty years. While the Space Launch System has undergone successful test flights, the integration of all systems and the establishment of a permanent lunar presence remain ongoing objectives for the agency.
