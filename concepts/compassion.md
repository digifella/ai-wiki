# Compassion

[[concepts/love|Compassion]] is an emotional and cognitive response triggered by the recognition of another person's suffering. It involves two primary components: the perception of distress in others and an internal [[concepts/motivation|motivation]] to reduce or alleviate that suffering. This dual nature distinguishes it from purely affective states, as it combines [[concepts/empathy|emotional resonance]] with a proactive [[concepts/ambition|drive]] to intervene.

While closely related to empathy, compassion is distinct in its functional outcome. Empathy refers to the capacity to understand and share the emotional state of another, which can be experienced passively without leading to further action. Compassion, however, inherently includes concern and a directional [[concepts/impulse|impulse]] toward helping. Where empathy provides the understanding of the pain, compassion provides the motivation to address it.

Compassion also differs from sympathy, which involves feelings of pity or sorrow for someone else's misfortune but does not necessarily imply a desire to help. In the context of health and [[concepts/health|wellbeing]], compassion is often studied as a protective factor against burnout and [[concepts/stress|stress]], as it fosters [[concepts/human-connection|social connection]] and prosocial behavior. It is considered a key component of [[concepts/emotional-intelligence|emotional intelligence]] and is frequently cultivated through [[concepts/mindfulness|mindfulness]] and interpersonal training [[concepts/software|programs]] to enhance [[concepts/mental-toughness|mental resilience]] and social [[concepts/cohesion|cohesion]].
