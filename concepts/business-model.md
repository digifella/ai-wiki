# Business Model

A business model describes the rationale of how an organization creates, delivers, and captures value. It encompasses the production, outreach, and revenue strategies of an entity.

## Key Dynamics

- **Hardware vs. Service Shift**: Traditional hardware sales are increasingly supplemented or replaced by recurring [[concepts/revenue-streams|revenue streams]] derived from data and services.
- **Data as Asset**: Modern devices often function as data collection [[concepts/nodes|nodes]], where user behavior informs advertising and content strategies.
- **[[concepts/pricing-structure|Cost Structure]] Evolution**: The affordability of consumer electronics is frequently driven by cross-subsidization from digital services rather than hardware margins alone.

## Case Study: Smart TVs

The evolution of the [[concepts/smart-tv]] market illustrates a fundamental pivot in value capture. Historically, revenue was generated primarily through one-time hardware sales. Contemporary models leverage low hardware prices to acquire users, subsequently monetizing through data collection and targeted advertising.

- **Price Paradox**: Modern smart TVs are significantly cheaper than predecessors from 15 years ago, despite advanced capabilities. This affordability is not due to hardware [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] but to a shift in the underlying business model.
- **Surveillance Capitalism**: Devices like those from LG collect extensive user data, transforming the TV from a passive display into an active data source for third-party monetization.
