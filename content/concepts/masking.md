# Masking

[[concepts/layer-masks|Masking]] is a [[concepts/photo-editing|photo editing]] technique that allows photographers and editors to apply [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] selectively to specific areas of an image rather than to the entire photo. By isolating regions through masks, editors can enhance certain elements—such as brightening a subject's face, deepening a sky, or sharpening specific details—without affecting the rest of the composition. This selective approach provides greater control and precision than global adjustments.

## Implementation in Software

Modern photo editing applications like Adobe Lightroom and [[concepts/photoshop|Photoshop]] offer masking tools that have evolved significantly with advances in [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]]. Traditional masks were created manually using brushes, but contemporary software increasingly features automated AI-powered mask presets that can intelligently identify and isolate common subjects like skies, people, or landscapes with minimal user input. Lightroom Classic, for instance, includes single-click automated [[concepts/ai-masks|AI masks]] designed for landscape and [[concepts/portrait-photography|portrait photography]], streamlining what was previously a more labor-intensive process.

## Common Applications

Masking is particularly valuable in [[concepts/landscape-photography|landscape photography]], where photographers frequently need to adjust skies differently from foregrounds, and in portrait work, where [[concepts/skin-tones|skin tones]] and facial features may require targeted enhancement. The technique also supports creative post-processing styles, such as darkening and moodiness adjustments applied selectively to enhance dramatic effects. By combining multiple masks and adjustments, editors can achieve sophisticated results while maintaining natural-looking [[concepts/images|images]].
