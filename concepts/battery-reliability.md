# Battery Reliability

Battery [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] represents a critical performance metric for electric vehicles, encompassing the ability of battery systems to maintain capacity, sustain performance, and operate safely throughout their intended lifespan. Lithium-ion batteries, which power the vast majority of modern EVs, [[concepts/experience|experience]] gradual chemical degradation through repeated [[concepts/charging-cycle-life|charge-discharge cycles]], [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] to temperature extremes, and manufacturing variations. The rate and extent of this degradation directly impacts vehicle longevity, resale value, and total cost of ownership.

## Degradation Mechanisms

Several interconnected processes contribute to battery [[concepts/battery-degradation-rate|capacity loss]] over time. [[concepts/solid-electrolyte-interphase|Solid electrolyte interphase]] (SEI) layer growth on the anode, loss of active lithium ions, and structural changes in the cathode material all reduce the number of charge carriers available for energy [[entities/storage|storage]]. Environmental factors play a significant role: exposure to high temperatures accelerates chemical reactions and side reactions, while very low temperatures reduce ionic conductivity and can cause lithium plating. Manufacturing defects and inconsistencies in cell production also influence individual battery longevity within a pack.

## Measurement and Standards

Manufacturers and researchers typically measure battery degradation as the percentage of original capacity retained after a specified number of charge cycles or years of service. Industry standards generally define end-of-life when a battery retains approximately 70-80% of its original capacity, though this threshold varies by application. Real-[[entities/earth|world]] [[concepts/monitoring-systems|monitoring systems]] use voltage, current, and temperature data to estimate state of [[concepts/health|health]], though these estimates contain inherent uncertainties due to the complexity of degradation processes.

## Practical Implications

Battery warranty periods, typically ranging from 8 to 10 years or 100,000 to 160,000 kilometers, reflect manufacturers' confidence in reliability under normal use conditions. However, reliability varies significantly across vehicle models and battery chemistries, influenced by thermal management systems, charging protocols, and pack design. Understanding individual battery reliability profiles helps consumers make informed decisions about EV purchases and long-term vehicle value.
