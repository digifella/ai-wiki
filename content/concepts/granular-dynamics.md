# Granular Dynamics

Granular dynamics is the study of how collections of discrete particles—such as sand, gravel, or other [[concepts/granular-materials|granular materials]]—behave and evolve under the influence of physical forces. Unlike fluids or continuous solids, granular materials exhibit unique mechanical properties that emerge from the interactions between individual grains. These materials are ubiquitous in [[entities/nature|nature]] and industry, appearing in geological formations, avalanches, industrial processing, and construction materials.

## Key Phenomena

A fundamental concept in granular dynamics is the **[[concepts/angle-of-repose|angle of repose]]**, the maximum angle at which a pile of granular material can rest on a slope before it becomes unstable and collapses. This angle depends on factors including grain size, shape, [[concepts/friction|friction]], and moisture content. However, granular materials often exhibit more complex behavior than simple [[concepts/slope-stability|slope stability]] would predict; they can self-sort by size and density, segregate during motion, and develop ordered or disordered internal structures depending on how they are disturbed or compacted.

## Applications and Significance

Understanding granular dynamics is essential for predicting [[concepts/geological-hazards|geological hazards]] such as landslides and debris flows, optimizing industrial processes like ore processing and [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]], and designing structures that interact with granular materials. The field bridges [[concepts/physics|physics]], [[concepts/geology|geology]], and engineering, combining laboratory experiments, theoretical modeling, and field observations to explain how seemingly simple systems of particles can produce surprisingly rich and varied behaviors.
