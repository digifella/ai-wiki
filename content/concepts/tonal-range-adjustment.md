# Tonal Range Adjustment

Tonal [[concepts/range|range]] adjustment is a fundamental technique in digital [[concepts/photo-editing|photo editing]] that involves modifying the [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] of tones—from [[concepts/shadows|shadows]] through midtones to [[concepts/highlights|highlights]]—across an image. This process allows photographers and editors to enhance [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], recover detail in overexposed or underexposed areas, and establish the overall luminosity and mood of a photograph. The tonal range refers to the span between the darkest and lightest values present in an image.

## Implementation in Camera Raw

In [[concepts/photoshop|Adobe Photoshop]], tonal range adjustment is commonly performed using the [[concepts/camera-raw-filter|Camera Raw Filter]], a [[concepts/non-destructive-editing|non-destructive editing]] interface that provides [[concepts/granular-control|granular control]] over luminosity values. The filter offers several tools for this [[concepts/motivation|purpose]], including [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] sliders, highlights and shadows recovery controls, and the curves adjustment panel. These tools enable editors to selectively brighten or darken specific tonal regions without affecting others, preserving detail across the entire image while maintaining natural color [[concepts/relationships|relationships]].

## Practical Applications

Tonal range adjustment serves multiple purposes in photography workflows. It can rescue detail from blown highlights or crushed shadows, increase overall [[concepts/image-contrast|image contrast]] for visual impact, or create specific aesthetic effects through targeted [[concepts/tone|tone]] mapping. The technique is equally valuable in [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|color grading]], where establishing proper tonal relationships provides the foundation for subsequent color adjustments and ensures the final image communicates the intended visual narrative.
