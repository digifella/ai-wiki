# Supply Lines

**Definition:** The network of routes and facilities through which an army or other force receives its Logistics support, including ammunition, fuel, food, medical supplies, and equipment.

## Core Principles

- **[[concepts/vulnerability|Vulnerability]]:** Longer supply lines increase [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] to disruption via interdiction, sabotage, or attrition.
- **Throughput vs. Reach:** Trade-off between the volume of goods delivered and the distance from forward [[concepts/number-systems|bases]] to combat zones.
- **Critical [[concepts/nodes|Nodes]]:** Bridges, rail junctions, ports, and fuel depots serve as high-value targets for asymmetric warfare.

## Strategic Impact

- **Operational Tempo:** Effective Logistics sustains high-intensity operations; failure leads to stalemate or retreat.
- **Target Selection:** Modern doctrine prioritizes "deep strike" capabilities to hit supply hubs behind enemy lines before they reach the front.
- **[[concepts/resilience|Resilience]]:** Redundancy in transport routes mitigates single-point failures.
- **Geopolitical Leverage:** Control over strategic supply corridors (e.g., in the Middle East) influences diplomatic outcomes and [[concepts/conflict|conflict]] de-escalation.
