# Nanotechnology

Nanotechnology is the engineering and manipulation of matter at the nanoscale, typically involving structures and devices between 1 and 100 nanometers in size. At this scale, materials exhibit different physical and chemical properties compared to their bulk counterparts due to increased surface area and quantum effects. These differences enable novel applications that would be impossible using conventional manufacturing approaches.

## Properties and Behavior

The distinctive behavior of nanoscale materials arises from their high surface-area-to-volume ratio and the dominance of quantum mechanical effects. As particle size decreases, the proportion of atoms at the surface increases significantly, leading to higher reactivity and catalytic activity. Simultaneously, quantum confinement alters electronic and optical properties, allowing for precise tuning of material characteristics such as conductivity, fluorescence, and magnetic susceptibility.

## Applications and Manufacturing

Nanotechnology intersects with various fields, including materials science, biology, and electronics. In semiconductor manufacturing, it enables the creation of transistors with dimensions measured in nanometers, which is critical for increasing processing power and energy efficiency in computing devices. Techniques such as chemical vapor deposition, lithography, and self-assembly are employed to construct these structures with atomic precision. The integration of nanoscale components into larger systems drives advancements in medical diagnostics, energy storage, and high-performance computing.
