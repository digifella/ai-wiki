# Skillet Cooking

Skillet cooking is a fundamental cooking method that uses a flat-bottomed pan with sloped or curved sides to prepare food over direct heat. The design of a skillet—typically featuring a wide cooking surface and angled edges—facilitates efficient heat distribution and allows cooks to manage temperature control and ingredient placement during preparation. Skillets are employed across numerous culinary traditions and kitchen environments, valued for their ability to adapt to various cooking techniques and heat sources, including stovetop burners, ovens, and open flames.

## Common Applications

Skillets are used for a wide range of cooking tasks including sautéing vegetables, searing meats, pan-frying, and making curries and stews. The sloped sides make it easy to flip ingredients or transfer food to serving dishes, while the flat bottom provides stable contact with the heat source. Different skillet materials—such as cast iron, stainless steel, and non-stick surfaces—offer distinct heat retention and cooking properties suited to particular dishes and cooking styles.

## Heat Distribution

The efficiency of skillet cooking relies on its geometry, which promotes even heat distribution across the cooking surface. This design allows cooks to create and control temperature gradients within the same pan, enabling simultaneous cooking of ingredients that require different levels of heat exposure. Skillets retain heat effectively, making them practical for techniques that benefit from sustained or residual heat.
