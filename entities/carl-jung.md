# Carl Jung

Carl Jung (1875–1961) was a Swiss psychiatrist and psychoanalyst who founded analytical psychology, one of the major schools of depth psychology. He studied medicine at the University of Zurich and developed an early interest in psychiatric practice, eventually becoming a prominent figure in the psychoanalytic movement of the early twentieth century.

## Relationship with Freud

Jung collaborated extensively with Sigmund Freud beginning in the early 1900s, and Freud identified him as his intellectual successor. However, their professional relationship deteriorated during the 1910s as Jung’s theoretical divergences regarding the nature of the libido and the unconscious became irreconcilable with Freud’s psychoanalytic framework. This split led Jung to develop his own distinct approach to the psyche.

## Analytical Psychology and Key Concepts

Jung established analytical psychology, which emphasizes the process of individuation and the integration of the conscious and unconscious mind. He introduced several foundational concepts, including the collective unconscious, archetypes, and psychological types. His work significantly influenced not only psychiatry and psychotherapy but also anthropology, literature, and religious studies, establishing a legacy that extends far beyond clinical practice.
