# Focal Length

Focal length is the distance, measured in millimeters, between a camera's lens and its sensor when the lens is focused at infinity. It is a fundamental optical property that determines how the lens captures and magnifies a scene. Common focal lengths range from 14mm (ultra-wide angle) to 600mm or longer ([[concepts/telephoto-lens|telephoto]]), with 50mm traditionally considered a standard lens that approximates human [[concepts/computer-vision|vision]].

## Relationship to Field of View

Focal length directly determines a camera's field of view—the extent of the scene visible through the lens. Shorter focal lengths (wide-angle lenses) capture a broader field of view, while longer focal lengths (telephoto lenses) narrow the field of view and magnify distant subjects. This relationship is fundamental to composition and framing decisions in photography.

## Application in Panoramic Photography

In panoramic photography, focal length selection affects how individual frames are captured and subsequently stitched together. Shorter focal lengths require more frames to cover a panoramic span, potentially resulting in higher resolution composites, while longer focal lengths reduce the number of frames needed but with a narrower perspective. Understanding focal length is essential for planning panoramic sequences and achieving consistent results across multiple exposures.
