# Theoretical Frameworks

Theoretical frameworks are structured systems of concepts, principles, and [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] used to organize knowledge and understand phenomena within a particular domain. They serve as foundational scaffolding for research, analysis, and [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] by providing coherent ways to interpret data and make predictions. Different fields develop frameworks suited to their particular concerns, though many frameworks share common structural features and can be adapted across disciplines.

## Knowing Versus Understanding

[[concepts/feynman|Richard Feynman]] distinguished between merely knowing the name or facts about something and genuinely understanding its underlying principles. According to this distinction, one can memorize that a phenomenon exists and what it is called without comprehending why it occurs or how its components relate to one another. True understanding involves grasping the causal mechanisms and logical connections that explain a phenomenon, allowing for prediction and application in new contexts. This distinction highlights a fundamental limitation in [[concepts/knowledge-acquisition|knowledge acquisition]]: factual information alone is insufficient without comprehension of the theoretical relationships that give those facts meaning.

## Framework Equivalence and Progress

When multiple theoretical frameworks successfully describe the same domain of phenomena, they may be considered equivalent in their predictive power despite differing in their conceptual [[concepts/structure|structure]] and assumptions. However, progress in understanding often involves moving beyond mere equivalence to develop frameworks that offer deeper insight into underlying mechanisms, even when older frameworks remain practically functional. This process reflects how the [[concepts/history-of-science|history of science]] involves not just the accumulation of facts but the refinement of explanatory structures that reveal previously hidden relationships between phenomena.
