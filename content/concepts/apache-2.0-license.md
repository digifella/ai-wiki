# Apache 2.0 License

The [[concepts/apache-2.0|Apache License 2.0]] is a permissive [[concepts/open-source|open-source]] [[concepts/software|software]] license maintained by the Apache Software Foundation. First released in 2004, it grants users the right to use, modify, and distribute software under clear legal terms. The license is permissive, meaning it imposes fewer restrictions than copyleft licenses, allowing licensed [[concepts/code|code]] to be incorporated into proprietary software. It has become one of the most widely adopted licenses in enterprise software, data processing, and [[concepts/machine-learning|machine learning]] projects.

## Key Permissions and Conditions

The [[concepts/apache-2-0|Apache 2.0 License]] permits users to freely use, modify, and distribute both source and binary forms of licensed software. Modifications and derivative works may be distributed under different terms, including proprietary licenses. However, the license requires that users provide notice of any changes made to the original code, include a copy of the original license, and state significant modifications clearly. Users must also include any notice [[concepts/files|files]] and patent grants associated with the work.

## Patent Protection

A distinguishing feature of Apache 2.0 is its explicit patent grant. The license grants an irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free patent license from contributors to users. This means that contributors cannot later sue users for patent infringement related to the licensed code. Additionally, if a user initiates patent litigation against a contributor, their patent license automatically terminates. This provision addresses [[concepts/legal-risks|legal risks]] and provides clearer protections than some other open-source licenses.
