# Color Temperature

Color temperature refers to the visual warmth or coolness of light, measured in Kelvin (K). In photography and digital imaging, understanding color temperature is essential for achieving natural-looking colors and setting the appropriate white balance. Warm light sources (such as incandescent bulbs) have lower color temperatures around 2700K, while cool light sources (such as daylight or overcast skies) have higher temperatures ranging from 5500K to 6500K or above. When editing portraits and other [[concepts/images|images]], adjusting color temperature can dramatically affect the mood and believability of a photograph.

## Color Temperature in Photoshop 2025

Photoshop 2025 introduced an updated [[concepts/adjustment-layers|Color and Vibrance tool]] that provides photographers and editors with more intuitive controls for managing color temperature in post-processing. This tool allows users to fine-tune the warmth or coolness of an image after capture, correcting unwanted [[concepts/color-casts|color casts]] or intentionally shifting the temperature to achieve a desired aesthetic. The improved interface makes color temperature [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] more accessible to users at all skill levels, reducing the need for multiple adjustment layers or manual white balance corrections.
