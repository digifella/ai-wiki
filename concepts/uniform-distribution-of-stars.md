# Uniform Distribution of Stars

The hypothesis that matter in the universe is distributed evenly on large scales. This concept is central to the **Cosmological Principle**, which assumes homogeneity and isotropy.

## Key Implications

*   **[[concepts/olbers-paradox|Olbers' Paradox]]**: If the universe were infinitely old, infinitely large, and uniformly filled with stars, the [[concepts/night-sky|night sky]] should be as bright as the surface of a star. The observed darkness of the night sky contradicts this static, infinite model.
*   **Resolution**: The paradox is resolved by recognizing that the universe has a finite age and is expanding, limiting the observable volume and redshifting light from distant sources.
*   **Statistical Homogeneity**: On scales larger than ~300 million light-years, the distribution of galaxies and clusters approximates uniformity, though local structures (clusters, voids) exist.
