# Geological Hazards

Geological hazards are naturally occurring [[entities/earth|Earth]] processes that pose direct risks to human life, property, and [[concepts/infrastructure|infrastructure]]. These phenomena stem from the dynamic nature of Earth's crust, internal heat [[concepts/flow|flow]], and surface processes, operating across timescales ranging from seconds to centuries. Unlike [[concepts/environmental-risks|environmental hazards]] driven by weather or biological systems, geological hazards are fundamentally caused by planetary-scale physical forces within and at the surface of the Earth.

## Primary Types

Major geological hazards include [[concepts/volcanic-eruptions|volcanic eruptions]], earthquakes, and tsunamis. [[concepts/volcanic-eruption|Volcanic activity]] involves the [[concepts/deployment|release]] of magma, ash, and gases, often accompanied by secondary events such as [[concepts/landmass-collapse|flank collapse]] or lahars. Earthquakes result from the sudden release of energy in the Earth's lithosphere, creating seismic waves that can cause significant [[concepts/earthquakes|ground shaking]] and structural damage. Tsunamis are typically generated by underwater earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, or landslides, producing large ocean waves that can inundate coastal areas with devastating force.

## Mechanisms and Scope

These hazards arise from the interaction of tectonic plates, mantle convection, and surface erosion processes. The classification of these events distinguishes them from meteorological or hydrological hazards by their origin in the solid Earth. Understanding the underlying geological [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] is essential for [[concepts/risk-assessment|risk assessment]], as the frequency and intensity of these events are governed by complex [[concepts/fundamental-laws-of-physics|physical laws]] and historical geological patterns.
