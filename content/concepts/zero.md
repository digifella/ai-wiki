# Zero
Zero is a fundamental number in modern [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]], serving as both a placeholder and an operand in arithmetic operations. Its acceptance and [[concepts/integration|integration]] into mathematical systems have been pivotal for the development of algebra, calculus, and numerous scientific disciplines. The concept of zero was independently developed by several ancient cultures, including the Babylonians, Mayans, and Indians. However, its journey to widespread acceptance across different parts of the world has not been without resistance.

- Zero's philosophical implications challenged Western thinkers who struggled with the idea of nothingness.
- Eastern philosophies generally accepted the concept more readily due to their understanding of emptiness or void (e.g., [[concepts/buddhism]] and Daoism).
- The [[concepts/number-zero|number zero]] was banned in the Western world for over 1500 years, primarily due to [[concepts/philosophical-resistance|philosophical resistance]] rather than practical objections.
