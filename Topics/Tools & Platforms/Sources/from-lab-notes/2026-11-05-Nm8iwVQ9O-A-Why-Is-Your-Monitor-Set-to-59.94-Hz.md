## Why Is Your Monitor Set to 59.94 Hz?

### Summary
Here's a summary of the video content:

The video explores the origin of specific refresh rates like 59.94 Hz and 29.97 Hz commonly found in computer and television displays. The speaker explains that these odd numbers are descendants of changes made during the early days of color television. In 1953, the National Television System Committee (NTSC) introduced a color television standard, which required a tiny 0.1% adjustment to the broadcast signal's timing to accommodate color information without disrupting existing black and white signals.

The speaker notes that this fractional adjustment was calculated to prevent interference between the color subcarrier and audio/visual signals, resulting in a refresh rate of approximately 59.94 Hz for 60 Hz signals and 29.97 Hz for 30 Hz signals. Over time, these fractional rates became standard in broadcasting and digital video, persisting even into modern display technologies.

The video concludes that while these fractional refresh rates might seem unusual, they are a legacy of early television engineering and do not significantly impact the viewer's experience. The speaker notes that the difference between 60 Hz and 59.94 Hz is negligible, and that modern software and hardware can handle these minor timing discrepancies.
