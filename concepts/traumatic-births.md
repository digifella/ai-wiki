# Traumatic Births

Traumatic birth refers to childbirth experiences that result in significant psychological distress for those present, including the birthing person and their support partners. Research indicates that approximately 30% of births may be experienced as traumatic by attendees. These experiences can stem from various factors including medical emergencies, unexpected complications, loss of control during labour, inadequate pain management, or feeling unsupported during the birthing process. The psychological impact of traumatic birth can persist long after delivery, affecting mental health and bonding.

## Impact on Fathers and Support Partners

The presence of fathers and support partners during birth plays a critical role in mitigating trauma and enhancing service delivery. Men who attend births are better positioned to provide immediate emotional support, advocate for the birthing person’s preferences, and participate in decision-making processes. This active involvement can reduce feelings of helplessness and anxiety for both the father and the birthing person, fostering a more positive shared experience.

## Service Delivery Improvements

Enhancing service delivery to accommodate and encourage paternal attendance requires structural and cultural shifts within healthcare systems. Facilities must ensure that policies allow for flexible visiting hours, adequate space for support persons, and staff training on inclusive care practices. By normalizing and supporting the presence of fathers during labor and delivery, healthcare providers can help create an environment that reduces the likelihood of traumatic outcomes and promotes better psychological recovery for all parties involved.
