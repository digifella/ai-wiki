# Skillet Cooking

Skillet cooking is a fundamental culinary technique that utilizes a flat-bottomed pan with sloped or curved sides to prepare food over direct heat. The design of a skillet, typically featuring a wide cooking surface and angled edges, facilitates efficient heat distribution and allows cooks to manage temperature control and ingredient placement during preparation. This method is valued for its adaptability across various kitchen environments and culinary traditions.

The versatility of skillets allows them to be employed on numerous heat sources, including stovetop burners, ovens, and campfires. This broad compatibility makes the skillet a staple in both professional kitchens and home cooking. The ability to sear, sauté, braise, and finish dishes in a single vessel reduces cleanup and preserves the integrity of flavors by minimizing the transfer of ingredients between different pots.

In specific culinary contexts, such as North Indian cuisine, skillet cooking is essential for developing complex flavor profiles. For instance, Chef Neel Kajale’s North Indian Skillet Chicken Curry demonstrates how direct heat and the pan's geometry contribute to the even cooking of proteins and the reduction of sauces. The technique allows for precise control over the browning process, which is critical for building the depth of taste in curry-based dishes.
