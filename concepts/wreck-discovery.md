# Wreck Discovery

The wreck of the RMS Titanic was discovered on September 1, 1985, by a team led by oceanographer Robert Ballard. The expedition took place in the North Atlantic Ocean, approximately 370 miles south-southeast of Newfoundland, at a depth of roughly 12,500 feet. The ship was found lying largely intact on the ocean floor, split into two main sections separated by a debris field spanning several miles.

The mission was officially authorized by the United States Navy under the cover of locating two lost nuclear submarines, the USS Thresher and the USS Scorpion. Ballard’s team utilized the deep-tow vehicle Argo and the submersible Alvin to survey the ocean floor. While searching for the submarines, the team identified debris fields that led them to the Titanic, which had been resting undisturbed for 73 years.

The discovery revealed that the ship had broken apart upon impact with the ocean floor, a finding that contradicted earlier theories that the vessel had remained largely intact. The debris field provided crucial evidence for reconstructing the final moments of the ship's sinking. This accidental discovery shifted the focus of the expedition from military objectives to historical and archaeological investigation, marking a significant milestone in deep-sea exploration.
