# Digital Conservation

Digital conservation refers to the strategies and practices employed to preserve digital materials and digitized content for long-term access and usability. As organizations increasingly convert physical archives and records into digital formats, the challenge of maintaining these materials over extended periods becomes a significant concern. This field encompasses technical, organizational, and strategic approaches to ensure that digital assets remain readable, accessible, and authentic across changing technological landscapes.

The primary challenges in digital conservation stem from the rapid obsolescence of hardware, software, and file formats. Unlike physical media, which may degrade slowly, [[concepts/digital-information|digital information]] is vulnerable to bit rot, format incompatibility, and media failure. Consequently, institutions must actively manage the migration of data to new [[entities/storage|storage]] media and the conversion of files into current standards to prevent loss of access.

Organizational and strategic frameworks are equally critical to successful [[concepts/preservation|preservation]]. This involves establishing [[concepts/policies|policies]] for [[concepts/metadata|metadata]] management, [[concepts/honesty|integrity]] checking, and disaster recovery. By integrating these practices into broader business and archival strategies, organizations can mitigate the risks associated with digital decay and ensure that historical records remain available for future research and reference.
