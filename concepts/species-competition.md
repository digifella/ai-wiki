# Species Competition

Species competition in biological contexts refers to the struggle between different species for limited resources such as food, habitat, and energy. This fundamental ecological process drives natural selection and shapes the structure of ecosystems over time. Competitive interactions can result in resource partitioning, where species evolve to occupy distinct ecological niches, or in competitive exclusion, where one species outcompetes another for essential resources.

Competition occurs through both direct interference, where organisms physically confront each other, and indirect exploitation, where species consume shared resources faster than competitors can. These dynamics influence population sizes, distribution patterns, and evolutionary trajectories, often leading to adaptive radiation or local extinction.

In the context of Sakana AI's digital ecosystems, this concept is simulated to model the survival and coexistence of artificial species. By applying biological principles of competition to AI agents, the system explores how digital entities adapt, specialize, or eliminate one another within constrained computational environments.
