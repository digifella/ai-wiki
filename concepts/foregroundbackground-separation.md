# Foregroundbackground Separation

Foreground/background separation is a post-processing technique that isolates distinct areas of a photograph to apply independent [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] to each region. By utilizing [[concepts/masking-tools|masking tools]] in [[entities/adobe-lightroom|Adobe Lightroom]] and [[concepts/camera-raw|Camera Raw]], photographers can selectively modify [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], [[concepts/clarity-slider|clarity]], saturation, and color across different parts of the image. This approach allows the main subject to be enhanced or refined separately from its surroundings, improving overall [[concepts/combining-photos|image composition]] and [[concepts/hierarchy|visual hierarchy]].

## Creating and Refining Masks

The foundation of this technique relies on the accurate creation of selection masks. Modern versions of Lightroom and Camera Raw employ AI-driven subject and background detection to generate initial masks automatically. Users can refine these automatic selections by adjusting the mask's edge feathering and [[concepts/density|density]] to ensure smooth transitions between the foreground and background. Manual brush or [[concepts/gradient-tools|gradient tools]] are often used to correct areas where the automatic detection fails, particularly around complex edges like hair or foliage.

Once the masks are established, adjustments are applied independently to each layer. For instance, a photographer might increase the exposure and clarity of the foreground subject while simultaneously darkening or desaturating the background to create depth. This selective processing helps guide the viewer's eye and emphasizes the primary subject without affecting the [[concepts/honesty|integrity]] of the surrounding environment. The process requires careful monitoring of the mask boundaries to prevent [[concepts/halos|halos]] or unnatural artifacts along the separation line.
