# Engram

In [[concepts/neuroscience|neuroscience]] and [[concepts/psychology|psychology]], an [[entities/deepseek-engram|engram]] refers to the hypothetical physical or chemical trace in the brain that represents a specific [[concepts/memory|memory]]. The concept was introduced by German biologist [[concepts/feynman|Richard]] Semon in 1904 to describe the neural substrate of memory. Semon proposed that sensory experiences leave persistent marks on neural tissue, which could later be reactivated to produce memory [[concepts/recall|recall]]. Although his specific mechanistic proposals were not sustained by later research, the conceptual framework of engrams became central to the study of how information is encoded and stored in biological systems.

Modern neuroscience has largely shifted from the abstract [[entities/notion|notion]] of a static "trace" to identifying specific neural circuits and synaptic changes associated with [[concepts/storing|memory formation]]. Research indicates that engrams are not localized to single neurons but are distributed across networks of cells. Techniques such as optogenetics have allowed scientists to label and manipulate these engram cells, demonstrating that activating specific neural populations can trigger the recall of associated memories, thereby providing empirical support for the existence of physical memory traces.

In the context of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]], the term "engram" has been adopted by [[concepts/deepseek-ai|DeepSeek]] to describe its [[concepts/context-aware-knowledge-retrieval|context-aware knowledge retrieval]] system for language models. This usage diverges from the biological definition, referring instead to a computational mechanism for efficiently accessing and integrating relevant information during [[concepts/inference|model inference]]. While the metaphor draws on the idea of [[concepts/retrieving|retrieving]] stored data, it functions as a technical architecture component rather than a biological phenomenon.
