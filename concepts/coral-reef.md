# Coral Reef

**Coral reefs** are diverse underwater ecosystems held together by calcium carbonate structures secreted by corals. They are often referred to as the "rainforests of the sea" due to their high [[concepts/biodiversity|biodiversity]].

## Key Characteristics
- **Structure**: Built by colonial animals (polyps) that secrete limestone skeletons.
- **Biodiversity**: Support ~25% of all marine species despite covering <1% of the ocean floor.
- **Types**: Fringing, barrier, and atoll reefs.
- **Symbiosis**: Depend on mutualistic [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] with zooxanthellae (photosynthetic algae).

## Ecological Significance
- **Coastal [[concepts/secure|Protection]]**: Buffer shorelines against erosion and [[entities/storm|storm]] surges.
- **Nutrient Cycling**: Highly efficient nutrient [[concepts/storing|retention]] in oligotrophic waters.
- **[[concepts/value|Economic Value]]**: Tourism, fisheries, and pharmaceutical research.

## Threats
- **[[concepts/climate-change|Climate Change]]**: Ocean warming [[concepts/causes|causes]] Coral Bleaching; acidification weakens skeletal structures.
- **Pollution**: Runoff, plastics, and chemical contaminants.
- **Overfishing**: Disrupts [[concepts/ecological-balance|ecological balance]] (e.g., removal of herbivorous fish leads to algal overgrowth).

## Comparative Context: Terrestrial Analogues
While coral reefs are marine biodiversity hotspots, they share ecological parallels with ancient [[concepts/terrestrial-life|terrestrial ecosystems]] regarding [[concepts/resilience|resilience]] and species [[concepts/density|density]].
- **[[concepts/daintree-rainforest|Daintree Rainforest]]: Oldest Living Tropical Ecosystem, [[concepts/ancient-biodiversity|Ancient Biodiversity]], and [[concepts/geological-survival|Geological Survival]]**: The Daintree Rainforest in Queensland, Australia, represents the oldest living tropical rainforest on [[entities/earth|Earth]]. Like coral reefs, it serves as a critical reservoir for ancient biodiversity and demonstrates long-term geological survival. Recent findings highlight its role in preserving evolutionary lineages that predate many modern ecosystems.
