# Physics Simulation

Physics simulation is the computational modeling of physical systems through mathematical equations and numerical methods. It works by discretizing continuous differential equations—which describe motion, forces, energy transfer, and other physical phenomena—into discrete computational steps that approximate real-world behavior. By solving these equations numerically on computers, simulations enable prediction and analysis of complex physical systems that would be difficult or impossible to study through experiment alone.

## Applications and Methods

Physics simulations are foundational across numerous scientific and engineering disciplines, including fluid dynamics, structural mechanics, astrophysics, and molecular dynamics. Common numerical techniques include the Finite Element Method (FEM) for solid mechanics, Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) for fluid flow, and Particle-in-Cell (PIC) methods for plasma physics. These methods allow researchers to visualize internal states, test boundary conditions, and optimize designs without the cost or risk associated with physical prototyping.

Recent developments in the field have integrated artificial intelligence to enhance simulation efficiency and accuracy. Advances in large language models, such as GPT-5, have facilitated the automation of code generation for simulation setups, reducing the manual effort required to define complex boundary conditions and mesh structures. Furthermore, AI-driven surrogate models are increasingly used to approximate computationally expensive simulations, enabling real-time analysis and rapid iteration in design workflows.
