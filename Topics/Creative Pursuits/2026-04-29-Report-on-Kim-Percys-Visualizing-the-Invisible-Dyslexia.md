## Report on Kim Percy's Visualizing the Invisible: Dyslexia Through Art

### Summary
This video features an insightful interview with artist and [[entities/phd|PhD]] candidate [[entities/kim-percy|Kim Percy]], discussing her [[concepts/practice-based-research|practice-based research]] and accompanying exhibition, "Visualising the Invisible." Her project delves into the often-unseen experiences of dyslexia through the powerful [[entities/medium|medium]] of [[concepts/visual-art|visual art]], aiming to challenge stigma and create a visible representation of successful creative dyslexics for those who might come after her.

Percy’s personal journey forms the core of her research. Diagnosed with dyslexia later in life following her son's diagnosis, she embarked on a quest to understand her own cognitive differences. She found a distinct lack of visible role models or resources for creative dyslexics, particularly in Australia, leading her to investigate the strengths associated with dyslexia rather than its deficits. Her work explores the psychological and social aspects of living with dyslexia, including the "oscillation of [disclosure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disclosure)"—the internal debate about whether to reveal one's [neurodivergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurodivergence) due to persistent societal stigmas. Percy highlights that while dyslexia is the largest segment of neurodivergence, making up 10-20% of the population, many dyslexics are underemployed or face negative perceptions due to misconceptions about their intelligence or capability.

The exhibition itself is a multi-disciplinary exploration, reflecting Percy's diverse artistic practice. Artworks like "The Swing" and "Oscillation of Disclosure" utilize video and sculpture, often incorporating water imagery, to metaphorically represent the subconscious and the fluid, uncertain nature of disclosure. "The Real Thing," a [digital combine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_combine) incorporating [augmented reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmented_reality) (AR), delves into the concept of "[[concepts/masking|masking]]" or "passing" in neurodivergent individuals, illustrating how people might hide their challenges to fit in. Her photographic series featuring clouds and circles symbolize optimism and new perspectives, embodying the strengths of [visual-spatial awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual-spatial_awareness) and narrative [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] that often accompany dyslexia. Further, the painting series "Mountain of Words" and "The Space Between" visually translate the difficulty of reading by emphasizing the often-overlooked "white space" between letters, revealing new patterns and interpretations for viewers.

Ultimately, [[concepts/visual-arts|Kim Percy]]'s research and exhibition emphasize the unique capacity of [[concepts/visual-art|visual art]] to communicate complex internal experiences that language alone might obscure. By transforming abstract concepts of neurodiversity into tangible, accessible forms, her work opens a vital [[concepts/dialogue|dialogue]], fostering [[concepts/empathy|empathy]] and understanding. Her accompanying children's book, "The Girl Who Tried," written in an accessible [[concepts/style|style]] for adults and carers, further extends this conversation by illustrating the challenges and strengths of dyslexia. The project serves as a celebration of difference, [[concepts/resilience|resilience]], and creative strength, providing inspiration and a much-needed visible pathway for individuals navigating their own neurodivergent identities.
