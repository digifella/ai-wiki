# Psychological Safety

**Psychological safety** is a shared belief held by members of a team that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking. It allows individuals to ask questions, admit mistakes, and propose novel [[concepts/ideas|ideas]] without fear of negative consequences to self-image, status, or career.

## Core Definition & Origin
- Coined by **[[entities/amy-edmondson|Amy Edmondson]]** ([[entities/harvard-university|Harvard]] Business School).
- Distinct from interpersonal [[concepts/trust|trust]]; it is a group-level property, not an individual trait.
- Critical for [[concepts/learning|learning]] behavior, [[concepts/innovation]], and error reporting.
- Source reference: [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Psychological-Safety-Amy-C.-Edmondson|Psychological Safety – Amy C. Edmondson]] (amycedmondson.com).

## Key Mechanisms
- **Interpersonal Risk-Taking**: Willingness to speak up with suggestions, questions, concerns, or problems.
- **Contextual Intelligence**: Balancing confidence (personal efficacy) with humility (recognizing limits of one's knowledge).
- **Stigma Reduction**: Separating performance errors from competence failures.

## Leadership Drivers
- **[[concepts/inclusive-leadership|Inclusive Leadership]]**: Leaders actively model [[concepts/vulnerability|vulnerability]], invite input, and respond constructively to dissent to reinforce safety norms.
- Refer to [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-How-to-Drive-Psychological-Safety-Through-Inclusive-Lead|How to Drive Psychological Safety Through Inclusive Leadership]] for specific behavioral strategies.

## Application in High-Stakes Environments
- Essential in fields where errors have high consequences, such as [[concepts/health|Healthcare]], Aviation, and Finance.
- In medicine, lack of safety correlates with silent errors, delayed interventions, and poor [[concepts/medical-outcomes|patient outcomes]].
