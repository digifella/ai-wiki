# Kanban

Kanban is a project management methodology originating from lean manufacturing practices developed by Toyota in the 1940s. The term derives from Japanese and translates to "signboard" or "billboard." While initially designed for manufacturing workflows, Kanban has been widely adopted for knowledge work and software development. The methodology centers on visualizing work items, limiting concurrent tasks, and optimizing workflow to enhance efficiency and team responsiveness.

## Core Principles

The framework relies on visualizing the flow of work through a board, typically divided into columns that represent different stages of the process. This visual representation allows teams to identify bottlenecks and manage work-in-progress limits, ensuring that resources are not overallocated. By making the workflow explicit, teams can collaborate more effectively and maintain a steady pace of delivery.

## Evolution and Application

Originally implemented to synchronize production and reduce inventory, the principles of Kanban have been adapted for various industries beyond manufacturing. In software development and other knowledge-based fields, it serves as a method for continuous improvement rather than a rigid set of rules. Teams use Kanban to manage change, improve delivery speed, and increase the quality of output by focusing on the continuous flow of value.
