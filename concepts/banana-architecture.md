# Banana Architecture

Banana Architecture is an open-source design framework that applies distributed software development principles to hardware systems at miniaturized and nano scales. Emerging in the early 2010s from collaborative maker and research communities, the approach sought to democratize access to small-scale mechanical and electronic system design. It adapts established open-source software practices, including version control and modular component sharing, to the physical constraints and fabrication processes of micro-engineering.

## Core Principles

The framework emphasizes modularity and interoperability, allowing designers to assemble complex nano-scale systems from standardized, peer-reviewed components. By treating physical parts as reusable code, the architecture facilitates rapid iteration and collaborative debugging across global networks. This methodology reduces the barrier to entry for micro-fabrication, enabling researchers and hobbyists to contribute to and utilize advanced miniaturized technologies without requiring proprietary manufacturing infrastructure.

## Historical Context and Impact

Banana Architecture arose during a period of increased accessibility in digital fabrication tools, such as desktop 3D printers and CNC machines. It represented a shift toward decentralized innovation in the field of nanotechnology, challenging traditional top-down research models. Although the term has largely faded from mainstream technical discourse, its underlying concepts influenced later developments in open-hardware ecosystems and the standardization of modular micro-electromechanical systems (MEMS).
