# Four Year Experiment

A four-year experiment is an extended research, development, or manufacturing project characterized by sustained iterative work over a multi-year timeline. Such projects require repeated cycles of testing, refinement, and validation before achieving commercial viability or proof of concept. The extended duration allows teams to thoroughly test assumptions, identify unforeseen technical or operational challenges, and incrementally improve processes or products through accumulated learning rather than rushing to market with incomplete solutions.

## Business Applications

In business strategy, four-year experiments function as structured approaches to validating complex hypotheses about product-market fit, manufacturing feasibility, or operational efficiency. Organizations undertake such projects when the development challenge is sufficiently complex that shorter timelines would yield unreliable results, or when regulatory, safety, or quality requirements demand extensive validation periods. The commitment of resources across multiple years signals confidence in the underlying concept while building organizational capability and knowledge that extends beyond the immediate project outcome.

## Notable Example

Destin Sandlin's Smarter Every Day project executed a four-year experiment to design and manufacture an American-made grill brush as a safer alternative to traditional wire bristle brushes, which can shed bristles into food. Motivated by his family's background in union manufacturing, the project involved prototyping, material testing, tooling development, and establishing domestic production processes. The extended timeline allowed Sandlin to solve multiple engineering and manufacturing challenges while building a viable business model, ultimately resulting in a commercially available product.
