# AI Integrated Workflows

AI integrated workflows are systems that embed [[concepts/ai-agents|AI agents]] directly into local desktop environments to automate collaborative tasks. Rather than requiring users to switch between applications or interfaces, these workflows bring AI capabilities into the tools and spaces where work already happens. This approach aims to reduce context-switching and streamline the interaction between human [[entities/employees|workers]] and [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] by making AI assistance available within existing software applications.

## Integration Model

The core principle of AI integrated workflows is co-location of intelligence with existing work tools. Instead of operating as [[concepts/desktop-apps|standalone applications]], [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] like [[concepts/claude-ai|Claude]] operate as extensions or [[concepts/plugins|plugins]] within desktop environments, text editors, [[concepts/issue-trackers|project management tools]], and other professional software. This allows AI capabilities to be invoked directly within the context of ongoing work, with access to local files, documents, and application state without requiring manual data transfer between systems.

## Practical Applications

These workflows enable several categories of [[concepts/asana-integration|task automation]]: [[concepts/document-processing|document analysis]] and generation, code review and refactoring, research and [[concepts/information-synthesis|information synthesis]], and project [[concepts/coordination|coordination]] across team members. By maintaining [[concepts/continuity|continuity]] within familiar interfaces, users can [[concepts/leverage-ai|leverage AI]] assistance for routine or [[concepts/complex-tasks|complex tasks]] while maintaining their existing workflow patterns and tooling preferences.

## Considerations

Implementing AI integrated workflows involves managing API connections, [[concepts/privacy|data privacy]] for local processing, [[concepts/context-windows|context windows]] and token usage, and ensuring reliable integration with diverse desktop applications. The effectiveness of these systems depends on how seamlessly they embed into existing work practices and how well they handle the asynchronous nature of collaborative tasks.
