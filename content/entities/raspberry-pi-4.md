# Raspberry Pi 4

The Raspberry Pi 4 is a single-board computer (SBC) designed and manufactured by the [[entities/raspberry-pi|Raspberry Pi]] Foundation. Released in 2019, it features a quad-core ARM processor, configurable [[concepts/ram|RAM]] options (2GB, 4GB, or 8GB), and dual micro-HDMI ports for display [[concepts/output|output]]. The device runs Linux-based operating systems, primarily Raspberry Pi OS, though it also supports other distributions such as [[entities/ubuntu|Ubuntu]] and Fedora.

## Applications in Self-Hosting

The Raspberry Pi 4 has become a popular platform for self-hosting and personal server deployments due to its low [[concepts/power|power]] consumption, compact form factor, and reasonable processing capability. Users commonly run [[concepts/software|applications]] including home [[concepts/automation|automation]] systems, network [[entities/storage|storage]] solutions, media servers, and small-scale web services on the device. Its affordability makes it accessible for individuals seeking to establish private cloud infrastructure without significant capital investment.

## Technical Considerations

While capable for many self-hosting tasks, the Raspberry Pi 4 has limitations for resource-intensive workloads. Its ARM [[concepts/architecture|architecture]] and modest specifications [[entities/make|make]] it suitable for lightweight services but less appropriate for applications requiring substantial [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]]. Power management and thermal considerations are relevant for long-term deployments, particularly when [[concepts/running|running]] continuous server operations.
