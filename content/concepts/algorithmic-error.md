# Algorithmic Error

Algorithmic error refers to failures in automated decision-making systems that produce harmful, unjust, or unintended outcomes. These errors can arise from flawed logic, poor [[concepts/data-conceptsintegrityintegrity|data quality]], inadequate [[concepts/testing|testing]], or the inappropriate application of algorithms in contexts requiring human judgment. When algorithmic systems operate at scale—particularly in government, [[concepts/health|healthcare]], or financial services—errors can affect thousands of people simultaneously, amplifying the impact of mistakes that might otherwise remain isolated.

## The Robodebt Scheme

The Robodebt Scheme was an automated debt-recovery system operated by the Australian Department of Human Services between 2015 and 2019. The algorithm compared income data from the Australian Taxation Office with welfare records to identify recipients it deemed to have been overpaid. The system issued debt notices to approximately 400,000 people, many of whom had not actually been overpaid. The algorithm's core error was using annual income figures to estimate fortnightly welfare eligibility, failing to account for legitimate income variations throughout the year. The scheme was eventually ruled unlawful by the Federal Court, and the government committed to repaying all debts issued under the program.

The Robodebt Scheme exemplifies how algorithmic errors in welfare administration can have severe consequences, including financial hardship, [[concepts/mental-health|mental health]] deterioration, and loss of life. Multiple inquiries found the system was implemented without adequate testing or human oversight, and [[concepts/vulnerable-populations|vulnerable populations]]—including young people and those experiencing homelessness—were disproportionately affected. The case demonstrated that [[concepts/decision-making|algorithmic decision-making]] in social welfare contexts requires robust safeguards, transparency, and human review rather than fully automated processing.
