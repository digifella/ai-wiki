# Human Lunar Flyby

[[entities/artemis-ii|Artemis II]] is a crewed [[concepts/lunar-flyby|lunar flyby]] mission planned by [[concepts/nasa|NASA]] as the second flight of the [[concepts/artemis-program|Artemis program]]. The mission [[entities/will|will]] carry a crew of four astronauts aboard the [[entities/orion|Orion spacecraft]], which sits atop a Space Launch System (SLS) rocket. Unlike Artemis I, an uncrewed test flight completed in 2022, Artemis II will demonstrate the spacecraft's systems and life support [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] with human occupants aboard. The mission is scheduled for 2026 and will follow a similar trajectory to its predecessor, traveling to lunar orbit and returning to Earth without landing on the surface.

## Mission Profile

The flight will take approximately ten days from launch to splashdown. Artemis II will send the crew on a path that carries them around the Moon [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] a distance of approximately 4,600 nautical miles from the lunar surface, allowing for observation and photography of the Moon while remaining safely beyond the lunar [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]] well. This trajectory serves as a critical test of the [[concepts/trans-lunar-injection|trans-lunar injection]], course correction, and return procedures that will inform future crewed [[concepts/lunar-landing|lunar landing]] missions under the Artemis program.

## Objectives and Significance

Artemis II serves as an operational validation of human-rated deep [[concepts/space-exploration|space exploration]] systems before NASA [[concepts/commits|commits]] to landing astronauts on the lunar surface in subsequent missions. The flight will test Orion's thermal protection systems, propulsion systems, and crew systems in the lunar environment, gathering data on radiation [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], microgravity physiology, and vehicle performance. Success on this mission is a prerequisite for the Artemis III landing mission planned for the late 2020s.
