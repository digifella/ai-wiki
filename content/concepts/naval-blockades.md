# Naval Blockades

A naval blockade is a military operation in which a naval force restricts or prevents maritime traffic to and from a geographic area, typically a port, coastline, or entire nation. Blockades aim to deny an adversary access to seaborne trade, military supplies, and economic resources. They are considered an act of war under international law and have been employed throughout modern military history as both a standalone strategy and as part of broader military campaigns.

## Strategic Purpose and Effects

Naval blockades serve multiple strategic objectives: disrupting an opponent's economy by cutting off trade, preventing the [[concepts/exercise|movement]] of military materiel and reinforcements, and compelling political or military concessions through economic pressure. The effectiveness of a blockade depends on the blockading force's ability to maintain continuous presence, the target nation's dependence on maritime trade, and the availability of alternative supply routes. Historical examples demonstrate that sustained blockades can inflict significant economic damage over time, though they typically require substantial naval resources to maintain.

## Anti-Access and Area Denial

In contemporary military doctrine, naval blockades function as a component of anti-access and area denial ([[concepts/a2ad|A2/AD]]) strategies. A2/AD operations use a combination of military [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]]—including naval forces, air defenses, and missile systems—to prevent an adversary from entering or operating freely within a defined maritime region. Naval blockades represent the sea [[concepts/power|control]] dimension of such strategies, physically restricting movement while complementary systems deny the freedom to operate within contested waters.
