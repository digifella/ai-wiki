# Persistent Limitations In Accurately Translating Simulation Training To Real

[[concepts/simulation|Simulation]] [[concepts/training|training]] has become a standard tool across entertainment, [[concepts/gaming|gaming]], and professional development, offering a controlled environment to practice [[concepts/skills|skills]] before real-world application. However, a significant gap often exists between performance in simulated environments and actual execution in real [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]]. This transfer problem—sometimes called the "sim-to-real gap"—occurs because simulations, by necessity, abstract and simplify complex real-world conditions, potentially creating training that does not fully prepare participants for authentic situations.

## Environmental and Sensory Factors

Simulations typically cannot perfectly replicate all sensory inputs, environmental variability, and contextual pressures present in real settings. Visual fidelity, [[concepts/audio-modality|audio]] cues, tactile [[concepts/feedback|feedback]], and environmental [[concepts/physics|physics]] may be approximated rather than fully accurate. Additionally, real-world scenarios often involve unexpected elements, weather conditions, equipment failures, or human factors that simulations either omit or represent only partially. Trainees may perform well within a simulation's predictable [[concepts/parameters|parameters]] while struggling when encountering situations outside the simulation's [[concepts/design|design]] scope.

## Psychological and Behavioral Transfer

The psychological context of simulation training differs fundamentally from real performance. Simulation typically carries lower stakes, reducing [[concepts/stress|stress]] and emotional factors that [[concepts/power|influence]] [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] and execution in genuine situations. Trainees may develop [[concepts/habits|habits]] or strategies optimized for simulated conditions rather than those that generalize across real contexts. Overconfidence based on strong simulation performance can also hinder the [[concepts/adaptive-learning|adaptive learning]] necessary when confronted with actual real-world complexity and consequence.
