# Digital Scanning

Digital scanning is the process of converting physical film negatives or [[entities/google-slides|slides]] into digital image [[concepts/files|files]] using specialized equipment and [[concepts/software|software]]. This technique allows photographers and enthusiasts to preserve and work with analog film archives in digital formats, making them compatible with modern editing, [[entities/storage|storage]], and sharing systems.

## Equipment and Methods

[[concepts/camera-scanning|Home film scanning]] typically involves dedicated film scanners or flatbed scanners with transparency adapters. The choice of equipment depends on the format being scanned—35mm negatives and [[concepts/google-slides|slides]] have different dimensional requirements than [[entities/medium|medium]] or large format film. The scanning process requires careful [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to resolution, color [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]], and dust removal to produce high-quality digital files from the original film originals.

## Applications

Digital scanning serves multiple purposes: [[concepts/archival-preservation|archival preservation]] of aging film materials, quality enhancement through post-processing, and [[concepts/integration|integration]] with digital workflows. Photographers often scan film to create backups, to prepare [[concepts/images|images]] for printing or publication, or to combine film photography with digital editing techniques. The [[concepts/skill|skill]] is relevant both for professional archivists managing large collections and for individual photographers maintaining personal film libraries.
