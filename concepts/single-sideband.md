# Single Sideband

**Single Sideband** (SSB) is a modulation technique used in radio communications that transmits only one sideband of the signal, suppressing the carrier and the other sideband. This method significantly improves [[concepts/energy-efficiency|power efficiency]] and [[concepts/network-speed|bandwidth]] utilization compared to [[concepts/quadrature-amplitude-modulation-qam|Amplitude Modulation]] (AM).

## Core Principles
- **Bandwidth Efficiency**: SSB occupies approximately half the bandwidth of standard AM, allowing more channels in the same frequency spectrum.
- **Power Efficiency**: By eliminating the carrier wave and one sideband, more power is concentrated into the information-carrying sideband.
- **Signal Reconstruction**: Requires precise frequency stability at the receiver to correctly demodulate the signal, often involving a Beat Frequency Oscillator (BFO).

## Historical Context & Evolution
- Originated in the early 20th century as a [[concepts/solution|solution]] to the inefficiencies of AM.
- Gained prominence in Ham Radio communities for long-distance communication due to its [[concepts/clarity-slider|clarity]] and range.
- The technology required precise RF-filter designs to isolate the desired sideband, a challenge that drove significant [[concepts/engineering-innovation|engineering innovation]].

## Modern Relevance: From Hobby to Smartphone
The [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] challenges faced by radio hobbyists in the 1950s regarding SSB filtering directly influenced modern telecommunications hardware.
- **Origin Story**: Critical RF-filter components used in modern smartphones originated from problems faced by radio hobbyists in the 1950s who needed to isolate single sidebands.
- **Technological Transfer**: The [[concepts/accuracy|precision]] manufacturing and [[concepts/design|design principles]] developed for ham radio SSB filters were adapted for mass-market [[concepts/consumer-grade-hardware|consumer electronics]].
- **Ubiquity**: These filters are now essential components in every smartphone, enabling clear [[concepts/tone|voice]] and data transmission in mobile networks.
