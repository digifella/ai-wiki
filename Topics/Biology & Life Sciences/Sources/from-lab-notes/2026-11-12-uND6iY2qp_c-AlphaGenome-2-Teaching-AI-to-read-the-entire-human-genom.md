### Summary
The video explores the integration of artificial intelligence with genomics through AlphaGenome, a model developed by Google DeepMind to predict the function of non-coding DNA and understand genetic variation at the cellular level. 

Key points discussed:
- AlphaGenome is designed to read DNA sequences and predict cellular activities and the impacts of genetic mutations, moving beyond coding DNA to encompass the 98% of the genome that regulates gene expression.
- The model helps bridge the gap between genotype and phenotype by predicting how specific genetic variants affect different cell types and contribute to diseases.
- AlphaGenome aims to improve the understanding of complex genetic diseases and rare conditions, providing a research tool to prioritize and interpret genetic variants.
- The speakers discuss the challenges and future of AI in biology, emphasizing the complexity of biological systems and the need for high-quality data to improve model accuracy.

The video concludes that while AI models like AlphaGenome provide valuable insights and tools for researchers, fully simulating biology and predicting the exact outcomes of genetic variations remains a complex and ongoing challenge.

#### Description
98% of human DNA doesn't build proteins—it controls how our cells work, and holds vital clues to why diseases happen and why 90% of new drugs fail in clinical trials. Understanding the genome at the molecular level is the key to deciphering the blueprint of human life.

In this episode, Professor Hannah Fry speaks with Google DeepMind's Žiga Avsec and Adam Gayoso about AlphaGenome, the AlphaGenome Atlas, and AlphaGenome 2—exploring how AI reads non-coding DNA down to single-cell resolution to pinpoint disease-causing mutations and help researchers solve medical mysteries.
