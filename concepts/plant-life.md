# Plant Life

During the Carboniferous and Permian periods, approximately 360 to 250 million years ago, vast swamps and forests covered significant portions of Earth's landmass. The decomposition and subsequent burial of this extensive plant material, alongside marine organisms, created the organic deposits that would eventually become fossil fuels such as coal, oil, and natural gas. This massive accumulation of biomass represented an unprecedented concentration of carbon within the geological record.

## Atmospheric Changes

The formation of these fossil fuel deposits fundamentally altered Earth's atmospheric composition. As organic matter accumulated in anaerobic environments, it prevented the release of carbon back into the atmosphere through normal decomposition processes. This sequestration led to a significant increase in atmospheric oxygen levels and a corresponding decrease in carbon dioxide concentrations, creating distinct climatic and environmental conditions that influenced the evolution of terrestrial and marine life during this era.
