# USB 2.0

USB 2.0 is a widely adopted standard for data and [[concepts/power|power]] transfer between computers and peripherals. Released in 2000, it offered significantly faster data transfer rates than its predecessor USB 1.1, supporting speeds up to 480 Mbps. This standard became the dominant [[concepts/connection|connection]] method for keyboards, mice, printers, external hard drives, and countless other devices throughout the 2000s and 2010s, remaining in use today despite the emergence of newer [[concepts/open-standards|standards]].

## Modern Cable Compatibility

While USB 2.0 defined the protocol, the physical connectors and cables evolved over time. [[entities/usb-c-port|USB-C]] connectors, introduced later as a more reversible and versatile alternative, can technically carry USB 2.0 data signals, though not all USB-C cables support full USB 2.0 speeds. Compatibility between USB 2.0 devices and newer USB 3.x hubs or adapters is generally maintained through backward compatibility, though users may encounter performance limitations or functionality issues depending on the specific [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] and cable quality involved.

## Relevance and Decline

USB 2.0 remains functional on most computers and continues to power many legacy devices. However, its relatively slow transfer speeds—particularly for high-bandwidth [[concepts/software|applications]] like [[concepts/portable-ssds|external SSDs]] or video equipment—have led to gradual displacement by [[concepts/usb-30|USB 3.0]], USB 3.1, and USB-C standards. Most modern computers still include [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] least one USB 2.0 or backward-compatible port, ensuring continued interoperability with older peripherals.
