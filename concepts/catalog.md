# Catalog

A catalog in Lightroom is a database file that stores all organizational information about your image collection. It maintains records of metadata, keywords, ratings, flags, and editing adjustments for every photo you import. Rather than altering the original image files themselves, Lightroom keeps a separate catalog that references your photos and logs all modifications non-destructively. This architecture is fundamental to Lightroom's workflow, allowing you to experiment with edits without risk to your original files.

## Structure and Function

Each catalog is a self-contained database that acts as a central hub for your photographic library. When you import images, Lightroom creates references to their locations and begins tracking all subsequent organizational decisions and edits. The catalog stores this information independently, which means you can move your original image files around on your hard drive without breaking Lightroom's connection to them—as long as you move them through Lightroom's interface rather than directly through your file system.

## Multiple Catalogs

While many photographers work with a single catalog for their entire collection, Lightroom supports creating multiple catalogs for different purposes. Some photographers maintain separate catalogs for different projects, clients, or shooting styles. However, managing multiple catalogs requires deliberate organization, as photos in one catalog are not automatically visible in another.

## Maintenance and Optimization

Regular catalog maintenance helps preserve performance, especially as your collection grows. This includes backing up your catalog file regularly, optimizing it periodically to maintain speed, and ensuring your catalog and image files remain properly linked. Understanding your catalog's location and structure is essential for long-term digital asset management.
