# Neuroscience

Neuroscience is the scientific discipline that studies the nervous system, encompassing the brain, spinal cord, and peripheral nerves, along with the cellular and molecular mechanisms that underlie their function. It draws on methods and theories from biology, chemistry, [[concepts/physics|physics]], psychology, and medicine to investigate how the nervous system processes information, generates behavior, and produces consciousness and [[concepts/cognition|cognition]].

## Scope and Methods

Neuroscience operates across multiple [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] of analysis. At the molecular level, researchers examine neurotransmitters and ion channels; at the cellular level, they study individual neurons and glial cells; at the systems level, they investigate how brain regions interact; and at the behavioral level, they explore how neural activity correlates with perception, emotion, and action. Modern neuroscience employs diverse experimental techniques including electrophysiology, neuroimaging (fMRI, PET), genetic manipulation, computational modeling, and behavioral [[concepts/testing|testing]].

## Major Research Areas

Key areas of neuroscience inquiry include sensory processing, motor control, [[concepts/learning|learning]] and [[concepts/memory|memory]], [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]], language, decision-making, and the biological bases of psychiatric and neurological disorders. Understanding brain development, neural plasticity, and how experience shapes neural circuits remains an active frontier. Applied neuroscience also contributes to the development of treatments for conditions such as Parkinson's disease, Alzheimer's disease, and [[concepts/depression|depression]].
