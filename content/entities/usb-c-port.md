# USB-C Port

A USB-C port is a universal connector standard used for charging, data transfer, and video [[concepts/output|output]] on modern electronic devices. It features a small, reversible [[concepts/design|design]] that can be inserted in either orientation, eliminating the frustration of traditional directional USB connectors. The USB-C standard supports multiple protocols and [[concepts/power|power]] delivery specifications, making it highly versatile across different device categories.

## Power and Data Capabilities

USB-C ports can deliver varying levels of power depending on the Power Delivery (PD) specification supported by the device. This ranges from standard charging for smartphones and tablets to high-power delivery for laptops and larger peripherals. The connector simultaneously supports high-[[concepts/speed|speed]] data transfer, with speeds varying based on the USB version ([[concepts/usb-30|USB 3.0]], 3.1, or Thunderbolt) implemented in the specific port.

## Adoption and Standardization

Major electronics manufacturers have increasingly adopted USB-C as a standard port across smartphones, tablets, laptops, and accessories. Regulatory bodies in various regions have mandated USB-C as a common charging standard to reduce electronic waste and improve consumer convenience. This widespread [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] has made [[concepts/usb-20|USB-C cables]] and accessories widely available and interchangeable across different brands and device types.
