# Martin Luther King Jr

Martin Luther King Jr. (1929–1968) was an American Baptist minister and civil rights activist who became the most prominent leader of the Civil Rights Movement during the 1950s and 1960s. Born in Atlanta, Georgia, he emerged as a central figure in the struggle against racial segregation and discrimination in the United States, particularly in the South. King's approach was grounded in nonviolent resistance and civil disobedience, principles he adopted from Mahatma Gandhi's philosophy.

## Major Contributions

King's most significant achievements include organizing and leading major demonstrations such as the 1955 Montgomery bus boycott and the 1963 March on Washington, where he delivered his iconic "I Have a Dream" speech. He played a pivotal role in the passage of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965. In 1964, he was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for his nonviolent struggle for racial equality in the United States.

## Legacy

King was assassinated in Memphis, Tennessee, in 1968, an event that sparked nationwide mourning and further accelerated the civil rights agenda. His philosophy of nonviolent resistance continues to influence social justice movements globally. In the United States, Martin Luther King Jr. Day was established as a federal holiday in 1986 to honor his life and contributions to American history.
