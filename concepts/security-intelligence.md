# Security Intelligence

**[[concepts/security|Security]] Intelligence** refers to the knowledge gained from the collection, processing, analysis, and interpretation of information related to current or potential threats against an organization's assets. It transforms raw data into [[concepts/actionable-insights|actionable insights]] to support risk management and defensive postures.

## Core Components

- **[[concepts/threat-intelligence|Threat Intelligence]]**: Data about external threats, including adversary TTPs (Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures).
- **[[concepts/vulnerability|Vulnerability]] Intelligence**: Information regarding weaknesses in systems, software, and configurations.
- **Contextual Analysis**: Correlating threat and vulnerability data with asset criticality and business impact.

## Emerging Domains

### AI/LLM Security
The rapid [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of [[concepts/large-language-model]]s and [[concepts/ai-technologies|Artificial Intelligence]] has introduced novel attack surfaces. Security intelligence must now encompass model-specific vulnerabilities.

- **Adversarial AI**: Techniques such as prompt injection, data poisoning, and model inversion.
- **Discovery Methodologies**: New frameworks are required to identify flaws in AI [[concepts/supply-chains|supply chains]] and [[concepts/model-behavior|model behavior]].

### Open Source Security & AI Integration
Recent developments in [[entities/ibm]] and [[entities/red-hat]] initiatives highlight the convergence of AI and open source [[concepts/governance|governance]].

- **[[entities/project-lightwell|Project Lightwell]]**: An initiative leveraging AI to enhance security within [[concepts/open-source]] ecosystems, addressing vulnerabilities and [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] at scale.
- **Podcast Insights**: The IBM Security Intelligence podcast has covered the integration of AI into enterprise security landscapes, specifically focusing on how automated analysis improves threat detection in open source libraries.
