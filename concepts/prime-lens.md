# Prime Lens

A [[concepts/prime-number|prime]] lens is a camera lens with a single, fixed [[concepts/focal-length|focal length]] that cannot zoom. Unlike zoom lenses, which offer variable focal lengths across a range, [[entities/prime-lenses|prime lenses]] maintain one unchanging perspective angle. This fixed design simplifies the optical system, requiring fewer internal lens elements than zoom lenses need to achieve their variable functionality.

## Optical Performance

Prime lenses generally deliver superior optical quality compared to zoom lenses at equivalent price points. With fewer glass elements to manage, they typically exhibit less distortion, better [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], and sharper image quality across the frame. The simpler optical path also allows manufacturers to optimize performance for a specific focal length rather than compromising across a range of focal lengths.

## Practical Considerations

The main limitation of prime lenses is their lack of flexibility. Photographers must physically move closer to or farther from their subject to change [[concepts/writing|composition]], rather than adjusting the lens itself. However, this constraint has practical benefits: prime lenses are usually more compact and lightweight than comparable zoom lenses, and they often feature wider maximum apertures, making them better suited for low-[[concepts/light|light]] photography and achieving shallow depth-of-field effects.

Prime lenses remain popular among professional photographers and enthusiasts despite the convenience of modern zoom lenses, valued for their optical performance, portability, and cost-effectiveness across many focal lengths when purchased as a collection.
