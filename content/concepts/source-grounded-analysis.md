# Source Grounded Analysis

Source Grounded Analysis is a research methodology that requires conclusions and claims to be directly supported by documented sources. Rather than making assertions based on general knowledge or [[concepts/inference|inference]], this approach maintains explicit connections between findings and their source materials throughout the analytical process. This practice increases transparency, enables verification, and reduces unsupported speculation in research and [[concepts/writing|writing]].

## Tools and Implementation

Digital tools facilitate source grounded analysis by organizing source materials alongside analytical work. Platforms like [[entities/gartner|Google NotebookLM]] enable researchers to upload documents, articles, and other sources, then generate analyses, summaries, and synthesis that reference the original materials. Such tools can help identify connections across multiple sources and flag analytical claims that lack documentary support.

## Related Approaches

Source grounded analysis shares methodological similarities with evidence-based practices in academic research, fact-checking workflows, and citation-dependent scholarship. The approach is particularly useful in fields where credibility depends on traceability, including journalism, academic writing, legal analysis, and policy research. The methodology complements both traditional source evaluation and emerging AI-assisted research synthesis by maintaining the requirement that [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] remain anchored to documented materials rather than generated content alone.
