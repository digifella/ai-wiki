# Technological Viability

**Technological viability** refers to the assessment of whether a technology can be successfully developed, scaled, and sustained within physical, economic, and [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] constraints. It serves as a critical filter for emerging energy technologies, distinguishing between theoretical possibility and practical implementation.

## Key Challenges in Fusion Energy

The viability of [[concepts/nuclear-fusion]] as a commercial [[concepts/energy-source|energy source]] is heavily scrutinized due to historical delays and immense capital requirements. Recent critiques highlight specific hurdles for major players like Helion Energy.

Despite significant private investment, the technical feasibility of Helion's approach [[concepts/faces|faces]] substantial skepticism from the [[concepts/physics|physics]] community.

## Assessment Criteria

To determine if a technology is truly viable, it must pass the following filters:

1.  **Physical Plausibility:** Does the underlying [[concepts/science|science]] hold up under [[concepts/document-review|peer review]] and independent critique?
2.  **Engineering Scalability:** Can the components be manufactured and assembled at the required scale?
3.  **Economic Sustainability:** Is the cost of energy produced competitive with existing alternatives?
4.  **Timeline Realism:** Are development milestones based on empirical data rather than optimistic projections?
