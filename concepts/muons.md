# Muons

Muons are fundamental particles belonging to the lepton family, similar to electrons but approximately 207 times more massive. They are produced when cosmic rays collide with Earth's atmosphere and decay into electrons and neutrinos with a mean lifetime of about 2.2 microseconds. Despite their brief existence, muons penetrate deep into Earth's atmosphere and reach the surface, making them detectable in particle physics experiments.

## Magnetic Properties

The magnetic moment of the muon is a critical parameter in testing the Standard Model of particle physics. The anomalous magnetic dipole moment, often denoted as $a_\mu$, quantifies the deviation from the Dirac equation prediction due to quantum loop corrections involving virtual particles. Precise measurement of this value allows physicists to probe for discrepancies that may indicate new physics beyond current theoretical frameworks.

## Muon g-2 Experiment

The Muon g-2 experiment at Fermilab investigates the muon's magnetic properties through specific methodologies involving a storage ring and a superconducting magnet. The experiment measures the precession frequency of muons in a magnetic field to determine the anomaly with high precision. Upcoming results scheduled for 2025 are expected to further refine these measurements, potentially reducing uncertainties and providing more stringent tests of the Standard Model against experimental data.
