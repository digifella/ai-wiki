# Milky Way

The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy with a diameter of approximately 100,000 light-years, housing an estimated 100 to 400 billion stars. Our solar system resides within the Orion Arm, one of the galaxy's major spiral structures, located roughly 26,000 light-years from the galactic center. The visible architecture of the galaxy comprises a central bar-shaped core, distinct spiral arms extending outward, and a flattened disk composed of stars and interstellar gas.

## Structure and Composition

The galaxy's visible components are organized into a central bulge surrounded by a thin disk. This disk contains the majority of the Milky Way's young stars, gas, and dust, which form the characteristic spiral pattern. Extending from the central bar are several major spiral arms, including the Perseus and Scutum-Centaurus arms, which serve as regions of active star formation. The overall shape is defined by the gravitational dynamics of these visible elements interacting within the galactic plane.

## Dark Matter and Mass Distribution

Surrounding the visible disk and halo is an extensive spherical halo composed of older stars and a significant concentration of dark matter. This dark matter halo extends far beyond the visible boundaries of the galaxy and accounts for the majority of its total mass. The presence of this dark matter is inferred from the rotation curves of the galaxy, which indicate that visible matter alone cannot account for the observed gravitational forces holding the galaxy together.
