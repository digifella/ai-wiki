# Salmon Poaching

Salmon poaching is a classical French cooking technique in which salmon fillets or steaks are gently simmered in a flavored liquid and served with a cream-based sauce. The method originates from Normandy, a region in northern France renowned for both its salmon fisheries and cider production. This traditional approach emphasizes delicate, moist results through controlled application of low to medium heat, allowing the fish to cook gently while absorbing subtle flavors from the poaching liquid.

## Technique and Preparation

The poaching liquid typically consists of cider, wine, or a combination of both, often enhanced with aromatics such as shallots, herbs, and court-bouillon. The salmon is placed in the liquid, which is brought to a gentle simmer rather than a rolling boil. This controlled temperature prevents the fish from becoming tough or dry. The cooking time varies depending on the thickness of the fish, usually ranging from 8 to 15 minutes. Once cooked through, the salmon is carefully removed and kept warm while the poaching liquid is reduced and incorporated into a cream sauce, often finished with butter and seasoning.

## Serving and Variations

The finished dish is traditionally served with the cream sauce spooned over the salmon, sometimes garnished with fresh herbs or lemon. The combination of poached salmon with a cider cream sauce reflects the regional ingredients and culinary traditions of Normandy. Modern variations may incorporate different aromatics or finish the sauce with Dijon mustard or fresh dill, while maintaining the fundamental principle of gentle, moist cooking and cream-based presentation.
