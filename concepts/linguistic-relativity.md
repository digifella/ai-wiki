# Linguistic Relativity

**Linguistic relativity**, often referred to as the [[concepts/language-influence-on-thought-and-perception|Sapir-Whorf hypothesis]], is the principle that a [[entities/speaker|speaker]]'s language influences their worldview or [[concepts/human-cognition|cognitive processes]]. It posits that the structure of a language affects its speakers' [[entities/earth|world]] view or [[concepts/cognition|cognition]], beyond just communication functions.

## Core Principles
- **Linguistic Determinism (Strong Version):** Language determines thought; concepts without linguistic labels are unthinkable. Largely discredited in contemporary [[concepts/science|science]].
- **Linguistic Relativity (Weak Version):** Language influences thought and perception, biasing cognitive [[concepts/habits|habits]] but not strictly determining them. Supported by various cross-[[concepts/cultural-anthropology|cultural studies]].

## Key Evidence & Examples
- [[concepts/spatial-perception|Spatial Perception]]: Speakers of languages using cardinal directions (e.g., [[entities/guugu-yimithirr|Guugu Yimithirr]]) maintain superior absolute orientation compared to speakers using relative directions (left/right). See detailed analysis in [[lab-notes/2026-06-11-Linguistic-Relativity-How-Native-Language-Shapes-Thought|Linguistic Relativity: How Native Language Shapes Thought and Spatial Perception]].
- Color Perception: Languages with fewer color terms may result in slower discrimination between shades that fall across category boundaries.
- **Grammatical Gender:** Speakers of gendered languages may attribute different characteristics to objects based on their grammatical gender.
