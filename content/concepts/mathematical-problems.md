# Mathematical Problems

Mathematical problems form the computational foundation of cryptographic security systems. These problems are carefully selected for their computational difficulty—easy to verify when solved, but extremely hard to solve without specific knowledge or keys. The security of cryptographic systems depends on the assumption that no efficient algorithm exists to solve these underlying mathematical problems within a practical timeframe.

## Classical vs. Post-Quantum Approaches

Traditional cryptographic systems, such as RSA and elliptic curve [[concepts/cryptography|cryptography]], rely on problems like integer factorization and discrete logarithms. These problems remain difficult for classical computers but are vulnerable to [[concepts/quantum-computing|quantum computing]] attacks. [[concepts/post-quantum-security|Post-quantum cryptography]] seeks alternative mathematical problems that resist both classical and quantum computational approaches.

## Lattice-Based Problems

[[concepts/lattice-cryptography|Lattice cryptography]] addresses post-quantum security concerns by basing its strength on lattice problems, such as the shortest vector problem (SVP) and [[concepts/learning|learning]] with errors (LWE). These problems appear to remain hard even for quantum computers, making them suitable candidates for long-term data security in a potential quantum computing era. Lattice-based schemes have gained significant [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] from cryptographic research communities and standardization bodies as viable replacements for current public-key systems.
