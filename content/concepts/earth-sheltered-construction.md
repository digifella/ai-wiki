# Earth Sheltered Construction

Earth sheltered construction is a building method in which structures are partially or fully buried underground or covered with earth material to provide thermal mass and environmental protection. By using soil as an insulating layer, these buildings maintain more stable interior temperatures year-[[concepts/rounding|round]], reducing heating and cooling demands. The earth surrounding the [[concepts/structure|structure]] acts as a thermal buffer, moderating temperature fluctuations that occur [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] the surface.

## Design and Implementation

Earth sheltered buildings [[concepts/range|range]] from partially submerged structures with one or more exposed walls to completely underground facilities. Common designs include bermed construction, where earth is piled against exterior walls, and fully underground designs that require careful [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to waterproofing and ventilation. Natural [[concepts/light|light]] and air circulation are managed through strategically placed [[entities/windows|windows]], skylights, and [[concepts/hardware|mechanical systems]]. The method is applicable to residential homes, commercial buildings, and large-scale facilities.

## Climate and Geological Considerations

The effectiveness of earth sheltered construction depends on local soil composition, groundwater conditions, and climate patterns. In stable geological areas with appropriate drainage, the technique can significantly reduce energy consumption. However, regions with high water tables, expansive soils, or seismic activity may present engineering challenges. The method is particularly advantageous in extreme climates, both hot and cold, where ground temperatures remain more moderate than surface air temperatures.
