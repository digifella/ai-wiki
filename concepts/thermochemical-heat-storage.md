# Thermochemical Heat Storage

**Thermochemical Heat [[entities/storage|Storage]] (TCS)** is a method of [[concepts/storing|storing]] thermal energy by utilizing reversible chemical reactions. Unlike sensible or latent heat storage, TCS stores energy in the chemical bonds of materials, allowing for long-term storage with negligible heat losses.

## Core Principles
- **Mechanism**: Energy is stored via endothermic reactions (charging) and released via exothermic reactions (discharging).
- **Advantages**: High energy [[concepts/density|density]], no standby losses, and suitability for seasonal storage.
- **Materials**: Common sorbent/sorbate pairs include salt hydrates, [[concepts/metal|metal]] oxides, and zeolites.
