# Compute Capacity

The total quantifiable processing power (CPU, GPU, TPU) available within a distributed system or cloud environment to execute computational workloads. It serves as the primary physical bottleneck for [[entities/ai]] [[concepts/training-process|model training]] and large-scale [[concepts/inference|inference]].

## Core Components
- **Hardware Architectures**: Includes general-purpose GPUs (e.g., [[entities/nvidia]]) and application-specific integrated circuits like [[entities/tpus|TPUs]].
- **Infrastructure [[concepts/scaling|Scaling]]**: The ability of [[concepts/cloud-computing]] environments to provision [[entities/high-performance|high-performance]] clusters on demand.

## Strategic Trends & Developments
- **Specialized [[concepts/silicon|Silicon]]**: Increasing strategic focus on [[concepts/tpu-development|TPU development]] to optimize [[concepts/computing-architecture|AI infrastructure]] efficiency and performance-per-watt.
- **Ecosystem Dependencies**: The necessity of [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]] capacity to support the massive training requirements of major model developers (e.g., [[entities/anthropic]]).
- **Economic [[concepts/causes|Drivers]]**: The shift in Monetization Strategy for cloud providers, focusing on the orchestration and availability of specialized, high-demand hardware.
- **Hardware Competition**: The interplay between general-purpose hardware (e.g., [[entities/nvidia]]) and custom-designed, proprietary [[concepts/silicon|silicon]] in large-scale AI workloads.
