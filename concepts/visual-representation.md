# Visual Representation

Visual representation serves as a bridge between internal experience and external communication, particularly valuable when conveying conditions that are often misunderstood or invisible to others. By transforming subjective or abstract phenomena into tangible forms, visual representation makes the private public and the intangible concrete, enabling both personal insight and shared understanding. This function is especially important for conditions like dyslexia, where the lived experience—characterized by specific perceptual and cognitive challenges—remains largely invisible to those without the condition.

## Art as Documentation of Experience

Artist Kim Percy has used visual art to document and communicate the subjective experience of dyslexia. Through her work, Percy translates the internal processing differences associated with dyslexia into observable visual forms, creating pieces that help viewers without dyslexia understand what dyslexic perception and cognition might involve. This approach moves beyond clinical or medical representation, instead offering a firsthand account rendered through artistic practice. By creating visual artifacts based on her own experience, Percy contributes to a broader cultural conversation about neurodiversity and how different minds process information.

## Function in Collective Awareness

When artists visualize conditions like dyslexia, they create opportunities for collective awareness and validation. Visual representation of personal experience can help others with the same condition recognize their own experiences reflected back to them, while simultaneously educating those without the condition. This dual function—personal recognition and public education—makes visual art a distinct form of communication about invisible conditions, one that operates beyond diagnostic language or scientific explanation.
