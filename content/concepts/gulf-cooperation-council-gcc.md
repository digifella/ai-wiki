# Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC)

The [[concepts/gulf-cooperation-council|Gulf Cooperation Council]] is a regional intergovernmental [[concepts/organization|organization]] established to promote political and economic cooperation among its member states in the Persian Gulf region. The organization comprises six nations: [[entities/saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, and [[entities/kuwait|Kuwait]]. Founded in 1981, the GCC serves as a framework for coordinated policy-making and collective action on matters of shared regional interest.

## Freshwater Resources and Desalination

The GCC member states face significant challenges related to freshwater availability, as most Gulf countries have limited [[concepts/renewable-freshwater-resources|renewable freshwater resources]] due to their arid climates. To address water security, GCC nations have become heavily dependent on [[concepts/desalination-technology|desalination technology]] to produce potable water from seawater. This reliance on desalination has made the region a major consumer of energy-intensive water production methods, with discussions within the GCC framework addressing both the technological and energy demands of large-scale desalination operations.
