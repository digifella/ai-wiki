# Galaxy Motion

Galaxy Motion refers to the study of kinematic patterns and dynamics exhibited by galaxies, particularly the [[concepts/milky-way|Milky Way]]. Understanding galactic motion requires accounting for the gravitational influence of all matter present, including the [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] that comprises approximately 85% of the universe's matter content. Observations of galactic rotation curves, stellar velocities, and large-scale structure movements provide crucial data for reconstructing the mass distribution within and around galaxies.

## Observational Methods

Astronomers study galaxy motion through multiple complementary observations. Spectroscopic measurements of stellar and gas velocities reveal rotation curves that extend far beyond the visible disk, indicating the presence of unseen mass. Proper motions of [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]], measured through astrometry, track individual stellar trajectories within the galactic system. On larger [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]], redshift surveys map the three-dimensional distribution and velocities of galaxies across cosmic space, revealing patterns of gravitational influence and structure formation.

## Dark Matter Constraints

The dynamics of galactic motion provide indirect evidence for dark matter's existence and distribution. Rotation curve observations show that galaxies rotate too quickly at their outer edges to be explained by visible matter alone, implying substantial dark matter halos. Recent observations of gamma-ray emissions from the Milky Way's [[concepts/galactic-center|galactic center]] have been analyzed for signatures potentially consistent with dark matter [[concepts/particle-dynamics|particle interactions]], such as [[concepts/wimp|WIMP]] (Weakly Interacting Massive Particle) annihilation, though interpretations remain debated among cosmologists.

## Broader Implications

Galaxy motion studies contribute to understanding cosmic evolution and structure formation. Galactic motions relative to the [[concepts/cosmic-microwave-background|cosmic microwave background]] reveal our own motion through space, while peculiar velocities of nearby galaxies help map the large-scale gravitational field. These measurements inform models of dark matter distribution and constrain theories of [[concepts/galaxy-formation|galaxy formation]] and merger histories across [[concepts/cosmic-time|cosmic time]].
