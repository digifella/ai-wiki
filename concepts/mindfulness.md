# Mindfulness

Mindfulness is a mental practice involving deliberate focusing of [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] on present-moment [[concepts/experience|experience]] without judgment or elaboration. Rather than engaging with thoughts about the past or future, practitioners maintain [[concepts/conscious-thought|awareness]] of immediate sensory perceptions, emotions, and thoughts as they arise. This non-judgmental observation of present experience forms the core of the practice across diverse cultural and philosophical traditions, though contemporary approaches have been substantially shaped by Buddhist meditation practices.

## Origins and Development

Mindfulness practices have roots extending back thousands of years within Buddhist contemplative traditions. In recent decades, mindfulness has been adapted into secular contexts, particularly within clinical [[concepts/psychology|psychology]] and medical settings. [[concepts/software|Programs]] such as Mindfulness-Based [[concepts/stress|Stress]] Reduction (MBSR) and Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (MBCT) have incorporated these principles into structured interventions designed to address specific health concerns.

## Applications and Evidence

Mindfulness is commonly employed as a tool for [[concepts/stress-management|stress management]], [[concepts/anxiety|anxiety]] reduction, and emotional [[concepts/regulation|regulation]]. Research has examined its effects across various conditions including [[concepts/chronic-pain|chronic pain]], [[concepts/depression|depression]], and [[concepts/sleep|sleep]] disturbance. While some studies demonstrate measurable benefits for psychological [[concepts/health|wellbeing]] and symptom reduction, the extent and [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] of these effects remain subjects of ongoing scientific investigation.

## Practice Methods

Mindfulness can be cultivated through formal meditation practices—typically involving seated or walking meditation with sustained [[concepts/attention|attention]] to breath or bodily sensations—or through informal integration into daily activities. Many practitioners use combinations of both approaches, with some supported by smartphone applications or guided [[concepts/audio-modality|audio]] recordings.
