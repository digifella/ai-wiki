# Numerical Analysis

Numerical analysis is a branch of [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] and computational [[concepts/science|science]] that develops and studies [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]] for solving [[concepts/mathematical-problems|mathematical problems]] using approximate [[concepts/calculation-methods|computational methods]]. Rather than seeking exact analytical solutions, numerical analysis focuses on techniques that can be efficiently implemented on computers to obtain numerical approximations to problems that may be difficult or impossible to solve symbolically. This field is essential for practical applications across science, [[entities/national-academies|engineering]], and finance, where exact solutions are often unavailable or computationally infeasible.

## Core Methods and Applications

The discipline encompasses a wide range of techniques including root-finding algorithms, interpolation, numerical integration, and the [[concepts/solution|solution]] of differential equations. Numerical analysts develop methods to approximate derivatives and integrals, solve systems of linear and nonlinear equations, and [[concepts/compute|compute]] eigenvalues of matrices. These techniques are fundamental to simulating physical phenomena, analyzing experimental data, and optimizing complex systems.

## Error Analysis and Stability

A central concern in numerical analysis is understanding and controlling computational errors. These arise from approximation errors inherent in the chosen method and [[concepts/rounding|rounding]] errors from finite-[[concepts/accuracy|precision]] arithmetic. Numerical analysts study convergence properties—how closely approximate solutions approach true solutions—and stability, which measures how errors propagate through calculations. Careful analysis of these factors ensures that computed results are reliable for practical use.

Numerical analysis underpins modern computational science, enabling solutions to problems in climate modeling, structural engineering, [[concepts/molecular-dynamics|molecular dynamics]], and countless other fields where analytical solutions are impractical or nonexistent.
