# Mount Vesuvius

**Mount Vesuvius** is a stratovolcano located on the Gulf of Naples in Southern [[entities/italy|Italy]], best known for the catastrophic eruption of AD 79 that destroyed the Roman cities of Pompeii and Herculaneum.

## Archaeological Significance

The eruption of Mount Vesuvius preserved not only the physical structures of the affected cities but also organic materials, most notably the [[concepts/herculaneum-papyri]]. These scrolls, carbonized by the intense heat of the pyroclastic flows, have provided invaluable insights into Hellenistic [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]], particularly the works of the Epicurean school.

### Recent Developments in Papyrology

*   **[[concepts/virtual-unrolling|Virtual Unrolling]]:** Traditional physical unrolling of the carbonized Herculaneum scrolls often results in their destruction. Recent advancements in AI and [[concepts/x-ray-tomography|X-ray tomography]] have enabled "virtual unrolling," allowing scholars to read the text without physical [[entities/contact|contact]].
*   **AI Decipherment:** [[concepts/ai-models|Artificial Intelligence models]] are now being deployed to decipher the ink patterns within the rolled papyri, revolutionizing the [[concepts/speed|speed]] and accuracy of [[concepts/herculaneum-papyri]] research.
*   **Key Resource:** For detailed technical breakdowns of the virtual unrolling process and recent findings, see [[lab-notes/2026-06-27-AI-Revolutionizes-Herculaneum-Scroll-Decipherment-Throug|AI Revolutionizes Herculaneum Scroll Decipherment Through Virtual Unrolling]].
*   **Scope:** The collection comprises over 1,800 papyrus scrolls, primarily found in the Villa of the Papyri in Herculaneum.
