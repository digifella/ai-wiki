# Indian Civilization

Indian civilization encompasses the complex societies and cultures that developed across the Indian subcontinent over more than five thousand years. The earliest known urban civilization in this region, the Indus Valley Civilization, flourished from approximately 3300 to 1300 BCE across what is now Pakistan and northwestern [[entities/india|India]]. Contemporary with ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia, it featured planned cities with standardized drainage systems, consistent [[concepts/parameters|weights]] and measures for trade, and a writing system that remains only partially deciphered. Major settlements such as Harappa and Mohenjo-daro demonstrate sophisticated [[concepts/city-planning|urban planning]] and [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] capabilities that characterized this early period.

Following the decline of the Indus Valley Civilization, the Vedic period emerged, laying the foundational religious and social structures for subsequent Indian history. This era saw the [[concepts/writing|composition]] of the Vedas and the development of the caste system, which would influence social organization for millennia. The region subsequently experienced the rise of major empires, including the Maurya and Gupta dynasties, which facilitated significant [[concepts/innovations-in-mathematics|advancements in mathematics]], [[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]], and literature. These periods also marked the spread of [[concepts/hinduism|Hinduism]] and [[concepts/buddhism|Buddhism]], shaping the spiritual landscape of South and Southeast Asia.

The subcontinent’s strategic location made it a central hub in [[concepts/global-trade|global trade]] networks, influencing and being influenced by external powers. From the ancient Silk Road to maritime routes connecting with Rome, the Middle East, and Southeast Asia, Indian civilizations engaged in extensive commercial exchange. This interaction facilitated the [[concepts/flow|flow]] of goods such as spices, textiles, and precious stones, while also enabling the transmission of cultural and religious [[concepts/ideas|ideas]]. The legacy of these early interactions established the Indian subcontinent as a pivotal [[entities/nodejs|node]] in the history of global economic and cultural development.
