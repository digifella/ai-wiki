## Emperor Ashoka

Emperor Ashoka (r. c. 268 BCE – c. 232 BCE) was the third emperor of the [[concepts/mauryan-empire|Mauryan Empire]] in India. His reign is marked by significant developments in [[concepts/buddhism|Buddhism]], statecraft, and the spread of [[concepts/indian-civilization|Indian culture]] through [[concepts/global-trade|international trade]] and diplomacy.

- **Early conquests:** Ashoka's rule began with military campaigns aimed at expanding his empire. Notably, he conquered [[entities/kalinga|Kalinga]] (modern-day Odisha) around 261 BCE.

- **[[concepts/kalinga-war|Kalinga War]]:** The Battle of Kalinga was a turning point in Ashoka's life and reign. This brutal war resulted in the deaths and injury of thousands of soldiers and civilians.

- **Conversion to Buddhism:** Following the devastation of Kalinga, Ashoka experienced profound remorse and converted to Buddhism under the influence of Buddhist monks like Upagupta.

- **Promotion of Buddhism:** Upon his conversion, Ashoka became a strong advocate for Buddhism. He sent missionaries to distant lands such as Sri Lanka, Tibet, Kashmir, Nepal, and other parts of Southeast Asia.

- **Rock Edicts:** A series of edicts were carved on pillars and rocks throughout the empire. These inscriptions detailed Ashoka's dharma (duty) and his desire for peace and justice.
