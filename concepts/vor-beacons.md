# VOR beacons

**VOR** (VHF Omnidirectional Range) is a type of radio navigation beacon for aircraft. It provides magnetic bearing information to the aircraft, allowing pilots to determine their position relative to the station.

## Historical Context
Before the widespread [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of VOR and modern avionics, aircraft relied on [[concepts/low-frequency-radio-range]] systems for navigation. These earlier systems were pioneering auditory navigation aids that guided pilots through sound rather than visual or digital instruments.

## Key Characteristics
- **Frequency Band**: Operates in the VHF band (108.0 to 117.95 MHz).
- **Function**: Provides azimuth information (direction) relative to magnetic north.
- **Evolution**: Replaced older [[concepts/low-frequency-radio-range]] systems which required pilots to interpret [[concepts/audio-modality|audio]] signals (e.g., "A" and "N" Morse code signals) to stay on course.
- **Modern Usage**: Still widely used as a primary ground-based navigation aid, often integrated with DME (Distance Measuring Equipment) as VOR/DME stations.
