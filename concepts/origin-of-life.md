# Origin of Life

The [[concepts/abiogenesis]] of life on Earth is a subject of ongoing scientific inquiry, with recent evidence challenging traditional models.

## Theoretical Frameworks

*   **[[concepts/primordial-soup|Primordial Soup]] Theory**: The classical hypothesis suggesting life arose from a mixture of organic compounds in early Earth's oceans, energized by lightning or UV radiation.
*   **Hydrothermal Vent Hypothesis**: Proposes that life originated in [[concepts/hydrothermal-vents|deep-sea vents]], where mineral-rich, alkaline fluids provided energy and catalytic surfaces for chemical reactions.
*   **Dual Origins Model**: Emerging evidence suggests life may not have a single linear origin but rather two distinct pathways converging.

## Recent Developments: Dual Origins in Hydrothermal Vents

New research challenges the singular [[concepts/last-universal-common-ancestor|Last Universal Common Ancestor]] ([[concepts/luca|LUCA]]) narrative, pointing toward a more complex genesis [[lab-notes/2026-08-28-Rethinking-LUCA-Evidence-for-Dual-Origins-of-Life-in-Hyd|Rethinking LUCA: Evidence for Dual Origins of Life in Hydrothermal Vents]].

*   **Challenge to Primordial Soup**: Recent studies indicate that the "primordial soup" model may be insufficient to explain the complexity of early biological molecules.
*   **Evidence for Two Origins**: Scientific findings suggest life on Earth had two separate origins, potentially occurring in different hydrothermal vent environments.
*   **LUCA Re-evaluation**: The concept of LUCA is being rethought to accommodate a dual-origin scenario, implying that early life forms may have merged or co-evolved rather than descending from a single unique cell.
