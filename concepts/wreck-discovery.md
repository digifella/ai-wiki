# Wreck Discovery

The wreck of the RMS Titanic was discovered on September 1, 1985, by a team led by oceanographer Robert Ballard. The discovery took place in the North Atlantic Ocean, approximately 370 miles south-southeast of Newfoundland, at a depth of roughly 12,500 feet. The ship lay largely intact on the ocean floor, split into two main sections separated by a debris field spanning several miles.

## The Official Mission

The expedition was officially authorized by the United States Navy as a mission to locate two lost nuclear submarines: the USS Thresher and the USS Scorpion. This cover story provided funding and military support for what was primarily an oceanographic research endeavor. The dual objectives allowed Ballard's team to conduct deep-ocean exploration techniques while ostensibly serving Navy interests in understanding the seafloor environment where the submarines were lost.

## The Discovery and Its Significance

Once the Navy mission objectives were completed, Ballard's team redirected their efforts toward locating the Titanic, which had been missing since its sinking in 1912. The discovery provided the first visual evidence of the wreck and allowed researchers to document the ship's condition and the surrounding debris field. Subsequent expeditions yielded thousands of artifacts and extensive photographic documentation, fundamentally advancing understanding of the disaster and deep-sea exploration technology.
