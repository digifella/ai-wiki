# Interlocking Teeth

Interlocking teeth are the paired [[concepts/metal|metal]] or plastic components that form the core mechanical element of a [[concepts/zipper-history|zipper]]. Positioned on opposite edges of two zipper tapes, these teeth are engineered to mesh together when a slider moves along the track. The teeth alternate between left and right sides in a precise sequence, allowing them to interlock in a continuous, overlapping pattern. This interlocking creates the [[concepts/secure|secure]] closure that makes [[concepts/buttons|zippers]] functional for fastening clothing, bags, and other applications.

## Mechanical Design

The effectiveness of interlocking teeth depends on the precise geometry of their profiles and the alignment of the zipper tapes. In traditional metal zippers, the teeth are often coiled or stamped to ensure consistent engagement, while modern plastic zippers utilize molded teeth that snap together under tension. The slider applies lateral [[concepts/pressure|pressure]] to force the teeth into a locked state, while the opposing force of the materials' elasticity helps maintain the seal. Variations in tooth shape, such as the rounded profile of coil zippers versus the rigid structure of molded plastic teeth, dictate the flexibility, durability, and sealing capability of the final product.
