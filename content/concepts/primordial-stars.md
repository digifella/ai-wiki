# Primordial Stars

[[concepts/first-generation-of-stars|Primordial stars]] refer to the first generation of stars to form in the universe, theoretically predicted to have emerged within the first few hundred million years after the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]]. These objects, often designated as Population III stars in astronomical classification, would have formed from pristine hydrogen and helium with negligible heavy element content, distinguishing them fundamentally from all subsequently formed stars. Their extreme age and unique composition make them of particular interest to cosmologists studying early [[concepts/galaxy-formation-and-evolution|galactic formation]] and chemical evolution.

## Observational Evidence

Direct observation of primordial stars has proven challenging due to their vast distance and age. However, in 2026, the [[concepts/jwst|James Webb Space Telescope]] (JWST) detected evidence suggesting the presence of primordial stars within the galaxy [[entities/gn-z11|GN-z11]], one of the most distant galaxies known. The spectroscopic data and light signatures consistent with Population III stellar populations provided the first concrete observational support for their existence, though the detection remains subject to ongoing analysis and verification by the astronomical community.

## Primordial Black Holes

Alongside evidence of primordial stars, gravitational wave detections have provided indirect evidence for primordial black holes of sub-solar mass. These objects may represent a distinct population formed in the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]] through mechanisms different from stellar collapse, potentially offering insights into the extreme conditions and [[concepts/physics|physics]] of the primordial cosmos. The [[concepts/connection|connection]] between [[concepts/galaxy-formation|primordial star formation]] and primordial black hole production remains an active area of investigation.
