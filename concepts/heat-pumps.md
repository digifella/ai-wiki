# Heat Pumps

**Heat pumps** are devices that transfer thermal energy from a low-temperature source to a high-temperature sink, typically using a refrigeration cycle. They are widely used for space heating, cooling, and water heating, offering high efficiency compared to direct electric resistance heating or fossil fuel combustion.

## Core Principles
- **Coefficient of Performance (COP):** Ratio of heat output to electrical input; typically 3–4 for residential units.
- **Reversibility:** Many systems can switch between heating and cooling modes.
- **Sources:** Air-source, ground-source (geothermal), and water-source heat pumps.

## Integration with Renewable Energy
Heat pumps are a key component in decarbonizing building energy use, especially when paired with Solar Power or wind energy. However, their efficiency and grid impact depend on the availability of renewable generation during peak heating/cooling demand.

## Long-Term Storage Challenges
While heat pumps [[entities/excel|excel]] at immediate thermal transfer, they do not inherently store energy for extended periods. Seasonal mismatches between renewable generation (e.g., summer solar surplus) and heating demand (winter) require complementary [[entities/storage|storage]] solutions.

- **Thermal Storage Limitations:** Conventional water tanks lose heat over weeks/months.
