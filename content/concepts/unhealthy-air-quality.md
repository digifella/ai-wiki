# Unhealthy Air Quality
Unhealthy air quality refers to atmospheric conditions where pollutant concentrations exceed levels considered safe for human [[concepts/health|health]]. This can include particulate matter (PM), ground-level ozone (O₃), nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), and sulfur dioxide (SO₂). [[concepts/exposure|Exposure]] to such pollutants is linked with respiratory and cardiovascular issues, especially in [[concepts/vulnerable-populations|vulnerable populations]] like the elderly, children, and those with pre-existing conditions.

### Climate Change and Air Quality
- Increased frequency of [[concepts/wildfires|wildfires]] due to [[concepts/climate-change|climate change]] exacerbates air pollution levels.
- [[concepts/extreme-weather-events|Extreme weather events]], including heatwaves and storms, can disrupt air quality monitoring systems and increase pollutant emissions.
- Rising temperatures contribute to higher ozone concentrations in urban areas.

### Health Impacts
- Respiratory diseases such as asthma are more prevalent among populations exposed to poor air quality.
- Cardiovascular issues like heart attacks and stroke are also linked with long-term exposure to air pollutants.
- Vulnerable groups, including children and the elderly, face increased health risks from unhealthy air quality conditions.
