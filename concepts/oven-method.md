# Oven Method

The oven method is a cooking technique for preparing bacon that uses controlled, uniform heat to achieve consistently crispy results across entire batches. Unlike stovetop pan-frying, which produces uneven browning due to hot spots and requires manual turning, the oven method distributes heat evenly throughout the cooking chamber. This uniform heat application cooks all bacon strips at the same rate, resulting in consistent texture across the batch and eliminating the need for individual attention during cooking.

## Procedure

To use the oven method, bacon strips are arranged in a single layer on a baking sheet, often lined with parchment paper or aluminum foil for easier cleanup. The tray is placed in a preheated oven, typically set between 400°F and 425°F (200°C to 220°C). Cooking time varies depending on the thickness of the bacon and desired crispness, generally ranging from 15 to 20 minutes. The bacon is monitored closely toward the end of the cooking process to prevent burning, as residual heat continues to cook the meat after removal from the oven.

## Advantages

This method allows for the simultaneous preparation of large quantities of bacon, making it efficient for feeding crowds. Because the bacon does not splatter as aggressively as it does in a pan, it reduces kitchen mess and smoke production. The consistent environment ensures that every strip reaches the same level of doneness, addressing the common issue of undercooked or overcooked pieces often found in batch cooking.
