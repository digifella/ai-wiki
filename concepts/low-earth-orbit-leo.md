# Low Earth Orbit (LEO)

**Low [[entities/earth|Earth]] Orbit** is a circumterrestrial orbital regime within which the altitude ranges from 200 km to 2,000 km above sea level. It is the most cost-effective region for [[concepts/satellite-launch|satellite deployment]] and human presence due to lower launch energy requirements compared to higher orbits.

## Characteristics
- **Altitude**: 200–2,000 km.
- **Orbital Period**: ~90 minutes (approx. 16 orbits per day).
- **Environment**: Subject to residual atmospheric drag, requiring periodic reboosting for station-keeping. Contains significant concentrations of radiation belts (Van Allen belts) at higher boundaries, though generally [[concepts/space-jetpacks|safer]] than [[concepts/deep-space-exploration|deep space]] for short-duration missions.
- **Infrastructure**: Hosts major international stations including the International [[concepts/space-station|Space Station]] (ISS) and constellations of communication satellites (e.g., Starlink, OneWeb).

## Medical & Operational Context
While LEO provides a controlled environment for initial [[concepts/human-spaceflight|human spaceflight]] training, missions extending **Beyond Low Earth Orbit** (e.g., to the Moon or [[concepts/mars|Mars]]) present distinct physiological challenges. Key areas of focus include:

- **Medical [[concepts/skill-gaps|Capability Gaps]]**: Transitioning from LEO to deep space requires addressing unique [[concepts/health|health]] risks such as prolonged radiation [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], microgravity-induced bone [[concepts/density|density]] loss, and psychological [[concepts/disconnection|isolation]].
- **[[concepts/gap-analysis|Gap Analysis]]**: Recent assessments highlight critical deficiencies in current medical countermeasures for long-duration flights. See [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Identifying-and-Closing-Medical-Capability-Gaps-for-Huma|Identifying and Closing Medical Capability Gaps for Human Spaceflight Missions Beyond Low Earth Orbit]] for a detailed review of [[concepts/nasa|NASA]]'s strategic priorities in this domain.
- **[[concepts/telehealth|Telemedicine]]**: LEO operations rely heavily on real-time ground support, whereas deep [[concepts/space-exploration|space missions]] necessitate autonomous diagnostic capabilities and delayed communication protocols.

## Strategic Importance
LEO serves as the critical proving ground for technologies and medical protocols destined for cislunar and interplanetary missions. Data gathered regarding crew health and equipment [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] in LEO directly informs the design of systems required for sustainable exploration beyond Earth's immediate vicinity.
