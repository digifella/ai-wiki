# Semiconductor Supply Chain

The semiconductor supply chain encompasses the interconnected stages required to design, manufacture, and distribute integrated circuits. Historically, this supply chain has been characterized by significant specialization and geographic fragmentation, with different companies specializing in distinct phases—design, fabrication (foundries), packaging, testing, and distribution. This division of labor has enabled efficiency and expertise concentration, but has also created vulnerabilities through concentrated dependencies on specific suppliers and geographic regions.

In response to these structural vulnerabilities, certain technology firms have pursued vertical integration strategies to gain greater control over critical components. Tesla and SpaceX have explored such approaches to secure their supply of advanced hardware, including the development of custom silicon for their respective operations. This shift represents a departure from the traditional model of relying exclusively on external foundries for all manufacturing needs.

A notable example of this strategic pivot involves the development of specialized chips for artificial intelligence workloads. Reports indicate that these companies are investigating the production of 2nm AI chips, potentially through a facility referred to as Terafab. This initiative aims to reduce reliance on third-party manufacturers and accelerate the deployment of proprietary hardware solutions tailored to specific computational requirements.
