# Small Form Factor Pc

A small form factor PC is a compact personal computer built in a chassis significantly smaller than a standard desktop tower. These systems typically measure 10-20 liters in volume and maintain full desktop [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] through careful component selection, particularly using mini-ITX or even smaller motherboards paired with low-[[concepts/power|power]] [[concepts/central-processing-units|processors]] and compact cooling solutions. Common [[concepts/scenarios|use cases]] include media centers, portable workstations, and server [[concepts/software|applications]] where space efficiency is valued alongside performance.

## Self-Hosting Applications

Small form factor PCs serve as practical [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] foundations for self-hosting services and personal cloud servers. Their reduced power consumption compared to traditional servers makes them economical for continuous operation, while their compact size allows placement in home environments without requiring dedicated server infrastructure. Users can deploy applications ranging from file [[entities/storage|storage]] and backup systems to media servers and [[concepts/developer-platforms|development environments]] on these machines.

## Hardware Considerations

Effective small form factor PC builds require [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to thermal management and power efficiency, as limited case volume restricts airflow options. Component selection typically prioritizes efficiency over raw performance, with emphasis on processors and power supplies designed for low thermal [[concepts/output|output]]. Storage can be expanded through external solutions or carefully arranged internal drives, and many enthusiasts opt for solid-state drives to minimize heat generation and space requirements.
