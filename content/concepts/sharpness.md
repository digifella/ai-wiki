# Sharpness

Sharpness in photography refers to the clarity and definition of details in an image. It is determined by several factors including focus [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]], aperture settings, shutter [[concepts/speed|speed]], and lens quality. In [[concepts/landscape-photography|landscape photography]], achieving sharp [[concepts/images|images]] across the entire scene—from foreground to background—presents a particular challenge, as the depth of field available [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] any given aperture may not be sufficient to keep all elements in focus simultaneously.

## Focus Stacking

[[concepts/blur-correction|Focus stacking]] is a technique that addresses the depth-of-field limitations in landscape photography. The method involves capturing multiple images of the same scene, each focused at a different distance, then blending them together in post-processing to create a final image with sharp detail throughout the composition. This approach allows photographers to achieve sharpness across elements at varying distances that would otherwise be blurred if captured in a single [[concepts/exposure|exposure]].

## Technical Considerations

[[concepts/camera-settings|Camera settings]] significantly [[concepts/power|influence]] sharpness in landscape work. Aperture, measured in f-stops, controls the depth of field available in each image—smaller apertures (higher f-numbers) increase depth of field but may introduce diffraction at extreme settings. Shutter speed must be fast enough to prevent motion blur, while ISO should be kept as low as practical to minimize noise. The choice of lens and its optical quality also plays a fundamental role in the final sharpness of an image.
