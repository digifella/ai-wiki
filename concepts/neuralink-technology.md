# Neuralink Technology

Neuralink Technology refers to the suite of neurotechnology systems developed by the company Neuralink, focusing on high-bandwidth brain-computer interfaces (BCI). The core infrastructure relies on surgically implanted electrode arrays, specifically the "Neuralink Link" device, which is designed to record neural signals from specific regions of the brain. These signals are processed by an onboard transmitter and receiver unit, establishing a direct communication pathway between the human brain and external digital devices. This invasive methodology distinguishes the technology from non-invasive alternatives, requiring specialized surgical intervention to achieve the necessary signal fidelity for complex data transmission.

The implementation of this technology involves transdural surgical procedures, where the device is implanted through the dura mater into the brain tissue. As of 2026, the system has undergone clinical trials with documented patient impact, demonstrating the ability to translate neural activity into digital commands. The surgical approach utilizes a robotic assistant to insert flexible thread-like electrodes, aiming to minimize tissue damage while maximizing the number of recorded neurons. This infrastructure supports real-time data exchange, allowing users to control external devices such as computers or prosthetics through thought alone.

Clinical outcomes have focused on restoring function for individuals with severe motor impairments. Early human trials have shown that patients can interact with digital interfaces with varying degrees of accuracy and speed, depending on the specific neural regions targeted. The technology continues to evolve, with ongoing research aimed at improving long-term biocompatibility, signal stability, and surgical safety profiles. The documented impact as of 2026 highlights both the potential for therapeutic applications and the significant medical risks associated with invasive brain surgery.
