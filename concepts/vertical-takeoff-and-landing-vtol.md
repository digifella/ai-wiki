# Aviation Engineering

[[concepts/vertical-takeoff-and-landing|Vertical takeoff and landing]] ([[concepts/vertical-take-off-and-landing|VTOL]]) aircraft are capable of ascending, descending, and hovering without requiring a runway. This capability eliminates the need for extensive ground infrastructure and enables operations in confined spaces, making VTOL systems valuable for military, emergency response, and urban mobility applications. The technology represents a significant departure from conventional fixed-wing aircraft design, as it must reconcile competing aerodynamic requirements between hover and forward flight modes.

## Design Approaches

Several technical approaches have been developed to achieve VTOL capability. [[concepts/tiltrotor-technology|Tiltrotor]] systems, exemplified by the [[entities/v-22-osprey|V-22 Osprey]], use dual rotors mounted on rotating wings that transition between helicopter and airplane configurations. Other designs include pure helicopters with horizontal rotors, multicopter drones with multiple fixed rotors, and experimental jet configurations.
