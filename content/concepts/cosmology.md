# Cosmology

Cosmology is the scientific study of the origin, [[concepts/structure|structure]], and evolution of the universe as a whole. It combines observations from [[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]] and [[concepts/physics|physics]] to develop [[concepts/models|models]] explaining how the universe began, how it has changed over time, and what its ultimate fate may be. Modern cosmology is grounded in the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]] [[concepts/theory|theory]], which describes the universe as originating from an extremely hot, dense state approximately 13.8 billion years ago and continuing to expand ever since.

## Observational Foundations

Contemporary cosmology relies heavily on observations of the [[concepts/observable-universe|observable universe]]—the portion of the universe from which [[concepts/light|light]] has had time to reach Earth. Key observational tools include telescopes that detect light across the electromagnetic spectrum, as well as instruments measuring [[concepts/cosmic-microwave-background|cosmic microwave background]] radiation, [[concepts/gravitational-waves|gravitational waves]], and other cosmic phenomena. These observations have revealed that the universe is expanding, that most of its content is in forms we do not yet fully understand ([[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] and dark energy), and that it appears largely uniform on the largest [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]].

## Major Questions

Cosmologists investigate fundamental questions about the universe's [[entities/nature|nature]] and history: What caused the Big Bang? What is the geometry and ultimate fate of the universe? How did galaxies, [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]], and planets form? What are dark matter and dark energy, which together comprise over 95% of the universe's content? These questions [[concepts/ambition|drive]] both theoretical development and observational campaigns that continue to refine our understanding of cosmic history and structure.
