# Uta Frith

**[[entities/uta-frith|Uta Frith]]** is a prominent British psychologist and Emeritus Professor of Cognitive Developmental Neurosciencex at University College London. A central figure in the history of [[concepts/autism|autism]] research, her work has fundamentally shaped the conceptualization of [[concepts/autism-spectrum|autism spectrum]] disorder (ASD), particularly through the development of the [[concepts/theory|Theory]] of [[concepts/the-mind|Mind]] framework.

## Key Contributions & Theoretical Frameworks

- **Theory of Mind (ToM):** Pioneered the hypothesis that autistic individuals struggle with attributing mental states (beliefs, intents, desires) to themselves and others, a deficit often referred to as mindblindness.
- **Triad of Impairments:** Helped formalize the triad comprising [[concepts/social-interaction|social interaction]] deficits, communication difficulties, and restricted/repetitive behaviors, which influenced DSM and ICD [[concepts/diagnostic-criteria|diagnostic criteria]].
- **Cognitive Phenomenology:** Investigated the link between [[concepts/human-cognition|cognitive processes]] and behavioral manifestations in neurodevelopmental conditions.

## Critical Perspectives & Recent Developments

Frith has periodically critiqued the expansion of diagnostic criteria, arguing that broadening the spectrum may dilute the specificity of the original phenotype she helped identify.
