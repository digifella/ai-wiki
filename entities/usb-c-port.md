# USB-C Port

A USB-C port is a standardized connector used for charging, data transfer, and video output on modern electronic devices. The connector features a small, reversible design that can be inserted in either orientation, eliminating the directional constraints of earlier USB standards. This reversibility was a significant practical improvement over previous connector types, as users no longer need to identify the correct insertion direction before plugging in a cable.

## Technical Specifications

USB-C supports multiple communication protocols and power delivery standards. The connector can handle USB 3.1 and USB 3.2 data transfer speeds, with USB 3.2 capable of speeds up to 20 gigabits per second. USB-C also supports video output through alternate modes, allowing connection to displays and projectors. Power delivery through USB-C can range from lower power applications to high-wattage charging for laptops and other devices, with some implementations supporting up to 240 watts.

## Adoption

Since its introduction in 2014, USB-C has been increasingly adopted across consumer electronics, including smartphones, tablets, laptops, and peripherals. Regulatory initiatives in various regions have promoted or mandated USB-C adoption as a standard charging port to reduce electronic waste and consumer inconvenience from multiple proprietary connectors.
