# Self Hosted AI

Self-hosted AI refers to [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems deployed and operated on private infrastructure controlled by the user or [[concepts/organization|organization]], rather than accessed through [[concepts/cloud-based-services|cloud-based services]] managed by third parties. This approach enables users to run [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] locally, maintaining direct [[concepts/power|control]] over data, computation, and system configuration while avoiding dependency on external service providers.

## Key Characteristics

Self-hosted AI systems typically involve [[concepts/running|running]] [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]] or other [[concepts/ai-powered-applications|AI applications]] on personal servers, local machines, or private cloud infrastructure. This [[concepts/setup|setup]] allows for greater [[concepts/privacy|privacy]], as data remains within the user's environment rather than being transmitted to external servers. Users can customize [[concepts/active-parameters|model parameters]], fine-tune systems for specific [[concepts/scenarios|use cases]], and maintain full operational autonomy.

## Practical Applications

[[concepts/automated-information-pipelines|OpenClaw]] (also known as [[concepts/local-ai-assistants|Clawdbot]]) exemplifies a practical self-hosted [[concepts/adoption|AI implementation]], functioning as a personal [[entities/ai-assistant|AI assistant]] that runs on private infrastructure while integrating with [[concepts/productivity-tools|productivity tools]] including [[entities/gmail|Gmail]], [[entities/asana|Asana]], [[entities/slack|Slack]], and [[entities/telegram|Telegram]]. This demonstrates how self-hosted systems can interface with existing workflows without relying on [[concepts/cloud-ai|cloud-based AI]] platforms.

## Technical Context

Self-hosted AI builds on server-based [[concepts/large-language-models|large language models]] and represents a shift toward [[concepts/decentralized-ai-supercomputer|decentralized AI]] [[concepts/deployment|deployment]]. As [[concepts/llm-optimization|model optimization]] improves and [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] becomes more accessible, self-hosting has become increasingly feasible for individuals and organizations seeking alternatives to commercial AI services, though it requires technical knowledge and [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]] to maintain effectively.
