# Wright Brothers

Orville and Wilbur Wright were American aviators and engineers who designed and built the world's first successful airplane. On December 17, 1903, near Kitty Hawk, North Carolina, they achieved the first controlled, sustained, powered flight in history. Orville piloted the aircraft for 12 seconds, covering a distance of 120 feet. This achievement culminated years of systematic experimentation and represented a fundamental breakthrough in human transportation.

## Research and Development

The Wright brothers approached aviation through rigorous scientific methodology rather than intuition. They conducted extensive wind tunnel experiments to understand aerodynamics, designed their own engines, and built custom propellers. Their systematic testing of wing designs and control surfaces distinguished their work from other aviation pioneers of the era who relied more heavily on trial and error.

## Legacy and Relevance

The Wright brothers are frequently cited in discussions of innovation and leadership for their clear sense of purpose—they pursued powered flight with singular focus despite widespread skepticism. Their work demonstrates how a well-defined mission, combined with disciplined experimentation and incremental problem-solving, can achieve transformative results. Their story has become emblematic in business and organizational contexts, particularly in frameworks emphasizing purpose-driven leadership and the importance of knowing one's "why."
