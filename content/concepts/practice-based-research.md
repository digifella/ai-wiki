# Practice Based Research

Practice-based research is a methodological approach that integrates creative or artistic practice with formal investigation into a specific subject or problem. Rather than treating artistic work and research as separate activities, this method uses the act of making, creating, or practicing as the primary means of generating knowledge and understanding. The approach is particularly suited to exploring complex, often abstract phenomena that may resist traditional empirical methods.

## Application in Dyslexia Visualization

[[concepts/visual-arts|Kim Percy]]'s work on [[concepts/visualizing-dyslexia|visualizing dyslexia]] exemplifies practice-based research in action. Through artistic practice, Percy investigates how dyslexia is experienced and perceived, creating visual representations that communicate aspects of the condition that are difficult to articulate through conventional academic or clinical frameworks. The research emerges from the [[concepts/iterative-refinement|iterative process]] of artistic creation itself, where each artwork becomes both an expression and an investigation.

This approach offers distinct advantages for studying subjective or perceptual experiences. By anchoring research in creative practice, artists and researchers can explore the sensory, emotional, and cognitive dimensions of their subject matter. The resulting works serve as both research outputs and communication tools, bridging the gap between specialized knowledge and public understanding.
