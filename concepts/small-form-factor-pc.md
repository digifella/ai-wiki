# Small Form Factor PC

A small form factor (SFF) PC is a compact personal computer built in a chassis significantly smaller than a standard desktop tower, typically measuring 10–20 liters in volume. These systems use mini-ITX or smaller motherboards paired with space-efficient components, including low-power processors and compact cooling solutions, to achieve full desktop computing capability within tight physical constraints. The deliberate engineering required to balance performance with reduced size has made SFF PCs increasingly viable for both consumer and specialized applications.

## Hardware considerations

Building an SFF PC requires careful component selection, as standard tower components often cannot fit. Storage typically relies on 2.5-inch SSDs rather than 3.5-inch drives, while power supplies must be compact and adequately rated. Cooling presents particular challenges due to limited airflow; many SFF systems use either passive cooling or small high-speed fans. Processors and graphics cards, when needed, are chosen for their thermal output and physical dimensions rather than maximum performance alone.

## Applications

SFF PCs serve various purposes depending on their intended use case. They function well as media centers, development machines, or dedicated network appliances. Their low power consumption and minimal footprint make them particularly suited for self-hosting applications, including personal cloud servers, home automation hubs, and network storage systems. The combination of portability, reasonable performance, and energy efficiency has made them attractive for users seeking computing capability without the space requirements of traditional desktops.
