# Autofocus Mode

Autofocus mode defines the operational strategy a Fujifilm camera employs to detect subjects and adjust lens elements for optimal sharpness. Rather than relying on the photographer to manually rotate the focus ring, the system analyzes scene data to determine the correct focal plane. This automation is particularly useful in dynamic environments where subjects move or where rapid shooting speeds are required. Fujifilm cameras typically offer distinct autofocus modes to suit different shooting scenarios.

## Single-Servo Autofocus (S-AF)

Single-servo autofocus, often designated as S-AF, is designed for stationary subjects. When the shutter button is pressed halfway, the camera focuses once and locks that focus distance until the shutter is fully depressed or the button is released. This mode prevents the focus from shifting if the subject moves slightly after initial acquisition, making it ideal for portraits, landscapes, and still life photography where precision is prioritized over speed.

## Continuous-Servo Autofocus (C-AF)

Continuous-servo autofocus, or C-AF, is optimized for moving subjects. The camera continuously adjusts focus as long as the shutter button is held halfway or the AF-L button is engaged. This mode tracks changes in subject distance, ensuring that moving objects remain sharp throughout the exposure. It is the standard choice for sports, wildlife, and street photography where the distance between the camera and subject is constantly changing.

## Autofocus Lock

The autofocus lock feature allows the photographer to manually override the automatic focus adjustment. By pressing the AF-L button after achieving focus, the camera maintains the current focal plane regardless of subject movement or recomposition. This function is essential for off-center composition techniques, such as the rule of thirds, where the subject is not located in the center of the frame. It provides greater control over focus placement in complex scenes.
