# Solar gravity

**Solar [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]]** refers to the gravitational force exerted by the Sun on surrounding celestial bodies. It is the dominant force governing orbital dynamics within the Solar System, particularly in the inner regions where gravitational potential wells are deepest.

## Key Characteristics

- **Inverse-Square Law**: Gravitational acceleration decreases with the square of the distance from the Sun's center ($g \propto 1/r^2$).
- **Orbital [[concepts/velocity|Velocity]]**: Proximity to the Sun requires significantly higher orbital velocities to maintain stable orbits, increasing [[concepts/kinetic-energy|kinetic energy]] requirements for spacecraft.
- **Gravitational Well**: The Sun's mass creates a deep potential well, making it energetically difficult to reach inner planets compared to outer planets or escaping the system entirely.

## Mission Challenges: BepiColombo

Reaching Mercury is counter-intuitively more difficult than traveling to Jupiter due to the immense solar gravity well.

- **Delta-V Requirements**: A spacecraft must shed significant orbital energy to fall inward toward the Sun. This requires extensive use of propulsion and gravity assist maneuvers.
- **Thermal Management**: Proximity to the Sun exposes missions to extreme heat, necessitating advanced [[concepts/starship-version-3|thermal protection systems]].
- **[[concepts/project-mercury|BepiColombo Mission]]**: The joint [[entities/esa]] and JAXA mission, launched in 2018, exemplifies these challenges. It utilizes multiple gravity assists ([[entities/earth|Earth]], Venus, Mercury) to gradually reduce orbital velocity and spiral into Mercury's orbit.
- **Complexity**: The mission profile is more protracted and complex than missions to gas giants, highlighting the unique orbital mechanics of the inner solar system.
