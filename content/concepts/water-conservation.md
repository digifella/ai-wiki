# Water Conservation

Water conservation refers to the practice of using water efficiently to reduce unnecessary consumption of [[concepts/renewable-water-resources|freshwater resources]]. As global freshwater availability becomes increasingly constrained by [[concepts/population-growth|population growth]], [[concepts/climate-change|climate change]], and competing demands from [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]], industry, and domestic use, water conservation has become essential for sustainable resource management. The goal is to maintain adequate water supplies for human needs and ecosystems while minimizing waste and degradation of water quality.

## Strategies and Methods

Water conservation approaches operate [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] multiple [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]], from individual household practices to [[concepts/large-scale-infrastructure|large-scale infrastructure]] and policy interventions. Common strategies include improving irrigation efficiency in agriculture, fixing leaks in water [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] systems, installing water-efficient fixtures in buildings, and adopting water-recycling technologies. Industrial processes can be redesigned to reduce water consumption, and landscape management practices such as drought-resistant plantings can decrease outdoor water demand. Policy measures like water [[concepts/pricing|pricing structures]], building codes, and watershed management planning also play important roles in promoting conservation.

## Interconnections with Climate and Ecosystems

Water conservation is closely linked to broader climate and ecological concerns. Reducing freshwater extraction supports ecosystem [[concepts/health|health]] by maintaining adequate streamflow and groundwater levels for natural habitats. In regions vulnerable to drought and flood extremes, water conservation practices contribute to climate [[concepts/resilience|resilience]] by building adaptive capacity. Additionally, many water conservation measures reduce the energy required for water treatment and distribution, thereby lowering greenhouse gas emissions associated with [[concepts/water-management-systems|water systems]].
