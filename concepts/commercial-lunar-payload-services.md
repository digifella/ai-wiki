# Commercial Lunar Payload Services

Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) is a NASA program that contracts with private companies to deliver scientific instruments and equipment to the lunar surface. Established in the late 2010s, the program represents NASA's strategy to leverage commercial spaceflight providers for lunar exploration missions. By partnering with private vendors rather than relying solely on government-operated systems, CLPS aims to reduce costs and increase the frequency of lunar surface deliveries.

## Program Structure and Operations

Under CLPS, NASA provides task orders to selected commercial lunar lander providers, who compete to deliver payloads to specified lunar locations. Participating companies are responsible for developing and operating their own landers and launch vehicles, while NASA focuses on providing science instruments and defining mission objectives. This model allows NASA to access lunar surface access points more regularly and affordably than previous approaches.

## Integration with Artemis

CLPS serves as a complementary element to NASA's broader Artemis program, which aims to return humans to the Moon. The program delivers scientific instruments and technology demonstrations to the lunar surface ahead of crewed Artemis missions, supporting site characterization and resource assessment. However, achieving full readiness for Artemis 3—particularly coordinating with Human Landing System (HLS) and Space Launch System (SLS) timelines—presents ongoing technical and programmatic challenges that continue to shape the initiative's development and schedule.
