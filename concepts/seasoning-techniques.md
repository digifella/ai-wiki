# Seasoning Techniques

Seasoning is the practice of enhancing food flavor through the addition of salt, spices, herbs, and other flavor compounds. Effective seasoning requires understanding how different salt types and their crystalline structures affect flavor distribution and intensity. Table salt, kosher salt, and sea salt have different densities and grain sizes, which means they don't measure equally by volume and dissolve at different rates during cooking. This variation directly impacts how evenly seasoning distributes throughout a dish and how pronounced the saltiness becomes.

## Salt Types and Application

The choice of salt affects both flavor delivery and technique. Table salt has fine crystals and higher density, so a teaspoon contains more salt than the same volume of kosher salt with its larger, more porous grains. Kosher salt dissolves more slowly and is often preferred for finishing dishes or seasoning meat surfaces, where visible crystals and gradual dissolution enhance flavor perception. Sea salt and specialty salts add mineral complexity but function similarly to kosher salt in cooking applications.

## Timing and Distribution

When seasoning is applied matters significantly for flavor development. Seasoning early in cooking allows salt to penetrate and season food throughout, while finishing salt added near the end provides textural contrast and concentrated flavor bursts. The cooking method also influences seasoning strategy—braising requires earlier salting for proper penetration, while quick-cooking techniques like sautéing benefit from layered seasoning at different stages. Tasting and adjusting seasoning progressively, rather than adding all at once, helps achieve balanced flavor without oversalting.
