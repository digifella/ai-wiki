# World Building

World building refers to the process of constructing fictional or hypothetical universes with internally consistent rules, geography, history, and social systems. It encompasses the creation of settings for narratives across various media, including literature, games, film, and interactive experiences. Effective world building establishes the foundation upon which characters act and plots unfold, providing audiences with a coherent context for engagement.

## Core Elements

Successful world building typically addresses several interconnected dimensions. Physical geography—including terrain, climate, and natural resources—shapes how societies develop and interact. Historical frameworks establish causality and cultural memory, explaining why civilizations have their current values and conflicts. Social systems encompassing governance, economics, religion, and class structures determine how characters navigate their environment. Magic systems, technology levels, and other rule-sets define what is possible within the world's logic.

## Applications

World building practices originate in literary fantasy and science fiction but have expanded across entertainment media. Game designers use world building to create immersive environments where player choices feel meaningful. Filmmakers employ it to establish visual and narrative coherence across scenes. The discipline also extends to educational contexts, where hypothetical scenarios help students understand historical processes or social dynamics.
