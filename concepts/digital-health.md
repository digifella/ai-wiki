# Digital Health

Digital health encompasses the use of digital technologies, tools, and platforms to improve health outcomes and [[concepts/health|healthcare]] delivery. This includes [[concepts/electronic-health-records]], [[concepts/telehealth|telemedicine]], [[concepts/health-surveillance|health monitoring]] applications, [[concepts/wearable-devices|wearable devices]], and [[entities/big-data|data analytics]] systems that support [[concepts/clinical-reasoning|clinical decision-making]] and patient engagement. The field represents a convergence of [[concepts/health-care|healthcare]] practice, information technology, and [[concepts/population-health|public health]] initiatives aimed at making healthcare more accessible, efficient, and data-driven.

Recent developments include the FDA utilizing [[concepts/agentic-ai]] in regulatory processes and ongoing surveys regarding digital health familiarity. Foundational context is provided by global frameworks such as the [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-WHO---Global-strategy|WHO - Global strategy]], which outlines critical components for implementation:

*   **[[concepts/business-philosophy|Guiding Principles]] & Strategic Objectives**: Establishes the core values and goals for global digital health [[concepts/adoption|adoption]].
*   **[[concepts/framework-for-action|Framework for Action]]**: Provides a structured approach to deploying digital solutions across health systems.
*   **Implementation & Action Plan**: Details specific steps for stakeholders, including member states and the WHO Secretariat.
*   **Proposed Actions for Member States**: [[concepts/recommendations|Directives]] for national-level adoption and integration of [[concepts/digital-interventions|digital health tools]].
