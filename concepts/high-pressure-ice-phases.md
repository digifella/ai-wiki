# High-Pressure Ice Phases

## Overview
Contrary to common intuition, "ice" is not a singular substance but encompasses at least 15 distinct solid phases Ice phases. Each [[concepts/phase|phase]] exhibits unique [[concepts/density|density]] and crystal structure, determined by specific [[concepts/pressure]] and Temperature conditions.

## Key Characteristics
*   **Phase Diversity:** Water forms multiple polymorphs under extreme conditions, challenging the binary view of solid/liquid states.
*   **Density Anomalies:** Unlike most substances, Ice Ih (standard ice) is less dense than [[concepts/liquid-water|liquid water]], but high-pressure phases (e.g., Ice VII, Ice X) are significantly denser.
*   **Structural Complexity:** High-pressure phases involve complex [[concepts/hydrogen-bonding|hydrogen bonding]] networks and proton ordering/disordering transitions.
