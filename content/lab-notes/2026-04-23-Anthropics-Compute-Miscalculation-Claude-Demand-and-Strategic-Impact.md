### Summary
The video details what the presenter calls a "miscalculation" by [[entities/anthropic|Anthropic]], leading to a "[[concepts/compute-crunch|compute crunch]]" and a public relations nightmare, which competitors like [[entities/openai|OpenAI]] are actively exploiting. The main topic revolves around [[entities/anthropic-institute|Anthropic]]'s struggles to meet demand for its [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]], particularly [[concepts/claude|Claude]], due to insufficient [[concepts/computing-infrastructure|computing infrastructure]]. This has led to a series of confusing and restrictive [[concepts/policy-changes|policy changes]], frustrating their user base and potentially jeopardizing their long-term market position.

A core issue highlighted is Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's strategic decision, made roughly 18 months prior, not to invest heavily in [[concepts/compute-capacity|compute capacity]]. Unlike OpenAI, which aggressively bought GPUs for its ambitious [[concepts/agi|AGI]] goals, Anthropic believed that superior algorithms and a focus on [[concepts/enterprise-ai|enterprise AI]] coding would allow them to maintain efficiency and avoid significant [capital expenditure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_expenditure). This was a critical misjudgment, as the demand for AI models, especially with the rise of [agentic engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agentic_engineering), surged past all projections. This imbalance has caused Anthropic's "flywheel" – a virtuous cycle of coding models generating revenue and data to train better models – to falter, as they can no longer adequately serve their existing user base.

To cope with the [[concepts/compute-scarcity|compute shortage]], Anthropic has implemented several unpopular measures. These include removing [[concepts/claude-code|Claude Code]] from lower-tier subscription plans (though it was later partially restored for Pro users), imposing stricter [[concepts/session|session]] limits during peak hours, and issuing confusing communications regarding the use of third-party tools and [[concepts/api-keys|API keys]]. The company explicitly stated that their subscriptions were not designed for the heavy usage patterns of these third-party tools, effectively targeting [[concepts/power-users|power users]] and developers building [[concepts/agents|agents]]. These actions have alienated a significant portion of their community, who feel that Anthropic is restricting access to [[concepts/tokens|tokens]] they have already paid for, creating a sour taste and trust issues. This is further compounded by abysmal [[concepts/uptime|uptime]] rates for their services compared to competitors. The video also suggests Anthropic is selling [[concepts/tokens|tokens]] at a loss, and [[concepts/power-users|power users]], who utilize their tokens most, are exacerbating this [[concepts/financial-strain|financial strain]].

In stark [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], OpenAI is actively capitalizing on Anthropic's woes. Having heavily invested in compute, OpenAI has positioned itself as the "hero" by offering more flexible usage, frequently resetting [[concepts/usage-limits|usage limits]], and even acquiring [[entities/peter|Peter]] Steinberger, the [[concepts/creator|creator]] of the popular third-party tool OpenClaude. While Anthropic [[concepts/faces|faces]] a PR crisis and growing user frustration, OpenAI and its CEO [[entities/sam-altman|Sam Altman]] are seen to be taking every opportunity, including social media, to highlight their own [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]] and developer-friendly stance. The competitive landscape is described as a spectrum: [[concepts/xai|XAI]] ([[entities/elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s company) is compute-rich but demand-poor; Anthropic is demand-rich but compute-poor; Google is massively compute-rich and serves both its own models and competitors; and OpenAI, while having strong demand, has aggressively built out compute to stay ahead. Although Anthropic has recently announced a major compute deal with Amazon, the capacity is not expected to be available for months, leaving them vulnerable to further market share loss in the interim.
