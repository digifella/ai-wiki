# Supply Chains

Supply chains are the networks of organizations, people, activities, and resources involved in delivering products or services from production to consumption. They encompass procurement of raw materials, manufacturing, logistics, [[concepts/distribution|distribution]], and retail, often spanning multiple countries and involving complex coordination between suppliers, manufacturers, and retailers. Modern supply chains have become increasingly globalized, with companies sourcing components and labor from wherever costs are lowest, though this approach involves tradeoffs regarding [[concepts/speed|speed]], [[concepts/quality-control|quality control]], and [[concepts/resilience|resilience]] to disruptions.

## Domestic Manufacturing and Competitiveness

A notable contemporary question in supply chain management concerns whether products can be manufactured entirely within a single country—particularly the [[entities/united-states|United States]]—while remaining price-competitive in global markets. This question reflects tensions between labor costs, [[concepts/automation|automation]] [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]], regulatory environments, and consumer preferences for both affordability and [[concepts/domestic-production|domestic production]]. A documented [[concepts/four-year-experiment|four-year experiment]] by [[entities/video-creator|content creator]] [[entities/destin-sandlin|Destin Sandlin]] explored this question directly, investigating the practical and economic feasibility of keeping an entire manufacturing process within [[entities/america|America]] rather than distributing it across multiple countries as is standard industrial practice.

## Contemporary Significance

Interest in domestic supply chains has grown due to concerns about supply chain vulnerabilities exposed during global disruptions, geopolitical tensions, and debates about economic self-sufficiency. However, companies must balance nationalist or resilience-focused production goals against the [[concepts/cost|cost]] advantages and efficiency gains achieved through globalized supply networks. The outcome of experiments [[concepts/testing|testing]] domestic manufacturing viability has implications for policy discussions around trade, labor, and industrial strategy.
