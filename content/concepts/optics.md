# Optics

Optics is the branch of [[concepts/physics|physics]] that studies light and its interactions with matter. It encompasses the behavior of light across a wide range of wavelengths, from ultraviolet through visible to infrared radiation, and examines how light propagates, reflects, refracts, and is absorbed by different materials. As a discipline, optics bridges [[concepts/theoretical-physics|theoretical physics]] with practical [[concepts/software|applications]] in everyday technology and scientific instruments.

## Core Phenomena

The fundamental phenomena studied in optics include reflection, where light bounces off surfaces according to the law of reflection; [[concepts/refraction|refraction]], where light bends as it passes between media of different densities; and diffraction, where light bends around obstacles and apertures. Other key concepts include interference, polarization, and dispersion. Understanding these phenomena requires consideration of both the wave [[entities/nature|nature]] of light and, in certain contexts, its particle nature as [[concepts/light|photons]].

## Applications and Branches

Optics has extensive practical applications in lenses, mirrors, fiber optics, microscopes, telescopes, and laser technology. The field is typically divided into geometrical optics, which treats light as rays and is useful for analyzing mirrors and lenses, and physical optics, which accounts for the wave properties of light and explains phenomena like interference and diffraction. Modern optics also includes quantum optics, which studies light at the quantum mechanical level.
