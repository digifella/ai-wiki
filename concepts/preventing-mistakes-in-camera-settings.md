# Preventing Mistakes In Camera Settings

Mistakes in [[concepts/camera-settings|camera settings]] can result in unusable footage, wasted shooting time, and lost creative opportunities. Preventing these errors requires a combination of systematic practices, consistent workflows, and deliberate [[concepts/habits|habits]] that reduce the likelihood of [[concepts/accidental-changes|accidental changes]] or oversights during production.

## Establishing Baseline Settings

Creating and documenting standard camera configurations for different shooting [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]] provides a reliable foundation. Before beginning a shoot, establish baseline settings for ISO, aperture, shutter [[concepts/speed|speed]], white balance, and recording format appropriate to the lighting conditions and creative intent. Recording these preferences—either in written form or through camera presets—allows for quick [[concepts/verification|verification]] and reduces decision fatigue during active shooting.

## Verification and Double-Checking

Implementing a simple pre-shoot checklist ensures critical settings are reviewed before recording begins. This includes verifying focus mode, [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] compensation, frame rate, [[concepts/solution|resolution]], and file format. During longer shoots or between scene changes, periodic spot-checks of key settings can catch unintended drift before significant footage is compromised. Delegating verification tasks to a second team member, when available, provides an additional safeguard.

## File Management and Documentation

Consistent [[concepts/file-naming-conventions|file naming conventions]] and [[concepts/metadata|metadata]] organization prevent confusion and data loss, particularly when managing footage from multiple cameras or shooting days. Recording metadata such as camera settings, lens information, and timestamp details alongside footage creates a reliable reference for [[concepts/video-editing|post-production]] decisions and helps identify settings that produced desired results. Structured directory hierarchies and backup protocols further protect against accidental overwriting or loss of critical files.
