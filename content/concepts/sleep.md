# Sleep

Sleep is a fundamental biological process and [[concepts/health|health]] practice that plays a critical role in [[concepts/physical-health|physical health]], cognitive function, and emotional well-being. During sleep, the body undergoes essential restorative processes including [[concepts/memory|memory]] [[concepts/consolidation|consolidation]], hormonal [[concepts/regulation|regulation]], and immune system maintenance. For adolescents, adequate sleep is particularly important as it supports [[concepts/academic-performance|academic performance]], emotional regulation, and healthy development during a period of [[concepts/significant-change|significant change]].

## Sleep and Mental Health

Research in [[concepts/science-of-well-being|well-being science]] has established sleep as a core behavioral practice for supporting psychological health. Consistent, quality sleep improves mood regulation, reduces [[concepts/anxiety|anxiety]] and [[concepts/depression|depression]] symptoms, and enhances overall [[concepts/quality-of-life|life satisfaction]]. Sleep deprivation, conversely, impairs emotional processing and decision-making, making adequate rest foundational to mental well-being interventions.

## Sleep Recommendations for Teens

Most adolescents require 8-10 hours of sleep per night to support optimal functioning. This need reflects biological changes in teenage [[concepts/circadian-rhythms|circadian rhythms]], where sleep naturally shifts later in the evening. Establishing regular sleep schedules and [[concepts/sleep-hygiene|sleep hygiene]] practices—such as limiting screen time before bed and maintaining consistent bedtimes—are evidence-based strategies for improving [[concepts/sleep-quality|sleep quality]] in young people.
