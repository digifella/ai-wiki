# Orbital Nodes

Orbital nodes refer to the designated points in space where satellite constellations operate and interact. In the context of [[concepts/satellite-internet|satellite internet]] infrastructure like [[entities/spacex|SpaceX]]'s Starlink, orbital nodes represent the physical positions and operational hubs from which services are delivered to ground-based users. These nodes function as part of a larger network [[concepts/architecture|architecture]] that enables [[concepts/global-connectivity|global connectivity]].

## Beyond Connectivity Infrastructure

While orbital nodes are commonly understood as components of internet [[concepts/service-delivery|service delivery]] systems, their potential [[concepts/software|applications]] extend significantly further. The infrastructure required to maintain and operate satellite constellations at scale creates foundational capabilities that could support additional functions, including data relay, sensing networks, and distributed computing platforms. The economic viability of deploying and maintaining large-scale orbital node systems for a primary service like internet connectivity can create opportunities for secondary applications that leverage the same infrastructure.

## Investment and Strategic Implications

The commercialization of orbital node networks through public offerings and investment has drawn [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] primarily to their internet service potential. However, the underlying infrastructure—including satellite positioning, communication protocols, and orbital management systems—represents a more fundamental technological asset. Understanding orbital nodes requires consideration of how the initial business case for [[concepts/deployment|deployment]] interacts with the broader capabilities these systems enable once operational.
