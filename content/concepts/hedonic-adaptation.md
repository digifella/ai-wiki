# Hedonic Adaptation
[[concepts/happiness-baseline|Hedonic adaptation]], also referred to as the "[[concepts/happiness|hedonic treadmill]]," is a psychological principle that describes how humans tend to return to a stable baseline level of [[concepts/happiness|happiness]] regardless of major positive or negative life changes. This concept explains why people often find themselves seeking constant novelty and achievement in an attempt to maintain or achieve higher levels of [[concepts/happiness|happiness]], only to revert back to their baseline over time.

- Humans adapt quickly to both positive and negative changes.
- The concept suggests that our emotional response to events is temporary and that we eventually return to a [[concepts/set-point-theory|set point]] of [[concepts/wellbeing|well-being]].
- This idea challenges the [[entities/notion|notion]] that material possessions or achievements can lead to long-term happiness.
