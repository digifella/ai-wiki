# Foregroundbackground Separation

Foreground/background separation is a post-processing technique used to isolate and enhance subject areas in photographs by applying different [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] to distinct regions of an image. Using [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] tools in [[entities/adobe-lightroom|Adobe Lightroom]] and [[concepts/camera-raw|Camera Raw]], photographers can selectively modify [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], clarity, and color for the foreground subject independently from the background. This approach allows for more precise [[concepts/power|control]] over the final image appearance than applying adjustments globally across the entire photograph.

## Masking and Edge Refinement

The core technical challenge in foreground/background separation lies in creating accurate masks that cleanly distinguish between the subject and its surroundings. Both Lightroom and [[entities/camera-raw-filter|Camera Raw]] offer [[concepts/masking|masking]] features—including brush masks, range-based selections, and [[concepts/object-detection|object detection]]—that help define these boundaries. Refining mask edges is critical to achieving natural-looking results; poorly defined edges create visible halos or unnatural transitions between adjusted and unadjusted areas. Modern versions of these tools include [[concepts/edge-refinement|edge refinement]] options that allow photographers to feather, soften, or otherwise adjust how sharply the mask transitions between regions.

## Practical Applications

This technique is particularly useful for enhancing subject [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]] and clarity while keeping backgrounds softer, adjusting subject exposure without affecting background brightness, or applying different [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|color grading]] to separate elements within a composition. The method works across various photography genres, from portraits to landscape work, provided the foreground subject and background are sufficiently distinct or the photographer has sufficient control over the masking process.
