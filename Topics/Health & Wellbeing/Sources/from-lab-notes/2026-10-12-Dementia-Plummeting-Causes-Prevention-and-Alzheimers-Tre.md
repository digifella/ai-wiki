## Dementia Plummeting: Causes, Prevention, and Alzheimer's Treatment Progress
**Clip title:** [[concepts/dementia|Dementia]] Is Plummeting. Scientists Think They Know Why
**[[entities/tasia-custode|Author]] / channel:** What Could Go Right? Podcast
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gTPz4Xv7-Q0

### Summary
The video presents an optimistic "Progress Report" on several significant global advancements, focusing on improvements in brain [[concepts/health|health]], innovative medical technology, and successful environmental [[concepts/secure|protection]] through [[concepts/international-diplomacy|international diplomacy]]. The overarching theme is that despite widespread pessimism and nostalgic views of the past, [[concepts/human-ingenuity|human ingenuity]] and collective effort are leading to remarkable progress in critical areas.

The first major topic discussed is the "[[concepts/dementia|Dementia]] Drop," highlighting a significant decrease in dementia rates among older Americans. Forty years ago, one in three Americans aged 85-89 had dementia; this figure has now fallen to one in ten. This improvement is attributed to healthier lifestyles (reduced smoking, better cholesterol management) and unexpected medical advancements like the shingles vaccine, [[concepts/depression|depression]] treatments, and improved vision/hearing care. Furthermore, better public education is believed to play a role in maintaining [[concepts/cognitive-abilities|cognitive skills]]. The video also details breakthroughs in [[concepts/alzheimers-disease|Alzheimer's disease]] treatment, with the recent [[concepts/fda-approval-process|FDA approval]] of drugs like Lecanemab and Donanemab. These drugs, which work by clearing amyloid beta proteins, have been shown to slow [[concepts/cognitive-impact|cognitive decline]] by about 30% in early stages, potentially granting patients an extra ten months of independent living. New research on compounds like Catastatin, which target both amyloid and tau proteins, and advanced diagnostic blood tests capable of detecting Alzheimer's years before symptoms appear, contribute to what experts call an "era of extreme optimism," despite past failures and instances of research [[concepts/fraud|fraud]].

Beyond brain [[concepts/health|health]], the video explores the development of "eating batteries" and the successful [[concepts/secure|protection]] of Antarctica. Scientists at MIT have created tiny, ingestible batteries made from human-safe materials like magnesium and molybdenum trioxide. These batteries can power internal sensors for monitoring vital signs, delivering drugs, or detecting overdoses, and are designed to function for about three days before being safely absorbed by the body. This [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] offers a [[concepts/space-jetpacks|safer]] and more environmentally friendly alternative to traditional coin batteries or external [[concepts/energy-source|power sources]] for ingestible medical devices. The final segment celebrates the international agreement to protect Antarctica, a continent crucial for global [[concepts/thermal-regulation|climate regulation]] due to its ice sheet reflecting solar [[concepts/rays|rays]] and its cold waters influencing ocean circulation. Triggered by a 1973 oil [[concepts/crisis|crisis]] and solidified after a 1989 oil spill, nations ultimately signed the 1991 Madrid Protocol, designating Antarctica as a natural reserve devoted to peace and [[concepts/science|science]], effectively blocking mining and oil drilling.

In conclusion, the video underscores the power of human collaboration and scientific endeavor to address complex global challenges. From reversing trends in dementia and developing groundbreaking [[concepts/clinical-diagnostics|medical diagnostics]] and treatments, to creating innovative ingestible technologies and securing international agreements for planetary protection, these stories offer a compelling counter-[[concepts/storytelling|narrative]] to prevalent pessimism. The key takeaway is an encouragement to acknowledge and actively focus on these positive developments, recognizing that long-term [[concepts/human-cognition|thinking]] and global cooperation are essential for future progress.
