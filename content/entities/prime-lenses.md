# Prime Lenses

[[concepts/prime-lens|Prime]] lenses are fixed [[concepts/focal-length|focal length]] lenses that cannot zoom. Unlike zoom lenses, which offer variable focal lengths, prime lenses have a single, unchanging focal length such as 35mm, 50mm, or 85mm. This fixed perspective requires photographers to physically move closer to or farther from their subject to frame an image, rather than adjusting the lens itself.

## Optical Advantages

Prime lenses typically offer superior optical quality compared to zoom lenses [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] equivalent focal lengths. Because they have fewer glass elements and a simpler optical [[concepts/design|design]], prime lenses generally produce sharper [[concepts/images|images]] with less distortion and better color rendering. They also tend to have wider maximum apertures, allowing more [[concepts/light|light]] to reach the sensor and enabling better performance in low-light conditions and greater [[concepts/power|control]] over depth of field.

## Practical Considerations

The fixed focal length of prime lenses can initially feel limiting but often encourages more intentional composition and creative [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]]. Photographers must consider their positioning and perspective before shooting rather than relying on zooming to frame shots. Prime lenses are generally more compact and lightweight than comparable zoom lenses, making them practical for travel and situations where equipment weight is a consideration. They are also often less expensive than quality zoom lenses with similar maximum apertures.
