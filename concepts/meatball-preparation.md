# Meatball Preparation

## Core Principles
Meatball [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] relies on the manipulation of protein structures to achieve desired [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]], binding, and moisture [[concepts/storing|retention]]. The primary mechanism is **[[concepts/myosin-development|myosin development]]**, which creates a gel-like matrix that holds the meatball together during cooking.

## Myosin Development
Myosin is the protein responsible for binding [[concepts/ground-meat|ground meat]]. Proper extraction and development are critical for preventing crumbly textures.

- **Mechanism**: [[concepts/mechanical-agitation|Mechanical agitation]] (mixing/kneading) and salt extraction cause myosin to unfold and become sticky, forming a cohesive network.
- **[[concepts/uniform-crispness|Texture Control]]**:
  - **Burgers**: Minimal mixing to preserve tenderness; avoid over-working to prevent a rubbery [[concepts/texture|texture]].
  - **Sausages/Meatballs**: Vigorous mixing to develop a "snap" or firm bite.
- **Key Factors**:
  - **Salt**: Essential for extracting myosin from [[concepts/muscle-fibers|muscle fibers]].
  - **Temperature**: Keep meat cold to prevent fat smearing and premature protein coagulation.
  - **Agitation**: The degree of mixing directly correlates to the firmness of the final product.
