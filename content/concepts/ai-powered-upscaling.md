# AI-Powered Upscaling

AI-powered upscaling refers to [[concepts/photo-enhancement|image enhancement]] techniques that use [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] to increase [[concepts/image-resolution|image resolution]] while maintaining or improving visual quality. Unlike traditional interpolation methods that simply estimate pixel values between existing data, AI-based approaches learn patterns from large datasets to intelligently reconstruct detail and [[concepts/texture|texture]] when enlarging [[concepts/images|images]]. This technology has become integrated into professional image editing [[concepts/software|software]], enabling photographers and designers to enlarge images beyond their original dimensions with improved results compared to conventional methods.

## How It Works

AI upscaling models are typically trained on pairs of low-resolution and high-resolution images, [[concepts/learning|learning]] to recognize patterns and textures that characterize different subject matter. When applied to a new image, the trained model predicts plausible high-frequency details that would logically exist at higher resolutions. This differs fundamentally from pixel interpolation, which cannot add genuinely new visual information. The effectiveness of AI upscaling depends on the quality of [[concepts/training-data|training data]] and how well the model generalizes to new images outside its [[concepts/training|training]] set.

## Integration in Professional Software

[[concepts/photoshop|Adobe Photoshop]] has integrated [[concepts/neural-filters|AI upscaling tools]] including Generative Upscale and [[concepts/image-size|Preserve Details 2.0]], which offer different approaches to enlargement. Preserve Details 2.0 focuses on maintaining existing image characteristics during [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]], while Generative Upscale uses generative AI to add plausible detail. These tools represent the broader [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of machine learning techniques in mainstream creative software, making advanced image enhancement accessible to non-specialist users.
