# Collection Management Systems

Collection Management Systems (CMS) are software platforms designed to organize, preserve, and provide access to institutional collections in both digital and physical formats. These systems function as centralized repositories where [[entities/employees|staff]] can [[concepts/catalog|catalog]] materials, manage descriptive and technical [[concepts/metadata|metadata]], track item locations, and control user access permissions. By consolidating collection information into a single integrated system, institutions can streamline workflows, coordinate activities across departments, and maintain consistent documentation standards.

A primary function of these systems is the management of metadata, which ensures that collection items are discoverable and contextually understood. This includes handling descriptive data for public discovery, technical metadata for [[concepts/preservation|preservation]], and administrative data for rights management. The integration of these data types allows for interoperability with other [[concepts/digital-infrastructure|digital infrastructure]], such as [[concepts/asset-management|digital asset management]] systems and library catalogs, facilitating broader access and long-term sustainability of the records.

Institutional implementation varies based on the nature of the holdings, with some systems tailored for archival records and others for museum objects or library holdings. For example, the [[concepts/digital-repository|University of Melbourne Archives]] utilizes specific CMS tools to manage its diverse range of physical and born-digital materials, ensuring that provenance and context are preserved alongside the items themselves. Such implementations highlight the role of CMS in supporting [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] with archival standards and facilitating research access while protecting sensitive information.
