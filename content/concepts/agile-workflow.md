# Agile Workflow

Agile workflow is an iterative approach to development and task management that prioritizes adaptability, continuous [[concepts/feedback|feedback]], and incremental progress. Rather than following rigid, predetermined plans, agile workflows emphasize responding to changing requirements and [[concepts/learning|learning]] from successive [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] cycles. Work is broken into smaller, manageable increments that can be reviewed, tested, and refined based on stakeholder feedback and evolving project needs.

## Core Principles

Agile workflows operate on the principle that requirements and solutions evolve through collaborative effort. Teams work in short cycles, typically one to four weeks, delivering functional increments regularly. This cadence enables rapid detection of issues and course correction before significant resources are invested in the wrong direction. Communication between team members and stakeholders is continuous rather than formal and infrequent, reducing misalignment and accelerating [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]].

## Implementation and Context

Effective agile workflows require clear definition of work items, transparent progress tracking, and mechanisms for incorporating feedback at regular intervals. Teams typically employ practices such as daily standups, sprint planning, and retrospectives to maintain alignment and continuously improve their processes. The approach is particularly suited to projects where requirements are uncertain or subject to change, as the iterative [[entities/nature|nature]] allows teams to validate assumptions and adjust strategy based on actual results rather than initial estimates.
