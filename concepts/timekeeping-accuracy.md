# Timekeeping Accuracy

**Timekeeping Accuracy** refers to the precision with which a timekeeping device measures the passage of time, minimizing deviations from a standard reference (e.g., atomic time or solar time). Accuracy is influenced by mechanical stability, environmental factors, and design innovations aimed at mitigating error sources.

## Key Factors Influencing Accuracy

*   **[[concepts/gravity-compensation|Gravity Compensation]]:** Variations in position can cause rate errors in mechanical movements due to gravity acting on the balance wheel and escapement. Mechanisms like the Tourbillon [[concepts/watch-mechanism|Watch Mechanism]]: Purpose, Gravity Compensation, and 3D-Printed Model are designed to rotate the escapement and balance wheel to average out these positional errors.
*   **Isochronism:** The ability of a timekeeper to maintain the same period of oscillation regardless of the amplitude of the swing.
*   **Temperature Stability:** Thermal expansion/contraction affects spring tension and metal dimensions, requiring compensation alloys (e.g., bimetallic compensation balances).
*   **Friction Reduction:** Precision engineering of jewels and lubricants minimizes energy loss in the Escapement mechanism.
