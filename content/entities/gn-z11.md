# Gn Z11

Gn Z11 is a galaxy observed in the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]] whose observations have provided evidence for the presence of [[concepts/primordial-stars|primordial Population III]] [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]]. These hypothetical first-generation stars, composed almost entirely of hydrogen and helium from the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]], have long been predicted by [[concepts/stellar-evolution|stellar evolution]] [[concepts/theory|theory]] but had never been directly observed until potential evidence emerged from [[concepts/jwst-observations|JWST observations]] of this galaxy.

## Observational Significance

The detection of spectroscopic signatures consistent with [[concepts/first-generation-of-stars|Population III stars]] in Gn Z11 represents a major development in understanding stellar populations in the early universe. The data suggests the presence of extremely massive, short-lived stars that would have formed within the first few hundred million years after the Big Bang, offering insights into the initial conditions of star formation in the cosmos.

## Scientific Context

[[concepts/population-iii-stars|Population III stars]] are thought to have played a crucial role in the early universe by producing the first heavy elements and seeding subsequent generations of stars and galaxies. The observations of Gn Z11 provide empirical support for theoretical [[concepts/models|models]] of these primordial stellar populations, though confirmation and further study of similar candidates remain ongoing.
