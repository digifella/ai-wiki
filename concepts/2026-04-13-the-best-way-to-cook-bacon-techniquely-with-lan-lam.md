# 2026 04 13 The Best Way To Cook Bacon Techniquely With Lan Lam

[[concepts/bacon-cooking|Bacon preparation]] involves various techniques that influence [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]], [[concepts/crispy-coating|crispness]], and [[concepts/fat-rendering|fat rendering]]. Traditional methods such as [[concepts/oven-cooking-method|oven-baking]], [[concepts/skillet-frying|pan-frying]], and broiling rely on direct [[concepts/cooking-time|heat application]]. These conventional approaches differ in their [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]], cleanup requirements, and capacity to cook large batches simultaneously.

## The Water Method

The [[concepts/water-method|water method]] begins by placing bacon strips in a cold skillet with a small amount of water. As the skillet heats, the water gradually evaporates, allowing the bacon to cook in its own rendered fat once the liquid is gone. This process helps render fat more evenly and prevents the bacon from [[concepts/burning|burning]] or shrinking excessively during the initial heating [[concepts/phase|phase]].

## Comparison with Traditional Techniques

Unlike direct heat methods that can lead to uneven cooking or splatter, the water method offers a more controlled environment for fat rendering. It reduces the likelihood of burnt edges while promoting [[concepts/uniform-crispness|uniform crispness]]. This technique is particularly noted for its ability to manage the transition from raw to cooked states without the need for constant monitoring, making it a distinct alternative to standard pan-frying or broiling.
