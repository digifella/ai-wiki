# High Oxygen Period

The High Oxygen Period refers to a specific geological epoch characterized by elevated [[concepts/oxygen-saturation|atmospheric oxygen levels]] that profoundly influenced the composition and appearance of Earth's rocks and mineral deposits. During this time, large-scale accumulation of coal and oil deposits occurred, capturing organic material under conditions that would not be replicated in subsequent geological periods. The elevated oxygen availability during this epoch created distinctive geochemical conditions that affected rock formation and [[concepts/preservation|preservation]].

## Oxidation and Rock Formation

The abundance of atmospheric oxygen during this period caused widespread [[concepts/rock-oxidation|oxidation of rocks]] and minerals, a process commonly described as "rusting." This oxidation is particularly evident in the geological features of [[concepts/red-centre-of-australia|Australia's Red Centre]], where extensive iron oxide deposits give the landscape its characteristic reddish coloration. The oxidation of iron-bearing minerals during the High Oxygen Period created the visually distinctive iron oxide-rich rocks that define this region's [[concepts/geology|geology]].

## Significance for Understanding Deposits

The High Oxygen Period is notable for being the primary epoch in which coal and oil deposits were laid down. The specific atmospheric and environmental conditions of this time—particularly the elevated oxygen levels combined with suitable organic preservation mechanisms—were essential for the formation of these fossil fuel deposits. Understanding this period is therefore crucial for explaining both the [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] of economic mineral resources and the major features of Earth's geological landscape.
