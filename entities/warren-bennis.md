# Warren Bennis

## Overview
Warren Bennis (1925–2014) was a leading organizational theorist and one of the founding fathers of [[concepts/leadership]] studies. He is widely recognized for shifting the paradigm from "management" as administrative control to "leadership" as inspirational [[concepts/computer-vision|vision]] and change. Key works include *On Becoming a Leader*, *Leaders*, and *Geeks and Geezers*.

## Key Concepts
- **Leaders are made, not born**: Opposed the "Great Man" [[concepts/theory|theory]]; emphasized development through [[concepts/experience|experience]] and reflection.
- **The Leader's Challenge**: Focus on the future, while managers focus on the present.
- **Authenticity**: Leaders must be true to themselves and their values.
- **Vision**: The ability to create a compelling picture of the future that motivates others.
- **[[concepts/decision-making|Good Judgment]]**: Central to [[concepts/exemplary-leadership|exemplary leadership]]; the ability to combine [[concepts/personal-qualities|personal qualities]] with [[concepts/relevant-knowledge|relevant knowledge]] and experience to form [[concepts/opinions|opinions]] and make decisions when [[concepts/factual-knowledge|facts]] are ambiguous.
    - As detailed in [[lab-notes/2026-05-28-The-Elements-of-Good-Judgment|The Elements of Good Judgment]] (Likierman, 2020), judgment serves as the core of exemplary leadership when facts are assembled but no clear evidence supports a particular option.
    - It involves interpreting evidence to point to the right choice amidst [[concepts/ambiguity|ambiguity]], relying on the leader's ability to synthesize personal qualities with contextual knowledge.
