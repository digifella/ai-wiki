# Steel Failure

Steel failure refers to the degradation and loss of structural integrity in steel materials under various operational conditions. These degradation mechanisms can occur through multiple pathways, each with distinct physical and chemical characteristics. Understanding these mechanisms is essential for maintaining safety and reliability in applications ranging from pressure vessels to pipelines and chemical processing equipment.

## High-Temperature Hydrogen Attack

High-temperature hydrogen attack (HTHA) is a specific form of steel degradation that occurs when hydrogen diffuses into the steel lattice at elevated temperatures. The hydrogen reacts with carbides within the steel to form methane gas, which accumulates at grain boundaries and inclusions. This accumulation creates internal pressure that leads to microcracking, decarburization, and a subsequent loss of mechanical strength.

Detection of HTHA is critical for preventing catastrophic failures in industrial settings. Ultrasonic non-destructive testing (NDT) is commonly employed to identify the presence of methane bubbles and associated microstructural changes. By monitoring these indicators, engineers can assess the remaining life of steel components and implement maintenance strategies before structural integrity is critically compromised.
