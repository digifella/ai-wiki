# Human Landing System

The Human Landing System (HLS) is a spacecraft component designed to transport astronauts from lunar orbit to the Moon's surface and back as part of [[concepts/nasa|NASA]]'s [[concepts/artemis-program|Artemis program]]. The HLS operates within the broader Artemis architecture alongside the Space Launch System (SLS) and the [[concepts/orion-systems|Orion spacecraft]], collectively enabling crewed [[concepts/lunar-return|lunar missions]]. Unlike [[entities/artemis-ii|Artemis II]], which is planned as an uncrewed [[concepts/lunar-flyby|lunar flyby]], the HLS represents a critical element for achieving the program's goal of landing astronauts on the Moon during Artemis III.

## Technical Architecture

The HLS functions as the final stage of the crewed lunar mission profile, receiving the Orion spacecraft after it has been placed into lunar orbit by the SLS. Its primary role is to descend to the surface, support the crew during their extravehicular activities, and ascend to rendezvous with Orion for the return journey to [[entities/earth|Earth]]. The system must be capable of operating in the harsh lunar environment, including extreme temperature variations and vacuum conditions, while ensuring the safety and life support needs of the astronauts are met throughout the surface stay.

## Program Outlook and Readiness

The development and readiness of the HLS have been central to the timeline and [[concepts/success|success]] of the Artemis program. NASA has engaged multiple commercial partners to design and build HLS vehicles, aiming to reduce costs and increase flexibility compared to previous government-developed systems. The readiness of the HLS is a key dependency for Artemis III, as the mission cannot proceed without a verified vehicle capable of landing humans on the lunar surface. Challenges in manufacturing, testing, and integration have influenced the scheduling of early [[concepts/lunar-exploration|Artemis missions]], with the program outlook focusing on ensuring the HLS is fully operational and certified for crewed flight before the targeted launch date.
