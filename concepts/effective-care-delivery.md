# Effective Care Delivery

**Effective care delivery** refers to the provision of [[concepts/health-care|health services]] that improve health outcomes based on valid [[concepts/scientific-evidence|scientific evidence]], while avoiding low-value or harmful practices. It sits at the intersection of **clinical effectiveness**, **efficiency**, and **equity**.

## Core Principles
- **[[concepts/evidence-based-decision-making|Evidence-Based Practice]]**: Aligning care with current best evidence.
- **Patient-Centeredness**: [[concepts/respect|Respect]] for patient preferences, needs, and values.
- **Safety**: Minimizing risks of injury from care.
- **Timeliness**: Reducing harmful delays for both those who receive and those who give care.
- **Equity**: Providing care that does not vary in quality because of [[concepts/personal-qualities|personal characteristics]] (gender, ethnicity, geographic location, socioeconomic status).

## Key Drivers & Barriers
- **System Design**: Fragmented care pathways hinder [[concepts/continuity|continuity]].
- **Workforce Competencies**: Gaps in clinical [[concepts/skills|skills]] and health-literate communication.
- **Information [[concepts/flow|Flow]]**: Inadequate interoperability between [[concepts/patient-information|health records]].
- **Policy Alignment**: Misalignment between funding models and outcome incentives.
