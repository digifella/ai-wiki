## Fujifilm Advanced Filters: Location, Activation, and Film Simulation Differences
**Clip title:** The Fujifilm Filter Mode Hiding on Your Camera
**Author / channel:** pal2tech
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=65u7adbJTYA

### Summary
The video from [[entities/pal2tech|Pal2Tech]] addresses a common confusion among [[entities/fujifilm|Fujifilm]] camera users regarding a specific icon on their camera's display, which turned out to be the "[[concepts/partial-color-orange|Partial Color Orange]]" setting within the "[[concepts/advanced-filters|Advanced Filters]]" mode. The presenter highlights that most people incorrectly guessed its meaning, [[concepts/prompting|prompting]] him to dedicate this "Fast Friday" segment to explain these often-misunderstood features.

The core of the video focuses on how to locate and activate the Advanced Filter settings across different Fujifilm camera models. For cameras like the XS20, users need to rotate the top dial to the "FILTER" position, then navigate through the camera icon menu to "Filter Setting." On models such as the XT5 and XT3, the process involves rotating a ring beneath the ISO dial to the "ADV." (Advanced) position, and then selecting "ADV. FILTER SETTING" within the "[[concepts/ambition|Drive]] Setting" menu. The XT30 model offers two distinct Advanced Filter slots (Adv.1 and Adv.2) on its [[concepts/ambition|drive]] dial, illustrating that access methods can vary significantly between camera generations and models.

A crucial distinction is drawn between "Advanced Filters" and "Film Simulations." Advanced Filters are creative effects that the camera bakes directly into the JPEG file the moment the shutter is pressed. They operate exclusively in stills shooting mode, disable the live histogram, force auto-focus, and may gray out photogrammetry settings. In [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], [[concepts/film-simulations|Fujifilm Film Simulations]] are color [[concepts/science|science]] profiles that work with any shooting mode, including video, and are fully recoverable and adjustable from [[concepts/camera-raw|RAW files]] in post-processing.

The main takeaway and strong recommendation from the video is that if users choose to experiment with Advanced Filters, they should always shoot in both JPEG and RAW. This ensures that even if the applied filter is not desirable after the fact, a clean RAW file remains, providing full flexibility for post-processing without the baked-in filter effect. This dual-format shooting offers a safeguard against the irreversible nature of Advanced Filters on JPEG [[concepts/images|images]].
