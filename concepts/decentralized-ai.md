# Decentralized AI

[[concepts/decentralized-ai-supercomputer|Decentralized AI]] refers to [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems that distribute computation, data [[entities/storage|storage]], and [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] across a network of independent [[concepts/nodes|nodes]] rather than concentrating these functions in centralized servers or [[concepts/cloud-based-services|cloud infrastructure]] controlled by a single organization. This architectural approach contrasts with conventional [[concepts/cloud-ai|cloud-based AI]] models, where data and processing occur within infrastructure operated by a single provider.

## Core Architecture

In decentralized [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]], individual nodes perform portions of [[concepts/machine-learning|machine learning]] tasks, [[concepts/training-process|model training]], or [[concepts/inference|inference]] while maintaining local autonomy. Participants in the network retain control over their own data and [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]]. Communication between nodes typically occurs through peer-to-peer protocols or blockchain-based [[concepts/coordination|coordination]] [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]], enabling consensus on model [[concepts/software-updates|updates]] and validation without requiring a central authority.

## Technical Considerations

[[concepts/local-llm|Decentralized AI]] systems face distinct technical challenges compared to centralized alternatives. Coordinating model training across heterogeneous hardware and network conditions requires specialized [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]]. [[concepts/privacy|Data privacy]] can be enhanced through techniques like federated [[concepts/learning|learning]], where models are trained locally and only updates are shared. However, decentralized approaches often involve increased computational overhead, latency, and complexity in maintaining system coherence and [[concepts/security|security]].

## Current Applications

Decentralized AI architectures are being explored in contexts where data privacy, [[concepts/ecosystem-resilience|system resilience]], or elimination of single points of failure are priorities. Applications include federated learning for [[concepts/health|healthcare]] data, [[concepts/remote-inference|distributed inference]] networks, and blockchain-[[concepts/integrated-ai-systems|integrated AI systems]]. [[concepts/adoption|Adoption]] remains limited compared to centralized cloud [[concepts/ai-platforms|AI services]], with most decentralized projects still in experimental or early deployment stages.
