# Macro Lens

A macro lens is a specialized camera lens designed to achieve high magnification ratios, typically 1:1 or greater, enabling extremely close focusing distances. This optical characteristic allows photographers to capture small subjects [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] life-size or larger-than-life [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]], revealing fine details invisible to the naked eye. Macro lenses are essential tools in fields requiring detailed documentation, including product photography, scientific imaging, and [[entities/nature|nature]] documentation.

## Film Scanning Applications

Macro lenses have become increasingly practical for [[concepts/camera-scanning|home film scanning]], offering a cost-effective alternative to professional laboratory scanning services. By mounting a macro lens on a digital camera body and positioning it to photograph film [[entities/google-slides|slides]] or negatives against a [[concepts/light|light]] source, photographers can create high-resolution digital [[concepts/files|files]] without expensive equipment. This technique requires careful [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to focus, lighting, and camera stability, but produces results suitable for archival purposes and general use, while significantly reducing per-frame scanning costs compared to commercial services.

## Technical Considerations

When using a macro lens for [[concepts/film-scanning|film scanning]], lens quality and reproduction ratio directly affect image [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]] and detail capture. Most macro lenses suitable for this [[concepts/motivation|purpose]] feature minimum focusing distances of 10-20 centimeters and offer either manual or autofocus [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]]. Proper lighting [[concepts/setup|setup]]—typically a light table or backlit surface—is crucial for even [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] across the film frame. Camera stability, achieved through tripods or specialized copy stands, prevents motion blur and ensures consistent image alignment across multiple scans.
