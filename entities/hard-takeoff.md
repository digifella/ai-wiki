# Hard Takeoff

Hard takeoff is a theoretical scenario in artificial intelligence research describing a phase of extremely rapid AI development driven by recursive self-improvement. In this model, an AI system reaches a capability threshold where it becomes capable of meaningfully improving its own architecture and algorithms. Once self-directed enhancement begins, the system enters a positive feedback loop, with each iteration of improvement enabling faster subsequent improvements. This contrasts with a "soft takeoff" scenario, where AI capabilities develop more gradually over an extended period.

The concept is central to discussions regarding the intelligence explosion, a hypothetical event where AI systems rapidly surpass human intelligence. Proponents of the hard takeoff hypothesis argue that once an AI achieves artificial general intelligence (AGI), the speed of its self-modification could lead to a superintelligence in a very short timeframe, potentially measured in days or even hours. This rapid transition raises significant concerns about alignment and control, as there may be insufficient time for humans to intervene or adjust the AI's goals.

Matthew Berman has analyzed these concepts in video content, focusing on the mechanics of recursive self-improvement and the implications for AI safety. The hard takeoff model remains a subject of debate within the AI community, with some researchers favoring gradual development trajectories while others maintain that sudden capability jumps are plausible given certain architectural assumptions.
