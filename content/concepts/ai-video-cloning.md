# AI Video Cloning

AI [[concepts/ai-clone|video cloning]] is a technology that uses [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] to create [[concepts/digital-avatars|digital avatars]] capable of replicating a person's appearance and [[concepts/tone|voice]] in video form. The technology synthesizes [[concepts/computer-vision|computer vision]], facial recognition, and [[concepts/text-to-speech-generation|speech synthesis]] to generate realistic video performances of individuals without requiring their physical presence during filming. These synthetic videos can depict a person delivering speeches, performances, or other content based on [[concepts/text|text]] or [[concepts/audio-modality|audio]] input.

## Technical Foundation

The underlying technology relies on [[concepts/deep-learning-models|deep learning models]] trained on video footage and audio samples of a person. These models learn to map facial movements, expressions, and vocal characteristics from source material, then generate new video frames that depict the person performing novel actions or speaking new [[concepts/dialogue|dialogue]]. The process typically involves both visual synthesis—generating photorealistic facial movements and expressions—and audio synthesis to match lip movements with generated or modified speech.

## Applications and Considerations

AI video cloning has potential [[concepts/software|applications]] in entertainment, education, [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]], and business communications, such as creating content in multiple languages or allowing individuals to participate in events remotely. However, the technology raises significant concerns regarding consent, identity fraud, misinformation, and deepfakes. The ease with which synthetic videos can be created has prompted discussions about [[concepts/regulation|regulation]], [[concepts/authentication|authentication]] methods, and the need for transparency when such technology is used.
