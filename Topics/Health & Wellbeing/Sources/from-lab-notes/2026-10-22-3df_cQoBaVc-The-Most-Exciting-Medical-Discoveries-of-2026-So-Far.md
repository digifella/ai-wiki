## The Most Exciting Medical Discoveries of 2026 (So Far)

### Summary
Sure, here's a summary of the video content:

The video explores nine exciting medical developments of 2026, highlighting significant advancements in various fields.

Key points discussed in the video include:

1. **Daranoxrasib for Pancreatic Cancer:** A new pill, Daranoxrasib, nearly doubled median survival in a major trial this year, from 6.7 months to 13.2 months. The drug targets RAS proteins, which are often mutated in pancreatic cancer cells and send constant growth signals.

2. **Malaria Vaccination:** The RTS,S malaria vaccine has reduced childhood deaths in Ghana, Kenya, and Malawi by about 13%, demonstrating significant impact even with other competing health threats.

3. **Lonvoguran Ziclumeran for Hereditary Angioedema:** Researchers are developing Lonvoguran Ziclumeran, a single infusion treatment that edits DNA in liver cells to reduce pre-kallikrein production, offering potential long-term relief from hereditary angioedema attacks.

4. **Otarmeni for Genetic Deafness:** The FDA approved Otarmeni, a gene therapy designed to treat severe-to-profound sensorineural hearing loss caused by mutations in the OTOF gene, restoring otoferlin production and auditory signaling.

5. **Retatrutide and Weight Loss:** Retatrutide, a weekly injection, has delivered powerful weight loss approaching the range of bariatric surgery, acting on three hormone receptors to regulate appetite, insulin, and energy expenditure.

6. **CAR-T for Autoimmune Disease:** Researchers in Germany are exploring the use of CAR-T cell therapy, typically used for cancer, to remove B cells and potentially reset the immune system in patients with severe autoimmune diseases.

7. **Pig Kidney Bridge:** Massachusetts General Hospital performed a second groundbreaking xenotransplantation of a genetically edited pig kidney into a living patient, providing a bridge to allotransplantation and extending the patient's survival without dialysis.

8. **The Prima Retinal Implant:** The Prima retinal implant system has received a CE mark in Europe, offering a new treatment for severe visual impairment caused by geographic atrophy in age-related macular degeneration by converting near-infrared light into electrical currents.

9. **Brain Interface at Home:** A brain-computer interface (BCI) has enabled a patient with ALS to communicate effectively from home, recording electrical activity from the brain and translating it into speech and text.

The video concludes that these medical developments represent significant milestones in treating various diseases and conditions, offering hope for improved patient outcomes and quality of life.
