# Terrestrial Habitat Simulation

Terrestrial habitat [[concepts/simulation|simulation]] refers to the controlled recreation of Earth's ecosystems and environmental conditions for scientific study, research, and experimentation. These simulations [[concepts/range|range]] from small laboratory-scale [[concepts/models|models]] to large enclosed structures designed to replicate complex interactions between atmosphere, water, soil, and living organisms. The primary objective is to understand how terrestrial systems function and respond to various environmental changes without the variables and scale of real-world ecosystems.

## Biosphere 2 and Its Significance

The [[concepts/controlled-climate-experiment|Biosphere 2 experiment]], conducted in Arizona beginning in 1991, stands as the largest and most ambitious terrestrial habitat simulation to date. The facility enclosed 1.3 million cubic meters of space containing multiple biomes—including rainforest, ocean, marsh, and desert—along with an agricultural area and human habitat. The experiment aimed to determine whether a closed ecological system could sustain human life while maintaining its own environmental balance.

The results of [[concepts/closed-ecosystem|Biosphere 2]] revealed fundamental insights about Earth's systems that had previously been underestimated or poorly understood. The experiment demonstrated the complexity of atmospheric [[concepts/regulation|regulation]], the difficulty of maintaining [[concepts/ecological-balance|ecosystem stability]] in isolation, and the critical importance of microbial processes in sustaining life. These findings highlighted gaps in scientific knowledge about how interconnected terrestrial systems truly are, ultimately advancing understanding of Earth's biosphere and informing discussions about long-term human sustainability, whether on Earth or in [[concepts/space-exploration|space exploration]] contexts.
