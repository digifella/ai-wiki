# Lidar Scanning

LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) is a remote sensing technology that utilizes laser pulses to measure distances and generate detailed spatial maps. The system operates by emitting infrared light and calculating the time it takes for the reflections to return to the sensor. This time-of-flight measurement enables the device to compute precise depth information for objects and environments within its field of view, capturing spatial geometry with millimeter-level accuracy.

In the context of modern mobile hardware, specifically iPhone Pro devices, LiDAR scanners are integrated directly into the camera module to enhance augmented reality experiences and low-light photography. These compact sensors emit a pattern of invisible laser dots, allowing the device to construct a depth map of the surrounding environment in real-time. This integration brings high-precision spatial awareness to consumer-grade mobile devices, previously limited to larger, specialized equipment.

The depth data captured by these mobile LiDAR systems serves as a critical input for advanced 3D reconstruction techniques, such as 3D Gaussian Splatting. By combining the precise geometric data from the LiDAR scanner with high-resolution photographic imagery, researchers and developers can create detailed, photorealistic 3D models. This process allows for the efficient capture of complex scenes, enabling applications in digital archiving, virtual reality, and scientific visualization with greater speed and accuracy than traditional photogrammetry alone.
