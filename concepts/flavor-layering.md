# Flavor Layering

Flavor layering is a fundamental cooking technique that involves building depth and complexity in a dish by adding ingredients and seasonings in strategic stages rather than all at once. This method allows each component to develop its own character while contributing to a cohesive whole, resulting in more nuanced and satisfying flavors. By controlling when ingredients are introduced during cooking, chefs can maximize the extraction and interaction of flavors, preventing any single element from overwhelming the dish.

## Application in North Indian Cuisine

In North Indian cooking, this technique is essential for developing the rich profiles characteristic of the region's curries. The process typically begins with the tempering of whole spices in hot oil or ghee to release their essential oils. Following this, aromatics such as onions are sautéed until caramelized to provide a sweet, savory base. Spices are then added at specific intervals to ensure they bloom without burning, allowing their distinct notes to integrate with the base before liquids are introduced.

Chef Neel Kajale’s North Indian Skillet Chicken Curry exemplifies this approach by carefully sequencing the addition of ingredients. The chicken is often seared first to develop a crust, after which the spice paste and yogurt are incorporated to tenderize the meat and create a thick gravy. This staged introduction ensures that the spices do not lose their potency to excessive boiling and that the final dish maintains a balanced harmony between the heat of the spices, the acidity of the yogurt, and the richness of the fat.
