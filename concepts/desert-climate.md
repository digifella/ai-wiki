# Desert Climate

[[concepts/arid-climate|Desert climate]] refers to a climate system characterized by extremely low annual precipitation, typically less than 250 millimeters (10 inches) per year. In these regions, evaporation rates consistently exceed precipitation input, creating a persistent water deficit that fundamentally shapes both the physical environment and biological communities. This moisture [[concepts/limited-resources|scarcity]] is the defining feature that distinguishes desert climates from all other climate classifications.

## Formation and Geographic Distribution

Desert climates form through several [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]]. Subtropical deserts, such as the Sahara and Kalahari, result from descending air masses in the subtropics that inhibit cloud formation. Rain shadow deserts, including the Great Basin Desert, occur where mountain ranges block moisture-bearing winds from reaching inland areas. Cold ocean currents along some coastal regions suppress precipitation, creating coastal deserts like the Atacama. Deserts are distributed across all continents and occur at various latitudes, though they are most common between 15° and 35° north and south of the equator.

## Temperature and Thermal Characteristics

Desert climates exhibit high temperature variability as a key environmental process. During the day, intense [[concepts/solar-energy|solar radiation]] produces extremely high surface temperatures, while the sparse vegetation and low atmospheric moisture allow rapid heat loss at night, resulting in dramatic temperature fluctuations. These thermal extremes influence weathering processes, water availability through dew formation, and the adaptive strategies of desert organisms. Annual temperature ranges in continental deserts can exceed 30°C (54°F), with some locations experiencing diurnal temperature swings of 40°C (72°F) or more.

## Environmental Significance

The combination of low precipitation and [[concepts/heat-stress|thermal stress]] creates distinctive ecosystems with specialized vegetation and fauna adapted to [[concepts/water-scarcity|water scarcity]] and temperature extremes. Desert soils often accumulate salts and minerals due to limited leaching, affecting soil chemistry and plant growth. These climate regions play important roles in global [[concepts/atmospheric-dynamics|atmospheric circulation]] patterns and represent critical environments for understanding climate dynamics and [[concepts/evolutionary-adaptations|biological adaptation]].
