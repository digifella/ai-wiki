# Agile Workflow

Agile workflow is an iterative approach to development and task management that prioritizes adaptability and continuous feedback over rigid, predetermined plans. Rather than committing to fixed requirements at the outset, agile workflows break work into smaller, manageable increments—often called sprints or iterations—that can be reviewed, tested, and refined based on stakeholder input and evolving project needs. This approach acknowledges that requirements frequently change during development and that learning occurs throughout the process.

## Core Characteristics

Agile workflows emphasize regular delivery of working increments, direct communication between team members and stakeholders, and flexibility in responding to change. Work is typically organized into time-boxed cycles lasting one to four weeks, with daily standups or check-ins to track progress and identify blockers. Teams maintain a prioritized backlog of tasks that can be reordered based on emerging needs, allowing projects to adapt to new information or shifting priorities without abandoning the entire plan.

## Integration with Development Tools

Modern agile workflows often rely on specialized platforms and tools for task tracking, version control, and collaboration. These tools facilitate transparency across distributed teams, automate routine processes, and provide visibility into project status through dashboards and reporting features. When combined with systematic approaches to engineering—such as careful context management and clear documentation of system capabilities—agile workflows can scale effectively across complex projects while maintaining the responsiveness that characterizes the agile methodology.
