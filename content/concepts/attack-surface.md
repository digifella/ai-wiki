# Attack Surface

The **attack surface** is the total sum of all potential entry points where an attacker could compromise a system, including network interfaces, APIs, user inputs, and third-party integrations. A larger attack surface increases [[concepts/vulnerability|vulnerability]] to exploitation.

*   **Agentic [[concepts/ai-expansion|AI expansion]]**: "[[concepts/action-oriented-ai|Agentic AI]]" (systems that act, not just think) significantly expands the attack surface by enabling [[concepts/agents|agents]] to interact with APIs, move data, conduct transactions, and create [[concepts/sub-agents|sub-agents]] [[concepts/agentic-ai]].
*   **[[concepts/zero|Zero]] Trust implementation**: IBM's [[entities/jeff-crume|Jeff Crume]] (Distinguished Engineer) advocates for [[concepts/zero-trust]] principles in AI [[entities/agent|agent]] [[concepts/security|security]], requiring strict [[concepts/verification|verification]] of all agent interactions to mitigate risks from data [[concepts/exercise|movement]] and tool calls [[concepts/zero-trust]].
*   **[[concepts/trust-follows-verification|Zero Trust for AI Agents]]**: [[entities/jeff-crume|Jeff Crume]] discusses the challenges of securing [[concepts/agentic-ai|Agentic AI]] systems, emphasizing the need for strict verification of all agent interactions, including [[entities/api-calls|API calls]], tool usage, and data movement IBM [[entities/jeff-crume|Jeff Crume]] [[concepts/zero-trust|Zero Trust]] [[concepts/verification|verification]].
