# Kanban

Kanban is a project management methodology that originated in [[concepts/low-cost-manufacturing|lean manufacturing]] at Toyota in the 1940s before being adapted for [[concepts/knowledge-work|knowledge work]] and [[concepts/coding|software development]]. The term "kanban" comes from Japanese and means "signboard" or "billboard." The methodology is built around visualizing work, limiting work in progress, and managing flow to improve efficiency and responsiveness.

## Core Principles

The fundamental approach of Kanban involves making work visible through a board or card system, typically organized into columns representing different stages of a process (such as "To Do," "In Progress," and "Done"). Each task or work item is represented as a card that moves across these columns as it progresses. A key practice is setting work-in-progress (WIP) limits for each stage to prevent bottlenecks and maintain sustainable workflow. This focus on continuous flow distinguishes Kanban from time-boxed [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] methods.

## Application and Adoption

Kanban has become widely adopted across various industries beyond manufacturing, including software development, marketing, HR, and general business operations. It is often used as a standalone methodology or combined with other frameworks such as Scrum (a hybrid approach sometimes called "Scrumban"). The flexibility of Kanban makes it suitable for teams managing variable workloads or projects with continuously arriving requirements, as it does not require fixed sprint cycles.
