# Layer Masks

Layer masks are a fundamental technique in Photoshop that enable non-destructive editing by controlling the visibility of specific areas within a layer. Rather than permanently deleting or erasing content, a layer mask uses grayscale values to determine transparency: white reveals the layer content, black conceals it, and gray values create partial transparency. This approach preserves the original image data while allowing flexible adjustments that can be modified or removed at any time.

The primary utility of layer masks lies in their ability to seamlessly combine elements from multiple photos. By masking out unwanted backgrounds or isolating specific subjects, editors can composite images without losing the integrity of the original source files. This method is particularly effective for complex tasks such as correcting specific details in group images, such as replacing closed eyes with open ones from another frame within the same shot.

Because the mask is separate from the pixel data, it offers significant flexibility during the post-processing workflow. Editors can refine the edges of a mask, adjust its density, or invert its effects without altering the underlying image. This non-destructive nature ensures that creative decisions remain reversible, allowing for precise control over how different layers interact and blend within a composition.
