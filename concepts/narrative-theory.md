# Narrative Theory

Narrative theory is an academic discipline that examines how stories function across different media, including literature, film, games, and digital platforms. The field investigates the fundamental structures, conventions, and techniques through which narratives are constructed and how they convey meaning to audiences. By analyzing core components such as plot, character, point of view, and temporal structure, narrative theory develops frameworks for understanding how audiences interpret and derive meaning from stories.

## Key Areas of Study

Narrative theory encompasses several interconnected areas of inquiry. Structuralist approaches examine the underlying patterns and rules that organize narratives, while hermeneutic approaches focus on how meaning is produced through interpretation. The field also considers how narrative conventions vary across media—examining differences between linear storytelling in novels, visual composition in film, and interactive branching in games. Additionally, narrative theory investigates how cultural context, audience perspective, and historical period shape both the construction and reception of narratives.

## Application in Games and Interactive Media

Narrative theory has become particularly relevant in game studies, where it addresses how player agency and interactivity affect traditional narrative structures. Unlike passive media, games present unique challenges for narrative analysis because players make choices that influence story progression. This has led theorists to develop new frameworks for understanding how game narratives balance authorial intent with player autonomy, and how non-linear structures create meaning differently than conventional storytelling forms.
