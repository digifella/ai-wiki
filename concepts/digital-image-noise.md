# Digital Image Noise

**Digital Image Noise** refers to random variations of brightness or color information in images, produced by Sensors and Image Sensor readout circuits. It manifests as grainy [[concepts/texture|texture]], reducing [[concepts/sharpness|image clarity]] and detail.

## Types and Causes

- **Shot Noise**: Fundamental statistical fluctuation in photon arrival; inherent to the nature of [[concepts/light|light]].
- **Read Noise**: Electronic noise introduced during [[concepts/signal-processing]] and Analog-to-Digital Conversion.
- **Thermal Noise (Dark Current)**: Generated by heat within the sensor; increases significantly with [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] time and ambient temperature.
- **[[concepts/parameter-reduction|Quantization]] Noise**: Arises from the finite number of [[concepts/classical-bits|bits]] used to represent pixel values.
- **ISO Amplification**: Increasing ISO amplifies both signal and noise, lowering the [[concepts/camera-raw|Signal-to-Noise Ratio]] (SNR).

## Mitigation Strategies

### Capture Phase
- **Low ISO**: Use the base ISO whenever possible to maximize [[concepts/dynamic-range]].
- **Proper Exposure**: "Expose to the right" (ETTR) to maximize signal relative to noise floor, avoiding Clipping.
- **Cooler Sensors**: Minimize thermal noise by reducing exposure duration or using external cooling ([[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]]).
- **Lens Quality**: Use faster lenses to allow lower [[concepts/iso-settings|ISO settings]].

### Post-Processing
- **Noise Reduction [[concepts/algorithms|Algorithms]]**: Software tools (e.g., Denoise AI, Topaz DeNoise) that distinguish [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]] from noise.
- **Luminance vs. [[entities/chroma|Chroma]]**: Apply stronger reduction to chrominance (color noise) as it is more visually objectionable than luminance (grain).
- **Multi-Frame Averaging**: Combine multiple exposures to average out random noise, preserving signal.
