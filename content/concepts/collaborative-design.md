# Collaborative Design

Collaborative [[concepts/design|design]] is an approach to creative [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] that emphasizes teamwork, shared [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]], and integrated [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] throughout the [[concepts/design-thinking|design process]]. Rather than following traditional linear workflows where designers hand off completed work to stakeholders, collaborative design embeds input from multiple perspectives—including designers, developers, product managers, and end users—[[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] each stage. This methodology aims to improve outcomes by catching issues earlier, aligning diverse viewpoints, and building shared ownership of creative decisions.

## Technology and Tooling

Modern collaborative design increasingly leverages AI-assisted tools to streamline workflows and accelerate prototyping. [[concepts/ai-assisted-prototyping|Google Stitch]] exemplifies this evolution as an [[concepts/ai-native-design-canvas|AI-native design canvas]] that integrates generative [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] with design fundamentals. Such platforms aim to reduce repetitive tasks, enable rapid [[concepts/iteration|iteration]], and allow human designers to focus on higher-level creative and strategic decisions while AI handles elements like layout suggestions, component generation, and design [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]].

## Impact on Design Workflows

The shift toward collaborative, AI-augmented design affects how teams organize their work and measure success. Rather than viewing AI as a replacement for human designers, collaborative frameworks position these tools as assistants that enhance productivity and enable broader participation in design decisions. This can flatten hierarchies within creative teams and allow non-specialists to contribute meaningfully to design discussions, though it also raises questions about maintaining design quality and coherence across distributed workflows.
