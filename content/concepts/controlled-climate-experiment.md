# Controlled Climate Experiment

A controlled climate experiment is a large-scale environmental study conducted within an enclosed or highly regulated system to simulate and observe Earth's biosphere and climate interactions under controlled conditions. These experiments allow researchers to manipulate variables such as temperature, atmospheric composition, and resource availability while measuring their effects on ecosystems, without the confounding factors present in open natural systems. By isolating specific environmental conditions, scientists can test hypotheses about how living organisms and physical systems respond to [[concepts/climate-change|climate change]] and other environmental stressors.

## Biosphere 2

The most prominent example is [[concepts/closed-ecosystem|Biosphere 2]], a closed ecological experiment constructed in Arizona in the late 1980s. The facility contained multiple interconnected biomes—including a rainforest, ocean, and desert—along with human habitats, designed to function as a self-sustaining system. The initial two-year mission (1991–1993) aimed to demonstrate whether humans could live in a sealed environment while maintaining its biological systems. Although the experiment revealed significant challenges with atmospheric chemistry, nutrient cycling, and species survival, it generated valuable data about ecosystem dynamics and the complexity of maintaining closed-[[concepts/loop|loop]] life support systems.

## Scientific Impact

Controlled climate experiments have advanced understanding of how biotic and abiotic systems interact, particularly regarding carbon cycling, microbial ecology, and the [[concepts/resilience|resilience]] of ecosystems under [[concepts/stress|stress]]. Findings from these studies informed climate modeling efforts and highlighted the difficulty of predicting ecosystem behavior in changing conditions. Modern controlled environment facilities continue to contribute to climate research, though they are now typically smaller in scale and more narrowly focused on specific questions about soil processes, plant [[concepts/responses|responses]], or atmospheric chemistry.
