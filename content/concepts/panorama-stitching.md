# Panorama Stitching

Panorama stitching is a photographic technique in which multiple overlapping [[concepts/images|images]] are digitally combined to create a single, wider composition than any individual frame. Rather than relying solely on wide-angle lenses or smartphone panorama modes, photographers can intentionally shoot and stitch panoramas using standard or longer focal lengths to achieve specific aesthetic and technical results.

## Compression and Perspective

One key advantage of panorama stitching is the ability to create wide-angle compositions while retaining the visual characteristics of longer lenses. Long focal lengths compress perspective and flatten depth [[concepts/relationships|relationships]], which can enhance certain subjects and scenes. By stitching multiple frames taken with a longer lens, photographers obtain both the expansive field of view of a wide-angle shot and the compressed perspective typical of [[concepts/telephoto-lens|telephoto]] [[concepts/optics|optics]]—an effect difficult to achieve with a single image.

## Practical Application

The technique typically involves capturing a series of overlapping photographs across a scene, then using stitching [[concepts/software|software]] to align and blend them seamlessly. This approach offers advantages beyond simply capturing expansive landscapes, including greater resolution in the final image and improved control over perspective and composition compared to in-camera panorama functions found on smartphones and cameras.
