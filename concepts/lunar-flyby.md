# Lunar Flyby

A lunar flyby is a [[concepts/human-spaceflight|crewed spaceflight]] trajectory that brings a spacecraft and its crew near the Moon without entering lunar orbit or landing on the surface. This mission profile allows astronauts to travel beyond [[entities/earth|Earth]] orbit, conduct scientific observations, and test [[concepts/spacecraft-systems|spacecraft systems]] in the deep [[concepts/vacuum-of-space|space environment]] while returning to Earth on a predetermined trajectory. Lunar flybys serve as intermediate stepping stones in human spaceflight [[concepts/software|programs]], offering opportunities to validate life support systems, navigation procedures, and crew operations in environments far from Earth before committing to more complex missions.

## Mission Characteristics

Lunar flyby missions follow a trans-lunar trajectory that typically brings the spacecraft within several hundred kilometers of the lunar surface. The spacecraft reaches high velocity relative to the Moon due to its gravitational influence, which naturally redirects the vehicle back toward Earth. This approach requires less energy and fuel than entering lunar orbit, making it a cost-effective option for testing systems and training crews in deep space operations.

## Scientific and Operational Value

During a lunar flyby, the crew can conduct Earth observations, test communication systems at lunar distances, and gather scientific data about the cislunar environment. The mission validates critical procedures for human deep space exploration, including spacecraft performance in thermal and radiation conditions beyond low Earth orbit. The extended duration of the mission—typically lasting several days to over a week—provides meaningful data on crew physiology and system reliability under conditions approaching those required for sustained lunar exploration and eventual missions to other destinations.
