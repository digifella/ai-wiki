# Wearable Devices

Wearable devices are electronic instruments designed to be worn on the body, incorporating embedded sensors that continuously collect data on physiological parameters, movement, or environmental factors. These devices function through integrated hardware and software systems that monitor, record, and often transmit data in real time or at intervals. They have become established tools in both consumer health applications and clinical allied health practice.

## Clinical and Allied Health Applications

In allied health settings, wearable devices are used for patient monitoring, rehabilitation tracking, and chronic disease management. Physical therapists utilize motion-sensing wearables to assess gait, balance, and range of motion, providing objective data to guide treatment plans. Cardiologists and pulmonologists employ devices that track heart rate variability, oxygen saturation, and respiratory patterns to monitor patient stability outside of hospital environments.

These tools facilitate remote patient monitoring, allowing healthcare providers to detect early signs of deterioration or non-compliance with therapeutic regimens. By capturing longitudinal data, wearables support evidence-based decision-making and enable more personalized care interventions. The integration of these devices into clinical workflows enhances the efficiency of allied health services while improving patient outcomes through continuous, non-invasive assessment.
