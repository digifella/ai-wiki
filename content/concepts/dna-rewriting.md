# DNA Rewriting

DNA rewriting refers to emerging gene-editing technologies that build upon and extend the capabilities of [[concepts/crispr|CRISPR]] systems. While CRISPR revolutionized [[concepts/gene-editing|gene editing]] by enabling precise cuts to DNA sequences, successor technologies aim to expand the range of editable mutations and improve [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]] and efficiency. These advances represent the next generation of molecular tools for modifying genetic material in living organisms.

## Research at the Broad Institute

[[entities/feng-zhang|Dr. Feng Zhang]] at the Broad Institute has been instrumental in developing next-generation gene-editing approaches beyond CRISPR. His research focuses on creating new systems that can address limitations of earlier technologies, including the ability to edit a broader spectrum of genetic variants and reducing off-target effects. These developments are documented in scientific publications and public outreach efforts, including appearances on educational platforms like [[entities/scishow-channel|SciShow]].

The progression from CRISPR to newer DNA rewriting methods reflects the iterative [[entities/nature|nature]] of biotechnology research, where each advancement in gene-editing capability opens new possibilities for understanding and potentially treating genetic diseases. As these technologies mature, they may enable more precise therapeutic interventions than currently possible with first-generation CRISPR systems.
