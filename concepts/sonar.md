# Sonar

Sonar is a detection system that uses sound waves to locate and identify objects underwater. The term is an acronym for "sound navigation and ranging." The technology operates by transmitting acoustic signals through water, which reflect off objects and return to the source as echoes. By analyzing these returning signals, operators can determine the distance, direction, speed, and other characteristics of detected objects. Sonar has been fundamental to underwater exploration, naval operations, and marine research since its development in the early twentieth century.

## Active and Passive Sonar

Sonar systems are classified into two main categories based on their mode of operation. Active sonar generates its own acoustic signals and listens for reflections, allowing operators to actively search for and map underwater features. Passive sonar, by contrast, listens to sounds produced by other sources—such as marine life, ship engines, or geological activity—without emitting signals of its own. Passive systems are useful for detecting moving objects without revealing the sonar's own position, making them valuable for military and research applications where stealth is important.

## Applications

Sonar technology serves diverse purposes across multiple fields. Naval forces use sonar for submarine detection and underwater navigation. Oceanographic research employs sonar to map seafloor topography and study marine ecosystems. Fishing industries use sonar to locate fish schools. Modern sonar systems also support underwater construction, pipeline inspection, and search-and-rescue operations. The technology continues to evolve with advances in signal processing and computer analysis, improving detection capabilities and reducing false readings.
