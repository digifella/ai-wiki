# Daniel Kahneman

**[[entities/daniel-miessler|Daniel]] Kahneman** (1934–2024) was an Israeli-American psychologist and economist, notable for his work on the [[concepts/psychology|psychology]] of judgment and [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]]. He won the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 2002 for integrating psychological research into economic [[concepts/science|science]].

## Key Concepts

*   **[[concepts/human-cognition|Thinking]], Fast and Slow**: Proposed a dual-system model of [[concepts/the-mind|the mind]]:
    *   **System 1**: Fast, automatic, frequent, emotional, stereotypic, subconscious.
    *   **System 2**: Slow, effortful, infrequent, logical, calculating, conscious.
*   **Prospect [[concepts/theory|Theory]]**: Describes how people choose between probabilistic alternatives involving risk, where the values of gains and losses are measured relative to a reference point rather than final wealth.
*   **[[concepts/cognitive-biases|Cognitive Biases]]**: Identified numerous systematic deviations from rationality, including:
    *   Anchoring Effect
    *   Availability Heuristic
    *   Confirmation Bias
    *   Halo Effect
    *   Overconfidence Effect
    *   Sunk Cost Fallacy

## Modern Relevance & AI

Kahneman's framework is increasingly applied to evaluate [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems, particularly in distinguishing between [[concepts/generative-ai|generative models]] and decision-support models.

*   **System 1 Analogues in AI**: Recent developments in AI focus on models that prioritize rapid, probabilistic decision-making over [[concepts/text-generation|text generation]], mirroring Kahneman's "System 1" characteristics.
*   **[[concepts/system-1-classification|JEV Model]]**: The JEV: [[concepts/probabilistic-ai-models|Probabilistic AI]] for Fast, Confident Decision Support represents a novel approach developed by TypeSafe. Unlike traditional [[concepts/demystifying-llms|Large Language Models]] (LLMs) that operate primarily through text generation, JEV focuses on rapid probabilistic [[concepts/ai-inference|inference]].
    *   It is explicitly categorized as a "[[concepts/system-one-architecture|System One]]" model, drawing direct analogy to Kahneman's theory.
    *   This highlights the shift from generative AI to decision-support AI, aligning with Kahneman's distinction between intuitive (fast) and deliberative (slow) processing.
