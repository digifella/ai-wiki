# Metal Refining

**[[concepts/metal|Metal]] refining** is the process of removing impurities from crude metals to produce high-purity materials. Techniques range from physical methods (distillation, zone melting) to chemical and [[concepts/energy-applications|electrochemical processes]].

## Key Methods

### Electrolytic Refining
*   **Mechanism**: Uses electric current to dissolve anode metal and deposit pure metal at the cathode. Impurities either remain in [[concepts/solution|solution]] or fall as anode slime.
*   **Applications**: Standard for high-purity copper, [[concepts/major-gold-deposit|gold]], silver, and nickel production.
*   **DIY/Experimental Context**:
    *   Demonstrated via simple setups using affordable ingredients and DIY ion-exchange membranes to separate ions effectively.
    *   Clarifies that electricity drives redox reactions rather than "splitting" water molecules in the context of metal deposition.
    *   See detailed [[concepts/scientific-experiment|experiment]]: [[lab-notes/2026-06-09-Electrolysis-and-DIY-Ion-Exchange-Metal-Refining-and-Ene|Electrolysis and DIY Ion-Exchange: Metal Refining and Energy Applications]]

### Chemical Refining
*   **[[concepts/solvent-extraction|Solvent Extraction]]**: Uses liquid-liquid extraction to separate metal ions based on solubility differences.
*   **Precipitation**: Selective precipitation of impurities or target metals using pH control or reagents.

## Ion-Exchange Technology
*   **Principle**: Utilizes resin beads to swap ions in solution, purifying electrolytes or recovering specific metals from dilute solutions.
*   **DIY Implementation**:
    *   Membranes can be fabricated for low-cost laboratory settings to enhance separation efficiency.
    *   Enables selective recovery of rare [[entities/earth|earth]] elements or precious metals from waste streams.
