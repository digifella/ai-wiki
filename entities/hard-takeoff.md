# Hard Takeoff

Hard takeoff is a theoretical scenario in artificial intelligence research describing a phase of extremely rapid AI development driven by recursive self-improvement. In this model, an AI system reaches a capability threshold where it becomes capable of meaningfully improving its own architecture and algorithms. Once self-directed enhancement begins, the system enters a positive feedback loop, with each iteration of improvement enabling faster subsequent improvements. This concept is often contrasted with a "soft takeoff" scenario, where AI capabilities develop more gradually over a longer period.

The core mechanism of a hard takeoff relies on the speed at which an artificial intelligence can rewrite its own code. If an AI can improve its own intelligence faster than humans can monitor or intervene, the transition from human-level to superintelligent capabilities could occur in a very short timeframe, potentially days, hours, or even minutes. This rapid acceleration raises significant concerns regarding alignment and control, as there may be insufficient time to correct errors or ensure the AI's goals remain consistent with human values.

Matthew Berman has discussed these concepts in the context of AI recursive self-improvement and the intelligence explosion. His analysis highlights the potential risks associated with such a rapid transition, emphasizing the importance of understanding the technical and safety implications of AI systems that can autonomously enhance their own cognitive abilities. The debate surrounding hard takeoff remains a central topic in AI safety research, influencing discussions on how to manage the development of advanced AI systems.
