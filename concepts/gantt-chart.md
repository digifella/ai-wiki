# Gantt Chart

A Gantt chart is a horizontal bar chart utilized in project management to visualize project schedules and task timelines. Each task or activity is represented as a horizontal bar positioned along a timeline, with the length of the bar corresponding to the task's duration. The horizontal axis represents time in increments such as days, weeks, or months, while the vertical axis lists individual tasks or work packages. This visual format enables project managers and team members to quickly assess project scope, task dependencies, and overall project duration.

The structure of a Gantt chart typically includes a timeline at the top, a list of tasks on the left, and the corresponding bars in the center. These bars often indicate start dates, end dates, and the critical path of the project. Dependencies between tasks are frequently shown with arrows or lines connecting the bars, illustrating which tasks must be completed before others can begin. This [[concepts/clarity-slider|clarity]] helps in identifying bottlenecks and managing resource allocation effectively.

Originally developed by Henry Gantt in the 1910s, the chart has become a standard tool in [[concepts/pmi-frameworks|project management methodologies]]. It allows for the tracking of progress against the planned schedule, facilitating [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] when deviations occur. Modern implementations often include interactive features, allowing users to update task statuses, adjust timelines, and view real-time project [[concepts/health|health]]. Despite the evolution of digital tools, the fundamental principles of the Gantt chart remain central to strategic planning and execution in business environments.
