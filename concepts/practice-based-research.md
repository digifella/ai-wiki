# Practice Based Research

Practice-based research is a methodological approach that integrates creative or artistic practice with formal investigation into a specific subject or problem. Rather than treating artistic work and research as separate activities, this method uses the act of making, creating, or practicing as the primary means of generating knowledge and understanding. The approach is particularly suited to exploring complex phenomena that may resist traditional empirical methods, by embedding inquiry directly within the creative process itself.

In the domain of AI agents, this framework is applied to investigate the visualization of cognitive differences, specifically dyslexia. By combining artistic practice with technical investigation, researchers aim to translate abstract neurological experiences into tangible digital representations. This method allows for the exploration of subjective data through generative systems, where the output of the AI agent serves as both an artistic artifact and a research instrument.

Kim Percy’s work exemplifies this intersection within the field. Her projects utilize practice-based research to examine how dyslexic perception can be modeled and visualized through algorithmic processes. By treating the development of AI-driven visualizations as a form of inquiry, the research highlights the potential for creative technologies to foster deeper understanding of neurodiversity. This approach demonstrates how artistic practice can drive technical innovation while simultaneously addressing social and educational challenges related to learning differences.
