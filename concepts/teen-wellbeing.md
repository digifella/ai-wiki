# Teen Wellbeing

Teen [[concepts/wellbeing|wellbeing]] encompasses the physical, mental, and [[concepts/mental-health|emotional health]] of adolescents, typically aged 13 to 19. During this developmental period, teenagers [[concepts/experience|experience]] significant biological, psychological, and social changes that directly affect their overall health and functioning. Wellbeing during these years establishes patterns and [[concepts/resilience|resilience]] that often extend into adulthood, making it a critical area of focus for families, educators, and [[concepts/health|healthcare]] providers.

## Physical Health

[[concepts/physical-health|Physical wellbeing]] in adolescence involves adequate [[concepts/nutrition|nutrition]], regular [[concepts/exercise|physical activity]], and sufficient [[concepts/sleep|sleep]]—all of which support both growth and mental health. Teenagers face particular challenges in maintaining healthy sleep patterns due to circadian rhythm shifts during puberty, [[entities/tomasz-janowski|academic]] pressures, and increased screen time. Chronic conditions, sports injuries, and reproductive health are also significant physical health considerations during the teen years.

## Mental and Emotional Health

Mental health and emotional wellbeing are increasingly recognized as central to overall teen health. Adolescents navigate identity formation, peer [[concepts/relationships|relationships]], academic [[concepts/stress|stress]], and family dynamics while experiencing neurological changes that affect mood [[concepts/regulation|regulation]] and [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]]. Common mental health challenges include [[concepts/anxiety|anxiety]], [[concepts/depression|depression]], and stress-related conditions. [[concepts/human-connection|Social connection]], [[concepts/meaning-in-life|sense of purpose]], and access to supportive relationships contribute significantly to emotional resilience during this period.

## Support and Prevention

Promoting teen wellbeing requires coordinated efforts across multiple contexts—homes, schools, healthcare settings, and communities. Early identification of mental health concerns, accessible [[concepts/offering-counseling-services|counseling]] services, and evidence-based interventions can prevent serious outcomes. Protective factors such as strong family relationships, school engagement, and healthy peer connections are essential components of comprehensive teen wellbeing approaches.
