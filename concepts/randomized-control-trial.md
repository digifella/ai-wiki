# Randomized Control Trial

A randomized controlled trial (RCT) is a research design in which participants are randomly assigned to either receive an intervention (treatment group) or standard care or placebo (control group). Random assignment is the defining feature of an RCT and helps ensure that any observed differences in outcomes between groups are likely due to the intervention itself rather than to pre-existing differences between participants. This approach reduces selection bias and creates groups that are comparable at baseline across known and unknown variables.

## Key methodological aspects

The validity of an RCT relies on strict adherence to protocol, including blinding where possible to prevent observer and participant bias. In the context of mental health and digital health interventions, RCTs are frequently used to evaluate the efficacy of therapeutic programs, mobile health applications, and behavioral nudges. By isolating the effect of the specific treatment, these trials provide high-quality evidence for clinical guidelines and policy decisions.

## Application in digital health and SES One

In digital health, RCTs help determine whether technology-mediated interventions offer superior outcomes compared to traditional care models. The SES One Intervention utilizes this methodology to assess the impact of its specific components on user well-being. By comparing participants who receive the full SES One package against those receiving standard care, researchers can quantify the incremental benefit of the digital platform and identify which elements drive positive health outcomes.
