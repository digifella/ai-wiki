# Small Language Models (SLMs)

Small Language Models are [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] engineered to operate within constrained [[concepts/memory|memory]] environments, specifically targeting systems with 4GB of RAM or less. Unlike larger language models that require substantial [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]], SLMs maintain practical general [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] capabilities while prioritizing efficiency and [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]]. This design approach enables [[concepts/deployment|deployment]] on edge devices, embedded systems, and resource-limited environments where conventional [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]] are impractical.

## Performance and Benchmarking

The effectiveness of SLMs is measured through standardized benchmarking frameworks that evaluate general problem-solving capabilities within the 4GB memory constraint. These assessments identify which compact models deliver the best balance between task performance and resource consumption, informing decisions about model selection for specific [[concepts/software|applications]].

## Integration with AI Agents

SLMs function as viable components within [[concepts/ai-agent|AI agent]] architectures, where their efficiency gains become particularly valuable. Research indicates that combining SLMs with code-based enhancements produces more efficient agent behavior than [[concepts/markdown|markdown]]-based approaches, particularly for tasks like [[concepts/web-crawling|web scraping]] where precise, executable [[concepts/instructions|instructions]] are required. This combination allows [[concepts/agents|agents]] to maintain sophisticated functionality while respecting [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] limitations.
