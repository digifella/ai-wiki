# Self Improvement

Self-improvement in AI systems refers to processes where an [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] iteratively enhances its own capabilities, performance, or behavior without external intervention. This occurs through recursive self-optimization, where a system analyzes its outputs, identifies inefficiencies or errors, and modifies its own [[concepts/parameters|parameters]], algorithms, or approaches to perform better on subsequent iterations. The concept represents a departure from traditional machine [[concepts/learning|learning]], where improvement typically requires human-directed retraining or external [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] loops.

## Applications in Scientific Discovery

Recent implementations of self-improving AI have shown particular promise in scientific research contexts. Systems like [[entities/deepmind|DeepMind]]'s [[concepts/automated-scientific-research|Aletheia]] exemplify this approach by combining self-correction mechanisms with domain-specific [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]]. These systems can autonomously refine their hypotheses, experimental designs, and analytical methods across multiple iterations, accelerating the pace of scientific discovery and reducing dependence on human guidance during the research process.

## Technical Mechanisms

Self-improvement relies on several technical foundations: the ability to evaluate performance against [[concepts/defined-metrics|defined metrics]], access to mechanisms for modifying behavior or parameters, and sufficient [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]] to implement changes. Self-correcting AI architectures incorporate feedback from validation steps directly into their optimization loops, allowing errors to inform immediate [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]]. The scope and speed of these improvements vary depending on system design, with some systems operating at timescales from microseconds to hours.
