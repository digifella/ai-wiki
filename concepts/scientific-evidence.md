# Scientific Evidence

**Scientific evidence** refers to data obtained through observation or experimentation that supports a [[concepts/theory|scientific theory]], hypothesis, or conclusion. It forms the backbone of the [[concepts/scientific-method]] and is critical for distinguishing between anecdotal claims and established [[concepts/factual-knowledge|facts]].

## Core Characteristics

- **Empirical Basis**: Derived from sensory [[concepts/experience|experience]] or instrument-mediated measurement rather than pure [[concepts/open-source-philosophy|logic]] or authority.
- **Reproducibility**: Results must be replicable by independent researchers under similar conditions to validate findings.
- **Falsifiability**: Claims must be structured such that they can potentially be proven false, allowing for refinement or rejection of theories.
- **[[concepts/statistical-significance|Statistical Significance]]**: Data analysis must account for variability and [[concepts/probability|chance]], often using p-values or confidence intervals to determine [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]].

## Hierarchy of Evidence

In fields like medicine and [[concepts/public-health]], evidence is ranked by methodological rigor:

1. **Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses**: Aggregate data from multiple [[concepts/excellence|high-quality]] studies.
2. **Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs)**: The [[concepts/major-gold-deposit|gold]] standard for determining causality, minimizing bias through randomization.
3. **Cohort Studies**: Observe outcomes in groups over time without intervention.
4. **Case-Control Studies**: [[concepts/feynmans-three-step-scientific-method|Compare]] individuals with a condition to those without, looking backward for exposures.
5. **Expert [[concepts/opinions|Opinion]] and Case Reports**: Lowest tier; useful for hypothesis generation but insufficient for establishing general truths.

## Application in Supplement Efficacy

Evaluating dietary supplements requires rigorous application of these evidentiary standards due to the heterogeneity of products and individual biological responses. Recent analyses highlight specific nuances:

- **[[concepts/omega-3-fatty-acids|Omega-3 Fatty Acids]]**: Evidence suggests benefits are most pronounced in individuals with high baseline triglycerides or those at risk for [[concepts/cardiovascular-events|cardiovascular events]], rather than the general population for [[concepts/primary-prevention|primary prevention]].
- **[[concepts/folic-acid|Folic Acid]]**: Strong evidence supports supplementation for pregnant women to prevent neural tube defects; however, recent studies question universal high-dose supplementation in non-pregnant populations due to [[concepts/diminishing-returns|diminishing returns]] and potential [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] of B12 deficiencies.
- **Vitamin D**: While deficiency is well-linked to bone [[concepts/health|health]] issues (e.g., osteoporosis), evidence for its role in preventing acute respiratory infections or cancer remains mixed and often overstated in popular media.
