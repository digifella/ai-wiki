# Milky Way

The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy with a diameter of approximately 100,000 light-years, housing an estimated 100 to 400 billion [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]]. Our solar system resides within the [[entities/orion|Orion]] Arm, one of the galaxy's major spiral structures, located roughly 26,000 light-years from the [[concepts/galactic-center|galactic center]]. The visible architecture of the galaxy comprises a central bar-shaped core, distinct spiral arms extending outward, and a flattened disk composed of stars and interstellar gas.

## Structure and Composition

The galaxy's mass is dominated by a massive halo of [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]], which extends far beyond the visible disk and influences the rotational dynamics of the outer regions. At the heart of the galaxy lies a supermassive black hole known as Sagittarius A*, which anchors the dense central bulge. The disk contains the majority of the galaxy's visible matter, including young stars, nebulae, and interstellar dust, arranged in a pattern of spiral arms that facilitate ongoing star formation.

## Observation and Classification

From Earth, the Milky Way appears as a diffuse band of light stretching across the night sky, resulting from the combined glow of unresolved stars and interstellar gas within the galactic plane. Astronomers classify it as a barred spiral galaxy (type SBb or SBc) based on its central bar structure and the openness of its spiral arms. Research into its kinematics and stellar populations continues to refine models of its formation, suggesting it evolved through the accretion of smaller satellite galaxies over billions of years.
