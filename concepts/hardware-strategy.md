# Hardware Strategy

Apple’s hardware strategy has shifted toward integrating [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] capabilities directly into devices rather than relying primarily on cloud-based processing. This transition reflects broader changes in computing economics, where [[concepts/local-execution|on-device processing]] offers advantages in latency, [[concepts/network-speed|bandwidth]] costs, and data handling. By embedding [[concepts/artificial-intelligence-models|machine learning models]] into products such as iPhones, iPads, and Macs, Apple reduces the computational burden on remote servers and the data transmission requirements between devices and [[concepts/cloud-based-computer|cloud infrastructure]].

## Technical Implementation

The company utilizes [[concepts/custom-ai-hardware|custom silicon]], specifically the [[concepts/neural-engine|Neural Engine]] within its A-series and M-series chips, to accelerate [[concepts/on-device-machine-learning|on-device machine learning]] tasks. These dedicated [[concepts/hardware|hardware components]] allow for efficient execution of complex [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]], such as [[concepts/image-recognition|image recognition]] and [[concepts/language-processing|natural language processing]], without requiring constant connectivity to [[concepts/external-data|external data]] centers. This architecture enables features like real-time [[concepts/photo-editing|photo editing]], voice assistance, and [[concepts/auto-complete|predictive text]] to function with minimal delay and reduced power consumption.

## Economic and Privacy Implications

Moving [[concepts/computation|computation]] to the edge lowers [[concepts/operational-costs|operational costs]] associated with maintaining massive cloud [[concepts/techno-economics|data centers]] and reduces bandwidth expenses for both the provider and the user. From a privacy perspective, keeping sensitive data local minimizes the risk of [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] during transmission and storage in third-party facilities. This approach aligns with Apple’s broader emphasis on user privacy, as personal information remains on the device rather than being uploaded to remote servers for analysis.
