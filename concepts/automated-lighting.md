# Automated Lighting

**Automated lighting** refers to systems that control illumination without direct manual intervention, utilizing sensors, timers, or remote control [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]]. While often associated with modern IoT ecosystems, the conceptual foundation for automated domestic lighting was established in the late 19th century.

## Historical Precedents

The earliest documented instance of automated lighting in a private residence occurred at Cragside, the Northumberland estate of industrialist and inventor [[concepts/william-george-armstrong]].

*   **First Smart Home:** Cragside is widely recognized as the [[entities/earth|world]]'s first "smart home," featuring integrated automation systems for heating, hot water, and lighting.
*   **Hydroelectric [[concepts/innovation|Innovation]]:** Armstrong utilized [[concepts/hydroelectric-power|hydroelectric power]] generated on-site to run electrical devices, including his lighting system.
*   **Automated Mechanisms:** The house featured automatic lighting controls that responded to ambient [[concepts/light|light]] levels and occupancy, predating modern smart home technology by over a century.
*   **Key Figure:** William Armstrong, often called "the magician of the North," pioneered these innovations to demonstrate the practical application of electrical [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] in domestic settings.

## Key Concepts

*   **Sensor-Based Control:** Early systems relied on mechanical or electromechanical sensors to detect light levels or presence.
*   **Centralized Power:** Unlike modern decentralized smart [[concepts/grids|grids]], early automated homes like Cragside relied on localized power generation (e.g., hydroelectric turbines).
*   **Integration:** The concept of integrating lighting with other home systems (heating, [[concepts/security|security]]) was pioneered at Cragside.
