# Demography

Demography is the scientific study of human populations, examining their size, [[concepts/writing|composition]], and the processes that change them over time. Demographers analyze quantitative data on births, deaths, migration, and other factors that affect population structure. The field provides statistical frameworks for understanding how societies evolve and for projecting future [[concepts/earths-population|population trends]].

## Core Measurements

The discipline rests on several key measurements. Birth rates and fertility patterns describe reproductive behavior across populations, while mortality rates and [[concepts/life-expectancy|life expectancy]] indicate health outcomes and survival probabilities. Migration statistics track the [[concepts/exercise|movement]] of people across geographic boundaries, influencing both local and global population distributions. Age and sex distribution further define the structural makeup of a population, which is critical for planning social services and economic [[concepts/policies|policies]].

## Applications and Significance

Data derived from demographic analysis is essential for [[concepts/population-health|public health]], [[concepts/city-planning|urban planning]], and economic forecasting. By understanding population dynamics, governments and organizations can anticipate needs for [[concepts/health|healthcare]], education, and housing. These insights also inform policy decisions regarding pension systems, labor markets, and environmental sustainability, ensuring that resources are allocated efficiently in response to current and future demographic shifts.
