# Seasoning Techniques

Seasoning is the practice of enhancing food flavor through the addition of salt, spices, herbs, and other flavor compounds. Effective seasoning requires understanding how different [[concepts/salt-types|salt types]] and their crystalline structures affect flavor [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] and intensity. Table salt, kosher salt, and sea salt have different densities and grain sizes, which means they don't measure equally by volume and dissolve [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] different rates during cooking.

## Salt Application Methods

The timing and method of salt application significantly impact final flavor. Salt added early in cooking dissolves gradually and distributes evenly throughout a dish, while salt added near the end of cooking can create concentrated flavor pockets. Different salt types suit different [[concepts/software|applications]]: finer salts work well for even distribution in liquids and doughs, while larger crystals are better suited for finishing dishes where [[concepts/texture|texture]] and visible seasoning are desired.

## Avoiding Common Errors

Understanding [[concepts/salt-density|salt density]] prevents over- or under-seasoning. A pinch of table salt is not equivalent to a pinch of kosher salt due to differences in crystal size and packing density. Proper seasoning also involves tasting throughout the cooking process, as some flavors intensify with heat while others diminish. Balancing salt with other flavor elements—acidity, fat, and sweetness—ensures well-rounded dishes rather than overly salty ones.
