Foundational Color is a [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|color grading]] technique employed in [[entities/adobe-lightroom|Adobe Lightroom]] to establish a consistent baseline color profile for an image prior to applying specific adjustments. This method prioritizes setting core color parameters—such as white balance, overall color cast, and tonal color [[concepts/relationships|relationships]]—at the beginning of the [[concepts/creative-workflow|editing workflow]]. By addressing these fundamental elements first, editors create a stable reference point that informs all subsequent color corrections.

The primary purpose of this approach is to ensure visual [[concepts/coherence|coherence]] across a series of images or within a single complex file. Rather than making isolated corrections that may [[concepts/conflict|conflict]] with one another, the foundational color technique prevents color shifts and inconsistencies that often arise from piecemeal adjustments. This systematic process helps maintain the [[concepts/honesty|integrity]] of the original scene's lighting while allowing for precise, cumulative enhancements.

Implementing Foundational Color involves a deliberate sequence where global adjustments take precedence over local ones. Editors typically begin by neutralizing unwanted [[concepts/color-casts|color casts]] and balancing the white balance to reflect the intended mood or reality of the scene. Once this base is secured, further refinements such as saturation, vibrance, and targeted hue adjustments are applied with greater accuracy, resulting in a more harmonious and professional final image.
