# Dry Vermouth

Dry vermouth is a type of aromatized, fortified wine characterized by its crisp, bitter-herbal profile and minimal residual sugar content, typically less than 4 grams per liter. It is produced by adding distilled spirits to a neutral white wine base, which raises the alcohol content to approximately 18–20% by volume. This fortification process stabilizes the wine and serves as a solvent for the subsequent infusion of botanicals.

## Production and Ingredients

The defining flavor profile is achieved through the infusion of a complex blend of botanicals, herbs, and spices. Key ingredients often include wormwood, which gives vermouth its name, along with quinine, gentian, and various citrus peels. The wine is macerated with these botanicals before being filtered and bottled, resulting in a clear, pale yellow liquid that distinguishes it from its sweeter counterpart, sweet vermouth.

## Culinary Use

Dry vermouth is primarily used as a mixer in cocktails, most notably the martini and the manhattan. Its dryness and herbal notes provide a balancing acidity and complexity to spirits like gin and vodka. It is also used in cooking to deglaze pans or add depth to sauces, where its alcohol content helps extract flavors from other ingredients while leaving a subtle aromatic finish.
