# Vintage Computing

[[concepts/retro-computing|Vintage computing]] is the practice of preserving, studying, and actively using older computer hardware and software systems that have been superseded by modern technology. The field generally encompasses machines from the 1970s through the 1990s, though definitions vary among practitioners. Some enthusiasts focus on early electromechanical systems and mainframes, while others extend their interest into the early 2000s. The primary [[concepts/motivation|motivation]] for this activity is typically [[concepts/historical-significance|historical significance]], technical curiosity, or cultural interest rather than practical utility in contemporary workflows.

## Preservation and Restoration

A core component of [[concepts/vintage-computing]] is the [[concepts/preservation|preservation]] of [[concepts/hardware|physical hardware]] and associated documentation. Collectors and historians work to maintain functional examples of early personal computers, minicomputers, and mainframes. This process often involves complex restoration efforts, including the repair of failing capacitors, replacement of obsolete [[entities/storage|storage]] media, and the adaptation of legacy systems to modern interfaces.
