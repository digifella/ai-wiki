# Scientific Method

The scientific method is a systematic process for investigating natural phenomena and developing reliable knowledge about the world. It consists of several interconnected steps: observation of natural phenomena or existing data, formulation of a testable hypothesis to explain observations, design and execution of controlled experiments to test the hypothesis, analysis of results, and validation or refinement based on evidence. This iterative cycle allows scientists to build understanding incrementally, with each round of investigation either confirming existing ideas or [[concepts/prompting|prompting]] revision.

## Core Principles

Central to the scientific method is the requirement that hypotheses must be testable and falsifiable—that is, capable of being proven wrong through [[concepts/empirical-evidence|empirical evidence]]. Observations must be made carefully and objectively, and experiments should be designed to control variables so that causal [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] can be identified. Reproducibility is essential: other researchers should be able to repeat experiments and obtain consistent results, which strengthens confidence in the findings.

## Application and Limits

The scientific method has proven effective across disciplines from [[concepts/physics|physics]] to biology to psychology, providing a framework for distinguishing reliable knowledge from speculation. However, the method works best for questions about observable, measurable phenomena and causal mechanisms. Some complex systems—including aspects of [[concepts/human-cognition|human cognition]], ecological dynamics, and social behavior—present challenges for controlled experimentation, requiring researchers to adapt the method through careful observational studies, comparative analysis, or computational modeling.
