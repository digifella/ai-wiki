# Vision Language Models

Vision Language [[concepts/models|Models]] (VLMs) are AI systems designed to process and interpret both visual and textual information simultaneously. Unlike traditional [[concepts/computer-vision|computer vision]] models that analyze [[concepts/images|images]] alone or language models that process [[concepts/text|text]] alone, VLMs integrate [[concepts/data-modality|multimodal data]] to perform tasks that require understanding [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] between images and [[concepts/natural-language-descriptions|natural language descriptions]]. This [[concepts/integration|integration]] enables [[concepts/software|applications]] ranging from image captioning and visual [[concepts/fact-based-queries|question answering]] to more [[concepts/complex-reasoning|complex reasoning]] tasks involving visual content.

## Limitations and Current Research

Despite their [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]], VLMs have demonstrated consistent limitations in tasks requiring precise quantitative analysis of visual information. A notable weakness is [[concepts/object-counting|object counting]]—VLMs frequently miscount or fail to accurately enumerate objects in images, particularly when images contain multiple instances of similar items or complex spatial arrangements. Recent research has focused on addressing these limitations through agentic approaches, where VLMs are enhanced with systematic [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] processes and [[concepts/iterative-refinement|iterative refinement]] mechanisms to improve [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]] in counting and [[concepts/spatial-understanding|spatial understanding]] tasks.

## Agentic Enhancements

[[concepts/agentic-visual-reasoning-pipeline|Agentic visual reasoning]] represents an emerging approach to strengthen VLM performance by incorporating agent-like [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] processes. Rather than relying solely on the model's immediate response to an image, agentic methods enable VLMs to break down complex visual tasks into smaller steps, reason through them systematically, and refine their answers iteratively. This approach has shown promise in improving both object counting precision and spatial [[concepts/reasoning-capabilities|reasoning capabilities]], suggesting a pathway toward more reliable VLM performance on quantitative and relational visual tasks.
