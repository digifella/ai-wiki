# World Building

World building is the discipline of constructing fictional or hypothetical universes characterized by internally consistent rules, geography, history, and social systems. It serves as the foundational framework for narratives across diverse media, including literature, video games, film, and interactive experiences. By establishing a coherent context, effective world building allows characters to act and plots to unfold within a believable environment, thereby enhancing audience immersion and engagement.

The process typically addresses several interconnected dimensions, beginning with physical geography and cosmology. This includes defining the landscape, climate, flora, fauna, and the fundamental laws of physics or magic that govern the setting. These physical constraints often dictate the available resources, transportation methods, and architectural styles, which in turn influence the daily lives of the inhabitants.

Social and historical structures form the next layer of construction. This involves creating timelines of past events, political systems, economic models, and cultural norms such as religion, language, and art. These elements provide the backdrop for conflict and cooperation, ensuring that societal interactions within the narrative feel logical and grounded in the established reality of the world.

Finally, world building extends to the integration of these elements into the narrative structure itself. In interactive media, this often requires designing systems that allow users to explore and influence the environment, while in traditional storytelling, it ensures that plot developments arise naturally from the established rules. The goal is to create a sense of depth and continuity that exists independently of the immediate plot, giving the audience the impression of a living, breathing universe.
