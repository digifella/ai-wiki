# Macro Photography

Macro photography is a technique for capturing highly magnified [[concepts/images|images]] of small subjects, revealing fine details invisible to the naked eye. The magnification ratio typically begins [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] 1:1, where the subject is reproduced at life-size on the camera sensor or film, and can extend to 10:1 or greater for extreme close-ups. This approach requires specialized equipment and careful technique to achieve sharp, usable images at such high magnifications.

## Equipment and Methods

Macro photography can be achieved through dedicated macro lenses, extension tubes, or reverse-mounted lenses attached to standard cameras. The depth of field becomes extremely shallow at high magnifications, often requiring precise focus and sometimes [[concepts/blur-correction|focus stacking]]—combining multiple images focused at different distances—to achieve sharp detail across the entire subject. Lighting becomes critical, as close proximity to subjects can block ambient [[concepts/light|light]], necessitating supplementary illumination such as ring lights or diffused flashes.

## Film Scanning

For photographers working with film, macro photography extends to the scanning process itself. High-resolution [[concepts/digital-scanning|film scanning at home]] can employ macro techniques to capture fine grain detail and color information from negatives or [[entities/google-slides|slides]]. Dedicated film scanners use optical magnification to preserve detail, while some photographers use macro lenses with specialized copying setups to photograph film directly, allowing greater [[concepts/power|control]] over the final digital image quality.
