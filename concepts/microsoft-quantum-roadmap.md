# Microsoft Quantum Roadmap

[[entities/microsoft|Microsoft]]'s strategy for achieving scalable [[concepts/quantum-computing|quantum computing]] relies on the development of **topological [[concepts/qubits|qubits]]**, leveraging the theoretical stability of Majorana [[concepts/concept-of-nothingness|zero]] modes to reduce error rates without excessive overhead. The roadmap is characterized by a cautious, [[concepts/hardware-centric-strategy|hardware-first approach]], prioritizing the validation of topological [[concepts/secure|protection]] before [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]] qubit counts.

## Core Strategy: Topological Protection
Unlike superconducting or trapped-ion approaches that require massive [[concepts/bug-fixing|error correction]] codes, Microsoft aims to build intrinsic [[concepts/robustness|fault tolerance]] into the physical qubit. This involves:
- Utilizing semiconductor-superconductor hybrid nanowires.
- Isolating **Majorana [[concepts/zero|zero]] modes** at the ends of these wires.
- Achieving "topological protection" where local noise does not affect the global quantum state.

## Recent Developments & Hardware Milestones

### Majorana 2 Chip (2026)
In July 2026, Microsoft unveiled the **[[entities/majorana-2|Majorana 2]]** quantum chip, marking a significant step toward their goal of a scalable topological quantum computer by 2029.
- **Claims:** Microsoft asserts dramatic advances in coherence and control, suggesting the path to scalability is now clear.
- **Skepticism:** The quantum community remains divided. Critics argue that definitive [[concepts/proof|proof]] of topological protection is still lacking, with some suggesting observed effects may stem from non-topological [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]].

### Historical Context
- **2018:** Initial claims of Majorana zero mode observation were retracted due to data analysis errors, highlighting the difficulty of the experimental regime.
- **2022-2024:** [[concepts/incremental-progress|Incremental progress]] in creating "topological qubits" with improved coherence times, though still short of fault-tolerant thresholds.

## Key Challenges
1. **Material [[concepts/science|Science]]:** Achieving the precise interface between superconductors and semiconductors required for Majorana modes.
2. **[[concepts/verification|Verification]]:** Distinguishing true topological states from trivial mimics remains a primary scientific hurdle.
3. **[[concepts/scaling|Scaling]]:** Moving from single-qubit demonstrations to multi-qubit systems while maintaining topological protection.
