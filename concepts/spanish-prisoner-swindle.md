# Spanish Prisoner Swindle

The **Spanish Prisoner Swindle** is a historical Advance Fee [[concepts/fraud|Fraud]] scheme that serves as the direct ancestor to modern iterations such as the [[concepts/nigerian-prince-scam|Nigerian Prince Scam]]. Originating in the late 18th century, the fraud relies on social [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] to extract money from victims under the pretense of securing the [[concepts/deployment|release]] of a wealthy prisoner or accessing hidden funds.

## Core Mechanism
The scam typically involves a letter or message claiming that a wealthy individual (historically a Spanish noble or prisoner) is detained and requires funds for bribes, legal fees, or travel expenses to [[concepts/secure|secure]] their release. In exchange, the victim is promised a significant portion of the prisoner's fortune. The "advance fee" is the critical component; once paid, the scammer disappears or invents further obstacles requiring additional payments.

## Historical Evolution
*   **18th Century Origins:** The scheme emerged in [[entities/europe|Europe]], often involving letters from supposed prisoners in Spanish jails.
*   **Modern Adaptation:** The [[concepts/storytelling|narrative]] shifted geographically and culturally over time, evolving into the 419 Scam (named after the section of the Nigerian Criminal Code dealing with fraud).
*   **Digital Era:** With the advent of [[entities/email|email]] and social media, the scam became global, utilizing mass mailing lists and automated scripts.
