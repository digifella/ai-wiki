# Shame Resiliency

Shame resiliency is the capacity to recognize, process, and recover from experiences of shame without becoming overwhelmed or isolated by them. Rather than avoiding shame or attempting to suppress it entirely, this concept involves developing the skills to move through shame experiences constructively. It is closely tied to emotional regulation and the ability to maintain connection with others during moments of vulnerability.

The development of shame resiliency typically involves four interconnected abilities. These include the initial recognition of shame when it arises, understanding its triggers and underlying narratives, and employing strategies to regulate the associated physiological and emotional responses. Finally, it requires the capacity to re-engage with oneself and others in a way that restores a sense of worth and belonging.

This capacity is a key component of broader vulnerability work, allowing individuals to navigate social and personal challenges with greater psychological flexibility. By building shame resiliency, individuals can reduce the paralyzing effects of shame and foster healthier interpersonal relationships and self-compassion.
