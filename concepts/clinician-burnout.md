# Clinician Burnout

## Definition
A state of physical, emotional, and mental exhaustion caused by long-term [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] to chronic workplace stressors, particularly in [[concepts/health-care|healthcare]] settings. Characterized by emotional exhaustion, depersonalization, and reduced personal [[concepts/success|accomplishment]].

## Key Drivers
- **[[concepts/administrative-overload|Administrative Overload]]**: Excessive time spent on documentation, billing, and [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] rather than [[concepts/roles-in-community-settings|patient care]].
- **[[concepts/electronic-health-records|Electronic Health Records]] (EHR)**: Usability issues and data entry requirements contributing to "pajama time" (post-shift charting).
- **Staffing Shortages**: Increased patient-to-provider ratios.
- **Moral Distress**: Inability to provide desired care due to systemic constraints.

## Impact
- **[[concepts/financial-strain|Financial Strain]]**: High turnover costs, recruitment expenses, and decreased [[concepts/productivity|productivity]].
- **Patient Safety**: Increased medical errors and decreased [[concepts/quality-of-care|quality of care]].
- **Provider [[concepts/well-being|Well-being]]**: [[concepts/mental-health|Mental health]] issues, substance abuse, and suicide rates among physicians.

## Mitigation Strategies
- **[[concepts/editing-workflow|Workflow Optimization]]**: Reducing clicks and automating documentation.
- **[[concepts/interprofessional-collaboration|Team-Based Care]]**: Distributing tasks among nurses, pharmacists, and social [[entities/employees|workers]].
- **Technological Intervention**: Leveraging AI to offload administrative tasks.
