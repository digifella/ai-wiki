# Character Archetypes

**Character Archetypes** are universal, recurring symbols or motifs that represent fundamental human patterns, behaviors, and personalities. They serve as foundational [[concepts/templates|templates]] in [[concepts/storytelling|storytelling]], allowing audiences to quickly recognize and relate to characters through shared cultural or psychological understanding.

## Core Functions
- **Narrative Efficiency**: Archetypes reduce [[concepts/cognitive-load|cognitive load]] by providing [[concepts/short-term-memory|immediate context]] for character motivations and roles.
- **Psychological Resonance**: They tap into collective unconscious themes (Jungian [[concepts/theory|theory]]), creating deeper [[concepts/emotional-engagement|emotional engagement]].
- **Structural Stability**: They provide a predictable framework within which subversion or variation can create meaningful tension.

## Common Archetypal Categories
- **The Hero**: Embodies [[concepts/courage|courage]], [[concepts/resilience|resilience]], and the journey of growth.
- **The Shadow**: Represents repressed aspects of the psyche or antagonistic forces.
- **The Everyman**: A relatable, ordinary figure who grounds the narrative in reality.
- **The Trickster**: Disrupts order, challenges authority, and introduces chaos or change.
