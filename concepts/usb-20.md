# Usb 20

USB 2.0 is a standardized interface for data and power transfer between computers and peripheral devices. Released in 2000, it succeeded USB 1.1 and offered substantially improved performance, supporting data transfer rates up to 480 Mbps. This represented a significant advancement that enabled practical use of higher-bandwidth devices like external hard drives and digital cameras.

## Specifications and Compatibility

The standard defines three speed modes: Low Speed (1.5 Mbps), Full Speed (12 Mbps), and High Speed (480 Mbps). While the physical connector shape remained largely consistent with previous versions, the electrical specifications and signaling methods were updated to support the higher throughput. The standard maintains backward compatibility with USB 1.1 devices, allowing older peripherals to function on newer ports, albeit at reduced speeds.

## Connector Types and Modern Context

Although USB 2.0 originally utilized Type-A and Type-B connectors, the standard has been adapted to various form factors over time. Modern implementations often appear in USB-C cables, which support USB 2.0 signaling alongside higher-speed protocols. Compatibility issues can arise when mixing legacy USB 2.0 peripherals with newer host controllers or when using adapters that do not properly manage power delivery and data line switching. Despite the prevalence of USB 3.x and USB4, USB 2.0 remains widely used for low-power devices and legacy equipment due to its cost-effectiveness and universal support.
