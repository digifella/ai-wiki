## Sciatica Relief: Spinal Extension Exercise for Nerve De-Irritation
**Clip title:** The boring [[concepts/exercise|exercise]] that fixes sciatica
**[[entities/tasia-custode|Author]] / channel:** Dr. Josh, PT
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CDtoxxIq6YA

### Summary
The video provides [[concepts/recommendations|guidance]] on effectively alleviating [[concepts/sciatica|sciatica]] pain, challenging the common instinct to stretch the affected areas. The presenter explains that while many people attempt to stretch tightness and pain in their buttocks and legs, this approach often backfires. Sciatica is characterized by inflammation and irritation of the [[concepts/sciatic-nerve|sciatic nerve]], and nerves do not typically respond well to being stretched; doing so can aggravate an already sensitive nerve, worsening symptoms. Instead, the video advocates for a method focused on calming the irritated sciatic nerve.

The root cause of sciatica, as explained in the video, often stems from prolonged periods spent in a forward-flexed position, common in daily activities like sitting at a desk, lounging on a couch, or even standing with poor posture. This sustained forward positioning can lead to discomfort in the back and irritation of the sciatic nerve. If not addressed, this irritation can escalate, causing pain to spread from the hip further down the leg, intensifying the symptoms.

To counter this, the video introduces a straightforward [[concepts/exercise|exercise]] involving spinal extension. The initial exercise involves simply lying on your stomach and propping yourself up on your elbows, similar to a sphinx pose. This position helps to gently reverse the forward posture that typically aggravates the sciatic nerve. It's recommended to hold this position for two to three minutes, two to three times daily, focusing on relaxing the muscles in the lower back, potentially aided by deep breathing.

As a progression for those who find the initial move comfortable, the presenter suggests lying flat and then pushing up onto the hands, lifting the chest while keeping the hips and pelvis on the ground. This provides a greater degree of spinal extension. This advanced version can be repeated 10 to 15 times, two to three times a day. The key takeaway is that consistent engagement in these spinal extension exercises can effectively calm the sciatic nerve over several weeks, leading to a noticeable reduction in sciatica pain by addressing the underlying postural issues rather than merely stretching the symptoms.
