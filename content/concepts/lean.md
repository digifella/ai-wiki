# Lean

Lean is a project management and business methodology focused on maximizing value while minimizing waste. Originating from [[concepts/low-cost-manufacturing|lean manufacturing]] principles developed in the automotive industry, particularly [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] Toyota, Lean has been adapted across various sectors including [[concepts/coding|software development]], [[concepts/health|healthcare]], and general project management. The methodology emphasizes continuous improvement, efficient resource allocation, and the elimination of non-value-adding activities.

## Core Principles

At its foundation, Lean operates on several key principles: delivering value to the customer, eliminating waste, empowering teams to [[entities/make|make]] decisions, and implementing continuous incremental improvements. The methodology identifies waste as any activity that consumes resources without creating customer value, such as unnecessary processes, delays, or redundant work. Teams using Lean aim to streamline workflows and reduce cycle times through systematic analysis and refinement.

## Application in Project Management

In project management contexts, Lean emphasizes delivering projects efficiently by removing obstacles and reducing inefficiencies. Teams working with Lean methodologies typically use visual management tools, regular [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] [[concepts/loops|loops]], and iterative development to respond quickly to changing requirements. This approach often results in faster project delivery and lower costs compared to more rigid methodologies, making it particularly valuable in fast-moving industries where adaptability is essential.
