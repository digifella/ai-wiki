# Strategic Pivot

A strategic pivot is a fundamental change in a company's business strategy, direction, or focus. Unlike incremental [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] to existing operations, a pivot represents a significant shift in how a company creates value, targets customers, or competes in its market. This may involve entering new markets, abandoning existing product lines, adopting new business [[concepts/models|models]], or reallocating resources to different areas of the [[concepts/organization|organization]].

## Common Drivers

Strategic pivots typically occur in response to changing market conditions, technological developments, competitive pressures, or shifts in customer demand. Companies may pivot when their original strategy proves unsustainable, when emerging opportunities align better with their [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]], or when internal analysis reveals more viable paths to growth. Early-stage companies particularly rely on pivots as they validate assumptions about their business model and market fit.

## Implementation Challenges

Executing a strategic pivot requires significant organizational change, including potential restructuring, reallocation of capital, talent transitions, and cultural shifts. The process carries inherent risks, as it diverts resources from existing operations and introduces uncertainty about outcomes. Success depends on clear communication of the strategic rationale, alignment of [[concepts/leadership|leadership]] and stakeholders, and sufficient runway to test and refine the new direction before existing [[concepts/revenue-streams|revenue streams]] deplete.
