## Lightroom Reflection Removal Limitations and Photoshop Generative Fill Workaround
**Clip title:** Reflection Removal NOT WORKING in Lightroom? Try this
**Author / channel:** Glyn Dewis
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qv_E9IyzCpU

### Summary
This video addresses the challenge of removing unwanted reflections from glass in photographs, specifically when [[concepts/adobe-camera-raw|Adobe Camera Raw]] and Lightroom's built-in "Distraction Removal > Reflections" feature proves ineffective. [[entities/glyn-dewis|Glyn Dewis]] explains that this native tool, which is not based on generative AI and does not consume credits, is primarily designed to remove reflections from *dominant windows* that constitute a significant portion of the image. Consequently, it fails to produce results when applied to smaller, less dominant reflective surfaces, such as the glass panels of a bookcase or a shop window.

To overcome this limitation for smaller reflective areas, Dewis demonstrates a workaround using [[entities/adobe-photoshop|Adobe Photoshop]]'s Generative Fill. The process involves opening the RAW image in [[entities/adobe-photoshop|Photoshop]], using a selection tool (like the Polygonal Lasso) to accurately outline the reflective glass area, and then employing Generative Fill. A key step is to input a specific prompt, such as "remove the reflection from off the glass, do not change the original contents," and to select the "Firefly Image 5" model, which is optimized for preserving original image detail.

Through two practical examples—a glass-fronted bookcase and a bakery window—Dewis showcases the impressive capabilities of Generative Fill. In both instances, the reflections are dramatically reduced or eliminated, allowing the underlying contents of the bookcase (books) and the bakery interior (pastries and a person) to become clearly visible. While zooming in on the bookcase revealed a slight softening of detail, the overall result was remarkably convincing. The bakery window example yielded even more striking results, appearing as if a polarizing filter had been used during capture.

In conclusion, while the native reflection removal tools in Camera Raw and Lightroom have their specific use cases for large windows, Adobe Photoshop's Generative Fill offers a powerful and precise solution for removing reflections from smaller, more intricate glass surfaces. The judicious use of a detail-preserving generative model, like Firefly Image 5, ensures that the original content behind the glass remains largely intact. Dewis anticipates that Adobe will eventually integrate more advanced, generative AI capabilities into Camera Raw and Lightroom to address these smaller reflective areas more seamlessly in the future.
