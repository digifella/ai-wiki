# Battery Storage

## Overview
Battery [[entities/storage|Storage]] refers to technologies and systems used to store electrical energy for later use. In modern energy [[concepts/grids|grids]], particularly those with high penetration of renewable sources, battery storage plays a critical role in stabilizing supply, managing peak demand, and mitigating the effects of intermittent generation.

## Key Concepts
- **Grid Stabilization**: Batteries provide fast-responding frequency control and voltage support.
- **Peak Shaving**: Discharging during high-demand periods to reduce reliance on expensive peaker plants.
- **Arbitrage**: Charging when [[concepts/electricity-prices|electricity prices]] are low and discharging when prices are high.
- **Renewable Integration**: Smoothing out the variability of solar and wind power.

## Case Study: Australia's Energy Market Transformation
Recent developments in Australia highlight the transformative impact of large-scale battery storage on electricity [[concepts/pricing|pricing]] and grid dynamics.

- **Mitigation of the [[concepts/duck-curve|Duck Curve]]**: The rapid [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of rooftop solar created a "duck curve" phenomenon, where mid-day prices drop to [[concepts/concept-of-nothingness|zero]] or negative values, followed by a steep evening price spike. Battery storage is actively flattening this curve.
- **Price Reduction**: Households and small businesses are experiencing lower electricity prices due to the increased supply of stored energy during peak hours.
- **Mechanism**: Mechanical engineers and energy analysts note that batteries are absorbing excess mid-day solar generation and releasing it during the critical evening ramp-up period, thereby reducing the need for fossil-fuel-based peaker plants.
