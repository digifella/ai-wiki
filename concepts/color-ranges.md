# Color Ranges

Color Ranges in [[entities/lightroom-classic|Lightroom Classic]] refers to the targeted adjustment of specific color ranges within an image using HSL (Hue, Saturation, Lightness) controls. Rather than applying [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] uniformly across an entire image, this approach isolates individual color ranges—such as reds, oranges, yellows, greens, cyans, blues, and magentas—for independent modification. This selective control enables precise [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|color grading]] without affecting other colors in the photograph.

## How It Works

The [[concepts/color-mixer|Color Mixer]] panel in Lightroom Classic provides eight color [[concepts/range-sliders|range sliders]] corresponding to the primary and secondary hues found in most photographs. Each slider allows the user to adjust the hue, saturation, and lightness of that specific color band. By narrowing the range of influence, editors can correct [[concepts/color-casts|color casts]], enhance [[concepts/skin-tones|skin tones]], or alter the mood of a sky without introducing unwanted artifacts in adjacent color areas.

## Practical Application

This feature is particularly useful for correcting white balance errors in specific elements, such as neutralizing a green tint in foliage while preserving the warmth of skin tones. It also facilitates creative color grading by allowing distinct manipulation of complementary colors, such as shifting blues toward teal or reds toward orange, to achieve a cohesive [[concepts/style|visual style]]. The [[concepts/accuracy|precision]] offered by Color Ranges distinguishes it from global adjustments, providing [[concepts/granular-control|granular control]] over the final image [[concepts/presence|appearance]].
