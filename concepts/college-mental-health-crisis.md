# College Mental Health Crisis

[[entities/racp|The college]] [[concepts/mental-health|mental health]] [[concepts/crisis|crisis]] refers to a widespread and escalating mental [[concepts/health|health]] challenge among undergraduate and graduate students in higher education institutions. Over the past two decades, college [[concepts/offering-counseling-services|counseling]] centers have reported significant increases in student visits for mental health concerns, including [[concepts/depression|depression]], [[concepts/anxiety|anxiety]], and suicidal ideation. The prevalence of these issues has prompted colleges and universities to expand their mental [[concepts/health-care|health services]] and prompted researchers to investigate underlying [[concepts/causes|causes]] and potential interventions.

## Contributing Factors

Multiple factors contribute to mental health challenges among college students, including [[entities/tomasz-janowski|academic]] pressure, social adjustment difficulties, financial [[concepts/stress|stress]], and the developmental transition to early adulthood. The [[concepts/covid-19|COVID-19]] pandemic exacerbated these issues through campus disruptions, [[concepts/disconnection|isolation]], and uncertainty about educational [[concepts/continuity|continuity]]. Additionally, social media use and environmental stressors have been identified as potential contributors to increased rates of anxiety and depression in this population.

## Research and Response

Researchers like [[entities/dr-laurie-santos|Laurie Santos]] have brought [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to college mental health through studies examining stress, [[concepts/well-being|well-being]], and evidence-based interventions. Santos's work, including her popular "The [[concepts/science-of-well-being|Science of Well-Being]]" course at [[entities/yale-university|Yale University]], has focused on teaching students evidence-based practices for improving mental health and [[concepts/resilience|resilience]]. Her research and educational approach exemplify broader institutional efforts to address the crisis through both direct counseling services and preventive [[concepts/wellness|wellness]] [[concepts/software|programs]] designed to build student [[concepts/stress-management|coping]] [[concepts/skills|skills]].

Higher education institutions have responded by increasing counseling [[entities/employees|staff]], implementing peer support programs, and integrating mental health literacy into campus curricula. Despite these efforts, gaps remain between student demand for mental health services and available resources at many institutions.
