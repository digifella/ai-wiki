# Geophysical Surveys

Geophysical surveys are non-invasive techniques used to investigate subsurface geological properties and structures by measuring variations in the [[concepts/material-properties|physical properties]] of rocks and soil. These methods rely on detecting contrasts in [[concepts/density|density]], magnetism, electrical conductivity, seismic [[concepts/velocity|velocity]], and natural radioactivity across a study area. By analyzing how these physical parameters change with depth and lateral position, geophysicists can construct detailed images and maps of subsurface features without the need for excavation or drilling.

The primary methods employed in these surveys include seismic, magnetic, [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]], electrical, and radiometric techniques. Seismic surveys utilize sound waves or controlled explosions to probe the [[entities/earth|Earth]]'s interior, measuring the travel time and reflection of waves to determine layer depths and compositions. Magnetic surveys detect variations in the [[concepts/earths-magnetic-field|Earth's magnetic field]] caused by magnetic minerals in the subsurface, while gravity surveys measure [[concepts/granular-control|minute changes]] in gravitational acceleration to identify density contrasts associated with different rock types or structural features.

Electrical and electromagnetic methods assess the subsurface by injecting current or inducing electromagnetic fields to measure conductivity variations, which is particularly useful for locating groundwater or mineral deposits. Radiometric surveys measure the natural [[entities/gamma|gamma]] radiation emitted by radioactive elements in rocks to identify lithological units. Together, these diverse approaches allow for the characterization of geological structures, resource exploration, and environmental assessments, providing critical data for understanding the Earth's internal architecture.
