# Portrait Editing

Portrait editing is a systematic process used to enhance photographs of individuals by addressing technical deficiencies and refining aesthetic qualities. This practice involves correcting issues related to lighting, exposure, and focus while simultaneously improving skin texture, facial features, and overall composition. It is a standard procedure in professional photography, made accessible to practitioners of varying skill levels through software such as Adobe Photoshop.

Educators like Piximperfect often structure efficient workflows around five core techniques to streamline this complex process. These methods provide a framework for addressing common portrait issues without compromising the natural appearance of the subject. The approach emphasizes a logical sequence of operations, allowing editors to make precise adjustments that maintain the integrity of the original image while achieving professional results.

The techniques typically cover essential aspects of digital retouching, including frequency separation for skin texture, dodge and burn for lighting control, and color grading for mood enhancement. By adhering to these structured methods, users can achieve consistent and high-quality outcomes. This standardized approach helps bridge the gap between amateur and professional editing, ensuring that technical corrections support rather than detract from the subject's likeness.
