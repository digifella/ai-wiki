# RNA

RNA (ribonucleic acid) is a nucleic acid molecule found in all living cells that plays a central role in gene expression and protein synthesis. Like DNA, RNA is composed of nucleotides containing a [[concepts/sugar|sugar]] backbone, phosphate groups, and nitrogenous [[concepts/number-systems|bases]], but it typically exists as a single strand rather than a double helix and contains the sugar ribose instead of deoxyribose. RNA carries genetic information from DNA to the cellular machinery responsible for protein production.

## Types and Functions

Several classes of RNA exist with distinct functions in cells. Messenger RNA (mRNA) carries genetic [[concepts/instructions|instructions]] copied from DNA to ribosomes. Transfer RNA (tRNA) brings amino acids to the ribosome during protein synthesis. Ribosomal RNA (rRNA) forms part of the ribosome itself. Additional RNA types, including regulatory RNAs like microRNA, help [[concepts/power|control]] gene expression [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] multiple levels.

## Role in Gene Editing

RNA has become increasingly important in [[concepts/gene-editing|gene editing]] technologies. [[concepts/crispr|CRISPR]] systems, which have revolutionized the ability to edit genes with precision, rely on guide RNAs to direct molecular machinery to specific DNA sequences. Beyond CRISPR, RNA-based approaches continue to expand the toolkit for modifying genetic information and studying cellular processes.
