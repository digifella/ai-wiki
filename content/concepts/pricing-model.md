# Pricing Model

A pricing model defines the [[concepts/structure|structure]] and strategy a company uses to set prices for its products or services. In the context of AI services, [[concepts/pricing|pricing]] models determine how customers are charged for access to models and their capabilities, typically based on factors such as usage volume, feature access, or subscription tier.

## Key Considerations

Pricing models must balance several competing interests: generating sufficient revenue for the provider, remaining accessible to target customers, and accurately reflecting the computational costs of [[concepts/service-delivery|service delivery]]. For [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]] and AI systems, pricing often [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] with input and output [[concepts/tokens|tokens]] processed, though alternative approaches such as flat subscription rates or tiered access levels are also employed.

## Impact of Model Improvements

As [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] improve in performance and capability—such as enhanced [[concepts/visual-understanding|visual understanding]] or faster processing speeds—pricing [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] may be necessary to reflect the increased value delivered. These adjustments must be communicated clearly to users to maintain trust and enable informed decision-making about which services best suit their needs and budgets.
