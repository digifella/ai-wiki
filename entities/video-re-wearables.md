# Video Re Wearables

Wearable devices equipped with embedded sensors are transforming clinical practice in allied health by enabling continuous, real-time monitoring of patient physiological data. These electronic instruments are designed to be worn directly on the body and collect metrics including heart rate, movement patterns, temperature, and other biometric indicators. Unlike consumer fitness applications, clinical-grade wearables are validated for medical accuracy and integrated into formal healthcare workflows.

## Clinical Applications

In allied health professions—including physiotherapy, occupational therapy, and sports medicine—wearable sensors provide objective data that complements traditional clinical assessment. Practitioners use this information to track patient progress during rehabilitation, monitor compliance with prescribed exercise programs, and detect changes in mobility or physical function. The continuous nature of sensor data allows for detection of patterns that might be missed during periodic clinical visits.

## Integration into Practice

The adoption of wearable technology in allied health requires consideration of data management, patient privacy, and clinical interpretation. Healthcare providers must validate that sensor readings align with clinical outcomes and integrate wearable data into existing electronic health record systems. Training and standardization of protocols remain important for consistent implementation across different clinical settings.
