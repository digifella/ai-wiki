# Cosmology

Cosmology is the scientific discipline dedicated to understanding the origin, large-scale structure, and evolution of the universe. It integrates principles from theoretical physics and astronomy to construct models that describe the cosmos on its grandest scales. The field addresses fundamental inquiries regarding the universe's composition, its historical development over billions of years, and its potential ultimate fate.

## Theoretical Framework

Modern cosmology is grounded in the general theory of relativity, which describes gravity as the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy. This framework allows for the modeling of the universe as a whole, leading to the development of the Big Bang theory as the prevailing explanation for the universe's early expansion. Key components of this model include the cosmic microwave background radiation, which provides evidence for the universe's hot, dense origin, and the distribution of large-scale structures such as galaxies and galaxy clusters.

## Observational Methods

Observational cosmology relies on data gathered from telescopes across the electromagnetic spectrum, as well as from gravitational wave detectors and neutrino observatories. Astronomers measure the redshift of distant galaxies to determine their recession velocities, providing evidence for the accelerating expansion of the universe. These observations have led to the discovery of dark matter and dark energy, which together constitute the majority of the universe's total energy density, though their exact nature remains a subject of active research.

## Open Questions

Despite significant progress, several fundamental questions remain unresolved. Cosmologists continue to investigate the nature of dark matter and dark energy, the mechanism of cosmic inflation that occurred fractions of a second after the Big Bang, and the ultimate fate of the universe. Ongoing experiments and space missions aim to refine measurements of cosmological parameters, such as the Hubble constant, to reduce discrepancies between different observational methods and theoretical predictions.
