# Immersive Audio

**Immersive [[concepts/audio-modality|audio]]** refers to sound systems that create a three-dimensional auditory [[concepts/experience|experience]], placing listeners within a sphere of sound rather than just a stereo field. While often associated with [[concepts/dolby-atmos]], the implementation varies significantly between professional [[concepts/object-based-audio|object-based audio]] and consumer-grade simulated experiences.

## Core Concepts

*   **Object-Based Audio:** The technical foundation of true immersive audio (e.g., [[entities/dolby|Dolby]] Atmos). Audio elements are treated as independent objects with [[concepts/metadata|metadata]] defining their position and [[concepts/exercise|movement]] in 3D space, allowing the renderer to adapt to the specific [[entities/speaker|speaker]] configuration of the playback device.
*   **[[concepts/simulated-home-theater-sound|Simulated Home Theater Sound]]:** Many consumer implementations rely on channel-based upmixing or psychoacoustic [[concepts/simulation|simulation]] to mimic immersion on limited hardware (e.g., soundbars or stereo headphones). Critics argue this creates a "deception" where the perceived height and [[concepts/accuracy|precision]] of true object-based audio are approximated rather than achieved.
*   **The "Atmos" Gap:** There is a significant disparity between the theoretical capabilities of [[concepts/dolby-atmos]] and the actual [[concepts/user-experience-design|user experience]] in widespread home setups. Many users experience a "fake" version of immersion due to [[concepts/hardware-limitations|hardware limitations]] or incorrect calibration, leading to misconceptions about the technology's efficacy.

## Critical Perspectives

*   **Marketing vs. Reality:** The term "Dolby Atmos" is frequently used as a marketing label for various audio technologies, some of which do not utilize true object-based [[concepts/fat-rendering|rendering]]. This has led to public skepticism regarding the authenticity of "immersive" claims in consumer electronics.
*   **Hardware Dependency:** True immersive audio requires specific speaker placements (including overhead or upward-firing [[concepts/causes|drivers]]) and decoding hardware. Without these, the experience degrades to simulated surround sound, which lacks the precise spatial [[concepts/solution|resolution]] of object-based audio.
