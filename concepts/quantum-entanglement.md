# Quantum Entanglement

**Quantum Entanglement** is a physical phenomenon that occurs when a group of particles is generated, interact, or share spatial proximity in a way such that the quantum state of each particle cannot be described independently of the state of the others, including when the particles are separated by a large distance.

## Core Principles
- **Non-locality**: Measurement of one entangled particle instantaneously correlates with the state of its partner, regardless of distance.
- **Superposition**: Particles exist in all possible states simultaneously until measured.
- **Bell's Theorem**: Proves that no local hidden variable theories can reproduce all predictions of quantum mechanics.

## Mathematical & Combinatorial Connections
Quantum mechanics provides novel solutions to classical combinatorial problems previously deemed impossible.

- **Euler's 6x6 Officer Problem**: A classic puzzle asking for the arrangement of 36 officers (6 ranks, 6 regiments) in a 6x6 grid such that no rank or regiment repeats in any row or column. [[entities/leonhard-euler|Leonhard Euler]] conjectured no solution existed for $n=6$.
- **Quantum Loophole**: Recent research demonstrates that by allowing officers to exist in **[[concepts/quantum-superposition|quantum superposition]]** of ranks and regiments, a valid solution for the 6x6 grid becomes possible. This exploits Quantum Entanglement to bypass classical combinatorial constraints.
- **Generalization**: This approach suggests that quantum resources can solve specific [[concepts/latin-square]] problems that are unsolvable classically for certain dimensions $n$.
