# Interdisciplinary Collaboration

[[concepts/interprofessional-collaboration|Interdisciplinary collaboration]] is the integration of theories, methodologies, and data from distinct disciplines to solve complex problems that exceed the scope of single-domain [[concepts/expertise|expertise]]. It moves beyond multidisciplinary adjacency by synthesizing knowledge structures to create emergent insights.

## Core Principles
- **Epistemic Integration:** Combining diverse ways of knowing to reduce blind spots inherent in siloed research.
- **Common Lexicon:** Establishing shared [[concepts/terminology|terminology]] to facilitate communication across domain boundaries.
- **Structural Alignment:** Overcoming institutional barriers (e.g., departmental funding, publication metrics) that incentivize isolation.

## Applications in Space Life Sciences
Recent commentary highlights critical barriers and strategies for collaboration in high-stakes environments. See [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Bridging-the-Gaps-in-Space-Life-Sciences-and-Health-Rese|Bridging the Gaps in Space Life Sciences and Health Research]] for detailed analysis by Nickerson, Ott, and Burer (Spring 2024).

Key findings include:
- **Data Silos:** Fragmentation between biological, physical, and [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] data streams hinders holistic [[concepts/health|health]] monitoring for astronauts.
- **Translational Gaps:** [[concepts/disconnection|Disconnection]] between fundamental space biology research and practical clinical applications in microgravity.
- **Collaborative Frameworks:** Necessity for unified data standards and joint mission planning to bridge life sciences and [[concepts/health-research|health research]] gaps.

## Challenges
- **Cultural Resistance:** Discipline-specific identity often resists external methodological intrusion.
- **Resource Allocation:** Funding bodies often categorize grants narrowly, discouraging broad interdisciplinary bids.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Traditional [[concepts/document-review|peer review]] struggles to assess novelty that spans multiple fields.
