# Orbital Nodes

Orbital nodes are designated positions in space where satellites maintain stable operational positions within a constellation network. These fixed points are mathematically determined by specific orbital parameters: altitude (the height above Earth's surface), inclination (the angle of the orbit relative to the equator), and the satellite's position within its orbital plane. Together, these parameters define where a satellite functions within a larger system and determine its coverage footprint on Earth.

## Function in Satellite Systems

In practice, orbital nodes function as part of a distributed network architecture. Multiple satellites occupy different nodes within the same orbital plane, or across different orbital planes, to provide continuous coverage of a geographic region or the entire planet. The density and distribution of these nodes determine the network's capacity, latency, and reliability. Satellite constellations like Starlink, OneWeb, and Kuiper each operate their own sets of nodes configured to meet specific service requirements.

## Beyond Communication Networks

While orbital nodes were historically associated primarily with telecommunications, the infrastructure and operational framework they represent have expanding applications. As space-based infrastructure becomes more commercially viable, orbital nodes increasingly serve purposes beyond internet connectivity, including Earth observation, weather monitoring, scientific research, and potential future applications in space-based resource management. The standardization and optimization of these orbital positions represents a foundational element for the growing space economy.
