# Molecular Biology

Molecular Biology is the branch of biology that studies the chemical structures and processes of biological phenomena at the molecular level. It focuses on the interactions between the various systems of a cell, including the interactions between DNA, RNA, and protein synthesis, and how these interactions are regulated.

Recent advancements highlight the integration of [[concepts/calculation-methods|computational methods]] with experimental biology to decode the "language" of the cell. Key developments include:

*   **AI and Cellular Complexity**: Silvana Konermann and others advocate for using [[concepts/ai-technologies|Artificial Intelligence]] to interpret the wild complexity of human cells, moving beyond traditional reductionist approaches to understand systemic biological functions [[lab-notes/2026-07-15-Decoding-Biologys-Language-AI-CRISPR-and-Single-Cell-Seq|Decoding Biology's Language: AI, CRISPR, and Single-Cell Sequencing for Disease]].
*   **[[concepts/gene-editing|Gene Editing]] Evolution**: While [[concepts/crispr]] remains a cornerstone, research continues into successor technologies and alternative molecular pathways inspired by natural antiviral defense [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]], such as those explored by [[entities/feng-zhang]] at the [[entities/broad-institute]] [[concepts/viral-defense-mechanism|Viral Defense Mechanism]].
*   **Single-Cell Sequencing**: High-[[concepts/solution|resolution]] mapping of individual cells allows for precise identification of disease markers and cellular states, providing granular data that [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] can utilize for predictive diagnostics and therapeutic design.

## Technical Approach

The field aims to leverage naturally occurring biological systems—such as bacterial immune responses—as tools for targeted gene editing. By understanding how organisms distinguish between self and foreign genetic material, researchers can potentially develop editing systems with improved specificity or expanded capabilities compared to earlier generations of tools.
