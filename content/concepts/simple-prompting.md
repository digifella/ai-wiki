# Simple Prompting

Simple Prompting refers to the practice of using overly simplistic or minimal prompts when interacting with [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]] (LLMs). According to [[entities/kevin-patrick-robbins|Kevin Patrick Robbins]], an advertising, editorial, and commercial photographer, this approach typically results in superficial outputs that fail to leverage the full capabilities of modern language models.

## The Problem with Surface-Level Prompts

When users rely on basic, straightforward prompts without sufficient context, specificity, or structural guidance, LLMs tend to produce generic, shallow [[concepts/responses|responses]]. This limitation stems not from the models themselves but from the quality of direction provided. Robbins argues that most users achieve disappointing results because they underestimate the importance of crafting thoughtful, detailed prompts that clearly communicate their intent and requirements.

## Implications for Business and Professional Use

The concept has practical implications for professionals seeking to use LLMs effectively in business contexts. Better [[concepts/prompting|prompting]] techniques—moving beyond simple queries toward more sophisticated [[concepts/prompt-based-modeling|prompt engineering]]—enable users to extract more nuanced, contextually appropriate, and useful responses from language models. This distinction becomes particularly relevant for advertising, editorial, commercial, and other professional [[concepts/software|applications]] where output quality directly impacts deliverables.
