# Multiple Exposure

Multiple exposure is a photographic technique in which a photographer captures two or more images of the same scene and combines them during post-processing. Rather than relying on a single exposure, this method allows photographers to blend multiple frames to achieve results that would be difficult or impossible to capture in a single shot. The technique has applications across various photographic genres, including portrait, still life, and landscape photography.

## Focus Stacking

In the context of landscape photography, multiple exposure is frequently employed as a method for focus stacking. This specific application involves capturing a series of images where the point of focus shifts incrementally from the foreground to the background. By combining these frames, the photographer creates a final image with enhanced sharpness and greater depth of field than a single exposure could provide. This process ensures that elements at varying distances remain crisp and detailed, addressing the optical limitations of aperture and sensor size.
