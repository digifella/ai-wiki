# Granular Materials Particulate Matter Such As Sand Grain Or Gravel That Behaves

[[concepts/granular-materials|Granular materials]] are collections of discrete solid particles that interact through mechanical [[entities/contact|contact]] rather than chemical [[concepts/link-aggregation|bonding]]. Common examples include sand, gravel, soil, powders, and industrial materials like coal and cereals. The defining characteristic of [[concepts/granular-matter|granular systems]] is that individual particles can move independently while the collection as a whole exhibits emergent [[concepts/material-properties|physical properties]] distinct from those of its constituent parts.

## Physical Properties

Unlike conventional solids with fixed intermolecular bonds, granular matter exhibits unique mechanical behaviors such as jamming, where the material transitions from a fluid-like state to a solid-like state under [[concepts/stress|stress]]. These systems do not have a well-defined temperature in the thermodynamic sense, as thermal fluctuations are negligible compared to gravitational and inertial forces. Consequently, they display complex responses to shear stress, including dilation, where the volume of the material increases during shearing, and hysteresis in their stress-strain [[concepts/relationships|relationships]].

## Flow and Stability

The [[concepts/flow|flow]] dynamics of granular materials are governed by [[concepts/friction|friction]] and inelastic collisions between particles. When poured, they form conical piles with a characteristic [[concepts/angle-of-repose|angle of repose]], beyond which the material becomes unstable and avalanches. In confined spaces or under vibration, granular materials can exhibit segregation, where particles of different sizes or densities separate into distinct layers or regions. This behavior is critical in industrial processing, [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]], and geological phenomena such as landslides and soil liquefaction.
