# Techno Economic Modeling

Techno-economic modeling is an analytical framework that combines technical feasibility assessment with economic viability analysis. It evaluates whether a proposed infrastructure project can achieve its operational goals within realistic cost constraints and market conditions. This approach integrates engineering specifications, capital requirements, operational expenses, and revenue projections to produce an integrated assessment of project viability.

The methodology is particularly relevant for evaluating large-scale, capital-intensive [[concepts/large-scale-infrastructure|infrastructure projects]] where technical performance and economic sustainability are interdependent. Space-based infrastructure projects—such as satellite networks, orbital manufacturing facilities, or data centers—exemplify cases where techno-economic modeling is essential, as they involve substantial upfront costs, novel engineering challenges, and uncertain market demand.

A comprehensive techno-economic model typically examines multiple layers of analysis: the engineering feasibility of proposed systems, the cost [[concepts/structure|structure]] across design, [[concepts/deployment|deployment]], and operational phases, and the market conditions that determine revenue potential. This multi-dimensional approach helps distinguish between technically possible projects and those that can sustain long-term economic viability. The model output informs investment decisions and identifies which design [[concepts/parameters|parameters]] or operational assumptions most significantly affect overall project economics.
