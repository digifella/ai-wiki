# Superparamagnetic Macrophages

**Superparamagnetic macrophages** are specialized immune cells containing superparamagnetic iron oxide nanoparticles that function as biological magnetometers. Recent research identifies these cells in the liver and spleen of [[concepts/homing-pigeons|homing pigeons]] as a primary mechanism for detecting [[concepts/earths-magnetic-field|Earth's magnetic field]], resolving long-standing questions regarding avian Magnetoreception.

## Mechanism
* **Superparamagnetism**: Unlike ferromagnetic materials that retain magnetization, superparamagnetic nanoparticles exhibit strong magnetic attraction only in the presence of an external magnetic field and lose magnetization when the field is removed. This allows for high sensitivity without permanent magnetic bias.
* **Cellular Location**: Found primarily in Liver and Spleen, distinct from the cryptochrome-based [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] often associated with retinal magnetoreception.
* **Mitochondrial Integration**: Emerging perspectives suggest [[concepts/mitochondrial-function|mitochondria]] act as sophisticated [[concepts/central-processing-units|central processing units]] rather than mere powerhouses, potentially influencing cellular magnetism and [[concepts/quantum-phenomena|quantum effects]] [[lab-notes/2026-10-11-Mitochondria-Beyond-Powerhouses-Running-the-Body-Quantum|Mitochondria: Beyond Powerhouses, Running the Body, Quantum Effects, and Magnetism]].
