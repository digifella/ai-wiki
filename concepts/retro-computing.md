# Retro Computing

[[concepts/historical-computing|Retro computing]] refers to the use, restoration, and [[concepts/preservation|preservation]] of computer hardware and software from earlier eras, typically spanning the 1970s through 1990s, though definitions vary among practitioners. The field encompasses both maintaining original machines in working condition and executing period-appropriate software, whether on authentic hardware or through emulation. As [[concepts/history-of-computing|computing history]] gained recognition as a subject worthy of preservation, [[concepts/legacy-computing|retro computing]] evolved from niche hobby activity into a more established practice with dedicated communities, museums, and archives.

## Hardware and Restoration

[[concepts/vintage-computing|Retro computing]] practitioners work with older computer systems including personal computers, [[concepts/gaming|gaming]] consoles, mainframes, and minicomputers. Restoration efforts involve repairing degraded components, replacing failed power supplies and capacitors, and recovering data from obsolete [[entities/storage|storage]] media.

## AI Integration and Modern Tools

The intersection of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] and retro computing is emerging as a significant sub-domain, focusing on enhancing legacy workflows and bridging historical systems with modern capabilities.

*   **[[concepts/development-speed|AI-Assisted Development]]**: Projects like [[lab-notes/2026-10-03-PDP-Gary-AI-Integration-Retro-Computing-Projects-and-AI|PDP Gary AI Integration, Retro Computing Projects and AI]] demonstrate the application of modern AI assistants to retro computing contexts, such as porting software and optimizing code for vintage architectures.
*   **[[concepts/code-optimization|Code Optimization]]**: AI-driven tools are being utilized to analyze and optimize legacy codebases, improving performance and compatibility on constrained hardware environments.
*   **Task Management**: Integration of AI with custom task managers (e.g., TMOG) on retro systems to streamline project workflows and manage complex restoration or emulation tasks.
*   **Limitations and Capabilities**: Ongoing discussions highlight the specific limitations of current [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] when applied to the unique constraints and quirks of historical computing platforms, necessitating hybrid approaches that combine AI assistance with deep [[concepts/experience|domain expertise]].
