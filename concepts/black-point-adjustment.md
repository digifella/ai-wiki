# Black Point Adjustment

Black point adjustment is a tonal correction technique in Adobe Photoshop used to enhance image contrast by establishing where the darkest tones in a photograph should begin. Rather than relying on the image's existing darkest pixels, this adjustment allows editors to manually set a reference point for pure black or near-black values. By defining the lower end of the tonal range, the technique increases overall visual depth and definition, making it particularly useful in portrait retouching where skin tones and shadows require precise control.

## Implementation

The adjustment is typically performed using the Levels or Curves adjustment layers. In the Levels dialog, the leftmost slider represents the black point; dragging this slider to the right shifts the darkest pixels in the image to true black (RGB 0,0,0). This action compresses the shadow tones and expands the midtones, effectively increasing contrast without necessarily affecting the highlights. Alternatively, the Curves tool allows for more granular control by clicking on the bottom-left point of the curve graph and dragging it downward to set the new black reference.

## Application in Portraiture

In portrait photography, black point adjustment helps to separate subjects from their backgrounds and adds structure to facial features. By ensuring that the deepest shadows in the hair, eyes, and clothing are rendered as true black, the technique prevents images from appearing flat or muddy. This process restores natural contrast that may have been lost due to lighting conditions or post-processing adjustments, resulting in a more dynamic and professional-looking final image.
