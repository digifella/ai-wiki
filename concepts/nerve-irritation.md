# Nerve Irritation

**Nerve irritation** refers to the inflammation or mechanical compression of a nerve, leading to pain, paresthesia, or weakness. It is distinct from nerve damage; it is often reversible through targeted interventions that reduce inflammation and correct biomechanical [[concepts/stress|stress]].

## Key Mechanisms
- **Inflammation:** Chemical mediators sensitize nerve endings, lowering the threshold for pain signals.
- **Mechanical Compression:** Physical [[concepts/pressure|pressure]] from surrounding tissues (muscles, discs, ligaments) restricts nerve gliding and blood [[concepts/flow|flow]].
- **Sensitization:** Chronic irritation can lead to central sensitization, where the nervous system becomes hyper-responsive to stimuli.

## Common Manifestations
- Radiating pain (e.g., [[concepts/sciatica]])
- Tingling or numbness (paresthesia)
- Muscle weakness in the affected nerve's distribution
- Aggravation by specific movements or positions

## Management Strategies

### Conservative Interventions
- **[[concepts/exercise|Movement]] Modification:** Avoiding positions that exacerbate compression.
- **Nerve Gliding Exercises:** Gentle movements to improve nerve mobility and reduce adhesions.
- **Anti-inflammatory Measures:** Rest, ice/heat, or NSAIDs to reduce chemical irritation.
