# Physical Chemistry

**Physical Chemistry** is the branch of chemistry concerned with understanding macroscopic, microscopic, and atomic phenomena in chemical systems by applying the principles, practices, and theories of [[concepts/physics]], particularly Thermodynamics, [[concepts/quantum-mechanics]], and Kinetics.

## Core Subfields

- **Thermodynamics**: Study of energy transfer and transformation, including Enthalpy, Entropy, and Gibbs [[concepts/limitless-energy|Free Energy]].
- **Quantum Chemistry**: Application of quantum mechanics to chemical systems, focusing on electronic structure and [[concepts/link-aggregation|bonding]].
- **Statistical Mechanics**: Bridging microscopic properties of atoms/molecules with macroscopic bulk properties.
- **Kinetics**: Study of reaction rates and [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]].
- **Spectroscopy**: Interaction of [[concepts/light|electromagnetic radiation]] with matter.

## Key Concepts & Recent Developments

### Water Anomalies and Structure
Water exhibits numerous [[concepts/anomalous-properties|anomalous properties]] (e.g., [[concepts/density|density]] maximum at 4°C, high specific heat capacity) that challenge simple molecular models. Recent research suggests these anomalies may stem from a dual-identity structure.

- **Dual Identity Hypothesis**: Evidence confirms that [[concepts/liquid-water|liquid water]] behaves as a mixture of two distinct local structures rather than a single homogeneous [[concepts/phase|phase]]. This duality helps explain its unique thermodynamic and dynamic properties.
- **Implications**: This structural complexity influences [[concepts/hydrogen-bonding]] networks, solvation shells, and biological interactions.
- **Source Integration**: See [[lab-notes/2026-07-07-Liquid-Waters-Dual-Identity-Confirmed-Explaining-Anomalo|Liquid Water's Dual Identity Confirmed: Explaining Anomalous Properties]] for detailed analysis of the "two separate substances" confirmation.

### Phase Transitions
- Study of changes between solid, liquid, gas, and plasma states.
- Critical points and supercritical fluids.

### Electrochemistry
- Study of chemical processes that cause electrons to move, including Batteries, Corrosion, and Electrolysis.
