# Shadow Matching

Shadow matching is the process of ensuring shadows in a compositing workflow align with the environmental light source, color temperature, and intensity. When combining visual elements from different sources—such as placing a photographed object into a new background—the shadows cast by that element must correspond to the same lighting conditions as the scene. This alignment is essential for maintaining visual consistency, as viewers intuitively recognize how light behaves in physical space.

## Technical Considerations

Effective shadow matching requires analyzing several interdependent factors to achieve photorealism. The direction of the shadow must match the angle of the primary light source in the target environment, while the softness of the shadow edge depends on the size and distance of that light source. Additionally, the color of the shadow is rarely pure black; it is influenced by ambient light bounce and the color temperature of the surrounding environment, requiring careful tinting to avoid a "floating" appearance.

Artists often use reference photography or 3D lighting setups to determine these parameters before applying digital adjustments. Techniques may include rotating, scaling, and blurring shadow layers to mimic the specific optical characteristics of the new scene. Proper integration ensures that the composite feels grounded in the same physical space, preventing the illusion from breaking due to inconsistent lighting logic.
