## Fundamental Laws of Physics

### Definition
The fundamental laws of physics encompass the basic principles that govern all natural phenomena, providing a framework to understand and predict the behavior of matter and energy across various [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]]. These laws are universal and invariant.

### Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/quantum-mechanics|Quantum Mechanics]]**: quantum-mechanics
  - Deals with particles at atomic and subatomic levels.
- **General Relativity**: general-relativity
  - Describes [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]] as a geometric property of space-time.
- **[[concepts/electromagnetic-interaction|Electromagnetism]]**: electromagnetism
  - Explains the behavior and interactions of electrically charged particles.

### Interconnectedness
The laws interconnect, forming a coherent framework for understanding the universe. For instance, quantum mechanics explains phenomena at small scales, while general relativity is crucial at large scales such as galaxies and [[concepts/black-holes|black holes]].
