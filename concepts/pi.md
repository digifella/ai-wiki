# Pi

Pi (π) is a mathematical constant representing the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter. Its value is approximately 3.14159, though it cannot be expressed exactly as a finite decimal or fraction. Pi is a [[concepts/transcendental-number|transcendental number]], meaning it is not the root of any polynomial equation with rational coefficients. As a result, its decimal expansion continues infinitely without repeating.

## Mathematical and Physical Applications

Pi appears throughout mathematics and physics in contexts far beyond basic circle geometry. It emerges in trigonometry, calculus, and complex analysis, often relating to periodic functions and wave phenomena. In physics, it is fundamental to equations describing electromagnetism, quantum mechanics, and general relativity. The constant also plays a critical role in number theory and probability, such as in the normal distribution and the Basel problem.

## Cultural and Computational Significance

The computation of pi has driven advances in algorithms and computer hardware for centuries. Modern calculations have determined trillions of digits, serving as a benchmark for testing supercomputers and verifying the correctness of arithmetic operations. In popular culture, pi is frequently referenced for its infinite, non-repeating nature. Notably, Carl Sagan’s novel *Contact* features a fictional plot point where hidden messages are encoded within the base-11 representation of pi, highlighting the constant’s enduring fascination with both mathematicians and the public.
