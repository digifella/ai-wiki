# Orbital Insertion

**[[concepts/orbital-deployment|Orbital insertion]]** is the process by which a spacecraft adjusts its trajectory to enter a stable orbit around a celestial body. This maneuver typically involves a critical burn to achieve the necessary [[concepts/velocity|velocity]] and altitude, transitioning from a sub-orbital or transfer trajectory into a closed orbit.

## Key Concepts
- **Delta-v Budget**: The change in velocity required to achieve insertion, heavily dependent on the target orbit's altitude and inclination.
- **Periapsis/Apoapsis**: The lowest and highest points of the orbit, respectively, often used to define the insertion parameters.
- **Circularization**: A secondary burn often performed at apoapsis to raise the periapsis and achieve a circular orbit.
- **[[concepts/gravitational-pull|Gravity]] Assist**: Utilizing the gravity of other bodies to alter trajectory and [[concepts/speed|speed]], crucial for missions requiring significant delta-v changes or direction shifts.

## Recent Developments
- **[[concepts/starship-flight-14|SpaceX Starship Flight 14]]**: Marked a significant milestone with the vehicle's first successful orbital insertion during its test flights.
- **[[concepts/project-mercury|BepiColombo Mission]]**: The joint ESA/JAXA mission to Mercury highlights the complexity of reaching the inner solar system. Due to the Sun's intense gravity and heat, the spacecraft employs a complex series of [[concepts/gravity-assist|gravity assists]] and orbital maneuvers to shed velocity and enter orbit, rather than a direct insertion. This approach is detailed in [[lab-notes/2026-10-12-BepiColombo-Mission-to-Mercury-Navigating-Solar-Gravity|BepiColombo Mission to Mercury: Navigating Solar Gravity and Extreme Heat]].
