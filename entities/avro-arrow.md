# Avro Arrow

The **[[entities/avro-canada|Avro Canada]] CF-105 Arrow** was a delta-winged, twin-[[concepts/engine|engine]], supersonic interceptor and reconnaissance aircraft developed by [[concepts/avro-canada]] in the 1950s. It is widely regarded as one of the most advanced aircraft of its era, featuring cutting-edge technology including a fully integrated digital flight control system and advanced radar capabilities. The program was abruptly cancelled by the Canadian government in February 1959, leading to significant economic and social repercussions in Canada and becoming a symbol of national industrial loss.

## Key Characteristics
- **Role:** Supersonic interceptor and reconnaissance
- **Manufacturer:** Avro Canada
- **First Flight:** 1958
- **Status:** Cancelled (1959); no operational service
- **Technology:**
  - Digital flight control systems (precursor to fly-by-wire)
  - Advanced radar and weapons integration
  - Supersonic capability at high altitudes

## Historical Context & Legacy
The cancellation of the Arrow program remains a contentious topic in Canadian political and industrial history. Critics argue that the decision undermined Canada's aerospace industry and technological sovereignty, while proponents cited cost overruns and changing strategic needs due to the advent of intercontinental [[concepts/ballistic-missiles|ballistic missiles]] (ICBMs).
