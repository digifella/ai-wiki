# Universe Age

The **Universe Age** is the time elapsed since the [[concepts/big-bang]], currently estimated at approximately 13.8 billion years. This metric serves as the fundamental temporal coordinate for cosmological models and the history of structure formation.

## Key Milestones

- **[[entities/planck|Planck]] Era & Inflation**: Occurred within $10^{-43}$ seconds to fractions of a second after the Big Bang, establishing initial conditions.
- **Recombination & CMB**: At ~380,000 years, electrons bound to nuclei, allowing [[concepts/light|photons]] to travel freely, creating the [[concepts/cosmic-microwave-background]].
- **Cosmic Dawn**: The [[concepts/first-generation-of-stars|first stars]] and galaxies began forming between 100 million and 500 million years after the Big Bang.

## Star Formation History

The rate of star formation across cosmic history is visualized via the [[concepts/madau-plot]], which tracks the [[concepts/stellar-mass|Stellar Mass]] [[concepts/density|Density]] over redshift.

- **Peak Formation**: Star formation rates peaked at "[[concepts/cosmic-noon|Cosmic Noon]]" (z ~ 2-3).

## Age vs. Observable Size

A common [[concepts/cosmological-paradox|cosmological paradox]] arises from the discrepancy between the universe's age (~13.8 billion years) and the current diameter of the [[concepts/observable-universe]] (~92 billion light-years). This is resolved by understanding [[concepts/metric-expansion-of-space|metric expansion]]:

- **Light Travel Time vs. [[concepts/proper-distance|Proper Distance]]**: While light has traveled for 13.8 billion years, the space it traversed has expanded during its journey.
- **Current Radius**: The comoving distance to the particle horizon is approximately 46 billion light-years, resulting in a diameter of ~92 billion light-years.
- **Expansion Dynamics**: The rate of expansion is governed by the [[concepts/hubble-constant|Hubble Constant]] and the energy density of the universe ([[concepts/the-composition-of-the-universe-extends-beyond-what-is-directly-observable|dark energy]], matter, radiation).

For a detailed explanation of this discrepancy, see [[lab-notes/2026-07-17-Observable-Universe-Expansion-Age-vs.-Current-Size-Expla|Observable Universe Expansion: Age vs. Current Size Explained]].
