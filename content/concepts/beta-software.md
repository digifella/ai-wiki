# Beta Software

Beta software is a pre-release version of an application or system made available to a limited external audience before its official public launch. During the beta phase, [[concepts/software|software]] typically contains unfinalized features, incomplete documentation, and both known and unknown bugs. The primary [[concepts/motivation|purpose]] of beta testing is to identify issues, gather [[concepts/user-feedback|user feedback]] from real-world usage, and refine the product before it reaches general availability.

## Role in Development

Beta versions occupy a specific position in the [[concepts/coding|software development]] lifecycle, following alpha [[concepts/testing|testing]] (which is conducted internally by the development team) and preceding the final release. Organizations use the beta phase to validate their product in conditions closer to actual use than controlled internal testing environments can provide. [[concepts/feedback|Feedback]] from beta testers helps developers prioritize bug fixes, identify edge cases, and determine whether features meet user needs.

## Distribution and Participation

Beta software is typically distributed to a selected group of users rather than the general public, though some organizations conduct public or open beta programs. Beta testers are often volunteers who accept the risks of using unstable software in exchange for early access and the opportunity to influence product development. Terms of service for beta software commonly include limitations on liability, requests for bug reporting, and restrictions on redistribution.
