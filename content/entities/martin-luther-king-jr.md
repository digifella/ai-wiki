# Martin Luther King Jr

Martin Luther King Jr. (1929–1968) was an American Baptist minister and civil rights activist who became the most prominent leader of the American Civil Rights [[concepts/exercise|Movement]] from the mid-1950s until his [[concepts/assassination|assassination]] in 1968. He is best known for advancing civil rights through nonviolent civil disobedience, inspired by [[concepts/gandhi|Mahatma Gandhi]]'s [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]] of nonviolent resistance.

## Leadership and Communication

King's approach to [[concepts/leadership|leadership]] centered on articulating a clear moral [[concepts/motivation|purpose]] and [[concepts/computer-vision|vision]] for social change. His speeches, particularly the "I Have a Dream" speech delivered during the 1963 March on Washington, exemplified how purpose-driven communication could mobilize large-scale action and inspire widespread support for civil rights. Leadership scholars, including [[entities/simon-sinek|Simon Sinek]] in his analysis of [[concepts/start-with-why|purpose-driven leadership]], have examined King's methods as a case study in how leaders inspire action by starting with a compelling "why"—a foundational belief or [[concepts/purpose|purpose]]—rather than focusing primarily on tactical goals.

## Legacy

King's approach to leadership and social change continues to be studied in discussions about effective advocacy, organizational leadership, and the [[concepts/power|power]] of visionary communication. His emphasis on moral clarity and unified purpose demonstrated how leaders can inspire sustained collective action toward transformative social goals. King was assassinated on April 4, 1968, in Memphis, Tennessee, but his legacy remains central to contemporary discussions about purposeful leadership and social movement building.
