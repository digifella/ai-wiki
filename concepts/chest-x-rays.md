# Chest X Rays

Chest X-rays are radiographic images of the thorax used to diagnose and monitor a wide range of pulmonary, cardiac, and mediastinal conditions. They remain one of the most commonly ordered imaging studies in clinical practice due to their accessibility, low cost, and minimal radiation exposure compared to other imaging modalities. A standard chest X-ray examination typically includes posteroanterior (PA) and lateral views, which provide complementary perspectives for evaluating the lungs, heart, and chest wall.

## Clinical Applications

These images are primarily utilized to detect abnormalities such as pneumonia, lung cancer, heart failure, and pneumothorax. They are also essential for monitoring the progression of chronic diseases like chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and for verifying the placement of medical devices such as endotracheal tubes and central venous catheters.

## Technical Aspects

The procedure involves directing a small amount of ionizing radiation through the chest onto a detector. The resulting image displays denser structures, such as bones and the heart, as white, while air-filled spaces like the lungs appear black. Radiologists analyze these contrasts to identify pathological changes, fractures, or fluid accumulation within the thoracic cavity.
