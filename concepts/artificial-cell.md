# Artificial Cell

**Artificial cells** are synthetic constructs designed to mimic the properties of living cells, ranging from simple lipid vesicles to complex systems capable of [[concepts/yeast-fermentation|metabolism]] and replication. They serve as foundational models for understanding the [[concepts/start-of-life|origins of life]] and developing novel biomedical applications.

## Key Developments

- **[[concepts/plant-animal-hybridization|Spud Cell]]**: A landmark [[concepts/success|achievement]] in [[concepts/digital-gene-technology|synthetic-biology]] representing the most complete bottom-up artificial cell constructed to date.
	- Demonstrates core life processes: eating (nutrient uptake), growing, and replication.
	- Detailed in [[lab-notes/2026-07-13-Spud-Cell-Bottom-Up-Artificial-Cell-Demonstrating-Eating|Spud Cell: Bottom-Up Artificial Cell Demonstrating Eating, Growing, and Replication]].

## Characteristics

- **Bottom-Up Construction**: Built from non-living components rather than modified existing biological cells.
- **Metabolic Activity**: Capable of processing external resources to sustain internal functions.
- **Self-Replication**: Ability to produce copies of itself, a critical threshold for defining artificial life.
