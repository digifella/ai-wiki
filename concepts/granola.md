# Granola

Granola is a breakfast cereal made from rolled oats combined with nuts, seeds, and a sweetening agent, typically honey or sugar. The mixture is baked until the ingredients bind together and brown, creating crunchy clusters. The resulting product can be eaten dry as a cereal with milk or yogurt, consumed as a snack on its own, or incorporated into other foods such as parfaits and baked goods.

## Composition and Variations

The basic formula for granola includes oats as the primary ingredient, supplemented with nuts such as almonds and walnuts, seeds like sunflower and pumpkin seeds, and dried fruits including raisins, cranberries, or dates. Beyond these core components, variations exist across regions and manufacturers, with some versions including coconut, chocolate chips, or spices such as cinnamon. The binding agent—typically honey, maple syrup, or oil—and the type of sweetener used can affect both flavor and nutritional profile.

## History and Popularity

Granola emerged as a health food in the late 19th century but gained widespread popularity during the health-conscious movements of the 1960s and 1970s. It has since become a staple breakfast option globally, available both as a commercial product and as a homemade preparation. The versatility and perceived nutritional benefits of granola, combined with its shelf stability, have contributed to its enduring presence in food markets.
