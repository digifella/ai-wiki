# Galaxy Clusters

Galaxy clusters are among the largest gravitationally bound structures in the universe, containing hundreds to thousands of galaxies held together by [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]]. They serve as crucial laboratories for [[concepts/testing|testing]] cosmological [[concepts/models|models]] and understanding the [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] of matter on cosmic [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]]. The study of galaxy clusters has revealed fundamental puzzles about the composition and dynamics of the universe.

## The Matter Problem

A central challenge in cluster [[concepts/physics|physics]] concerns the accounting of matter. Observations indicate that the visible [[concepts/baryonic-matter|baryonic matter]]—gas, dust, and [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]]—accounts for only a small fraction of the total mass needed to explain the gravitational dynamics of clusters. This discrepancy led to the introduction of [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] as the dominant mass component. However, recent observations have raised questions about whether current models adequately explain the distribution and behavior of both baryonic and dark matter within these structures, suggesting that either dark matter properties or cluster formation theories may require refinement.

## Current Questions

The precise relationship between baryonic and dark matter in galaxy clusters remains incompletely understood. Measurements of cluster masses derived from different techniques—such as gravitational lensing, X-ray observations of hot gas, and galaxy velocity dispersions—sometimes yield inconsistent results. These discrepancies point to gaps in current [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|theoretical frameworks]] and highlight the need for improved observations and models to fully explain how galaxy clusters assemble and evolve.
