# Candidate Screening Methods

Candidate screening methods are systematic approaches organizations use to evaluate and filter job applicants during the recruitment process. These techniques reduce large applicant pools to manageable numbers of qualified candidates for further assessment stages. Screening typically occurs early in the recruitment process and applies standardized criteria aligned with job requirements and organizational needs. By eliminating unsuitable candidates before more time-intensive assessment stages, screening improves hiring efficiency and reduces recruitment costs.

## Common Screening Techniques

Organizations employ several standard methods to screen candidates. Resume and application review remains the most basic approach, where hiring teams assess qualifications, work history, and credentials against job specifications. Keyword filtering and applicant tracking systems (ATS) automate this process by identifying relevant experience and qualifications. Phone screening or brief telephone interviews allow recruiters to verify information and assess basic communication skills before investing time in in-person interviews. Skills tests and technical assessments evaluate specific competencies required for the role, while background checks verify employment history, education, and criminal records where legally permissible.

## Effectiveness and Considerations

The effectiveness of screening methods varies depending on job type and organizational context. While resume review is cost-effective, it may miss qualified candidates with non-traditional backgrounds or formatting issues. Structured screening criteria reduce bias compared to unstructured approaches, though unconscious bias can still influence human evaluation. Organizations must balance the efficiency gains of aggressive screening against the risk of eliminating potentially strong candidates who do not present themselves conventionally. Legal compliance is essential, as screening methods must not discriminate based on protected characteristics and must comply with relevant employment laws.
