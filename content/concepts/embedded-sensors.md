# Embedded Sensors

Embedded sensors are miniaturized [[concepts/electronic-components|electronic components]] integrated into [[concepts/wearable-devices|wearable devices]] that detect and measure physiological and [[concepts/exercise|movement]] data from the wearer's body. These sensors continuously collect information such as heart rate, body temperature, blood oxygen levels, movement patterns, and [[concepts/sleep-quality|sleep quality]]. By capturing data throughout daily activities, embedded sensors enable real-time monitoring without requiring the user to visit a [[concepts/health|healthcare]] facility.

In [[concepts/allied-health|allied health]] practice, embedded sensors facilitate objective, longitudinal health tracking that supports [[concepts/clinical-reasoning|clinical decision-making]] and patient management. Physiotherapists, occupational therapists, and other [[entities/allied-health|allied health]] professionals use data from these devices to monitor rehabilitation progress, track activity levels, and identify patterns that may indicate health changes. This continuous data collection allows practitioners to tailor interventions based on empirical measurements rather than periodic assessments alone.

Common sensor types found in [[entities/iot-devices|wearable devices]] include accelerometers for detecting motion and body position, gyroscopes for measuring rotation, photoplethysmography sensors for heart rate monitoring, and thermistors for temperature measurement. The [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]] and [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] of these sensors vary depending on sensor quality, device [[concepts/design|design]], and individual factors such as skin [[concepts/tone|tone]] and movement artifacts. Data from embedded sensors typically transmits wirelessly to mobile [[concepts/software|applications]] or [[concepts/cloud-computing|cloud platforms]] where it can be analyzed and reviewed by both users and healthcare providers.
