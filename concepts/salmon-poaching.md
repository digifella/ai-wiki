# Salmon Poaching

Salmon poaching is a classical French cooking technique that involves gently simmering salmon fillets or steaks in a flavored liquid until cooked through. This method is distinct from other forms of seafood preparation due to its emphasis on maintaining the delicate texture of the fish while infusing it with subtle aromatic notes. The process typically utilizes low to medium heat to ensure the salmon does not break apart or become dry, resulting in a moist and tender final dish.

The technique originates from Normandy, a region in northern France historically renowned for its apple orchards and dairy production. Consequently, traditional preparations often feature cider as the primary poaching liquid, which imparts a slight acidity and fruity depth to the fish. The poaching liquid is frequently enriched with aromatics such as shallots, herbs, and white wine to create a complex base before the salmon is introduced.

Following the poaching process, the salmon is often served with a cream-based sauce derived from the reduced poaching liquid. This sauce, known as *sauce normande* in some variations, complements the richness of the fish and the tartness of the cider. The dish is typically garnished with fresh herbs like parsley or chives to enhance its visual appeal and fresh flavor profile.
