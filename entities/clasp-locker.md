# Clasp Locker

A clasp locker is an early mechanical fastening mechanism that emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries as an alternative to traditional buttons and laces. Unlike modern zippers that rely on interlocking teeth, this device employed a clasp-based system to join two fabric edges together. It represented one of several experimental approaches to mechanical fastening developed during a period of significant innovation in textile hardware technology.

The development of the clasp locker occurred within a broader context of fastening device evolution, preceding the widespread adoption of the modern zipper. While it offered a novel method for securing garments and luggage, the technology was eventually superseded by more reliable and efficient designs. Its historical significance lies primarily in its role as a transitional technology that helped pave the way for the dominance of zipper-based systems in the global market.
