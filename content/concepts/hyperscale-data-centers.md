# Hyperscale Data Centers

Hyperscale data centers are large computing facilities operated by major technology companies and cloud service providers to deliver services at massive scale. These installations typically house hundreds of thousands of servers and networking equipment, requiring substantial infrastructure for power [[concepts/distribution|distribution]], cooling, and physical security. The [[concepts/consolidation|consolidation]] of computing resources into fewer, larger facilities has become the dominant model for [[concepts/cloud-computing|cloud computing]] and data [[entities/storage|storage]].

## Water Consumption and Cooling Systems

Water usage represents a significant operational challenge for hyperscale data centers. Cooling systems account for a substantial portion of facility energy consumption, with many centers using water-based cooling methods to manage heat generated by densely packed servers. This water demand has become increasingly critical as data centers expand geographically and as computational workloads grow, particularly with the rise of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] and machine [[concepts/learning|learning]] [[concepts/software|applications]] that require intensive processing power.

## Environmental and Infrastructure Impact

The expansion of hyperscale data centers has created tension between [[concepts/infrastructure-development|infrastructure development]] and [[concepts/water-management|water resource management]], particularly in regions facing [[concepts/water-scarcity|water scarcity]]. As AI workloads continue to grow, data centers require more cooling capacity, intensifying competition for local water resources with agricultural and municipal users. This has prompted facility operators and regulators to explore alternative cooling technologies, including air-based systems and waste heat recovery methods, while site selection increasingly considers water availability and climate conditions.
