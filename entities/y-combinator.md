# Y Combinator

Y Combinator is a startup accelerator and venture capital firm founded in 2005 by Paul Graham, Jessica Livingston, Robert Morris, and Trevor Blackwell. Headquartered in San Francisco, the organization provides seed funding, mentorship, and operational resources to early-stage technology companies, typically in exchange for a small equity stake. It is widely recognized for its role in shaping the modern startup ecosystem through its standardized approach to early-stage investment.

The accelerator operates through intensive batch programs that last approximately three months. During this period, founders receive structured guidance on product development, fundraising strategies, and business operations. The program culminates in Demo Day, where participating companies present their progress to a curated audience of investors. This model has facilitated the growth of numerous high-profile technology firms, establishing Y Combinator as a central node in the global network of open-source AI agents and autonomous development workflows.

## Operating Model

Y Combinator's primary mechanism is its batch program, which selects companies twice a year for a fixed-term cohort. The organization emphasizes rapid iteration and scalable business models, providing capital and access to a vast network of alumni and industry experts. By standardizing the early-stage funding process, Y Combinator has influenced how technology startups are founded, funded, and scaled, particularly within the software and artificial intelligence sectors.
