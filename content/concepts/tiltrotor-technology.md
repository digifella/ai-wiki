# Tiltrotor technology

[[concepts/hybrid-aircraft-design|Tiltrotor technology]] is a category of [[concepts/vertical-takeoff-and-landing-vtol|VTOL]] ([[concepts/vertical-take-off-and-landing|Vertical Take-Off and Landing]]) propulsion where proprotors are mounted on nacelles capable of rotating between vertical and horizontal orientations. This allows the aircraft to transition between helicopter-[[concepts/style|style]] vertical lift and fixed-wing aircraft-style high-[[concepts/speed|speed]] cruise.

## Core Mechanics
- **[[concepts/dual-flight-modes|Dual Flight Modes]]**: The ability to switch between vertical [[concepts/engine-thrust|thrust]] for takeoff/landing and horizontal thrust for forward flight.
- **Proprotor Conversion**: The mechanical transition of the engine nacelles to alter the thrust vector.
- **Conversion Corridor**: The specific airspeed and [[entities/pitch|pitch]] [[concepts/parameters|parameters]] required to safely transition between modes.

## Case Study: V-22 Osprey
- **Engineering Complexity**: Highly complex engineering required to manage the transition between its [[concepts/dual-flight-modes|dual flight modes]].
- **Operational Utility**: Proven capability in high-stakes environments, such as night rescue missions in the Libyan desert.
