# Buttons

A button is a fastening device consisting of a small object attached to fabric that passes through a corresponding buttonhole or [[concepts/loop|loop]] to [[concepts/secure|secure]] two pieces of material together. Buttons are among the oldest and most common [[concepts/fastening-devices|closures]] used in clothing, appearing in various forms across cultures and centuries. They function through a simple mechanical principle: the button's diameter is larger than the buttonhole, creating [[concepts/friction|friction]] and tension that holds the closure in place.

## History and Development

The earliest buttons, dating back to around 2000 BCE, were primarily decorative or served as toggles for fastening cords rather than passing through holes. The button and buttonhole system as a practical fastening method developed gradually through the Middle Ages in [[entities/europe|Europe]], becoming widespread by the 13th and 14th centuries. This [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] represented a significant advancement over previous closure methods such as pins, [[concepts/laces|laces]], and [[concepts/brooches|clasps]], as it allowed for quicker dressing and more secure fastening.

## Materials and Design

Buttons have been manufactured from diverse materials depending on availability and intended use, including bone, wood, metal, shell, glass, and synthetic polymers. The [[concepts/design|design]] of a button typically includes a face (the visible decorative or functional surface) and a shank or holes through which thread passes to attach it to fabric. Button shapes, sizes, and finishes vary widely to accommodate different garment types and aesthetic preferences, from simple utilitarian designs to elaborate ornamental pieces.
