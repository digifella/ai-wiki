# Practical Commands

Practical Commands are actionable [[concepts/instructions|instructions]] designed to translate technical functionality into accessible steps for non-technical users. Derived from Sandy Lee AI's educational materials on [[concepts/ai-assisted-coding|Claude Code]], they prioritize what users need to accomplish rather than the underlying technical mechanisms. This approach makes complex processes understandable and applicable to [[concepts/real-world-tasks|real-world tasks]] without requiring programming knowledge or specialized [[concepts/expertise|expertise]].

## Purpose and Application

Practical Commands serve as a bridge between capability and usability. Rather than explaining how technology works internally, they focus on concrete outcomes and sequential steps users can follow. This framework is particularly valuable when introducing non-technical audiences to [[concepts/ai-assisted-coding|AI-assisted coding]] tools, where technical jargon often obscures the actual utility of features. By framing functionality around user goals, Practical Commands make sophisticated tools more approachable.

## Characteristics

Effective Practical Commands are clear, outcome-focused, and free of unnecessary technical terminology. They acknowledge that users care about solving specific problems—automating workflows, generating content, debugging code—rather than understanding the engineering behind these capabilities. This user-centered design principle ensures that guidance remains relevant and actionable across different skill levels and technical backgrounds.
