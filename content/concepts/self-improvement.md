# Self Improvement

Self-improvement in AI systems refers to processes where an [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] iteratively enhances its own [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]], performance, or behavior without external intervention. This occurs through recursive self-optimization, where a system analyzes its outputs, identifies inefficiencies or errors, and modifies its own [[concepts/parameters|parameters]], algorithms, or approaches to perform better on subsequent iterations. The concept represents a departure from traditional machine [[concepts/learning|learning]], where improvement typically requires human-directed retraining or external [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] [[concepts/loops|loops]].

## Applications in Scientific Discovery

Recent implementations of [[concepts/self-improving-ai|self-improving AI]] have shown particular promise in scientific research contexts. Systems like [[entities/deepmind|DeepMind]]'s [[concepts/automated-scientific-research|Aletheia]] exemplify this approach by combining self-correction mechanisms with domain-specific [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]]. These systems can autonomously refine their hypotheses, experimental designs, and analytical methods across multiple iterations, accelerating the pace of [[concepts/scientific-discovery|scientific discovery]] and reducing dependence on human guidance during the research process.

## Technical Mechanisms

Self-improvement relies on several technical foundations: the ability to evaluate performance against [[concepts/defined-metrics|defined metrics]], access to mechanisms for modifying behavior or parameters, and sufficient [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]] to implement changes. [[concepts/self-correcting-ai|Self-correcting AI]] architectures incorporate feedback from validation steps directly into their optimization loops, allowing errors to inform immediate [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]]. The scope and [[concepts/speed|speed]] of these improvements vary depending on system [[concepts/design|design]], with some systems operating [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] timescales from microseconds to hours.
