# Physical Activity Guidelines

Physical Activity Guidelines are evidence-based recommendations developed by [[concepts/health|health]] organizations to specify the type, duration, and intensity of [[concepts/exercise|physical activity]] needed to maintain health and reduce the risk of chronic diseases. These guidelines serve as [[concepts/population-health|public health]] tools that inform individual behavior, clinical practice, and policy decisions. Major guidelines have been established by organizations including the World Health [[concepts/organization|Organization]], the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, and numerous national health agencies.

## Recommended Standards

Current guidelines for adults typically recommend [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity or 75 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity per week, along with resistance [[concepts/training|training]] on two or more days per week. For children and adolescents, guidelines generally recommend at least 60 minutes of daily physical activity. Guidelines also emphasize the importance of reducing sedentary time and incorporating movement throughout the day, recognizing that any physical activity is better than none.

## Health Outcomes

The guidelines are based on research demonstrating that regular physical activity reduces the risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, certain cancers, obesity, and [[concepts/mental-health|mental health]] conditions including [[concepts/depression|depression]] and [[concepts/anxiety|anxiety]]. Physical activity also contributes to bone health, muscle strength, cognitive function, and overall [[concepts/quality-of-life|quality of life]]. Guidelines are periodically updated as new evidence emerges regarding the dose-response [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] between activity levels and health outcomes.

## Implementation and Variation

Guidelines recognize that recommendations may be adapted based on age, fitness level, and existing health conditions. Special populations, including older adults, pregnant individuals, and people with chronic diseases, have supplementary guidance. Despite widespread dissemination, adherence to physical activity guidelines remains low in many populations, [[concepts/prompting|prompting]] continued research into effective strategies for behavior change and policy interventions.
