# Survival Prognosis

**Survival prognosis** refers to the predicted outcome of a disease, typically measured by the probability of survival over a specific period. It is influenced by tumor biology, treatment response, and individual patient factors.

## Key Determinants
*   **Tumor Stage and Grade:** Extent of spread and cellular differentiation.
*   **Treatment Modality:** Efficacy of surgery, chemotherapy, radiation, or emerging therapies.
*   **Patient Physiology:** Age, comorbidities, and immune status.

## Emerging Therapeutic Interventions
Recent case studies highlight non-traditional approaches that may alter standard prognostic models:

*   **[[concepts/oncolytic-virotherapy|Oncolytic Virotherapy]]:** Use of genetically modified viruses to selectively infect and kill cancer cells while sparing normal tissue.
*   **Self-Administration Protocols:** Cases such as [[lab-notes/2026-09-16-Self-Administered-Oncolytic-Virotherapy-in-Recurrent-Bre|Self-Administered Oncolytic Virotherapy in Recurrent Breast Cancer: Dr. Halassy's Case]] demonstrate potential for patient-led management of [[concepts/recurrent-breast-cancer|recurrent breast cancer]].
    *   **Case Context:** [[entities/dr-beata-halassy|Dr. Beata Halassy]], a Croatian [[concepts/virologist|virologist]], utilized self-administered oncolytic virotherapy after multiple recurrences despite conventional surgery and chemotherapy.
    *   **Prognostic Implication:** Suggests that refractory cases may benefit from targeted viral therapies when standard options are exhausted.
