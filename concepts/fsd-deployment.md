# FSD Deployment

Tesla's Full Self-Driving Supervised system received regulatory approval in the Netherlands in April 2026, becoming the first authorization of the technology within the European Union. This approval marked a significant shift in the company's geographic expansion, as FSD had previously operated primarily under North American regulatory frameworks. The Dutch authorization demonstrated that EU member states could establish independent approval pathways for autonomous vehicle systems, rather than operating under unified EU-level regulation.

## Regulatory Framework

The Netherlands' approval of FSD Supervised followed a national assessment process that evaluated the system's technical capabilities and safety requirements. This approach reflected the decentralized nature of autonomous vehicle regulation within the EU, where individual member states retained authority to authorize new driving technologies. The Dutch precedent subsequently influenced discussions around harmonizing autonomous vehicle standards across other European jurisdictions.

## Implications for European Expansion

The Netherlands deployment served as a reference point for Tesla's broader European strategy and for other autonomous vehicle developers seeking market entry. The approval process provided evidence that European regulators could move forward with supervised autonomous driving systems while maintaining safety oversight, potentially accelerating similar deployments in other EU member states.
