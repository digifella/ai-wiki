# Wearable Devices

Wearable devices are electronic instruments designed to be worn on the body, incorporating embedded sensors that continuously collect data on physiological parameters, movement, or environmental factors. These devices function through integrated hardware and software systems that monitor, record, and often transmit data in real time or at intervals. They have become established tools in both consumer health applications and clinical allied health practice.

## Clinical and Allied Health Applications

In allied health settings, wearable devices are used for patient monitoring, rehabilitation tracking, and chronic disease management. Physical therapists utilize motion-sensing wearables to assess gait patterns and joint range of motion, providing objective metrics to guide therapeutic interventions. Cardiologists and pulmonologists employ devices that track heart rate variability, oxygen saturation, and respiratory rates to monitor patient stability outside of hospital environments.

These tools facilitate remote patient monitoring, allowing healthcare providers to detect early signs of deterioration or non-compliance with treatment plans. By aggregating longitudinal data, clinicians can adjust care strategies based on real-world performance rather than isolated clinical visits. This continuous feedback loop enhances the precision of allied health services and supports evidence-based decision-making in patient care.
