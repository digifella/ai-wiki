# Engine Oil

[[concepts/engine|Engine]] oil serves as the essential fluid [[entities/medium|medium]] for internal combustion engines, performing multiple critical functions that enable reliable mechanical operation. Its primary role is to lubricate moving parts, creating a protective film between [[concepts/metal|metal]] surfaces to reduce [[concepts/friction|friction]] and minimize wear. Additionally, the oil helps dissipate heat generated during combustion, preventing components from overheating, while simultaneously carrying away combustion byproducts and contaminants to be filtered out.

The circulation of engine oil is driven by the oil pump, which maintains specific [[concepts/pressure|pressure]] levels to ensure adequate distribution throughout the engine block. This pressure is vital for the formation of hydrodynamic lubrication films, particularly in high-load areas such as bearings and camshafts. Without sufficient [[concepts/oil-pressure|oil pressure]], these protective films collapse, leading to direct metal-to-metal [[entities/contact|contact]], rapid component degradation, and potential catastrophic [[concepts/engine-failure|engine failure]].

Modern vehicles monitor oil pressure and level through integrated warning [[concepts/light|light]] systems to alert [[concepts/causes|drivers]] to potential issues. These systems typically trigger alerts when pressure drops below safe thresholds or when the oil level becomes critically low. Regular maintenance, including oil changes and filter replacements, is necessary to preserve the oil's chemical properties and ensure it continues to perform its lubricating, cooling, and cleaning functions effectively.
