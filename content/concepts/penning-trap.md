# Penning Trap

A Penning trap is an electromagnetic device designed to confine charged particles in a small volume of space. It uses a combination of a static magnetic field and an electric field to create a potential well that holds particles in place. The trap is named after physicist Frans Michel Penning, who developed the technique in the 1930s. Penning traps are widely used in [[concepts/granular-physics|particle physics]] research, mass spectrometry, and precision measurements because they can isolate individual particles or small groups of particles for extended periods.

## Operation and Design

The device typically consists of a cylindrical or hyperboloid electrode configuration surrounded by a strong magnetic field. The electric field, generated by applying voltage to the electrodes, creates a potential that confines particles radially, while the magnetic field constrains their motion in the axial direction. This dual-field approach allows particles to be held with minimal loss, making Penning traps particularly valuable for containing exotic or short-lived particles.

## Application to Antimatter Transport

In 2026, researchers at CERN successfully used a Penning trap to transport antimatter by truck, marking the first time antimatter has been moved outside a laboratory facility in this manner. The trap's ability to reliably confine charged antiparticles made it suitable for this unprecedented transport operation, demonstrating practical [[concepts/software|applications]] beyond stationary research environments.
