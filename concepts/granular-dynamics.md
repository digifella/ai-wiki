# Granular Dynamics

[[concepts/particle-dynamics|Granular dynamics]] is the study of how collections of discrete particles, such as sand, gravel, or powders, behave and evolve under physical forces. Unlike fluids or continuous solids, [[concepts/granular-materials|granular materials]] exhibit unique mechanical properties that emerge from the interactions between individual grains. These materials are ubiquitous in nature and industry, appearing in geological formations, avalanches, industrial processing, and construction materials.

A central concept in this field is the [[concepts/angle-of-repose|angle of repose]], which represents the steepest angle of descent or dip relative to the horizontal plane to which a material can be piled without slumping. This phenomenon arises from the balance between gravitational forces pulling the particles downward and the frictional forces resisting their [[concepts/exercise|movement]]. The angle varies depending on the material's shape, size distribution, and surface [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]], serving as a critical parameter in predicting stability in slopes and hoppers.

Research in granular dynamics also explores how these systems self-organize and settle under vibration or shear. Granular materials can display both solid-like and fluid-like behaviors depending on the energy input and [[concepts/density|density]] of the packing. Understanding these transitions is essential for applications ranging from the design of silos and conveyors to the analysis of landslides and planetary regolith dynamics.
