# Karaage Recipe

[[concepts/japanese-fried-chicken|Karaage]] (唐揚げ) is a Japanese [[concepts/style|style]] of fried chicken consisting of bite-sized, boneless pieces marinated in a savory liquid before frying. The marinade typically includes soy sauce, sake, ginger, and garlic, which infuse the meat with umami and aromatic flavors. This [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] method distinguishes karaage from Western fried chicken traditions, as the seasoning penetrates the meat rather than relying primarily on a seasoned coating. The result is tender chicken with well-developed flavor throughout.

## Preparation

To prepare karaage, chicken thighs or breasts are cut into bite-sized pieces and submerged in the marinade for several hours or overnight. The meat is then coated in potato starch or wheat flour, which creates a [[concepts/light|light]], crispy exterior when fried. Traditional recipes often use potato starch to achieve a distinctively crunchy [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]] that holds up well against the moist interior.

## Serving and Variations

Karaage is commonly served hot, often accompanied by a squeeze of lemon juice or a dipping sauce such as tare or mayonnaise. It is a staple in Japanese home cooking and izakaya menus, frequently appearing as part of bento boxes or alongside rice and [[entities/miso-labs|miso]] soup. [[concepts/regional-differences|Regional variations]] may incorporate different ingredients into the marinade, such as mirin, rice vinegar, or shochu, allowing for slight [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] in sweetness and acidity.
