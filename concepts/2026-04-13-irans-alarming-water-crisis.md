# 2026 04 13 Iran's Alarming Water Crisis

Iran confronts a severe water shortage driven by its inherently arid geography combined with climate pressures. The country receives approximately 250 millimeters of annual rainfall on average—less than one-quarter of the global mean of 990 millimeters. Precipitation is distributed unevenly across seasons and regions, with winter months accounting for the majority of rainfall, which concentrates primarily in mountainous areas. This geographic reality leaves vast portions of Iran's territory with limited freshwater availability.

## Climate and Environmental Factors

Rising temperatures have intensified Iran's water stress by accelerating evaporation from lakes, rivers, and reservoirs while reducing seasonal snowmelt that traditionally contributed to spring water flows. This trend reflects broader climate change impacts on precipitation patterns and hydrological cycles. Groundwater reserves, historically critical to Iranian agriculture and urban water supplies, have experienced declining levels as extraction rates have exceeded natural recharge.

## Sectoral and Social Impacts

The water crisis carries significant consequences for agriculture, which traditionally consumes the majority of Iran's water resources, and for urban populations dependent on reliable freshwater supplies. Competition for scarce water resources has intensified across regions, creating tensions between agricultural, industrial, and domestic water demands. Rural communities and farming areas have experienced particular strain, with some regions facing restrictions on irrigation and livestock watering.
