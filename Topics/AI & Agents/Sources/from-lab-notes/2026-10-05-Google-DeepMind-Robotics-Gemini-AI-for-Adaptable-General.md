## Google DeepMind Robotics: Gemini AI for Adaptable, General-Purpose Robots
**Clip title:** [[concepts/2026-04-29-google-deepmind|Google DeepMind]] [[concepts/robotics|robotics]] lab tour with Hannah Fry
**[[entities/tasia-custode|Author]] / channel:** Google DeepMind
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UALxgn1MnZo

### Summary
This video provides an insightful tour into [[concepts/2026-04-29-google-deepmind|Google DeepMind]]'s [[concepts/robotics|robotics]] [[entities/labs|labs]], showcasing the significant advancements made in integrating [[concepts/multimodal-language-models|multimodal AI]], particularly Google's [[concepts/gemini|Gemini]], into physical robots. Hosted by Professor [[entities/hannah-fry|Hannah Fry]] and guided by [[entities/kanishka-rao|Kanishka Rao]], Director of Robotics, the central theme revolves around moving beyond rigidly pre-programmed robots to create open-ended, adaptable machines capable of understanding human [[concepts/instructions|instructions]] and flexibly responding to an unlimited array of tasks in the real [[entities/earth|world]]. This represents a fundamental shift in robotics, allowing for far greater generality and utility.

Rao highlights the rapid progress over the past four years, emphasizing that robots are now trained with much more robust visual backbones, making them less sensitive to environmental factors like lighting. A core breakthrough discussed is the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which embed physical actions on the same footing as visual and language [[concepts/tokens|tokens]]. This enables "action [[concepts/abstraction|generalization]]," allowing robots to plan and execute complex, [[concepts/long-horizon-tasks|long-horizon tasks]]. Furthermore, the latest [[concepts/iteration|iteration]], version 1.5, incorporates a "[[concepts/human-cognition|thinking]] component," wherein the robot generates [[concepts/internal-thoughts|internal thoughts]] before performing actions, akin to a [[concepts/multi-step-reasoning|chain-of-thought]] process in [[concepts/demystifying-llms|large language models]]. This internal deliberation significantly improves the robot's ability to generalize and perform tasks more effectively.

The video features several compelling demonstrations of these capabilities. Aloha robots are shown meticulously packing a lunchbox, demonstrating dexterity in handling delicate items like grapes and performing [[concepts/multi-step-tasks|multi-step tasks]], though still prone to occasional errors. Another robot, guided by a general policy layer built on [[concepts/gemini|Gemini]], successfully sorts colored blocks into designated trays based on spoken [[concepts/instructions|instructions]], even humorously declining a request to perform "as Batman." A particularly challenging demo involves a robot placing a squishy, novel object (a pink blob) inside a pear-shaped container, illustrating its ability to adapt to new objects and solve complex manipulation problems. The humanoid robot, Apptronik, further exemplifies end-to-end [[concepts/learning|learning]] by sorting laundry and even displaying its internal "thoughts" on a screen as it operates.

The progress demonstrated is astounding, marking a level of capability that was "completely inconceivable just a few years ago." Despite the robots sometimes being slow or making minor errors, the underlying ability to understand context, [[concepts/purpose|reason]] through tasks, and adapt to unforeseen situations is revolutionary. The primary limiting factor for further advancement, as emphasized, is the sheer quantity of real-[[entities/earth|world]] physical interaction data available for training. However, the researchers are optimistic that once this data barrier is overcome, potentially by leveraging the vast amounts of human activity captured in online videos, the world could be on the cusp of a genuine robot [[concepts/revolution|revolution]].
