# Meat Juiciness

Meat juiciness refers to the perceived moisture and tenderness of cooked meat, which is influenced by how well the meat retains its natural liquids during cooking. This quality is important for both palatability and the overall eating experience, as juicy meat is generally perceived as more flavorful and tender than dry meat. Juiciness depends on factors including the meat's initial moisture content, cooking temperature, cooking duration, and how the meat is prepared before cooking.

## Baking Soda and Texture Enhancement

Baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) is commonly used as a meat tenderizer and moisture retainer, particularly in ground meat preparations. When applied to ground meat before cooking, baking soda raises the pH of the meat's surface, which allows the proteins to denature and retain more moisture during the cooking process. This chemical change results in meat that remains juicier throughout cooking while also developing improved browning through the Maillard reaction, the chemical process responsible for flavorful, browned surfaces on cooked foods. The effect is typically achieved by coating ground meat with a small amount of baking soda (roughly 1/4 teaspoon per pound) and allowing it to sit for 15 minutes before cooking.
