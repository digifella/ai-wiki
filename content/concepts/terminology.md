# Terminology

Terminology in [[concepts/community-health-nursing|community health nursing]] refers to the standardized vocabulary and language used by nurses and [[concepts/health|healthcare]] professionals to describe conditions, interventions, patient outcomes, and care settings. Consistent and accurate use of terminology is essential for clear communication among healthcare team members, accurate documentation, and the ability to aggregate and analyze data across communities and populations. In community health nursing practice, terminology spans clinical descriptors, epidemiological measures, and organizational concepts specific to population-based care delivery.

## Standard Classification Systems

[[concepts/population-health|Community health]] nurses regularly use established classification and [[concepts/coding|coding]] systems to document and communicate about patient care. These include diagnostic nomenclature, procedure codes, and standardized nursing language such as NANDA nursing diagnoses, NIC (Nursing Interventions Classification), and NOC (Nursing Outcomes Classification). Additionally, nurses use epidemiological terminology to describe disease patterns, risk factors, and health determinants at the population level. Familiarity with these systems ensures interoperability between different health information systems and enables nurses to contribute meaningfully to quality improvement and public health surveillance efforts.

## Contextual Language in Population Health

Beyond clinical terminology, community health nursing employs specific language related to social [[concepts/determinants-of-health|determinants of health]], health equity, and community-based interventions. Terms such as [[concepts/vulnerable-populations|vulnerable populations]], health disparities, community assets, and upstream [[concepts/preventive-care|prevention]] reflect the distinctive focus of community nursing on systemic and environmental influences on health. Precision in this terminology helps nurses identify root causes of health problems and design interventions that address underlying social and structural factors rather than only individual-level risk factors.
