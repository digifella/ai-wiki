# Start With Why

**Start With Why** is a leadership and communication framework developed by Simon Sinek that prioritizes articulating organizational purpose before discussing strategy or tactics. The framework gained significant public attention following Sinek's 2009 TEDx talk "How Great Leaders Inspire Action," which became one of the most-watched TED presentations. The core premise holds that organizations and individuals achieve greater success and build stronger loyalty by first communicating their fundamental purpose—the deeper reason they exist beyond profit or product delivery.

## The Golden Circle

The model is visually represented by three concentric circles known as the Golden Circle. The outermost circle represents "What," describing the products or services an organization offers. The middle circle represents "How," detailing the processes or values that differentiate the organization from competitors. The innermost circle represents "Why," defining the core purpose, cause, or belief that drives the organization. Sinek argues that most organizations communicate from the outside in (What to How to Why), whereas inspired leaders and organizations communicate from the inside out (Why to How to What).

## Biological Basis

Sinek links the framework to human biology, specifically the structure of the brain. He posits that the "Why" corresponds to the limbic system, which controls feelings such as trust and loyalty, while the "What" corresponds to the neocortex, responsible for rational thought and language. Because the limbic brain lacks the capacity for language, it communicates through emotions and feelings rather than words. This biological distinction explains why people buy not just what a company does, but why it does it, as decisions are often driven by feeling before they are justified by logic.

## Application and Criticism

The framework has been widely adopted in business strategy, marketing, and leadership training to help organizations clarify their mission and align their actions with their stated values. Critics argue that the model oversimplifies complex organizational dynamics and that the distinction between the circles can be difficult to maintain in practice. Despite this, the concept remains influential in discussions regarding corporate culture and authentic leadership, emphasizing that clarity of purpose is a prerequisite for sustainable growth and employee engagement.
