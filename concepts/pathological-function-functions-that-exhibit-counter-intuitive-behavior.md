# Pathological Function Functions That Exhibit Counter Intuitive Behavior

In mathematical analysis, pathological functions are those that defy standard intuition derived from continuous or differentiable functions. These functions often serve as counterexamples to general conjectures, demonstrating that properties such as continuity, differentiability, or integrability do not always behave as expected. Common examples include the Weierstrass function, which is continuous everywhere but differentiable nowhere, and the Dirichlet function, which is discontinuous everywhere.

The specific association with base 13 representation suggests a focus on functions defined by digit properties in non-standard bases. In this context, pathological behavior may arise from the irregular distribution of digits or specific arithmetic properties unique to the thirteenth base. Such functions are often studied to understand the limits of number-theoretic functions and their continuity or differentiability across different radix systems.

Research into these functions typically involves rigorous proof techniques to establish their anomalous properties. By examining how digit-based definitions interact with topological structures, mathematicians can identify cases where intuitive expectations fail. This area of study highlights the complexity of real analysis and the necessity of precise definitions in handling functions with erratic behavior.
