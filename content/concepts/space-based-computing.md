# Space Based Computing

Space-based computing refers to the [[concepts/deployment|deployment]] of computational infrastructure, particularly [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] data centers, in orbital and extraterrestrial locations rather than terrestrial facilities. This concept addresses fundamental constraints of ground-based computing, including [[concepts/heat-dissipation|thermal dissipation]] limitations, electromagnetic interference, and the finite availability of suitable locations for large-scale server installations. By situating data centers in the [[concepts/vacuum-of-space|space environment]], operators could potentially leverage the vacuum as a cooling [[entities/medium|medium]] and access regions with minimal atmospheric obstruction.

## Engineering Requirements

Deploying functional [[concepts/computing-infrastructure|computing infrastructure]] in space presents substantial technical challenges. [[concepts/hardware|Hardware]] must withstand radiation [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], thermal cycling, and microgravity conditions while maintaining [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] over extended operational periods. Power generation in orbit typically relies on solar arrays or advanced nuclear systems, creating dependencies on consistent energy availability. Data transmission to and from orbital facilities requires robust communication infrastructure with sufficient bandwidth to justify the operational complexity, particularly for real-time AI processing tasks that demand low-latency connectivity.

## Feasibility and Economics

The economic viability of space-based computing depends on cost reductions in launch services, orbital maintenance capabilities, and long-term [[concepts/cost|operational efficiency]] gains. Current launch costs create significant barriers to deployment at scale, though emerging reusable launch technologies may alter this calculation. The advantages of enhanced cooling efficiency and freedom from terrestrial geographic constraints must be weighed against increased complexity in system management, repair, and eventual deorbiting. Integration with terrestrial networks and compatibility with existing [[concepts/computing-architecture|AI infrastructure]] frameworks remains a critical prerequisite for practical implementation.
