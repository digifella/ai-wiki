# Bmj

The British Medical Journal (BMJ) is a peer-reviewed medical journal founded in 1840 and published by the BMJ Publishing Group. It is recognized as one of the most widely read medical journals globally, serving a diverse readership that includes healthcare professionals, researchers, policymakers, and medical students. The publication maintains editorial independence and utilizes a hybrid model, operating under both subscription and open-access frameworks to ensure broad distribution of its content.

The journal publishes original research, systematic reviews, editorials, and analysis across a wide range of medical specialties. Its scope extends beyond clinical findings to include policy analysis and medical education, aiming to influence both practice and public health strategy. By combining rigorous peer review with accessible formats, BMJ seeks to bridge the gap between scientific evidence and practical application in healthcare settings.

Over its long history, the BMJ has established itself as a key voice in medical discourse. It continues to adapt to changes in the publishing landscape while adhering to its founding principles of integrity and independence. The journal remains a critical resource for those involved in the delivery, study, and administration of healthcare systems worldwide.
