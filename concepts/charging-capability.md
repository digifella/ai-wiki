# Charging Capability

Charging capability refers to the technical specifications that determine how much electrical power a cable, charger, or device can safely deliver or receive. These specifications include voltage (measured in volts), current (measured in amperes), and power output (measured in watts). USB-C connectors support a wider range of power delivery than earlier USB standards, theoretically enabling faster charging for compatible devices.

## The USB-C Standard and Power Delivery

The USB-C specification allows for power delivery up to 240 watts in its latest iterations, a significant increase from previous USB connector types. However, the standard itself does not guarantee that all USB-C cables or devices support these maximum capabilities. Individual cables and devices are rated for specific power levels, and charging speed depends on both the charger's output and the device's maximum input capacity—whichever is lower.

## Sources of Confusion

The broad range of possible USB-C implementations has created widespread confusion in the market. Not all USB-C cables support high-power charging; some are limited to lower wattages or specific voltage configurations. Manufacturers are not always consistent in labeling cable specifications, and devices may support different charging speeds depending on the charger used. Consumers often cannot easily determine whether a given cable or charger meets their device's requirements without consulting detailed specifications or manufacturer documentation.
