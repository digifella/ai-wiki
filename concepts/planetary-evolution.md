# Planetary Evolution

The process by which planets change over time through geological, atmospheric, and biological [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]]. Key drivers include internal heat, [[concepts/solar-energy|solar radiation]], impact events, and chemical differentiation.

## Mars: A Case Study in Stalled and Resurgent Evolution

Recent data challenges the view of [[concepts/mars|Mars]] as a static, dead [[entities/earth|world]], highlighting complex interactions between its [[concepts/geology|geology]], [[concepts/earths-atmosphere|atmosphere]], and potential biosignatures.

### Key Discoveries (2026)
- **Geological Activity:** Evidence of recent tectonic or [[concepts/volcanic-eruption|volcanic activity]] suggests Mars retains significant internal heat, contradicting earlier models of rapid cooling.
- **Atmospheric Dynamics:** Observations of an "[[concepts/expanding-shadow|expanding shadow]]" indicate complex [[concepts/atmospheric-dynamics|atmospheric circulation]] patterns and potential seasonal variations in atmospheric [[concepts/density|density]] or [[concepts/writing|composition]].
- **Organic Precursors:** Detection of [[concepts/organic-life-precursors|organic life precursors]] in surface and subsurface samples points to prebiotic chemistry potentially active in Mars' recent past or present.
- **Time Dilation Effects:** Localized relativistic effects due to Mars' lower mass and gravitational potential result in time running slightly faster compared to Earth, a factor in precise orbital mechanics and surface operations.

### Implications for Planetary Evolution
- Mars serves as a critical comparative planet for understanding the divergence of terrestrial planet evolution paths.
- The presence of organic precursors raises questions about the [[concepts/data-persistence|persistence]] of prebiotic conditions and the potential for extant or extinct microbial life.
- Dynamic atmospheric and geological processes suggest that planetary evolution is not strictly linear or unidirectional.
