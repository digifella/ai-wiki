# Intelligence Measurement

**Intelligence Measurement** refers to the methodologies and metrics used to quantify cognitive capabilities, [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]], and [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] abilities in both biological and artificial systems. In the context of [[concepts/ai-technologies|Artificial Intelligence]], this field [[concepts/faces|faces]] significant challenges regarding the validity of proxies for true understanding.

## Key Challenges & Critiques

- **Proxy Failure**: Traditional metrics often serve as imperfect proxies for [[concepts/true-intelligence|general intelligence]], leading to optimization errors where systems maximize the metric without improving the underlying capability.
- **Token vs. Intelligence**: A critical distinction exists between computational throughput (token generation/consumption) and actual cognitive depth.
	- Recent analysis highlights a "billion-dollar mistake" in the [[concepts/ai-industry|AI industry]]: the excessive focus on [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]] token volume rather than verifying genuine intelligence or [[concepts/productivity|productive output]] [[lab-notes/2026-07-11-Goodharts-Law-in-AI-The-Cost-of-Confusing-Tokens-with-In|Goodhart's Law in AI: The Cost of Confusing Tokens with Intelligence]].
	- This misalignment exemplifies [[concepts/perverse-incentives|Goodhart's Law]], where a measure ceases to be a good measure once it becomes a target.
