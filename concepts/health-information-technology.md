# Health Information Technology

**[[concepts/health|Health]] Information Technology (HIT)** refers to the application of information processing involving both computers and telecommunications to [[concepts/health-care|health care]]. It encompasses the systems, technologies, and tools used to create, store, manage, and exchange health information.

## History & Scope
HIT has been integrated into the health care industry since the 1960s, initially leveraging mainframes for financial transactions. Over decades, it has evolved to include [[concepts/electronic-health-record|Electronic Health Record]] (EHR) systems, clinical decision support, and [[concepts/telehealth|telehealth]] infrastructure, aiming to improve [[concepts/roles-in-community-settings|patient care]], reduce costs, and enhance [[concepts/population-health|population health]].

## Impact on Medical Outcomes
Research indicates a trajectory of positive impact on treatment outcomes, though early [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] faced scrutiny regarding efficacy.

*   [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Kruse---Health-Information-Technology-Continues-to-Show|Kruse - Health Information Technology Continues to Show Positive Effect]] highlights continuing positive effects of HIT on [[concepts/medical-outcomes|medical outcomes]].
*   Systematic reviews suggest that HIT contributes to improved **treatment outcome** metrics when implemented effectively.
*   Key components driving these improvements include robust [[concepts/wallet|Electronic Health Record]] integration and data interoperability.
