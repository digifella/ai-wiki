# Radiation Damage

Radiation damage refers to the harmful effects caused by [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] to ionizing radiation on matter, biological systems, and equipment. In astrophysical contexts, radiation damage is particularly relevant to observations of high-energy [[concepts/cosmic-rays|cosmic rays]] and their interactions with Earth's atmosphere and technological systems. The Large High Altitude Air Shower Observatory (LHAASO) studies [[concepts/high-energy-particles|cosmic ray sources]] and their properties, which directly relates to understanding radiation phenomena originating from the most energetic objects in the universe.

## Cosmic Ray Sources and LHAASO

LHAASO is an observatory designed to detect and analyze ultra-high-energy cosmic rays and [[concepts/gamma-rays|gamma rays]] from their sources. The observatory's observations have contributed to identifying some of the most powerful cosmic ray sources in the universe. Understanding these sources is essential for comprehending the mechanisms that produce and accelerate particles to extreme energies, which in turn helps scientists understand the radiation environment in space and near Earth.

## Implications for Technology and Science

The study of radiation damage becomes important when considering the effects of cosmic rays on satellite systems, spacecraft, and ground-based instruments. High-energy particles can cause degradation in [[concepts/electronic-components|electronic components]] and pose challenges for long-duration space missions. By mapping cosmic ray sources and their characteristics through facilities like LHAASO, researchers can better predict and mitigate radiation hazards for future [[concepts/space-exploration|space exploration]] and technological infrastructure.
