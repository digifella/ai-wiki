# Tiered Pricing

Tiered pricing is a business model in which a company offers multiple versions of a product or service at different price points, each with varying features, capabilities, or usage limits. This structure allows customers to select a plan that matches their needs and budget, while enabling providers to capture value across different market segments. Tiered pricing is commonly used in software-as-a-service (SaaS) products, cloud services, and digital platforms where marginal costs of serving additional users are minimal.

## Structure and Implementation

Typical tiered pricing models are organized into distinct levels, such as free, basic, standard, and premium. Each tier usually restricts specific functionalities, storage capacity, or usage frequency to incentivize upgrades. For example, a free tier may offer limited access to core features, while higher tiers unlock advanced tools, priority support, or increased quotas. This segmentation helps companies address diverse customer bases, from casual users to enterprise clients, by aligning cost with perceived value.

## User Reactions and Migration

Changes to tiered pricing structures often trigger significant user response, particularly when existing plans are altered or discontinued. In the context of Evernote’s pricing evolution, users experienced varied reactions to the introduction of new tiers and the migration of legacy accounts. Migration guidance typically involves communicating the benefits of new plans, outlining the loss of access to previous features, and providing clear pathways for existing subscribers to transition. Effective communication during these periods is critical to maintaining user trust and minimizing churn.
