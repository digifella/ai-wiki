# Landscape Photography

Landscape photography is the practice of capturing expansive natural or built environments with emphasis on wide vistas, spatial depth, and environmental context. The discipline encompasses diverse subjects including mountains, coastlines, forests, deserts, and urban skylines. Photographers working in this genre employ composition techniques specifically designed to convey scale, atmospheric conditions, and the spatial relationships between foreground, middle ground, and background elements. Traditional approaches rely on single exposures captured with wide-angle lenses, which inherently limit the horizontal field of view to the capabilities of the camera's sensor.

## Panoramic Stitching

Panoramic stitching extends the horizontal range of landscape photography beyond what standard single-frame capture allows. This technique involves taking multiple overlapping photographs across a scene, then digitally aligning and blending them to create a cohesive wide image. The resulting panorama can capture fields of view significantly broader than any individual lens, often exceeding 120 degrees horizontally while maintaining high resolution.

Panoramic stitching can be executed using dedicated camera functions, computer software, or smartphone applications. While many modern smartphones include built-in panorama modes, these typically apply automated algorithms with limited user control. Advanced panoramic stitching, particularly for professional landscape work, often employs specialized software that allows photographers to manually control alignment, exposure blending, and color correction across multiple frames. This approach proves especially valuable for capturing dramatic landscapes where the full environmental context cannot be conveyed within a standard frame dimensions.
