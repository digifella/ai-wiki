# Musical Scales

A musical scale is an ordered sequence of pitches arranged in ascending or descending order within an octave. Scales form the foundational framework for [[concepts/melody|melody]], [[concepts/musical-harmony|harmony]], and [[concepts/writing|composition]] across virtually all musical traditions. By defining which [[concepts/notes|notes]] sound consonant or natural together within a given key, scales constrain the [[entities/pitch|pitch]] choices available to musicians and composers, providing structure and coherence to musical works.

## Major and Minor Scales

The major scale and minor scale are the most prevalent scale types in Western music. The major scale follows a specific pattern of whole and half steps that produces a bright, resolved quality. The natural minor scale follows a different intervallic pattern, creating a darker or more introspective character. Variations of the minor scale, including harmonic minor and melodic minor, offer different harmonic or melodic characteristics and are used strategically in composition.

## Other Scale Systems

Beyond Western major and minor systems, numerous other scales exist across musical cultures and contexts. The pentatonic scale, containing five notes, appears frequently in folk and popular music traditions. Modal scales—including Dorian, Phrygian, and Lydian modes—[[concepts/provide-alternative|provide alternative]] tonal centers and intervallic [[concepts/relationships|relationships]]. Non-Western traditions employ entirely different scale systems, such as ragas in Indian classical music or maqam scales in Arabic music, each with distinct aesthetic and functional properties.

## Function in Music

Scales serve multiple practical purposes in musical practice. They establish the tonal center or key of a piece, guiding both melodic construction and harmonic progression. Understanding [[concepts/scale-relationships|scale relationships]] helps musicians and composers make informed choices about which notes and chords [[entities/will|will]] work together, enabling the creation of coherence while also allowing for intentional deviation and tension when desired.
