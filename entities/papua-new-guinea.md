# Papua New Guinea

Papua New Guinea is a sovereign nation occupying the eastern half of the island of New Guinea in Oceania, sharing the landmass with Indonesia to the west. Situated in the southwestern Pacific Ocean, the country features rugged mountainous terrain, extensive tropical rainforests, and high geographical diversity. It gained independence from Australia in 1975 and remains a member of the Commonwealth of Nations. The nation is renowned for its profound linguistic and cultural diversity, with hundreds of distinct languages spoken across its population.

## Kuru and Medical Significance

The country holds particular medical significance due to the historical prevalence of Kuru, a rare and fatal neurodegenerative disease among the Fore people of the Eastern Highlands. The study of Kuru in this region was pivotal in validating the Protein World Hypothesis, specifically regarding the role of prions in disease transmission. Research conducted by Carleton Gajdusek and others demonstrated that Kuru was transmitted through ritualistic endocannibalism, leading to the identification of prion proteins as the infectious agents. This discovery fundamentally changed the understanding of transmissible spongiform encephalopathies and earned Gajdusek the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1976.
