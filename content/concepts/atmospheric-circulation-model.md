# Atmospheric Circulation Model

An atmospheric circulation model is a mathematical and computational representation of how air moves across large [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] in Earth's atmosphere. These models simulate wind patterns, pressure systems, and heat [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] driven by solar radiation and planetary rotation. They form the foundation of [[concepts/weather-forecasting|weather prediction]], climate research, and understanding global climate patterns including trade winds, jet streams, and monsoon systems.

## Physical Foundation

Atmospheric circulation models are built on principles of [[concepts/fluid-dynamics|fluid dynamics]], thermodynamics, and conservation laws. They calculate the motion of air parcels using the Navier-Stokes equations, accounting for pressure gradients, the Coriolis effect, [[concepts/friction|friction]], and heating from solar radiation. The models integrate these equations over discretized three-dimensional grids of the atmosphere, with varying spatial and temporal resolution depending on the application and available [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]].

## Types and Applications

General circulation models (GCMs) represent the most comprehensive category, simulating global atmospheric behavior on timescales from days to centuries. Simpler models may focus on specific phenomena or regional scales. These tools are essential for numerical weather prediction, climate projections, and understanding atmospheric processes ranging from local convection to planetary-scale circulation patterns. Validation against [[concepts/empirical-evidence|observational data]] and controlled experiments—such as studies conducted in facilities like [[concepts/closed-ecosystem|Biosphere 2]]—helps refine model [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]] and identify limitations in their physical representations.
