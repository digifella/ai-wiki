# Greenhouse Gases

Greenhouse gases are atmospheric compounds that absorb and re-emit infrared radiation, effectively trapping heat within Earth's atmosphere. This natural phenomenon, known as the greenhouse effect, is critical for sustaining life by maintaining surface temperatures that are habitable for biological processes. Without this mechanism, Earth's average temperature would be approximately 60 degrees Fahrenheit colder than current conditions.

## Primary Constituents

The primary constituents of this category include water vapor, carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), and nitrous oxide (N₂O). Water vapor acts as the most abundant greenhouse gas, though its concentration is primarily controlled by temperature-dependent feedback loops rather than direct emissions. Carbon dioxide is the most significant long-lived greenhouse gas, largely driven by human activities such as fossil fuel combustion and deforestation. Methane and nitrous oxide are potent heat-trapping gases with shorter atmospheric lifetimes but higher global warming potential per molecule compared to carbon dioxide.

## Climate and Health Impacts

The accumulation of these gases due to anthropogenic emissions enhances the natural greenhouse effect, leading to global warming and climate change. This shift in atmospheric composition alters weather patterns, sea levels, and ecosystem stability. Additionally, the processes that release greenhouse gases often coincide with the emission of pollutants that degrade air quality. Consequently, high concentrations of greenhouse gases are frequently referenced in relation to unhealthy air quality impacts, as many sources of carbon dioxide and methane also emit particulate matter and other harmful substances that affect human respiratory health.
