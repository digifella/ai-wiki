# Neural Networks Nns

Neural networks are computational systems inspired by biological neural structures found in animal brains. They consist of interconnected nodes, known as artificial neurons, organized in layers that process information through weighted connections. Each node receives inputs, applies a mathematical function, and passes outputs to the next layer. This layered architecture enables neural networks to learn complex patterns from data through a training process in which the network adjusts its internal weights to minimize prediction errors.

The architecture typically comprises an input layer, one or more hidden layers, and an output layer. The input layer receives raw data, while the hidden layers perform intermediate computations using activation functions to introduce non-linearity. The output layer generates the final result, such as a classification or regression value. The depth and width of these layers determine the model's capacity to approximate complex functions, a concept often referred to as universal approximation.

In the context of AI agents, efficiency in neural network operations is critical for real-time decision-making. A relevant development involves a Tesla AI patent that addresses computational bottlenecks in neural network inference. This patent describes a method for converting multiplication operations into addition operations, thereby reducing the computational load and energy consumption required for processing. This breakthrough aims to optimize the performance of neural networks within autonomous systems and other resource-constrained AI applications.
