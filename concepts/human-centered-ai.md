# Human-Centered AI

**Human-Centered AI (HCAI)** is a design and research [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]] that prioritizes human needs, values, and capabilities in the development and deployment of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems. It emphasizes **collaboration** between humans and machines rather than full automation, ensuring AI augments [[concepts/human-intelligence|human intelligence]] rather than replacing it.

## Core Principles

- **Human Agency & Oversight**: Humans retain control over critical decisions; AI serves as a tool for augmentation Human-in-the-[[concepts/loop|Loop]].
- **Ethical Design**: Systems must be transparent, [[entities/meta-fair-lab|fair]], and accountable, adhering to [[concepts/responsible-ai-use|Responsible AI]] frameworks.
- **[[concepts/contextual-awareness|Contextual Awareness]]**: AI solutions are tailored to specific human contexts, respecting cultural and social nuances.
- **Inclusivity**: Design processes engage diverse stakeholders to mitigate bias and ensure [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]].

## Relationship with Organizational Dynamics

HCAI extends beyond technical implementation into **organizational behavior**. The [[concepts/success|success]] of [[concepts/ai-integration|AI integration]] depends heavily on how teams are structured and how [[concepts/trust|trust]] is established among human operators and between humans and [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]].

- **Trust Calibration**: Effective HCAI requires balancing user trust—avoiding both over-reliance and under-utilization of AI [[concepts/recommendations|recommendations]].
- **Team [[concepts/psychology|Psychology]]**: Insights from organizational research inform how [[concepts/ai-tools|AI tools]] should be introduced to minimize disruption and maximize [[concepts/adoption|adoption]].
	- See [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Project-Aristotle-Implications-and-Challenges|Project Aristotle: Implications and Challenges]] for analysis on how [[concepts/psychological-safety|psychological safety]] and [[concepts/team-composition|team structure]] impact the adoption of automated decision-support systems.

## Key Challenges

- **Algorithmic Bias**: Perpetuating existing societal inequalities if [[concepts/language-data|training data]] is skewed.
- **Explainability**: The "black box" problem hinders human oversight and [[concepts/accountability|accountability]].
- **[[concepts/skill|Skill]] Gap**: Workforce needs reskilling to collaborate effectively with [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]].
