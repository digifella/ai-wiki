# Hazy Backgrounds

Hazy backgrounds occur when atmospheric conditions reduce [[concepts/clarity-slider|clarity]] and [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] in the background elements of a photograph. This phenomenon happens when [[concepts/light|light]] scatters through particles suspended in the air, such as dust, moisture, pollution, or salt spray. These particles weaken the distinction between tones and details, resulting in a washed-out, flat [[concepts/presence|appearance]] that diminishes the visual impact of the image. This effect is particularly prevalent in outdoor photography and environmental conditions characterized by poor visibility.

The issue is most common in landscapes photographed in humid environments or areas with significant atmospheric [[concepts/granular-matter|particulate matter]]. The scattering of light reduces the saturation and [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]] of distant objects, making them appear lighter and less defined than they are in reality. This loss of depth and contrast can obscure important compositional elements, requiring post-processing intervention to restore the intended [[concepts/hierarchy|visual hierarchy]] of the scene.

To address this, tutorials such as those from [[entities/phlearn|PHLEARN]] demonstrate the use of the [[entities/adobe|Photoshop 2026]] [[concepts/subject-selection|Object Selection]] tool for precise dehazing. This method allows for targeted correction of background elements without affecting the foreground subject. By isolating the hazy areas, editors can apply [[concepts/masking-tools|localized adjustments]] to enhance contrast and saturation, effectively restoring clarity and depth to the background while maintaining the [[concepts/honesty|integrity]] of the overall [[concepts/combining-photos|image composition]].
