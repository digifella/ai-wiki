# Gravitational Mass

Gravitational mass is the property of matter that determines the strength of gravitational attraction it exerts on other objects. It is fundamentally defined by how an object responds to gravitational fields and how it generates them. In classical [[concepts/physics|physics]], gravitational mass is equivalent to inertial mass—the resistance an object presents to acceleration—a relationship codified in Einstein's equivalence principle and central to general relativity.

## Observational Challenges in Large-Scale Structures

Measurements of gravitational mass in [[concepts/galaxy-clusters|galaxy clusters]] have revealed significant discrepancies between predicted and observed values. When astronomers calculate the total mass of galaxy clusters based on visible matter alone, the [[concepts/gravitational-effects|gravitational effects]] observed—including rotation curves and lensing patterns—suggest substantially more mass is present than can be accounted for by [[concepts/baryonic-matter|baryonic matter]] (ordinary atoms and molecules). This has led to the widespread conclusion that [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]], a form of matter that does not interact with [[concepts/light|electromagnetic radiation]], comprises the majority of gravitational mass in such structures.

Recent findings have raised questions about whether all baryonic matter contributions in galaxy clusters have been properly accounted for. Some analyses suggest that previously underestimated quantities of ordinary matter may explain some discrepancies, though this remains an active area of investigation. The precise [[entities/nature|nature]] and [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] of both visible and dark matter in galaxy clusters continues to challenge our understanding of gravitational mass on cosmic [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]].
