# Layer Masks

Layer masks are a fundamental technique in [[concepts/photoshop|Photoshop]] that allow [[concepts/non-destructive-editing|non-destructive editing]] by controlling the visibility of specific areas of a layer. Rather than permanently deleting or erasing content, a layer mask uses grayscale values to determine transparency—white areas remain visible, black areas are hidden, and gray areas create partial transparency. This approach preserves the original image data while providing flexible [[concepts/power|control]] over what appears in the final composition.

## Practical Applications

A common application of layer masks is combining elements from multiple photographs. For instance, in group photography, it is often difficult to capture a single shot where all subjects have their eyes open and are smiling. Layer masks enable photographers to seamlessly blend the best versions of several similar [[concepts/images|images]]—taking a properly exposed face from one photo and combining it with a better expression from another. Similarly, layer masks can be used to correct other specific details, blend exposures, or create composite images by selectively revealing or hiding portions of different layers.

The technique is non-destructive, meaning [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] can be refined or reversed [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] any point without losing original image information. This makes layer masks an essential tool for professional [[concepts/photo-editing|photo editing]] and digital [[concepts/art|art]], offering greater creative control and flexibility than permanent deletion methods.
