# Selective Extraction

**Selective extraction** is a [[concepts/chemical-engineering|chemical engineering]] and separation process technique used to isolate specific components from a mixture while leaving others behind. It relies on differences in solubility, volatility, or affinity for a specific [[entities/medium|medium]] (solid, liquid, or gas) to achieve separation.

## Core Principles
- **Solubility Differential**: Exploiting the varying solubility of target compounds in specific solvents.
- **[[concepts/phase|Phase]] Transfer**: Moving a target compound from one phase (e.g., solid matrix) to another (e.g., liquid solvent) for easier removal.
- **Selectivity**: The ratio of the distribution coefficients of the target component versus impurities; high selectivity minimizes loss of desired non-target components.

## Applications in Food and Beverage
Selective extraction is critical in modifying the chemical profile of consumables without destroying their structural [[concepts/honesty|integrity]] or flavor base.

- **[[concepts/caffeine-removal|Decaffeination]]**: The removal of caffeine from coffee beans or tea leaves.
	- Methods include direct solvent extraction (using dichloromethane or ethyl acetate), indirect solvent extraction, and supercritical carbon dioxide extraction.
	- See detailed historical and scientific overview in [[lab-notes/2026-06-24-Decaffeination-and-De-alcoholization-Processes-History-a|Decaffeination and De-alcoholization Processes: History and Scientific Methods]].
- **[[concepts/ethanol-removal|De-alcoholization]]**: The removal of ethanol from alcoholic beverages to produce non-alcoholic alternatives.
	- Techniques often involve [[concepts/vacuum-distillation|vacuum distillation]] to lower the boiling point of alcohol, preserving volatile aroma compounds that would otherwise be lost at higher temperatures.
	- [[concepts/membrane-filtration|Membrane filtration]] and [[concepts/desalination-technology|reverse osmosis]] are also employed to separate alcohol from water and flavor compounds.
