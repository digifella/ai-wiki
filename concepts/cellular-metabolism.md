# Cellular Metabolism

**Cellular [[concepts/yeast-fermentation|metabolism]]** refers to the sum of all chemical reactions that occur within a cell to maintain life. It encompasses **catabolism** (breaking down molecules to obtain energy) and **anabolism** (synthesizing all compounds needed for cellular function).

## Core Components

*   **Energy Production:** Primarily driven by ATP synthesis via cellular respiration.
*   **Metabolic Pathways:** Includes glycolysis, Krebs cycle, and electron transport chain.
*   **[[concepts/regulation|Regulation]]:** Controlled by enzymes, hormones, and [[concepts/systems|feedback loops]].

## Mitochondrial Role & Emerging Research

While traditionally defined as the "powerhouse of the cell" due to ATP production, recent research highlights a more complex, regulatory role for [[concepts/cpu|mitochondria]].

*   **Central Processing Unit:** Mitochondria act as sophisticated "pilots" or [[concepts/central-processing-units|central processing units]], integrating signals to regulate cellular behavior beyond simple energy generation [[lab-notes/2026-10-11-Mitochondria-Beyond-Powerhouses-Running-the-Body-Quantum|Mitochondria: Beyond Powerhouses, Running the Body, Quantum Effects, and Magnetism]].
*   **Systemic Regulation:** They influence whole-body physiology, suggesting they "run the body" through systemic signaling rather than just local [[concepts/energy-source|energy supply]].
*   **[[concepts/quantum-phenomena|Quantum Effects]]:** Emerging evidence points to quantum mechanical effects within [[concepts/mitochondrial-function|mitochondrial function]], potentially influencing efficiency and signaling.
*   **Magnetism:** Mitochondria exhibit magnetic properties, which may play a role in their interaction with biological and environmental fields.
