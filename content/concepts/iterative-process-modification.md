# Iterative Process Modification

[[concepts/iterative-refinement|Iterative Process]] Modification refers to the technique by which [[concepts/action-oriented-ai|autonomous AI agents]] optimize their own operations by systematically altering the [[concepts/parameters|parameters]] and [[concepts/structure|structure]] of their [[concepts/iterative-execution-loops|iterative execution loops]]. Rather than requiring external human intervention to improve performance, these systems are designed to evaluate their own processes, identify inefficiencies, and adjust the mechanisms that govern their repeated cycles of [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] or action.

## Core Mechanism

The approach centers on what is termed an "[[concepts/iterative-harness-modification|iterative harness]]"—the framework that controls how an AI system repeats its core operations. By enabling an [[entities/agent|agent]] to modify this [[concepts/harness|harness]] autonomously, the system can adapt its [[concepts/loop|loop]] structure, transition rules, and [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] mechanisms in response to observed outcomes. This creates a feedback cycle where performance data directly informs structural changes to the process itself.

## Application in Self-Evolving Systems

Iterative Process Modification is a foundational concept in [[concepts/automated-diagnostic-analysis|self-evolving AI]] architectures, where [[concepts/agents|agents]] continuously improve without explicit human reprogramming. The technique allows systems to experiment with different [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] strategies, step-through depths, and error-correction approaches, gradually converging toward more efficient execution patterns. This capability is particularly relevant for research-oriented [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] that must adapt their investigative processes based on emerging results or changing problem contexts.
