# Phi

Phi is a family of [[concepts/small-language-models|small language models]] developed by [[entities/microsoft|Microsoft]] designed for efficient [[concepts/deployment|deployment]] on local devices and [[concepts/edge-computing|edge computing]] environments. These models prioritize [[concepts/computational-efficiency|computational efficiency]] and reduced [[concepts/memory|memory]] requirements, making them suitable for [[concepts/software|applications]] where full-scale [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]] would be impractical due to [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] constraints or latency requirements.

## Design and Purpose

The [[concepts/phi-models|Phi models]] are engineered to deliver reasonable language understanding and generation [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] within a much smaller parameter footprint than mainstream large language models. By reducing [[concepts/code-size|model size]], they enable faster [[concepts/inference|inference]] speeds and lower resource consumption, making them applicable to on-device [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]] such as mobile devices, IoT systems, and personal computers where bandwidth or [[concepts/privacy|privacy]] concerns [[entities/make|make]] cloud-based inference undesirable.

## Integration and Availability

Phi models have been integrated into various deployment frameworks and platforms. They are accessible through tools like [[concepts/task-specific-modeling|Ollama]], which simplifies [[concepts/running|running]] local language models. Microsoft has released multiple iterations of the Phi family with incremental improvements in performance and efficiency.
