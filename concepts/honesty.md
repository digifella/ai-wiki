# Honesty

**Honesty** is the quality of being truthful, straightforward, and free from deceit. It serves as a foundational [[concepts/ethics]] principle in [[concepts/philosophy]], [[concepts/psychology]], and sociology, facilitating trust and cooperation within social structures. In the context of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial-intelligence]], honesty refers to an agent's ability to represent reality accurately without fabrication or deception, a critical component of [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]].

## Philosophical & Psychological Dimensions
- **Deontological View**: Immanuel Kant argued that honesty is a categorical imperative; lying violates the universalizability of moral laws.
- **Social Function**: Promotes [[concepts/trust]] and reduces transaction costs in economics and social interactions.
- **Pathologies**: Pathological-lying and [[concepts/ai-hallucinations|confabulation]] represent breakdowns in the [[concepts/integrity|integrity]] of self-reporting and [[concepts/factual-recall|factual recall]].

## AI and Computational Honesty
In [[concepts/llm]], honesty is often conflated with truthfulness and factuality. Key challenges include:
- **[[concepts/data-hallucination|Hallucination]]**: The generation of plausible but incorrect information.
- **Adversarial [[concepts/robustness|Robustness]]**: The model's resistance to being manipulated into lying under specific prompts.
- **Self-Correction**: The ability to identify and rectify prior erroneous statements.
