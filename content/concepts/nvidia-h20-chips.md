# Nvidia H20 Chips

The Nvidia H20 is a graphics processing unit (GPU) designed for [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] and machine [[concepts/learning|learning]] workloads in data center environments. As a member of Nvidia's Hopper [[concepts/architecture|architecture]] family, it serves the growing computational demands of [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]], [[concepts/generative-apps|generative AI applications]], and other intensive machine learning tasks. The chip represents Nvidia's continued development of specialized [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] for the [[concepts/computing-architecture|AI infrastructure]] market.

## Technical Context

The H20 is positioned within Nvidia's lineup of data center accelerators alongside other high-performance options. Its design reflects ongoing engineering efforts to balance computational capacity with practical considerations such as power consumption and thermal management. Data center deployments of advanced GPU chips like the H20 have contributed to increased water usage for cooling infrastructure, a factor relevant to discussions about the environmental footprint of modern AI systems.

## Industry Relevance

The H20 has been discussed in the context of recent technology industry developments, including large-scale workforce [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] at major cloud and AI companies. These discussions reflect the rapid evolution of AI infrastructure as organizations implement and scale [[concepts/machine-learning-systems|machine learning systems]], with implications for data center requirements and capital investment patterns in the broader technology sector.
