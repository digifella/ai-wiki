# Lidar Scanning

LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) is a remote sensing technology that utilizes laser pulses to measure distances and generate detailed spatial maps. The system operates by emitting infrared light and calculating the time it takes for the reflections to return to the sensor. This time-of-flight measurement enables the device to compute precise depth information for objects and environments within its field of view, capturing spatial geometry with millimeter-level accuracy.

Modern smartphone hardware, specifically iPhone Pro models, integrates compact LiDAR sensors to facilitate this process on a mobile platform. By embedding these sensors into consumer devices, the technology allows for the rapid capture of depth data in various lighting conditions. This integration has lowered the barrier to entry for high-fidelity spatial mapping, enabling users to collect the necessary input data for advanced computational modeling without specialized industrial equipment.

The depth data captured by these mobile LiDAR systems serves as a critical input for creating 3D Gaussian Splatting models. This technique leverages the dense point clouds generated by the scanner to reconstruct complex scenes with high visual fidelity. The resulting models provide a detailed representation of the physical environment, supporting applications in augmented reality, digital preservation, and spatial computing by bridging the gap between raw sensor data and rendered 3D assets.
