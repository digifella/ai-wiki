# Black Point Adjustment

Black point adjustment is a tonal correction technique in Adobe Photoshop used to enhance image contrast by establishing where the darkest tones in a photograph should begin. Rather than relying on the image's existing darkest pixels, this adjustment allows editors to manually set a reference point for pure black or near-black values. By defining the lower end of the tonal range, black point adjustment increases overall visual depth and definition in an image, making it particularly useful in portrait photography where subtle tonal shifts can significantly affect the final appearance.

## Technical Implementation

Black point adjustment is typically performed using Photoshop's Levels or Curves dialog. In the Levels panel, the black point is represented by the left slider under the histogram. Moving this slider to the right remaps the darkest tones in the image to pure black, which can deepen shadows and strengthen contrast. The Curves tool offers more granular control, allowing editors to target specific tonal ranges while adjusting the black point. This flexibility makes curves adjustment valuable for complex images where a uniform black point shift would be inappropriate.

## Application in Portraiture

In portrait work, black point adjustment is used carefully to enhance facial definition without creating unnaturally harsh shadows. Subtle adjustments typically strengthen the appearance of eyes and facial contours by deepening the darkest values while maintaining natural skin tones in midtones and highlights. The technique is often combined with other tonal adjustments to achieve a cohesive final image, as an isolated black point adjustment may produce unbalanced results if the full tonal range isn't considered.
