## Marc Benioff: Salesforce's AI Strategy, Agents, Slack, and Work Transformation
**Clip title:** Salesforce CEO on Microsoft Blocking OpenAI Investment, [[concepts/ai-scapegoating|AI Scapegoating]], [[concepts/openclaw|OpenClaw]], and [[concepts/regulation|Regulation]]
**Author / channel:** Matthew Berman
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OzUqfN4mcrM

### Summary
In a captivating interview, Matthew Berman converses with Marc Benioff,
co-founder and CEO of Salesforce, delving into the profound impact of
[[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] (AI) and agents on the [[concepts/future-of-work|future of work]] and
communication. The central theme revolves around how Salesforce is
strategically positioning itself, particularly through its acquisition of
Slack, to harness these transformative technologies. Benioff emphasizes
that AI-powered "agents" are poised to revolutionize how work is done,
breaking free from traditional, siloed interfaces to permeate every aspect
of business operations.

Benioff credits Salesforce's Chief Futurist, [[entities/peter|Peter]] Schwartz, with a
decade-long foresight into the critical role of AI and agents, which
significantly influenced the decision to acquire Slack. Slack, with its
open, [[concepts/conversational-interface|conversational interface]] and rich ecosystem, has proven to be an
ideal platform for integrating [[concepts/ai-connectors|AI agents]], allowing them to live and operate
wherever work happens. This [[concepts/computer-vision|vision]] extends beyond Slack itself, with
Salesforce aiming to [[entities/make|make]] all its core applications, including Sales Cloud,
Service Cloud, and even analytical tools like Tableau, "Slack-first" and
powered by AI agents. This signifies a shift towards more intuitive,
conversational interactions with business software, potentially altering
the very nature of traditional user interfaces.

Discussing the evolving workforce, Benioff highlights the collaborative
imperative between humans and AI. While AI agents significantly boost
productivity – for instance, increasing Salesforce's engineering output by
over 30% through [[concepts/advanced-coding|advanced coding]] models – humans remain crucial. He notes
that [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]], particularly large language models, can still be inaccurate
and require human oversight, making people the "bottleneck" in ensuring
quality and [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]]. This necessitates a rebalancing of workforce skills,
emphasizing the value of both specialists and generalists who can
effectively manage and verify AI outputs. Benioff stresses that companies,
including leading AI developers, are actively hiring humans to build and
supervise AI systems, underscoring the enduring need for human talent in
this new era.

Looking ahead, Benioff expresses significant concerns about the potential
for AI misuse, drawing parallels to the issues faced with social media. He
advocates for a strong focus on "safety and trust" in [[concepts/ai-development|AI development]],
asserting that these are inextricably linked to growth and [[concepts/adoption|adoption]].
Salesforce's strategy involves investing in foundational AI companies like
[[entities/anthropic-institute|Anthropic]], Cohere, and Mistral, alongside building a robust data
architecture ("Data 360") to ensure ethical and effective AI. Ultimately,
Benioff envisions a future where technology empowers individuals and
organizations, but he cautions that this progress must be guided by
guardrails and a commitment to responsible [[concepts/innovation|innovation]], ensuring that human
values remain at the forefront of the AI revolution.
