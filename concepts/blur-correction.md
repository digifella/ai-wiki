# Blur Correction

Blur correction in photography refers to techniques used to achieve [[concepts/sharpness|sharp focus]] across an entire image, particularly in [[concepts/landscape-photography|landscape photography]] where both foreground and background elements require [[concepts/clarity-slider|clarity]]. The primary challenge stems from the limitations of depth of field—the zone of acceptable sharpness in a photograph. When shooting with wider apertures or longer focal lengths, cameras cannot maintain focus on all elements simultaneously, resulting in either sharp foreground with blurred background, or vice versa.

## Focus Stacking

[[concepts/focus-stacking|Focus stacking]] is the primary method for addressing these limitations. This technique involves capturing multiple images of the same scene, each focused on a different plane of distance. By combining these exposures, photographers can create a final image where all elements, from the nearest foreground object to the distant horizon, remain in sharp focus. This process effectively extends the depth of field beyond what a single exposure can achieve.

The technique requires precise alignment and exposure [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] across the bracketed shots. Modern software automates the blending process by analyzing each frame to identify the sharpest areas and merging them into a single composite image. While traditionally associated with landscape photography, focus stacking is also utilized in [[concepts/macro-photography|macro photography]] and product imaging to overcome extreme depth of field constraints.
