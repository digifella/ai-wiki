# Selective Reveal

Selective Reveal is a digital image editing technique used to composite the optimal elements from multiple similar photographs into a single, improved final image. By stacking corresponding layers in software such as Adobe Photoshop, editors can isolate specific details from each source file. This process allows for the retention of the sharpest focus, most natural expressions, and best lighting conditions from individual shots while discarding unwanted artifacts or flaws present in any single frame.

The method is particularly valuable in group photography, where maintaining consistent focus, eye contact, and facial expressions across all subjects is technically challenging. In these scenarios, individuals may blink, look away, or exhibit unflattering expressions in different frames. By utilizing layer masks, the editor can selectively reveal the best moments from each photo, ensuring that every subject appears at their peak within the composite result.

Implementation typically involves aligning the source images to ensure precise registration of subjects and background elements. Once aligned, layer masks are applied to hide portions of each layer that contain errors, such as closed eyes or motion blur. This non-destructive workflow enables photographers to correct common issues like inconsistent lighting or minor pose discrepancies, resulting in a polished image that would be difficult or impossible to capture in a single exposure.
