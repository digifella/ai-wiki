# Hinduism

Hinduism is one of the world's major religions, with over one billion adherents primarily in India and Nepal, and significant diaspora communities globally. Unlike religions with a single founder or central text, Hinduism developed organically over more than three thousand years through the synthesis of Vedic, indigenous, and philosophical traditions across the Indian subcontinent. The religion encompasses a wide range of beliefs, practices, and schools of thought that share common elements while maintaining considerable internal diversity.

## Core Concepts and Practices

Central to Hindu philosophy are concepts including dharma (duty or righteousness), karma (action and consequence), and samsara (the cycle of birth, death, and rebirth). Many Hindus worship a pantheon of deities understood as manifestations of Brahman, the ultimate cosmic principle or absolute reality. Major texts include the Vedas, Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita, and the epics Mahabharata and Ramayana. Common practices encompass puja (ritual worship), meditation, yoga, pilgrimage, and observance of festivals such as Diwali and Holi.

## Philosophical and Social Dimensions

Hinduism has generated multiple philosophical schools (darshanas) ranging from non-dualism to dualism, each offering distinct perspectives on the [[entities/nature|nature]] of reality and the path to liberation (moksha). Historically, the religion has been intertwined with the social [[concepts/structure|structure]] of the Indian subcontinent, including the caste system, though reform movements from the 19th century onward have challenged hierarchical aspects of this framework. Hindu thought has significantly influenced [[concepts/indian-civilization|Indian culture]], art, [[concepts/architecture|architecture]], and [[concepts/governance|governance]] throughout history.
