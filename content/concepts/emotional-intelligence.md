# Emotional Intelligence

Emotional intelligence (EI) refers to the capacity to recognize, understand, and manage emotions in oneself and in others. It encompasses the ability to perceive emotional cues, interpret their meaning, and respond appropriately in social and personal contexts. Emotional intelligence is distinct from cognitive intelligence and represents a form of social [[concepts/cognition|cognition]] that influences interpersonal effectiveness and personal [[concepts/health|wellbeing]].

## Core Components

Emotional intelligence typically involves four interrelated abilities: self-awareness (recognizing one's own emotions), self-[[concepts/regulation|regulation]] (managing emotional [[concepts/responses|responses]]), social awareness (understanding others' emotions), and relationship management (using emotional understanding to interact effectively with others). These dimensions work together to enable individuals to navigate complex social environments and respond to emotional demands in adaptive ways.

## Relevance to Wellbeing

Research has linked emotional intelligence to various health and wellbeing outcomes, including [[concepts/stress-management|stress management]], relationship satisfaction, and [[concepts/mental-health|mental health]] [[concepts/resilience|resilience]]. Individuals with higher emotional intelligence tend to make more effective decisions during emotionally charged situations and maintain more stable interpersonal [[concepts/relationships|relationships]]. The development of emotional intelligence [[concepts/skills|skills]] can support both individual psychological wellbeing and broader social functioning.
