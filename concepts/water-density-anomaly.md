# Water Density Anomaly

The Water [[concepts/density|Density]] [[concepts/anomaly|Anomaly]] refers to the counter-intuitive behavior of Water where its solid [[concepts/phase|phase]], Ice, is less dense than its [[concepts/liquid-state-of-matter|liquid phase]] at standard [[concepts/pressure|pressure]]. This property is critical for aquatic life and [[concepts/thermal-regulation|climate regulation]].

## Key Properties
- **Maximum Density:** [[concepts/liquid-water|Liquid water]] reaches its maximum density at approximately 4°C (277 K).
- **Expansion on Freezing:** Upon freezing at 0°C, water expands by approximately 9%, decreasing in density from ~1.00 g/cm³ to ~0.92 g/cm³.
- **[[concepts/hydrogen-bonding|Hydrogen Bonding]]:** The open hexagonal lattice structure of Ice Ih creates voids that lower density compared to the more compact, transient network of liquid water.

## High-Pressure Ice Phases
While Ice Ih is the common form at surface conditions, water exhibits complex phase behavior under extreme pressure. Contrary to the surface anomaly, [[concepts/high-pressure-ice-phases|high-pressure ice phases]] are generally denser than liquid water.

- **Phase Complexity:** "Ice" is not a singular substance but encompasses at least 15 distinct solid phases, each with unique density and crystal structures [[lab-notes/2026-08-07-Waters-High-Pressure-Ice-Phases-Freezing-in-Unbreakable|Water's High-Pressure Ice Phases: Freezing in Unbreakable Containers]].
- **Pressure-Induced Freezing:** In unbreakable [[concepts/containerization-technology|containers]], water can be forced to freeze into these denser phases (e.g., Ice VI, Ice VII) without expanding, defying the standard surface-level anomaly.
- **Phase Diagram:** The transition between these phases depends on both temperature and pressure, illustrating that the density anomaly is specific to low-pressure regimes.
