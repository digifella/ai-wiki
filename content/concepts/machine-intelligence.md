# Machine Intelligence

Machine intelligence refers to the computational [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] of artificial systems to perform tasks that typically require [[concepts/human-cognition|human cognition]]. The concept encompasses various forms of automated [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]], [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]], and information processing, and remains a subject of ongoing philosophical and [[concepts/scientific-method|scientific inquiry]]. A key question in this field concerns how machine intelligence compares to and differs from human [[concepts/cognition|cognition]] in both mechanism and capability.

## Feynman's Perspective

Physicist [[concepts/feynman|Richard Feynman]] addressed the question "[[concepts/silicon|Can Machines Think]]?" in a 1985 Q&A [[concepts/session|session]], contributing to the broader debate about machine versus [[concepts/human-intelligence|human intelligence]]. Rather than accepting [[concepts/simple-analogies|simple comparisons]], Feynman's approach emphasized the importance of understanding what "thinking" actually means and how different systems—whether biological or computational—process information and solve problems. His perspective highlighted that meaningful comparison requires clarity about the specific cognitive functions and processes under examination.

The discussion of machine intelligence remains relevant to questions about artificial systems' potential capabilities, limitations, and the extent to which they can replicate or simulate aspects of human reasoning. Contemporary work in [[concepts/ai-development|AI development]] continues to grapple with these fundamental questions about the [[entities/nature|nature]] of intelligence itself.
