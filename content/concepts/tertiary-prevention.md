# Tertiary Prevention

Tertiary prevention is the third level of [[concepts/health-promotion|disease prevention]], applied after a disease or condition has already been diagnosed in a patient. It focuses on managing the disease process, slowing its progression, and preventing complications that could worsen [[concepts/health|health]] outcomes or reduce [[concepts/quality-of-life|quality of life]]. Tertiary prevention bridges clinical treatment and rehabilitation, aiming to help patients live as fully as possible despite their existing condition.

## Examples and Applications

Common examples of tertiary prevention include medication management for chronic diseases like diabetes or hypertension, cardiac rehabilitation following a heart attack, physical therapy after stroke or injury, and cancer treatment to prevent recurrence or metastasis. These interventions reduce the burden of disease, minimize disability, and help patients maintain functional independence and wellbeing.

## Relationship to Other Prevention Levels

Tertiary prevention complements [[concepts/primary-prevention|primary prevention]] (preventing disease from occurring) and [[concepts/secondary-prevention|secondary prevention]] (detecting and treating disease early). While primary and secondary prevention target disease risk and early detection respectively, tertiary prevention addresses the reality that some patients will develop disease and require ongoing management. Together, these three levels form a comprehensive approach to public and individual health.
