# Flavor Layering

Flavor layering is a fundamental cooking technique that involves building depth and complexity in a dish by adding ingredients and seasonings in strategic stages rather than all at once. This method allows each component to develop its own character while contributing to a cohesive whole, resulting in more nuanced and satisfying flavors. By controlling when ingredients are introduced during cooking, chefs can maximize the extraction and interaction of flavors, preventing any single element from overwhelming the dish.

## Application in North Indian Cuisine

North Indian cooking exemplifies flavor layering through its use of spice-infused oils, sequential addition of aromatics, and graduated heat application. A characteristic approach involves tempering whole spices in hot oil or ghee to release their essential oils, then introducing onions and garlic at specific points, followed by tomatoes and protein, and finally finishing with fresh herbs and adjusted seasonings. This progression allows each layer to build upon the previous one, creating the complex taste profiles characteristic of dishes like curries and biryanis.

## Chef Neel Kajale's North Indian Skillet Chicken Curry

Chef Kajale's North Indian Skillet Chicken Curry demonstrates flavor layering through a methodical cooking sequence. The recipe begins by blooming spices in oil, continues with caramelizing onions, and proceeds through deliberate stages of adding tomatoes, yogurt, chicken, and finishing spices. This staged approach ensures that toasted spices contribute depth, caramelized aromatics provide sweetness and body, and fresh additions at the end brighten the overall dish. The technique produces a curry with balanced, fully developed flavors rather than a one-dimensional taste.
