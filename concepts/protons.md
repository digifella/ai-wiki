# Protons

Protons are positively charged subatomic particles that serve as fundamental constituents of atomic nuclei. Each proton carries an elementary positive charge of approximately $1.602 \times 10^{-19}$ coulombs and possesses a mass of roughly $1.67 \times 10^{-27}$ kilograms, which is approximately 1,836 times the mass of an electron. Along with neutrons, protons form the dense core of every atom, and their stability is a key factor in the structural integrity of matter. The number of protons in an atom's nucleus defines its atomic number and determines the chemical element to which the atom belongs.

## Composition and Structure

Protons are classified as baryons and are composed of three valence quarks: two up quarks and one down quark, held together by the strong nuclear force mediated by gluons. Despite the quarks contributing only a small fraction of the proton's total mass, the majority of the mass arises from the binding energy of the gluon field and the kinetic energy of the quarks, as described by quantum chromodynamics. This composite nature distinguishes protons from elementary particles like electrons, making them complex objects of study in high-energy physics.

## Role in Cosmic Ray Research

In the context of astrophysics, protons constitute the majority of cosmic rays reaching Earth. High-energy protons are accelerated by astrophysical sources such as supernova remnants and active galactic nuclei, traveling through interstellar space before interacting with Earth's atmosphere. Observatories like the Large High Altitude Air Shower Observatory (LHAASO) detect the extensive air showers produced when these cosmic ray protons collide with atmospheric nuclei. Analyzing these events allows researchers to infer the energy spectrum and composition of primary cosmic rays, providing insights into the mechanisms of particle acceleration in the universe.
