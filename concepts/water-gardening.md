# Water Gardening

Water gardening is the practice of growing plants in water rather than soil, a cultivation method particularly suited to indoor environments. In this approach, also known as hydroculture, plant roots are suspended in water or inert growing media such as clay pellets, perlite, or rockwool. The water itself delivers both hydration and dissolved nutrients to the plant roots, eliminating the need for traditional soil. This method removes common soil-related problems including pest infestations and soil-borne diseases.

## Suitable Plants

Many common houseplants adapt well to water gardening conditions. Philodendrons, pothos, lucky bamboo, peace lilies, and spider plants are among the most reliable choices for beginners. Herbs such as mint and basil can also be grown hydroponically, as can certain aquatic plants like water lettuce. The key requirement is that the chosen plant can tolerate consistent moisture around its roots without developing rot, and that it can obtain necessary nutrients from dissolved minerals rather than organic matter in soil.

## Basic Requirements

Water gardening systems require regular monitoring of water quality and nutrient levels. The water must be changed periodically to prevent stagnation and maintain adequate oxygen levels for root health. A balanced water-soluble fertilizer formulated for hydroponic plants is typically added to the water, as pure water alone cannot provide the minerals plants need. Light exposure remains essential, following the same requirements as soil-based indoor plants.
