# Oil Formation

Oil and [[concepts/coal-formation|coal formation]] occurred during the [[concepts/high-oxygen-period|High Oxygen Period]], a specific geological epoch when [[concepts/oxygen-saturation|atmospheric oxygen levels]] were significantly elevated. During this time, organic material accumulated in marine and terrestrial environments under conditions that favored the [[concepts/preservation|preservation]] and transformation of biomass into fossil fuels. The concentration of oil and coal deposits in rocks from this single epoch reflects the unique environmental conditions required for their formation.

## Oxidation and Rock Alteration

The elevated oxygen levels of this period caused widespread [[concepts/rock-oxidation|oxidation of rocks]] and minerals, a process sometimes described as the rocks "rusting." This oxidation is particularly evident in the geological formations of [[concepts/red-centre-of-australia|Australia's Red Centre]], where iron oxides give the landscape its distinctive red coloration. The iron-rich minerals in these rocks were oxidized during the High Oxygen Period, creating the visible geological features characteristic of the region today.

## Significance for Understanding Geology

The temporal clustering of oil and coal deposits in a single geological epoch demonstrates how atmospheric composition directly influences the types of deposits that form in the rock record. Once oxygen levels changed following the High Oxygen Period, the environmental conditions suitable for coal and oil formation no longer existed, which explains why these fossil fuel deposits are restricted to rocks of this specific age rather than being distributed throughout Earth's geological history.
