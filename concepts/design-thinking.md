# Design Thinking

Design thinking is a structured problem-solving methodology that prioritizes understanding user needs and context before developing solutions. Rather than beginning with predetermined answers, the approach emphasizes empathetic exploration of constraints and requirements, followed by iterative refinement. This human-centered orientation distinguishes design thinking from purely technical or intuitive problem-solving methods, making it broadly applicable across disciplines including product development, organizational strategy, and service design.

## Core Process

The methodology typically proceeds through distinct phases: empathizing with users to understand their actual needs, defining problems based on this understanding, ideating multiple potential solutions without initial judgment, prototyping candidate approaches, and testing these prototypes against real-world constraints. This cyclical process permits refinement based on feedback rather than assuming initial assumptions are correct. The emphasis on iteration acknowledges that complex problems rarely yield to single-pass solutions.

## Application in Design and Beyond

While originating in industrial and product design contexts, design thinking has been adapted for use in organizational problem-solving, policy development, and technical fields including cryptography and mathematics education. The methodology's value lies in its systematic approach to managing uncertainty and validating assumptions through evidence rather than speculation. By combining creative exploration with analytical evaluation, design thinking bridges intuitive and rigorous approaches to solving problems where requirements are not fully specified in advance.
