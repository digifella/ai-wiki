# Water Gardening

Water gardening, also known as hydroculture, is the practice of cultivating plants in water or inert media rather than traditional soil. This method is particularly suited to indoor environments, where plant roots are suspended in water or materials such as clay pellets, perlite, or rockwool. The water serves as the primary delivery system for both hydration and dissolved nutrients, effectively replacing the role of soil in the plant's growth cycle.

By eliminating soil, this cultivation technique removes common issues associated with earth-based gardening, such as pest infestations and soil-borne diseases. It offers a cleaner alternative for indoor spaces, allowing for precise control over nutrient intake and root health. The approach is versatile, accommodating a wide range of species that can adapt to aquatic or semi-aquatic conditions.

Many common houseplants thrive in water-based systems, making the practice accessible to a broad audience. Suitable candidates often include species with robust root systems capable of absorbing nutrients directly from the liquid medium. Success in water gardening depends on maintaining proper water quality and ensuring that the roots receive adequate oxygenation to prevent rot and promote healthy development.
