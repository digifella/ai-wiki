# Climate Science

Climate [[concepts/science|science]] is the [[concepts/systematic-study|systematic study]] of [[entities/earth|Earth]]'s climate systems and the long-term atmospheric patterns that characterize them. It integrates knowledge from multiple disciplines—including [[concepts/atmospheric-physics|atmospheric physics]], oceanography, [[concepts/geology|geology]], and ecology—to understand how climate operates and changes over time. Climate scientists examine interactions between the [[concepts/earths-atmosphere|atmosphere]], oceans, ice sheets, and the biosphere to explain both current conditions and past climates, as well as to project future changes.

## Methods and Data

Climate research relies on diverse observational and analytical techniques to reconstruct historical records and monitor present-day changes. Paleoclimatology utilizes proxies such as ice cores, tree rings, and sediment layers to analyze past climate variability over millennia. Modern monitoring employs satellite remote sensing, weather stations, and ocean buoys to collect real-time data on temperature, precipitation, and atmospheric [[concepts/writing|composition]]. These datasets are essential for validating climate models and understanding the physical [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] driving global change.

## Interdisciplinary Framework

The field operates at the intersection of several scientific domains, requiring a holistic approach to [[concepts/earth-system-dynamics|earth-system dynamics]]. Atmospheric science focuses on the composition and dynamics of the air, while oceanography examines the role of oceans in heat distribution and carbon [[entities/storage|storage]]. Geological studies provide context for long-term tectonic and volcanic influences on climate, and ecological research assesses the impact of [[concepts/climate-change|climate shifts]] on [[concepts/biodiversity|biodiversity]] and ecosystems. This interdisciplinary framework allows for a comprehensive understanding of [[concepts/systems|feedback loops]] and the complex interplay between natural processes and anthropogenic factors.
