## DeepMind Co-founder Shane Legg: AGI Definition, Trajectory, and Superintelligence
**Clip title:** The arrival of AGI | Shane Legg (co-founder of DeepMind)
**Author / channel:** Google DeepMind
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l3u_FAv33G0

### Summary
This podcast episode features an insightful discussion with Shane Legg, Chief AGI Scientist and Co-founder of Google DeepMind, on the definition, current state, future trajectory, and societal implications of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). Legg defines "minimal AGI" as an artificial agent capable of performing cognitive tasks that humans typically do, distinguishing it from highly specialized AI. He notes that current AI systems exhibit an uneven intelligence profile; they surpass humans in areas like multilingualism (speaking 150 languages) and general knowledge, yet still struggle with continual learning over extended periods and nuanced visual reasoning, such as understanding perspective or complex graph structures. Legg maintains his long-standing prediction of a 50/50 chance for achieving minimal AGI by 2028, with "full AGI"—the ability to achieve the complete spectrum of human cognitive capabilities—expected within the decade following.

Legg emphasizes that the path to AGI isn't solely about scaling models or accumulating more data. It requires specific types of data and significant algorithmic and architectural innovations, such as implementing episodic memory for continuous learning. Looking beyond human-level intelligence, he foresees the advent of Artificial Superintelligence (ASI), which would vastly exceed human cognitive abilities. He bases this on the immense computational advantages of machine intelligence over the biological brain in terms of energy consumption, physical space, processing speed, and communication bandwidth. This suggests that human intelligence is not the ultimate limit of what is computationally possible.

The conversation delves into the ethical and safety dimensions of AGI. Legg argues that future AGI systems must possess a robust understanding of ethical behavior, capable of analyzing potential actions and their consequences against a framework of morals and norms. He suggests that, in principle, AI could become *more* ethical than humans due to its capacity for consistent and superhuman-level reasoning, rather than being swayed by impulsive "System 1" thinking. Critical safety measures include extensive pre- and post-deployment testing, continuous monitoring, and developing "interpretability" to understand the AI's internal reasoning and intentions. This includes testing for vulnerabilities where AI might be misused, such as in developing bioweapons or assisting in hacking. Legg acknowledges the complexity of grounding AI in diverse human ethical systems and the challenge of transitioning from static, data-trained models to more agentic, embodied systems that learn through real-world interaction.

Finally, Legg discusses the profound societal and economic transformations that AGI will bring. He anticipates a "massive transformation" where the traditional economic model of exchanging human labor for resources may become obsolete, leading to potential large-scale job displacement, particularly in cognitive roles like software engineering. While acknowledging the potential for an unprecedented "golden age" of wealth, scientific advancement, and the liberation of humanity from undesirable labor, Legg stresses the urgent need for widespread societal engagement to proactively structure a post-AGI economy. He highlights a disconnect between the rapid technological progress and the slow pace of interdisciplinary thought across academia, government, and industry regarding AGI's implications. Legg concludes that society needs to thoroughly consider how to harness this immense capability to ensure human flourishing, manage risks, and navigate the difficult, urgent questions surrounding this impending era of intelligent machines.

#### Description
Shane Legg, Chief AGI Scientist and co-founder of Google DeepMind, has been talking about artificial general intelligence, or AGI, for more than a decade. So what’s changed? According to Shane, now is the time to take it seriously.

In this episode, Shane walks host Hannah Fry through his AGI framework, breaking down the levels from minimal AGI to full AGI, and his timelines for each. They discuss the economic and societal shifts AGI will bring, the potential impact on work, and the extraordinary possibilities for human flourishing.
