# Wavefunction Collapse

Wavefunction collapse is the theoretical process by which a quantum system transitions from a superposition of multiple possible states into a single definite state upon measurement. In quantum mechanics, a system's wavefunction describes the probability distribution of all possible measurement outcomes. According to the Copenhagen interpretation, the act of measurement fundamentally alters this state, causing the superposition to "collapse" into one of the eigenvalues corresponding to the measured observable.

This phenomenon highlights the measurement problem, a central issue in the foundations of quantum mechanics regarding how and why the transition from probabilistic potential to actualized reality occurs. The mathematical formalism of quantum mechanics predicts the probabilities of various outcomes but does not explicitly describe the physical mechanism of the collapse itself. Consequently, the nature of this transition remains a subject of intense debate and interpretation within the physics community.

Various interpretations of quantum mechanics offer different explanations for the collapse. While the Copenhagen interpretation treats collapse as a fundamental, non-unitary process, other frameworks such as the Many-Worlds interpretation suggest that no collapse occurs, with all possible outcomes realized in branching universes. Decoherence theory further explains how interaction with the environment can suppress interference between states, effectively mimicking collapse for all practical purposes without requiring a fundamental change in the underlying dynamics.
