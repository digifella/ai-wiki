# Love

Love is a complex emotional and psychological state characterised by deep affection, [[concepts/attachment-theory|attachment]], and care toward other people, oneself, or concepts. It plays a central role in human [[concepts/health|wellbeing]], influencing [[concepts/mental-health|mental health]], [[concepts/social-connection|social bonds]], and overall [[concepts/quality-of-life|life satisfaction]]. Psychologically, love involves multiple systems including neurochemical reward pathways, attachment mechanisms, and [[concepts/human-cognition|cognitive processes]] that shape how individuals relate to others and themselves.

## Ancient Greek Perspectives

Ancient Greek philosophers and mythologists identified several distinct types of love, each with different qualities and purposes. The Greeks distinguished between *eros* (romantic or passionate love), *agape* (universal or selfless love), *philia* (friendship or affection between equals), and *storge* (familial or comfortable love). These categorizations, preserved through mythology and philosophical texts, provided early frameworks for understanding love's varied expressions and their significance in human life.

## Contemporary Relevance

Modern research in psychology, [[concepts/neuroscience|neuroscience]], and medicine continues to examine love as both an emotional experience and a biological phenomenon. Understanding love's various forms remains relevant to personal [[concepts/relationships|relationships]], mental health treatment, and social wellbeing. The ancient Greek conceptualizations of love still inform contemporary discussions about different types of [[concepts/human-connection|human connection]] and their distinct impacts on individual and collective health.
