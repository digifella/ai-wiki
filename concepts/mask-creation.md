# Mask Creation

Mask creation is a fundamental technique in digital photo editing that involves isolating specific areas of an image for selective adjustments. In Lightroom and Camera Raw, masks allow photographers to apply edits to particular regions while leaving the rest of the image unaffected, enabling precise control over exposure, color, clarity, and other parameters. This selective editing approach is essential for refining complex images where global adjustments would compromise overall quality.

The process typically begins with automatic selection tools that detect subjects such as skies, people, or objects based on luminance and color data. These initial masks often require manual refinement to ensure accurate boundaries, particularly in areas with high contrast or fine details like hair or foliage. Techniques for fixing mask edges involve using the brush or gradient tools to add or subtract from the selection, ensuring that adjustments blend naturally with the surrounding pixels.

Advanced masking workflows may combine multiple layers of masks to address different parts of an image simultaneously. For instance, a photographer might use a radial mask to darken a background while employing a linear gradient to balance the horizon. By carefully adjusting the feathering and density of these masks, editors can achieve seamless transitions that maintain the integrity of the original scene while enhancing specific visual elements.
