# Conducting Health Screenings

[[concepts/health|Health]] screenings are systematic procedures used to identify disease, health risks, or abnormal conditions in individuals or populations who may have no symptoms. These screenings serve as a critical preventive tool in [[concepts/population-health|public health]] and clinical practice, allowing healthcare providers to detect conditions [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] earlier, often more treatable stages. Screenings differ from diagnostic [[concepts/testing|testing]] in that they are applied broadly to asymptomatic populations to stratify risk, whereas diagnostic tests are used to confirm suspected disease in symptomatic individuals.

## Types and Scope

Health screenings encompass a wide [[concepts/range|range]] of assessments, from simple measurements like blood pressure and body mass index to more complex laboratory tests such as blood glucose screening, cholesterol panels, and cancer screening [[concepts/software|programs]]. Screenings may target specific populations based on age, gender, family history, or occupational [[concepts/exposure|exposure]]. Common examples include mammography for breast cancer, colonoscopy for colorectal cancer, and prenatal screening for maternal and fetal health. Community-based screenings often address infectious diseases, chronic conditions, and [[concepts/mental-health|mental health]] status.

## Implementation and Outcomes

Effective health screening programs require careful planning regarding target populations, screening intervals, and appropriate follow-up pathways for positive results. The success of a screening initiative depends on factors including test [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]], [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]] of screening services, and the availability of treatment options for identified conditions. Screening results guide [[concepts/clinical-reasoning|clinical decision-making]] and patient [[concepts/offering-counseling-services|counseling]], helping individuals understand their health status and any necessary next steps for [[concepts/preventive-care|prevention]] or treatment. [[concepts/public-health|Public health]] agencies and healthcare systems use screening data to identify population health trends and allocate resources appropriately.
