# Pixel density

Pixel [[concepts/density|density]] refers to the concentration of pixels within a specific physical area, typically measured in **PPI** ([[concepts/pixels-per-inch|pixels per inch]]). It is a critical factor in determining the perceived [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]], level of detail, and overall quality of both digital displays and Printing.

## Core Concepts
- **PPI (Pixels Per Inch):** The standard measurement for digital [[concepts/image-resolution|image resolution]] and screen density.
- **DPI (Dots Per Inch):** Refers to the density of ink droplets in physical print; often used interchangeably with PPI in casual contexts but technically distinct.
- [[concepts/solution|Resolution]]: The total number of pixels in a digital image; high pixel density allows for higher resolution within the same physical footprint.
- [[concepts/digital-compositing]]: The process of combining multiple images, where maintaining consistent pixel density across layers is essential for [[concepts/hidden-engineering|seamless integration]].

## Practical Application & Workflow
In professional [[concepts/image-input-processing|image processing]], pixel density is a foundational setting for document [[concepts/preparation|preparation]]:
- **Print Standards:** High-fidelity output (e.g., for large-format prints) typically requires a density of **300 ppi** to ensure [[concepts/clarity-slider|clarity]] (ref: [[entities/julianne-kost|Julianne Kost]] Compositing in [[concepts/photoshop|Photoshop]]).
- **Document Configuration:** Professional workflows often tie specific physical dimensions (e.g., 20x20 inches) to high bit-depths (16-bit) and wide [[concepts/color-spaces|color spaces]] like [[concepts/adobe-rgb]] to preserve detail and color accuracy.
