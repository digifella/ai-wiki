# Meat Texture

Ground meat texture during cooking is influenced by protein [[concepts/structure|structure]] and moisture content. When meat is heated, proteins denature and contract, which can squeeze out moisture and result in a dense, dry final product. The surface [[concepts/texture|texture]] also affects browning quality, as moisture on the meat's surface can inhibit the [[concepts/maillard-reaction|Maillard reaction]]—the chemical process responsible for developing flavor and color.

## Baking Soda's Effect

Baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) improves ground meat texture by raising its pH level. This alkaline environment causes the proteins in meat to relax and denature more gradually during cooking. As a result, the meat retains more of its natural juices rather than expelling them rapidly, leading to a juicier final product. Additionally, the higher pH allows the meat's surface to brown more effectively, creating better flavor development.

## Application

The technique involves mixing a small amount of baking soda directly into [[concepts/ground-meat|ground meat]] before cooking—typically around one-quarter teaspoon per pound of meat. The meat should rest briefly after the baking soda is added, allowing the chemical reaction to take effect before heat is applied. This simple step works across different [[concepts/cooking-methods|cooking methods]] and ground meat types.
