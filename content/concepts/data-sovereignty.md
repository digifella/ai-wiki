# Data Sovereignty

Data sovereignty refers to the principle and practice of maintaining control over data assets through technical and organizational mechanisms. In the context of AI systems and [[concepts/digital-infrastructure|digital infrastructure]], it emphasizes the ability to access, export, and manage one's own data independent of any single platform or service provider. This concept addresses concerns about vendor lock-in, where users or organizations become dependent on a service provider's systems with limited ability to retrieve or migrate their information.

## Technical Implementation

Common mechanisms for implementing data sovereignty include data export functionality and version control integration. Data export allows users to retrieve their information in standard, portable formats that can be used across different systems. Integration with distributed version control systems, such as GitHub, provides another layer of control by enabling users to maintain synchronized copies of their data in accessible repositories. These tools ensure that critical information is not exclusively held within proprietary systems.

## Practical Significance

Data sovereignty has become increasingly relevant as organizations rely on [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] and cloud-based services for critical operations. By establishing clear pathways for data portability and backup, organizations reduce their dependency on any single provider and maintain autonomy over their information assets. This approach also supports disaster recovery, [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] with data protection regulations, and the ability to switch between service providers without loss of historical data.
