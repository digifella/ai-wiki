# 2026 04 13 Irans Alarming Water Crisis

Iran confronts a severe water shortage driven by its inherently arid geography combined with ongoing climate pressures. The country receives approximately 250 millimeters of annual rainfall on average, which is less than one-quarter of the global mean of 990 millimeters. This low precipitation baseline creates a structural deficit in freshwater resources, fundamentally limiting the nation's natural capacity to sustain its population and agricultural sectors.

## Climatic and Geographic Factors

The physical landscape of Iran is predominantly characterized by high evaporation rates and limited natural recharge of aquifers. Much of the territory consists of deserts and semi-arid plateaus, which restricts the formation of permanent surface water bodies. Consequently, the nation relies heavily on non-renewable groundwater reserves, which are being depleted faster than they can be replenished by the sparse seasonal rains.

## Agricultural and Demographic Strain

The structural lack of renewable water resources exacerbates tensions between urban consumption needs and agricultural demands. Irrigation practices, which account for the vast majority of water usage, are increasingly unsustainable given the shrinking availability of surface and subsurface water. This imbalance threatens food security and forces difficult policy decisions regarding land use and resource allocation in the coming decades.
