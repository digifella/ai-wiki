# Dodging

**Dodging** is a photographic and [[concepts/image-input-processing|image processing]] technique involving the selective lightening of specific areas of an image to control [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], guide viewer [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]], and enhance perceived depth. Historically rooted in analog darkroom practices, it involves reducing [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] time for targeted regions during printing. In digital workflows, it is achieved through local adjustment tools.

## Core Principles
- **Selective Brightening**: Increasing luminance in specific areas while leaving the rest of the image unaffected.
- **Contrast Control**: Often paired with [[concepts/burning|Burning]] (selective darkening) to manage [[concepts/image-contrast|local contrast]] and dimensionality.
- **[[concepts/hierarchy|Visual Hierarchy]]**: Directs the eye toward the subject or key focal points.

## Digital Implementation
In modern [[concepts/non-destructive-editing|non-destructive editing]] software (e.g., [[entities/adobe-lightroom|Adobe Lightroom]], [[concepts/photoshop|Photoshop]]), dodging is applied via:
- **Local Adjustment Brushes**: Targeted strokes with increased exposure, highlights, or [[concepts/clarity-slider|clarity]].
- **[[concepts/layer-masks|Masking]]**: AI-driven or manual masks to isolate subjects from backgrounds.
