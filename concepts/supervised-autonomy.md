# Supervised Autonomy

Supervised autonomy is a regulatory classification for autonomous vehicles that permits operation under human oversight rather than requiring continuous manual control. Under this framework, the vehicle autonomously manages routine driving tasks including acceleration, braking, steering, and lane maintenance, while a human operator remains attentive and capable of intervention. This approach represents an intermediate regulatory position between fully manual driving and complete vehicle autonomy, enabling manufacturers to deploy advanced driving systems while maintaining a layer of human accountability for safety-critical decisions.

The concept has gained traction in the European Union as a pragmatic pathway for integrating advanced driver-assistance systems into existing traffic laws. A notable precedent occurred in the Netherlands, where Tesla’s Full Self-Driving (FSD) software received approval to operate under supervised autonomy guidelines. This decision established a framework where the driver must remain ready to take control at any moment, effectively shifting the legal responsibility for immediate hazard response from the machine to the human occupant.

This regulatory model serves as a potential blueprint for broader EU expansion, balancing technological innovation with public safety concerns. By defining clear boundaries for when human intervention is required, supervised autonomy allows for the gradual deployment of higher-level automation without necessitating a complete overhaul of current liability and traffic regulations. It remains distinct from Level 4 or Level 5 autonomy, where the vehicle is designed to operate without human input under specific conditions.
