# [[entities/colin-smith|Colin Smith]], founder of [[entities/photoshopcafe|PhotoshopCAFE]], introduces [[entities/adobe-project-indigo|Adobe Project Indigo]]

---
---
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G8LKeQw7jlY>

Colin Smith, founder of PhotoshopCAFE, introduces **[[entities/adobe|Adobe]] Project Indigo**, an exciting new [[concepts/camera-app|camera app]] focusing on advanced [[concepts/mobile-photography|mobile photography]]. He argues that while traditional cameras (DSLRs, mirrorless) [[entities/excel|excel]] in certain areas like video, low [[concepts/light|light]], and [[concepts/dynamic-range|dynamic range]], smartphones have surpassed [[concepts/point-and-shoot-cameras|point-and-shoot cameras]] due to their convenience, larger viewfinders, and on-device editing/sharing [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]].
However, traditional [[concepts/smartphone-camera-apps|smartphone camera apps]] often suffer from a lack of [[concepts/manual-controls|manual controls]], over-processing (leading to soft, over-sharpened, oversaturated images), and limitations due to small sensors and lenses.
Smith [[concepts/highlights|highlights]] that the rapid improvement in mobile photography isn't primarily due to [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] changes (sensors are only updated every few years, and both [[entities/iphone|iPhone]] and Pixel use Sony sensors), but rather through **computational photography** – the use of [[concepts/software|software]] to enhance image quality.
**Project Indigo's Key Features & Benefits (currently iPhone-only beta):**

* **Computational Photography Core:** It captures a burst of up to 30 raw frames (when the viewfinder is active in Photo mode, not Night mode), even before the shutter is pressed. These raw frames are then aligned and merged to create a superior image.
* **Enhanced Image Quality:** **Noise Reduction:** By merging multiple frames, Indigo significantly reduces noise, especially in [[concepts/shadows|shadows]], leading to cleaner images. **Dynamic [[concepts/range|Range]] (HDR Native):** It captures all details in shadows and highlights simultaneously through bracketing and merging. This is "real HDR," not just an effect, resulting in photos that "[[entities/glow|glow]]" on compatible HDR displays (like modern iPhones, [[entities/apple|Apple]] XDR displays, and HDR TVs/monitors). It also provides a graceful fallback to SDR for non-HDR displays. **Raw (DNG) Support:** While shooting, it constantly captures raw [[concepts/files|files]]. When saving, it applies an "Adaptive Color Profile" (similar to those in Camera Raw/Lightroom) to the DNG, providing an excellent starting point for professional editing without baking in [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]]. **Smart JPEG Processing:** For JPEG files, it uses advanced [[concepts/tone|tone]] mapping, sharpening, and color tuning. It generates two profiles: one for HDR display and one for standard dynamic range, ensuring optimal viewing across different screens.
* **Improved Digital Zoom:** Unlike traditional digital zoom which simply crops and interpolates, Indigo utilizes Adobe's **Super Resolution** technology. By combining information from multiple frames and using AI, it dramatically improves the quality of digitally zoomed images, even at high magnifications (e.g., 25x pinch zoom).
* **[[concepts/low-light-performance|Low Light Performance]]:** The multi-frame capture and merging process makes Indigo highly effective in low-light conditions, significantly reducing noise and improving detail.
* **[[concepts/zero|Zero]] Shutter Lag (in Photo Mode):** Because it's constantly buffering raw frames, there's virtually no delay between pressing the shutter and capturing the exact moment. (Note: Night mode and other advanced modes may have a slight processing delay and require a stable tripod for long exposures.)
* **Additional AI Features:** Includes AI Denoising and [[concepts/reflection-removal|Reflection Removal]] (specifically for flat glass surfaces like [[entities/windows|windows]]), incorporating more of Adobe's existing [[concepts/ai-technologies|AI technologies]].

Smith concludes by encouraging viewers to consider the evolving landscape of photography. He poses the question of whether phone cameras [[entities/will|will]] eventually replace traditional cameras, or if traditional cameras will adapt to a more compact form factor, but emphasizes that computational photography is clearly the future. He invites viewers to share their thoughts in the comments.
