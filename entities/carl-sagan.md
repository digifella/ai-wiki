# Carl Sagan
## Overview
Carl Edward Sagan (November 9, 1934 – December 20, 1996) was an American astronomer, cosmologist, astrophysicist, astrobiologist, [[entities/tasia-custode|author]], [[concepts/science|science]] communicator, and [[concepts/science|science]] advocate. He is known for his work in popularizing science through books such as *[[entities/contact|Contact]]* and *The Demon-Haunted [[entities/earth|World]]*, as well as the television series *Cosmos: A Personal Voyage*. Sagan also made significant scientific contributions, including research on planetary surfaces, atmospheres, and the [[concepts/start-of-life|origins of life]].

### Key Themes
- **[[concepts/extraterrestrial-life|Extraterrestrial Life]] Communication**: Pioneered efforts to understand how we might communicate with intelligent extraterrestrial beings.
- **[[concepts/scientific-skepticism|Scientific Skepticism]] and [[concepts/purpose|Reason]]**: Advocated for a critical approach to understanding claims in science.
- **Philosophical Implications of Science**: Explored the impact of scientific discoveries on human culture, [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]], religion, and society.
- **Mathematical [[concepts/encoding|Encoding]] in Science Fiction**: In his novel *[[entities/contact|Contact]]*, depicted the concept of [[concepts/advanced-intelligence|advanced intelligence]] embedding messages within [[concepts/transcendental-numbers|transcendental numbers]] such as $\pi$, specifically through patterns found in base 11.
