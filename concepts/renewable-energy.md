# Renewable Energy

**Renewable Energy** refers to energy derived from natural processes that are replenished at a higher rate than they are consumed. Key sources include Solar Power, Wind Power, Hydropower, and Geothermal Energy.

## Market Dynamics & Grid Stability

The integration of high volumes of variable renewable energy sources (VRES) significantly impacts grid stability and electricity pricing structures.

### The Duck Curve Phenomenon
*   Traditionally, the "[[concepts/duck-curve|Duck Curve]]" describes the net load profile in grids with high solar penetration, characterized by a steep ramp-up in the evening as solar generation drops while demand remains high.
*   This phenomenon creates challenges for grid operators, requiring fast-ramping backup power (often fossil fuels) to meet peak demand.

### Recent Developments in Australia
*   **Transformation of the Duck Curve:** Recent data indicates that the traditional duck curve is being flattened and transformed in the Australian Energy Market Operator.
*   **Role of [[concepts/battery-storage|Battery Storage]]:** The deployment of Battery Energy Storage Systems is driving lower [[concepts/electricity-prices|electricity prices]] for households and small businesses.
    *   Batteries absorb excess solar generation during midday peaks.
    *   They discharge during evening peaks, reducing reliance on expensive peaker plants.
    *   This arbitrage and frequency control mechanism stabilizes prices.
