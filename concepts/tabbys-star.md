# Tabby's Star

**Tabby's Star** (formal designation: [[concepts/kic-8462852|KIC 8462852]]) is a main-sequence star located in the constellation Cygnus, approximately 1,470 light-years from Earth. It gained notoriety for its irregular and significant flux dips, which initially sparked speculation regarding Dyson spheres or other megastructures.

## Recent Developments: Chromatic Dust Hypothesis

Recent analysis suggests the dimming events are caused by [[concepts/chromatic-dust|chromatic dust]] clouds rather than artificial structures. This hypothesis is detailed in the following note:

- [[lab-notes/2026-08-14-Tabbys-Star-Dimming-Mystery-Chromatic-Dust-Explained-by|Tabby's Star Dimming Mystery: Chromatic Dust Explained by Companion Planet]]

### Key Findings from PBS Space Time Analysis
- **Source:** [[entities/pbs-space-time|PBS Space Time]] video *"Astronomy's Weirdest Star Has Been a Mystery for a Decade. We May Have Solved It."*
- **Core Theory:** The [[concepts/irregular-dimming|irregular dimming]] patterns are likely explained by the presence of a [[concepts/companion-planet|companion planet]] interacting with a debris disk or dust cloud.
- **Mechanism:** As the companion planet orbits, it perturbs the surrounding dust, creating clumps that pass in front of the star, causing the observed non-periodic and asymmetric light curves.
- **Implication:** This provides a natural astrophysical explanation for the anomalies, reducing the need for exotic SETI-related hypotheses.
