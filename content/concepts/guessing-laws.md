# Guessing Laws

Guessing Laws is the foundational first step in [[concepts/feynman|Richard Feynman]]'s three-step [[concepts/scientific-method|scientific method]]. In this phase, a scientist formulates a hypothesis or educated guess about how a natural phenomenon works, before proceeding to mathematical computation or empirical [[concepts/testing|testing]]. The "guess" is not arbitrary speculation but rather an informed conjecture based on existing knowledge, observations, or [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|theoretical frameworks]].

## Role in the Scientific Process

Within Feynman's framework, Guessing Laws precedes the two subsequent steps: [[concepts/compute|Compute]] (deriving predictions from the hypothesis through mathematical analysis) and Compare (validating those predictions against observations from [[entities/nature|nature]]). The hypothesis guides what aspects of a system to model mathematically and what experiments or observations are most relevant to test. A well-formulated guess channels scientific effort toward productive investigation rather than undirected observation.

## Practical Significance

The emphasis on guessing as a distinct step acknowledges that the initial creative act of proposing a mechanism or principle is itself a critical part of science. Rather than viewing [[concepts/hypothesis-formation|hypothesis formation]] as informal or non-scientific, Feynman's method treats it as a formal, necessary phase. This approach highlights how scientific progress requires both imaginative conjecture and rigorous validation.
