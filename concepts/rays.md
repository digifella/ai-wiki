# Rays

Rays are cartilaginous fish belonging to the superorder Batoidea, establishing them as close relatives of sharks and chimaeras. Like their shark counterparts, rays possess skeletons composed entirely of cartilage rather than bone. This shared anatomical trait reflects a common ancestry within the larger group of cartilaginous fishes, highlighting the evolutionary success of this body plan which has persisted for over 450 million years.

The group is distinguished by a flattened body shape and expansive, wing-like pectoral fins. These adaptations allow rays to propel themselves through the water using undulating movements, a mode of locomotion that differs significantly from the typical swimming patterns of sharks. This morphology supports their diverse ecological roles, ranging from benthic dwelling to pelagic life.

In the context of shark evolution and adaptation during the Golden Age of Sharks, rays represent a significant branch of cartilaginous diversification. Their continued existence alongside sharks demonstrates the resilience and adaptability of the Chondrichthyes class, illustrating how distinct lineages can thrive within similar marine environments while maintaining their unique structural and functional characteristics.
