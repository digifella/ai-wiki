# Health Promotion

Health promotion is a core [[concepts/population-health|public health]] function that aims to enable individuals and communities to increase control over and improve their [[concepts/health|health]] and [[concepts/wellbeing|wellbeing]]. Rather than focusing solely on disease treatment, health promotion takes a broader approach by addressing the conditions and factors that support good health. This includes both individual behaviors and the social, economic, and environmental structures that influence health outcomes.

## Core Strategies

Health promotion employs multiple complementary strategies to achieve its goals. These include health education to build knowledge and [[concepts/conscious-thought|awareness]], community engagement to develop local capacity and ownership, policy development to create supportive environments, and advocacy to address underlying [[concepts/determinants-of-health|determinants of health]]. Effective health promotion recognizes that sustainable improvements require action across multiple levels—from individual [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] to systemic and [[concepts/organizational-change|structural change]].

## Role in Public Health

As a function of [[concepts/public-health|public health]] and [[concepts/community-health-nursing|community health nursing]] practice, health promotion works alongside [[concepts/primary-prevention-interventions-designed-to-prevent-disease-before-it-occurs|disease prevention]] and health [[concepts/secure|protection]]. It emphasizes upstream interventions that create conditions for health rather than waiting to treat illness after it occurs. This preventive orientation can reduce the burden of disease and improve population health outcomes more efficiently than treatment-focused approaches alone.
