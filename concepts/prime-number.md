# Prime Number

A prime number is a natural number greater than 1 that has no positive divisors other than 1 and itself. The sequence begins with 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, and 13. By definition, the number 1 is not considered prime. The number 2 is the only even prime; all other primes are odd.

## Fundamental Role in Mathematics

Every integer greater than 1 is either prime or can be expressed as a product of primes in a unique way, disregarding the order of the factors. This result, known as the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, establishes primes as the basic multiplicative building blocks of the natural numbers. Because of this property, primes are central to number theory and serve as the foundation for many mathematical proofs and structures.

## Applications in Cryptography

Prime numbers are critical in modern cryptography, particularly in public-key encryption systems such as RSA. The security of these systems relies on the computational difficulty of factoring the product of two large prime numbers. While multiplying two large primes is straightforward, reversing the process to determine the original primes from their product is computationally intensive, providing a robust barrier against unauthorized decryption.
