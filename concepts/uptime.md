# Uptime

Uptime refers to the period during which a system, service, or application remains operational and accessible without interruption. It is typically expressed as a percentage of total time over a defined period—such as a month or year—and serves as a standard measure of system reliability. For instance, 99.9% uptime means a system can tolerate approximately 43 minutes of downtime per month. The complementary metric, downtime, represents the periods when a system is unavailable or non-functional.

## Measurement and Standards

Uptime is commonly measured using Service Level Agreements (SLAs) that define acceptable thresholds for availability between a provider and a user. These agreements often specify the methodology for monitoring, such as whether downtime is calculated based on ping checks, application-level responses, or user-reported incidents. High-availability systems often target "five nines" (99.999%) uptime, requiring rigorous redundancy and failover mechanisms to minimize the risk of service interruption.

## Relevance to AI Assistants

For personal AI assistants like Clawdbot (OpenClaw), uptime is critical for ensuring consistent access to automated tasks and data processing. While local instances may rely on the host machine's stability, cloud-hosted variants depend on the underlying infrastructure's reliability. Maintaining high uptime ensures that scheduled jobs, real-time interactions, and data synchronization occur without unexpected gaps, thereby preserving the utility and trustworthiness of the assistant.
