# Millennium Prize Problems

The **Millennium Prize Problems** are seven fundamental unsolved problems in mathematics identified by the Clay Mathematics Institute (CMI) in 2000. Each problem carries a $1 million prize for its solution. As of 2026, six remain unsolved; the P vs NP Problem is the only one with a widely accepted resolution pending final verification, while recent developments suggest potential breakthroughs in others.

## The Seven Problems

1.  **Poincaré Conjecture**: Solved by Grigori Perelman (2003).
2.  **Hodge Conjecture**: Unsolved. Relates algebraic geometry to topology.
3.  **Riemann Hypothesis**: Unsolved. Concerns the distribution of [[concepts/prime-numbers|prime numbers]] via the Riemann zeta function.
4.  **P vs NP Problem**: Unsolved. Questions whether every problem whose solution can be quickly verified can also be quickly solved.
5.  **Navier-Stokes [[concepts/existence-and-smoothness|Existence and Smoothness]]**: Unsolved. Concerns the [[entities/stokes|existence and smoothness]] of solutions to the [[entities/navier|navier-stokes-equations]]. Recent AI-driven analysis suggests a potential resolution regarding [[concepts/fluid-flow|fluid flow]] [[concepts/blow-up|blow-up]]. See [[lab-notes/2026-09-20-AI-Solves-Navier-Stokes-Millennium-Prize-Fluid-Flow-Blow|AI Solves Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize: Fluid Flow Blow-Up Explained]].
6.  **Yang-Mills Existence and Mass Gap**: Unsolved. Relates to quantum field theory and the mass gap in Yang-Mills theory.
7.  **Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture**: Unsolved. Relates to the algebraic properties of elliptic curves.
