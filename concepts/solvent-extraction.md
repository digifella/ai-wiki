# Solvent Extraction

**Solvent extraction** (also known as liquid-liquid extraction) is a method to separate compounds or [[concepts/metal|metal]] complexes, based on their relative solubilities in two different immiscible liquids, usually water (polar) and an organic solvent (non-polar). It is a fundamental unit operation in [[concepts/chemical-engineering|chemical engineering]] and food processing.

## Principles
- **Partition Coefficient**: The distribution of a solute between two phases is governed by its partition coefficient ($K_D$).
- **Immiscibility**: The two solvents must be immiscible to form distinct phases for separation.
- **Selectivity**: The solvent must selectively dissolve the target compound while leaving impurities in the original [[concepts/phase|phase]].

## Applications in Food Science
Solvent extraction is widely used in the food industry for removing specific components or extracting flavors.

### Decaffeination and De-alcoholization
Recent analyses highlight the evolution of methods for removing caffeine from coffee/tea and alcohol from beverages [[lab-notes/2026-06-24-Decaffeination-and-De-alcoholization-Processes-History-a|Decaffeination and De-alcoholization Processes: History and Scientific Methods]]. Key points include:
- **[[concepts/historical-context|Historical Context]]**: Early methods utilized harsh chemicals, leading to modern shifts toward [[concepts/space-jetpacks|safer]], selective solvents.
- **Chemical [[concepts/causes|Mechanisms]]**: The process relies on the differential solubility of caffeine or ethanol in specific organic solvents (e.g., ethyl acetate, methylene chloride) or supercritical fluids (e.g., [[concepts/supercritical-CO2|supercritical CO2]]).
- **[[concepts/non-alcoholic-beverages|Non-Alcoholic Beverages]]**: Similar extraction principles are applied to remove alcohol from wine and beer while preserving flavor profiles, a topic detailed in [[lab-notes/2026-06-24-Decaffeination-and-Non-Alcoholic-Beverages-Methods-Histo|Decaffeination and Non-Alcoholic Beverages: Methods, History, and Chemistry]].
