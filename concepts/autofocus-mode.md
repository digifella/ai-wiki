# Autofocus Mode

Autofocus mode defines the operational strategy a Fujifilm camera employs to detect subjects and adjust lens elements for optimal [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]]. Rather than relying on the photographer to manually rotate the focus ring, the system analyzes scene data to determine the correct focal plane. This automation is particularly useful in dynamic environments where subjects move or where rapid shooting speeds are required.

[[entities/fujifilm|Fujifilm cameras]] typically offer distinct autofocus modes to suit different shooting [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]]. Single-shot autofocus ([[concepts/single-autofocus|AF-S]]) locks focus on a stationary subject when the shutter button is half-pressed, maintaining that distance until the image is captured. [[concepts/continuous-autofocus|Continuous autofocus]] (AF-C) actively tracks [[concepts/moving-subjects|moving subjects]], continuously adjusting focus as the distance between the camera and subject changes. This mode is essential for sports, wildlife, or any situation involving unpredictable motion.

The underlying mechanism relies on phase-detection and contrast-detection technologies embedded within the camera's sensor. By evaluating [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] levels or [[concepts/phase|phase]] differences across the frame, the camera calculates the necessary lens [[concepts/exercise|movement]] to achieve maximum [[concepts/clarity-slider|clarity]]. Users can often customize the focus area, selecting between wide zones, specific points, or face/eye detection to prioritize the intended subject within the [[concepts/writing|composition]].
