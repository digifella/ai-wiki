# Carl Jung

Carl Jung (1875–1961) was a Swiss psychiatrist and psychoanalyst who founded analytical psychology, a major school of depth psychology. He studied medicine at the University of Zurich and developed an early interest in psychiatric practice, eventually becoming a prominent figure in the psychoanalytic movement of the early twentieth century.

## Relationship with Freud

Jung collaborated extensively with Sigmund Freud beginning in the early 1900s, and Freud identified him as his intellectual successor. However, their professional relationship deteriorated during the 1910s as Jung’s theoretical divergences regarding the nature of the libido and the unconscious became irreconcilable with Freud’s psychoanalytic framework. This split led Jung to develop his own distinct approach to the psyche.

## Key Concepts

Jung introduced several foundational concepts that remain central to modern psychology and popular culture. He proposed the collective unconscious, a layer of the unconscious mind shared by all humans, which contains archetypes—universal, archaic patterns and images. He also developed the theory of psychological types, distinguishing between introverted and extroverted attitudes, which later influenced personality assessment tools.

## Later Life and Legacy

In his later years, Jung focused on the process of individuation, the psychological integration of the conscious and unconscious parts of the self. He established the Bollingen Tower as a retreat for his work and continued to write and lecture until his death in 1961. His extensive corpus of work has had a profound impact on psychiatry, anthropology, literature, and religious studies, establishing analytical psychology as a enduring alternative to Freudian psychoanalysis.
