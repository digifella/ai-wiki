# Phd

A PhD (Doctor of Philosophy) is a terminal research degree awarded by universities across numerous academic disciplines, including sciences, engineering, humanities, and medicine. The degree represents the highest level of academic qualification in most fields and signifies expertise and original contribution to a discipline. PhD programs exist in virtually every major academic field, from traditional domains like physics and literature to contemporary fields such as computer science and public health.

## Academic Requirements

PhD programs typically require several years of study beyond the bachelor's or master's level. Candidates must conduct original research, often culminating in a dissertation that contributes new knowledge to their specific field. Admission usually involves rigorous evaluation of academic records, research proposals, and letters of recommendation.

## Professional Context

Holders of a PhD are frequently referred to by the title "Doctor" in professional and academic settings. The degree is a prerequisite for many careers in academia, high-level research, and specialized professional roles. In medical contexts, individuals may hold both a medical degree (MD) and a PhD, indicating dual expertise in clinical practice and scientific research.
