# Chest X Rays

Chest X-[[concepts/rays|rays]] are radiographic images of the thorax used to diagnose and monitor a wide range of pulmonary, cardiac, and mediastinal conditions. They remain one of the most commonly ordered imaging studies in [[entities/clinical-practice|clinical practice]] due to their [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]], low cost, and minimal radiation [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] compared to other imaging modalities. A standard chest X-ray examination typically includes posteroanterior (PA) and lateral views, which provide complementary perspectives for evaluating the structures within the chest cavity.

## Clinical Applications

Chest X-rays are employed to identify abnormalities such as pneumonia, lung cancer, heart failure, and pneumothorax. They are also utilized to assess the placement of medical devices, such as endotracheal tubes and central venous catheters, and to evaluate the status of the ribs and spine. In emergency settings, they serve as a rapid screening tool for acute respiratory distress or trauma.

## Technical Considerations

The interpretation of chest X-rays relies on the visualization of soft tissue, bone, and air [[concepts/density|density]] differences. While highly effective for initial assessment, they have limitations in detecting small lesions or early-stage diseases that may require advanced imaging techniques like [[concepts/x-ray-tomography|computed tomography]] (CT) for further characterization. The quality of the image is heavily dependent on patient positioning and [[concepts/camera-settings|exposure settings]], necessitating skilled radiological analysis for accurate diagnosis.
