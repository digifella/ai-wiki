# Protons

Protons are positively charged subatomic particles that form a fundamental component of atomic nuclei. Each proton carries one elementary positive charge (approximately 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ coulombs) and has a mass of approximately 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kilograms, roughly 1,836 times heavier than an electron. The number of protons in an atom's nucleus determines its atomic number, which identifies the chemical element and establishes its position in the periodic table. Protons are stable particles and, along with neutrons, constitute the dense core of every atom.

## Properties and Composition

Protons are classified as baryons and are composed of quarks—specifically two up quarks and one down quark—held together by the strong nuclear force through gluon exchanges. The proton's internal structure gives rise to its magnetic moment and contributes to its interactions with electromagnetic fields. Despite their fundamental role in atomic structure, protons exhibit complex behavior at high energies that continues to be investigated through particle physics research.

## Cosmic Ray Detection

High-energy protons constitute a significant portion of cosmic rays, the energetic particles constantly bombarding Earth from space. These cosmic ray protons are detected and studied by specialized observatories such as the Large High Altitude Air Shower Observatory (LHAASO) in China, which observes the secondary particle cascades produced when cosmic rays interact with the upper atmosphere. Studying cosmic ray protons provides insights into astrophysical processes, including supernova remnants, active galactic nuclei, and other high-energy phenomena in the universe.
