# Safety Regulations & Engineering Practices

Safety encompasses the technologies, regulations, and [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] practices designed to prevent accidents, minimize injury during collisions, and enhance system visibility. Key components include active safety systems (e.g., ABS, ESC), passive safety features (e.g., Airbags, Crumple Zones), and rigorous manufacturing standards.

## Automotive Safety Systems

### Visibility and Signaling Systems
Effective signaling is critical for collision avoidance. Modern standards mandate high-mounted third brake lights to improve rear visibility and reduce rear-end collisions.

*   **[[concepts/third-brake-light|Third Brake Light]] Integration**: Modern vehicles utilize high-mounted stop lamps to provide clear braking intent. ## Engineering Process & Structural Integrity Failures

Safety regulations are not limited to automotive contexts; they extend to high-risk engineering domains such as deep-sea exploration. Failures in these areas often stem from process inadequacies rather than material defects alone.

*   **[[lab-notes/2026-07-09-OceanGate-Titan-Implosion-Inadequate-Engineering-and-Man|OceanGate Titan Implosion: Inadequate Engineering and Manufacturing Failures]]**: Investigations into the [[concepts/oceangate-titan|OceanGate Titan]] [[concepts/submersible-implosion|submersible implosion]] concluded that the cause was not the carbon fiber material itself, but an **inadequate [[concepts/engineering-process|engineering process]]**.
