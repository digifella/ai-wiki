# 2026 04 11 Ritter Island 1888 Volcanic Flank Collapse And Mega Tsunami

On 13 March 1888, [[entities/ritter-island|Ritter Island]], a volcanic island located in the [[entities/bismarck-sea|Bismarck Sea]] north of [[entities/papua-new-guinea|Papua New Guinea]], experienced a catastrophic [[concepts/landmass-collapse|flank collapse]]. Approximately 7 cubic kilometers of rock and volcanic material failed suddenly and entered the ocean in a single event. This incident is recognized as one of the largest documented volcanic flank collapses in recorded history, driven by a combination of magmatic activity, hydrothermal alteration that weakened the volcanic structure, and gravitational instability.

The displacement of such a massive volume of material generated a significant [[concepts/tsunami|tsunami]] that propagated across the Pacific Ocean. The wave caused widespread devastation along the coastlines of New Britain and other nearby islands in the region. Historical accounts describe the tsunami as highly destructive, resulting in substantial loss of life and damage to coastal settlements. The event remains a critical case study in understanding the hazards associated with submarine volcanic flank failures and their potential to generate large-scale tsunamis.
