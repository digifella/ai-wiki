# AI Policy

[[concepts/regulation|AI Policy]] encompasses the formal guidelines, regulations, and frameworks that govern the development, deployment, testing, and operational behavior of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems. These [[concepts/policies|policies]] operate across multiple organizational and governmental levels—from internal corporate standards to national legislation and international agreements—establishing requirements and expectations for how [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] are created, evaluated, and used in practice. As AI systems become more prevalent across sectors, policy frameworks have become increasingly necessary to address technical, ethical, and societal concerns.

## Scope and Levels

AI policies vary significantly in their scope and authority. At the organizational level, companies establish internal standards for [[concepts/knowledge-acquisition|model development]], safety testing, and responsible deployment. National governments develop legislation addressing specific concerns such as algorithmic [[concepts/opacity|transparency]], data [[concepts/privacy|privacy]], and liability for AI-caused harm. International bodies work toward harmonized standards and agreements, though global consensus remains limited. Policies may focus on particular applications—such as AI use in hiring, criminal justice, or [[concepts/health|healthcare]]—or establish broader principles applicable across domains.

## Key Governance Areas

Common policy areas include requirements for algorithmic transparency and explainability, [[concepts/data-management|data governance]] and privacy protections, bias and fairness assessment, human oversight [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]], and [[concepts/accountability-frameworks|accountability frameworks]] for failures or harms. Policies also address [[concepts/ai-system|AI system]] testing and validation before deployment, ongoing monitoring during operation, and documentation requirements. Some frameworks establish specialized review processes for high-risk applications, while others create liability structures to assign [[concepts/accountability|responsibility]] when AI systems cause damage or discrimination.

## Evolution and Challenges

AI policy frameworks continue to develop as technologies advance and [[concepts/societal-impact|societal impact]] becomes clearer. A significant challenge lies in creating policies flexible enough to accommodate rapid technical progress while remaining substantive enough to meaningfully constrain harmful outcomes. The international nature of [[concepts/ai-development|AI development]] and deployment complicates regulatory [[concepts/coordination|coordination]], and disagreement persists over appropriate levels of restriction versus [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] enablement.
