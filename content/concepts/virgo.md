# Virgo

Virgo is a gravitational wave detector located in Cascina, Italy, operated by the European Gravitational-Wave Observatory (EGO). As one of the primary instruments in the global network of gravitational wave observatories alongside LIGO's facilities in the [[entities/united-states|United States]], Virgo uses laser interferometry to detect ripples in spacetime caused by violent cosmic events.

## Detection Capabilities

The detector is sensitive to [[concepts/gravitational-waves|gravitational waves]] produced by merging [[concepts/neutron-stars|neutron stars]] and [[concepts/black-holes|black holes]] across a range of masses. In recent observations, Virgo has contributed to the detection of signals from sub-solar mass compact objects—bodies with masses below that of our Sun. These detections are of particular scientific interest because they may provide evidence for [[concepts/primordial-black-hole|primordial black holes]], hypothetical objects formed in the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]] rather than through conventional stellar collapse.

## Scientific Significance

The detection of sub-solar mass mergers through gravitational waves helps constrain models of primordial black hole populations and their potential role as [[concepts/wimp-particles|dark matter candidates]]. By combining observations from Virgo with data from other detectors in the gravitational wave network, astronomers can better characterize the properties and [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] of compact objects throughout the universe, offering insights into fundamental processes occurring both in the early cosmos and in [[concepts/stellar-evolution|stellar evolution]].
