# Pareto Frontier

A Pareto frontier is a set of solutions to a multi-objective optimization problem where no single [[concepts/solution|solution]] is superior across all objectives simultaneously. Each solution on the frontier represents a point where improving performance on one objective necessarily requires degrading performance on [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] least one other objective. The concept is named after economist Vilfredo Pareto and is fundamental to understanding trade-offs in [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] contexts.

## Characteristics

Solutions on a Pareto frontier are termed Pareto optimal or Pareto efficient. A solution is Pareto optimal if there exists no alternative solution that improves at least one objective without worsening another. The frontier itself forms a boundary in the objective space, with all feasible solutions either lying on this boundary or in dominated regions where at least one other solution outperforms them across all objectives.

## Applications

Pareto frontiers appear across diverse fields including economics, engineering, machine [[concepts/learning|learning]], and resource allocation. In [[concepts/machine-learning|machine learning]] contexts, they are used to evaluate systems with competing [[concepts/performance-data-gathering|performance metrics]]—such as retrieval [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]] versus [[concepts/computational-efficiency|computational efficiency]] in [[concepts/knowledge-bases|information retrieval]] systems. Decision-makers use Pareto frontiers to understand feasible trade-offs and select solutions appropriate to their specific priorities rather than seeking a single "best" answer.
