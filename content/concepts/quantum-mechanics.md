# Quantum Mechanics

Quantum mechanics is the fundamental [[concepts/theory|theory]] describing the behavior of matter and energy [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] atomic and subatomic [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]]. Developed primarily in the early twentieth century through the work of physicists including Max [[entities/planck|Planck]], Werner Heisenberg, and Erwin Schrödinger, it replaces classical Newtonian mechanics in regimes where objects are extremely small or energies are extremely high. The theory successfully explains phenomena such as atomic [[concepts/structure|structure]], chemical bonding, and the behavior of semiconductors, and has enabled technological [[concepts/software|applications]] ranging from lasers to medical imaging devices.

## Core Principles

Quantum mechanics introduces several counterintuitive principles that distinguish it from classical [[concepts/physics|physics]]. Particles exhibit wave-particle duality, behaving as both discrete objects and waves depending on how they are measured. The uncertainty principle, formulated by Heisenberg, establishes fundamental limits on how precisely certain pairs of [[concepts/material-properties|physical properties]]—such as position and momentum—can be simultaneously known. The theory is inherently probabilistic rather than deterministic; it predicts the probability distributions of measurement outcomes rather than definite future states.

## Foundational Debates

Despite its empirical success, quantum mechanics remains philosophically contested regarding the [[entities/nature|nature]] of [[concepts/physical-reality|physical reality]]. The interpretation of quantum mechanics involves questions about whether measurement fundamentally determines physical properties or merely reveals pre-existing ones, and whether [[concepts/local-realism|local realism]]—the idea that objects possess definite properties independent of observation and that no [[concepts/power|influence]] travels faster than [[concepts/light|light]]—can be maintained. Alternatives to standard interpretations, including [[concepts/superdeterminism|superdeterminism]], propose that apparent quantum randomness may emerge from deeper deterministic structures, though such proposals remain speculative and outside mainstream physics.
