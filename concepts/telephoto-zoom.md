# Telephoto Zoom

Telephoto Zoom is a photographic technique that combines telephoto lenses with panoramic image stitching to create extended wide-angle compositions. Rather than using a camera's built-in panorama mode, the method involves capturing multiple overlapping frames using a telephoto lens, then digitally stitching the images together into a single panoramic photograph. This approach circumvents the limitations of standard smartphone panorama functions and produces results with greater detail and tonal consistency.

## Technical Process

The technique requires capturing a series of photographs with significant overlap between consecutive frames while maintaining consistent exposure and focus settings. A telephoto lens compresses perspective and magnifies distant subjects, allowing the photographer to capture detail across a wider area than would be possible with a standard lens at the same shooting distance. The overlapping frames are then aligned and blended using image stitching software to create a seamless panoramic composition.

## Advantages and Applications

Because telephoto lenses reduce perspective distortion compared to wide-angle lenses, panoramic images created through this method maintain natural proportions across their full width. This makes the technique particularly useful for landscape photography, architectural documentation, and situations where capturing broad scenes without geometric distortion is important. The method also allows photographers to work with optical magnification while achieving panoramic coverage, making it suitable for distant subjects that would appear too small in a traditional wide-angle panorama.
