# Focus Stacking

[[concepts/blur-correction|Focus stacking]] is a photography technique that combines multiple [[concepts/images|images]] taken at different focal distances to produce a single photograph with greater overall [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]] than any individual shot. The method is particularly valuable in [[concepts/landscape-photography|landscape photography]], where photographers often struggle to keep both foreground and background elements in sharp focus simultaneously due to the optical limitations of depth of field.

## The Technical Process

The photographer captures a series of photographs of the same scene, adjusting the focus point between each shot to cover different planes within the composition. These images are then combined during post-processing, typically using [[concepts/software|software]] like Lightroom or specialized stacking tools, which selectively blends the sharpest portions from each frame. The result is a final image where details across the entire depth of the scene maintain acceptable sharpness.

## Practical Applications

Focus stacking is most useful when working with wide-angle lenses or when a scene contains significant depth—such as landscapes with prominent foreground elements and distant mountains. It allows photographers to achieve the visual effect of increased depth of field without resorting to extremely small apertures, which can introduce diffraction and other optical artifacts. Modern editing software has increasingly automated this process, making the technique more accessible to photographers of varying skill levels.
