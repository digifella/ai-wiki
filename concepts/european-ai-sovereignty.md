# European AI Sovereignty

**European AI Sovereignty** refers to the strategic imperative for the European Union and its member states to develop, control, and regulate [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] infrastructure and models within its own jurisdiction. This concept emphasizes reducing dependency on non-European tech giants, ensuring data privacy under GDPR, and fostering indigenous innovation in high-performance computing and [[concepts/demystifying-llms|large language models]] (LLMs).

## Key Drivers
- **Strategic Autonomy:** Reducing reliance on US and Chinese AI ecosystems.
- **Regulatory Compliance:** Aligning with the [[concepts/eu-ai-act]] and data sovereignty laws.
- **[[concepts/global-competitiveness|Economic Competitiveness]]:** Building a robust European AI industry capable of competing globally.

## Major European Initiatives
- **Mistral AI:** A leading French AI company developing frontier models.
- **Gaia-X:** A European data infrastructure initiative.
- **EuroHPC:** Joint undertaking for supercomputing resources.

## Case Study: Mistral Large 4
Mistral AI's latest frontier model, **Mistral Large 4**, represents a significant milestone in European AI sovereignty. Developed entirely in Europe from scratch, it demonstrates the region's capability to produce world-class [[concepts/model-customization|open-weight models]].

### Technical Specifications
- **Architecture:** [[concepts/mixture-of-experts|Mixture of Experts]] (MoE) with 1 trillion total parameters and 49 billion active parameters.
- **Licensing:** Open-weight and open-source, promoting transparency and local deployment.
- **Capabilities:** Multimodal input support, unifying [[concepts/instruction-following|instruction following]] and complex reasoning.
- **Development:** Built from scratch in Europe, ensuring supply chain independence.

### Performance & Impact
- Demonstrates competitive performance against global proprietary models.
- Supports the ecosystem of [[concepts/open-source-ai]] by providing high-quality weights for local fine-tuning and deployment.
- Highlights the viability of European talent and infrastructure in frontier AI research.

## Challenges
- **Compute Access:** Securing sufficient high-performance computing resources compared to US counterparts.
- **Talent Retention:** Preventing brain drain to non-European markets.
- **Funding:** Sustaining long-term investment for frontier model development.
