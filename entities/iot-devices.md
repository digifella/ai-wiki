# IoT Devices

[[concepts/internet-of-things|IoT devices]] are electronic devices equipped with [[concepts/embedded-sensors|embedded sensors]], [[concepts/central-processing-units|processors]], and connectivity capabilities that enable them to collect, process, and transmit data over networks. These devices range from consumer products like smart home systems and wearable fitness trackers to industrial equipment and environmental monitoring sensors. The defining characteristic of IoT devices is their ability to operate with minimal human intervention while continuously gathering information from their environment.

## Applications in Healthcare and Wellness

Wearable IoT devices have become particularly significant in [[concepts/allied-health|allied health]] and personal [[concepts/wellness|wellness]] monitoring. Devices worn on the body—such as smartwatches, fitness bands, and specialized health monitors—use embedded sensors to track metrics including heart rate, [[concepts/exercise|movement]] patterns, [[concepts/sleep-quality|sleep quality]], and other physiological [[concepts/indicators|indicators]]. This continuous data collection enables both individuals and [[concepts/health|healthcare]] providers to monitor health status in real-time without requiring traditional clinical visits.

## Technical Considerations

The deployment of IoT devices involves considerations around [[concepts/energy-efficiency|power efficiency]], data [[concepts/security|security]], and [[concepts/on-device-processing|on-device processing]] capabilities. Recent developments in efficient [[concepts/artificial-intelligence-models|machine learning models]], including techniques for running [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]] on [[concepts/resource-constrained-devices|resource-constrained devices]], have expanded the analytical capabilities of IoT hardware. This allows more sophisticated data analysis to occur directly on the device rather than requiring constant transmission to external servers.
