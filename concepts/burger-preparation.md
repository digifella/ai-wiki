# Burger Preparation

## Core Principles
Burger [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] balances structural [[concepts/honesty|integrity]] with tenderness. The primary variable is the degree of **Myosin** extraction and development, which dictates [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]] ranging from loose and tender to dense and snappy.

## Myosin Development
Myosin is the protein responsible for binding [[concepts/ground-meat|ground meat]]. Its development level determines the final [[concepts/texture|texture]] of the patty.

- **Low Development (Tender Burgers):** Minimal mixing preserves individual fat and muscle particles. Results in a loose, crumbly texture that is tender but may fall apart if handled roughly.
- **High Development (Snappy Texture):** Vigorous mixing or pounding extracts myosin, creating a sticky protein matrix that binds meat and fat tightly. This yields a dense, elastic texture common in Sausage and Meatballs.

## Technique Integration
- **Mixing Strategy:** For standard burgers, mix gently just until combined to avoid over-developing myosin. For cohesive patties or meatballs, mix vigorously until the meat becomes tacky and sticky.
- **[[concepts/thermal-regulation|Temperature Control]]:** Keep meat cold during mixing to prevent fat from smearing, which can interfere with [[concepts/sausage-binding|myosin binding]] and lead to dry results.
