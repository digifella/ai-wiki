# Six Sigma

Six Sigma is a data-driven project management and quality improvement methodology designed to reduce defects and variation in business processes. The name derives from a statistical concept: six standard deviations from the mean, which in manufacturing corresponds to a defect rate of 3.4 per million opportunities. Originally developed by Motorola in the 1980s, Six Sigma was later popularized and refined by General Electric under Jack Welch's leadership. The methodology has since been adopted across manufacturing, healthcare, finance, and service industries.

## Core Approach

The methodology relies on a structured problem-solving framework known as DMAIC, which stands for Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, and Control. This cycle guides teams through identifying process problems, collecting data to understand current performance, analyzing root causes, implementing solutions, and establishing controls to sustain improvements. For new product or process development, the DMADV framework (Define, Measure, Analyze, Design, Verify) is often used to ensure that new designs meet customer requirements and Six Sigma standards from the outset.

## Organizational Roles

Six Sigma utilizes a hierarchical structure of certified professionals to drive implementation. Green Belts typically handle smaller projects while maintaining their regular job functions, while Black Belts lead complex, cross-functional initiatives full-time. Master Black Belts serve as internal coaches and mentors, providing technical expertise and training to Green and Black Belts. This tiered system ensures that quality improvement efforts are supported by varying levels of specialized knowledge and authority.
