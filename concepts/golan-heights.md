# Golan Heights

**Definition:** A basalt plateau in the Levant spanning ~1,800 km², positioned between the [[entities/jordan|Jordan]] River, Sea of Galilee, and Mount Hermon. Historically administered by Syria, captured by [[entities/israel]] during the Six-Day War, and annexed in 1981 under Israeli law. Annexation remains unrecognized internationally per UN [[concepts/security|Security]] Council [[concepts/solution|Resolution]] 242 and UN Security Council Resolution 497.

## Geography & Strategic Value
- Elevation 600–2,814 m provides observational and artillery dominance over northern [[entities/israel]], the Valley of Jezreel, and the Bekaa Valley in southern [[entities/lebanon|Lebanon]].
- Critical hydrological [[entities/nodejs|node]]: captures ~30% of [[entities/israel]]'s freshwater runoff, feeds the Banias River, and sustains the Sea of Galilee watershed.
- Serves as military depth for the northern front, mitigating direct border [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] to Syria and Hezbollah.
- Hosts Druze-majority communities alongside Israeli civilian settlements and IDF forward operating [[concepts/number-systems|bases]].

## Historical & Political Context
- 1967: Israeli occupation followed Syrian shelling of northern towns.
- 1973: Heavily contested during the Yom Kippur War; subsequent 1974 Israel–Syria [[concepts/disengagement|Disengagement]] Agreement established a UN buffer zone (UNDOF).
- 1981: Golan Heights Law extended Israeli civil jurisdiction; rejected by UN General Assembly.
- Post-2011: Syrian Civil War collapsed [[entities/syria|Damascus]]' oversight, enabling Iranian proxy entrenchment along the Green Line and intensified cross-border incidents.
