# Version Numbers

Version numbers are numerical or alphanumeric identifiers assigned to different releases of [[concepts/software|software]], [[concepts/hardware|hardware]], or other products to track changes and updates over time. They serve as a concise way to distinguish between iterations, allowing users and developers to understand which variant they are using and whether updates are available. Version numbering systems vary widely across industries and organizations, ranging from simple sequential integers to complex multi-part schemes.

## Common Versioning Schemes

The most prevalent versioning approach is semantic versioning, typically expressed as MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH (for example, 2.3.1). The major number increments for significant, often backward-incompatible changes; the minor number increases for new features that maintain backward compatibility; and the patch number updates for bug fixes and minor improvements. Other schemes include date-based versioning, which incorporates release dates, and continuous versioning, where builds are numbered sequentially without thematic grouping.

## Practical Applications

Version numbers play a critical role in software maintenance, allowing developers to communicate the scope and [[entities/nature|nature]] of changes to users. They facilitate dependency management in complex systems where one program may rely on specific versions of libraries or frameworks. Clear versioning conventions also help users [[entities/make|make]] informed decisions about whether to upgrade, particularly when updates address [[concepts/security|security]] vulnerabilities or introduce breaking changes that might affect their workflows.
