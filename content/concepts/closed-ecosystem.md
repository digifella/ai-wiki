# Closed Ecosystem

A closed ecosystem is a self-contained environmental system with minimal or no exchange of matter with the outside world, though energy typically enters (such as sunlight). In a closed ecosystem, organisms, soil, water, and atmosphere interact in a cycle where waste products from one organism become resources for another, [[entities/theoretically-media|theoretically]] allowing the system to sustain itself indefinitely without external inputs beyond energy.

## Biosphere 2

[[entities/biosphere-2|Biosphere 2]] was a large-scale experimental facility built in Arizona in the late 1980s to test whether humans could live in a sealed, self-sustaining ecosystem. The [[concepts/structure|structure]] enclosed 3.15 acres and contained multiple biomes including rainforest, desert, savanna, and ocean environments, along with human habitats. Between 1991 and 1993, teams of researchers lived inside the sealed structure for extended periods to study how closed systems function and respond to human presence.

## Scientific Insights

The [[concepts/controlled-climate-experiment|Biosphere 2 experiment]] revealed critical complexities in ecological systems that had not been fully appreciated. Researchers discovered unexpected population fluctuations, nutrient cycling challenges, and the difficulty of maintaining stable conditions in isolation. The project demonstrated that even carefully designed closed systems exhibit emergent behaviors and instabilities that are difficult to predict or [[concepts/power|control]], providing valuable lessons about Earth's biosphere and the interdependence of ecological processes.
