# Sugar Cane Beetle

The **[[concepts/sugar|Sugar]] Cane Beetle** refers to various beetle species that act as significant pests in sugar cane [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]]. Historically, its control has driven major ecological interventions, most notably the introduction of the [[concepts/cane-toad]] in Australia.

## Historical Context & Biological Control

The introduction of the [[concepts/cane-toad]] (*Rhinella marina*) to Australia in 1935 was a direct response to infestations by sugar cane beetles. The toads were originally sourced from Hawaii, which had previously imported them from Puerto Rico, in an attempt to establish a biological control agent.

*   **Initial Intent:** The primary [[concepts/purpose|objective]] of the 1935 introduction was to combat sugar cane beetles by having toads prey on them.
*   **Ecological Outcome:** The strategy failed because cane toads do not effectively prey on the beetles in the field, leading instead to a massive invasive species [[concepts/crisis|crisis]].
*   **Current Control Strategies:** Modern efforts focus on managing the toad population itself, leveraging unique biological traits such as [[concepts/intraspecific-cannibalism]] to reduce their numbers.
