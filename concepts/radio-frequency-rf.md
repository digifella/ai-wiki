# Radio Frequency RF

Radio frequency refers to [[concepts/light|electromagnetic radiation]] in the frequency range typically used for wireless communications, spanning from approximately 3 kHz to 300 GHz. In [[concepts/satellite-communication|satellite communication]] systems, RF signals serve as the fundamental mechanism for transmitting data between ground stations and orbiting satellites. The characteristics of RF signals—including frequency band, [[concepts/network-speed|bandwidth]], power, and modulation—determine the capacity, range, and efficiency of wireless networks.

## RF Signal Characteristics

The performance of RF systems depends on several key parameters. Frequency band selection affects propagation characteristics and atmospheric absorption; higher frequencies enable greater bandwidth but experience more signal loss over distance. Power levels determine transmission range and signal strength at receiving terminals. Modulation schemes encode information onto the carrier signal and influence data rate and noise tolerance. Together, these factors establish the trade-offs between coverage area, data capacity, and equipment complexity in satellite networks.

## RF Architecture in Satellite Systems

Satellite communication systems employ RF architectures that translate signals between ground stations and space-based terminals. This includes transmit chains that generate and amplify RF signals, receive chains that capture and process incoming signals, and antenna systems that focus electromagnetic energy. The architecture must account for factors such as Doppler shift from satellite motion, signal path loss over the distance to orbit, and interference management when multiple satellites or ground systems operate in adjacent frequency bands. Modern satellite constellations like Starlink v3 utilize RF architectures optimized for Ka-band and other higher frequencies to achieve the bandwidth necessary for broadband internet service at scale.
