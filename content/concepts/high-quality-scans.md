# High Quality Scans

High quality scans enable film photographers to digitize their negatives and [[entities/google-slides|slides]] at home without relying on commercial lab services. This approach offers cost savings over time and allows greater control over the final image quality and digital workflow. Scanning at home requires basic equipment and understanding of technique, making it accessible to photographers at various skill levels.

## Equipment and Setup

A dedicated film scanner or a camera-based scanning setup forms the foundation of [[concepts/camera-scanning|home film scanning]]. Key considerations include the scanner's optical resolution, the quality of the light source, and proper film holders or mounting systems that ensure [[concepts/sharpness|sharp focus]] and even [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] across the frame. Many photographers find camera-based scanning—using a [[concepts/macro-lens|macro lens]] and controlled lighting—to be a flexible and cost-effective alternative to dedicated film scanners.

## Technique and Process

Successful [[concepts/film-scanning|film scanning]] depends on careful [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to focus, exposure, and color [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]]. Proper lighting setup, whether using a light box or backlight, ensures even illumination across the film. Color correction and dust removal in post-processing are typically necessary steps. Taking time to develop a consistent workflow and understanding how different film types and densities affect scan results will improve output quality and efficiency.
