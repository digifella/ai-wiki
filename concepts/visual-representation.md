# Visual Representation

Visual representation functions as a critical interface between internal subjective experience and external communication. This process is particularly significant for conditions that are often misunderstood or invisible to observers, such as dyslexia. By converting abstract cognitive phenomena into tangible forms, visual representation renders private experiences public and intangible concepts concrete. This transformation facilitates both personal insight and shared understanding among those who do not share the specific perceptual challenges.

Artist Kim Percy has utilized this approach to visualize the experience of dyslexia through her artwork. Her work serves to bridge the gap between the internal cognitive processes of dyslexic individuals and the external perception of neurotypical observers. By translating these complex neurological experiences into visual art, Percy aims to make the invisible aspects of dyslexia accessible and comprehensible to a broader audience.

The artistic practice focuses on rendering the specific perceptual challenges associated with dyslexia into concrete forms. This method allows for a more direct engagement with the condition, moving beyond textual descriptions to offer a sensory understanding. The resulting works provide a platform for dialogue, enabling those who do not experience dyslexia to gain insight into the daily realities faced by those who do.
