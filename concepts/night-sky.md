# Night Sky

The appearance of the sky at night, characterized by darkness interspersed with stars, planets, and other celestial bodies. This phenomenon is central to several cosmological questions, most notably [[concepts/olbers-paradox]].

## Key Concepts

### Olbers' Paradox
The observation that if the universe were infinite, static, and uniformly filled with stars, the night sky should be as bright as the surface of a star. The fact that it is dark implies specific constraints on the universe's age, expansion, and structure.

- **The Paradox**: In an infinite, eternal universe, every [[concepts/line-of-sight|line of sight]] should eventually end on a star, making the entire sky luminous.
- **Resolution**: The universe has a finite age (~13.8 billion years) and is expanding. Light from distant stars has not had enough time to reach us, and the expansion of space redshifts light from distant sources, reducing its energy.
