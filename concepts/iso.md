# ISO

**ISO** (International Organization for Standardization) in photography refers to the sensitivity of the camera's image sensor to [[concepts/light|light]]. It is a critical component of the [[concepts/exposure-triangle]], alongside [[concepts/aperture]] and [[concepts/shutter-speed]].

## Core Concepts
- **Sensitivity Control**: Higher ISO values increase sensor sensitivity, allowing for faster shutter speeds or smaller apertures in low-light conditions.
- **Noise Trade-off**: Increasing ISO typically introduces [[concepts/digital-image-noise|digital noise]] or grain, reducing image quality and [[concepts/dynamic-range|dynamic range]].
- **Base ISO**: The native sensitivity of the sensor (often ISO 100 or 64) where the sensor performs optimally with the highest dynamic range and least noise.

## Strategic Usage
- **Low Light**: Increase ISO when aperture and shutter [[concepts/speed|speed]] limits are reached to maintain [[concepts/exposure|exposure]].
- **Action Photography**: Use higher ISO to freeze motion with fast shutter speeds.
- **Noise Management**: Modern [[concepts/image-processing]] [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]] allow for higher usable ISOs, but best practice remains keeping ISO as low as possible for the given lighting conditions.

## Professional Workflow Insights
Recent analysis of professional workflows highlights a preference for strategic semi-automatic modes over pure manual control to balance speed and quality:
- **Efficiency Over Perfection**: Professionals often prioritize capturing the moment over perfect [[concepts/manual-settings|manual settings]], leveraging Semi-Automatic Modes like Aperture Priority or Shutter Priority.
- **ISO Auto-Range**: Many pros use Auto ISO with a defined maximum limit to handle rapid lighting changes without manual intervention.
