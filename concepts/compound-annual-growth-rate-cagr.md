# Compound Annual Growth Rate Cagr

Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) is a financial metric that measures the mean annual growth rate of an investment or business metric over a specified period of time, assuming profits are reinvested at the end of each year. Unlike simple year-over-year growth rates, CAGR smooths out the effects of volatility and fluctuations in returns to provide a single, standardized growth figure. This makes it particularly useful for comparing performance across different time periods or investments with varying levels of annual volatility.

## Calculation

CAGR is calculated by dividing the ending value of an investment by its beginning value, raising the result to the power of one divided by the number of years, and then subtracting one. The formula is expressed as $CAGR = (Ending Value / Beginning Value)^{(1 / Number of Years)} - 1$. This geometric progression approach ensures that the rate reflects the compounded nature of growth, providing a more accurate representation of long-term performance than arithmetic averages.

## Applications and Limitations

In business strategy, CAGR is commonly used to evaluate the historical performance of stocks, mutual funds, and business units, as well as to project future revenue or earnings growth. It allows stakeholders to [[concepts/feynmans-three-step-scientific-method|compare]] the growth trajectories of different [[concepts/nodes|entities]] regardless of their size or initial investment scale. However, CAGR does not account for the risk or volatility within the period; it presents a smoothed curve that may mask significant interim fluctuations, potentially giving an overly optimistic view of an investment's stability.
