# Voice Assistants

[[concepts/tone|Voice]] assistants are [[concepts/software|software]] applications designed to recognize and respond to spoken [[concepts/commands|commands]] from users. They process natural language input through [[concepts/speech-recognition|speech recognition]] technology and execute tasks or provide information based on the user's request. Common examples include [[entities/amazon|Amazon]]'s Alexa, [[entities/apple|Apple]]'s [[concepts/siri|Siri]], [[concepts/google-search|Google]] Assistant, and [[entities/microsoft|Microsoft]]'s Cortana. These systems typically rely on cloud-based processing to interpret complex queries and access relevant data or services.

## Core Functionality

Voice assistants perform a [[concepts/range|range]] of functions including answering questions, controlling smart home devices, playing media, setting reminders, making calls, and initiating transactions. The effectiveness of a voice assistant depends on its speech recognition [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]], natural language understanding [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]], and [[concepts/integration|integration]] with other services and platforms. Most modern voice assistants operate continuously in listening mode, activated by specific wake words or phrases.

## Privacy and Technical Considerations

Voice assistants raise [[concepts/privacy|privacy]] concerns due to their always-on microphones and data collection practices. Users' voice recordings, interaction histories, and personal information may be stored and analyzed by service providers. The technology also [[concepts/faces|faces]] limitations including difficulty understanding regional accents, background noise interference, and challenges with context-dependent or ambiguous requests. [[concepts/security|Security]] vulnerabilities can expose users to unauthorized access or command injection attacks.
