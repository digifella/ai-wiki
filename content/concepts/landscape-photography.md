# Landscape Photography

Landscape photography captures expansive natural or built environments, emphasizing wide vistas, spatial depth, and environmental context. The discipline encompasses diverse subjects including mountains, coastlines, forests, deserts, and urban skylines, with composition techniques specifically designed to convey scale and atmospheric conditions.

## Panoramic Stitching

Panoramic stitching extends the [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] of standard smartphone panorama modes by combining multiple overlapping [[concepts/images|images]] into seamless, high-resolution compositions. This technique allows photographers to capture fields of view wider than any single lens can achieve, producing images with exceptional detail and dimensionality. The process involves precise overlap alignment between consecutive frames and sophisticated [[concepts/software|software]] that blends exposures and corrects perspective distortion.

## Technical Considerations

Achieving [[concepts/sharpness|sharpness]] across landscape compositions requires careful [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to depth of field, focus distance, and camera stabilization. Optimal results typically involve smaller apertures (f/8 to f/16 [[concepts/range|range]]), appropriate shutter speeds to eliminate motion blur, and tripod use to maintain [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] during multi-frame capture. [[concepts/digital-post-processing|Digital post-processing]] tools have increasingly automated aspects of landscape enhancement, including selective [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] and [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|tonal adjustments]] tailored to environmental elements like skies and water.
