# European Union

The European Union is a political and economic union comprising 27 member states, primarily located in Europe. Established through the Maastricht Treaty in 1992, the EU evolved from the European Economic Community, which was founded in 1957. The union operates as both a single market and a political entity, with the primary objective of eliminating internal trade barriers and facilitating the free movement of goods, services, capital, and people among its member states.

## Institutions and Governance

The EU is governed through a complex institutional framework consisting of several key bodies. The European Commission functions as the executive branch, responsible for proposing legislation and enforcing EU law. The Council of the European Union represents member state governments, while the European Parliament is directly elected by EU citizens and shares legislative power. The Court of Justice ensures compliance with EU law and resolves disputes between member states and institutions.

## Membership and Scope

Member states participate in various EU policies and programs to different degrees. All members participate in the single market, though not all have adopted the euro as their currency. The Schengen Area, which encompasses most EU states plus several non-members, allows passport-free travel across participating countries. EU citizens enjoy certain rights including the ability to live, work, and study in any member state.
