# Primary Prevention Interventions Designed To Prevent Disease Before It Occurs

Primary prevention encompasses health interventions applied before disease occurs, targeting populations or individuals who are currently healthy. These interventions work by modifying risk factors, strengthening protective factors, or creating conditions that prevent disease onset. Primary prevention differs from secondary prevention, which identifies early-stage disease through screening and early treatment, and tertiary prevention, which manages existing disease to prevent complications.

## Types and Examples

Primary prevention interventions operate at both population and individual levels. Population-level approaches include public health measures such as water fluoridation, vaccination programs, and food safety regulations. Individual-level interventions focus on personal behavior change and include health education, counseling on diet and exercise, smoking cessation support, and immunization. Environmental modifications—such as workplace safety standards, traffic calming measures, and pollution control—also constitute primary prevention by reducing exposure to hazards.

## Mechanisms of Action

These interventions prevent disease through several mechanisms. Some reduce exposure to pathogens or harmful substances, while others strengthen immune function or physiological resilience. Health promotion activities build knowledge and skills that support healthy decision-making. Policy and regulatory approaches create structural conditions that make healthy choices easier and protect populations from preventable harms.

Primary prevention is generally considered the most cost-effective approach to improving population health, as preventing disease onset avoids the substantial costs of treatment and reduces disease burden. However, effectiveness depends on sustained implementation, equitable access, and engagement from both individuals and communities.
