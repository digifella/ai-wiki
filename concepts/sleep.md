# Sleep

Sleep is a fundamental biological process essential to physical health, cognitive function, and emotional well-being. During sleep, the body undergoes critical restorative processes including memory consolidation, hormonal regulation, and immune system maintenance. These physiological functions are necessary for learning, growth, and disease prevention across all age groups.

## Sleep and Adolescent Development

Adolescents require 8-10 hours of sleep per night according to sleep science research, yet many teens experience insufficient sleep due to biological shifts in circadian rhythms, academic demands, and social factors. Sleep deprivation during the teenage years can impair emotional regulation, decision-making, and academic performance. Adequate sleep has been identified as a science-backed behavior that supports teen well-being and is discussed as part of evidence-based wellness practices by researchers like Dr. Laurie Santos.

## Sleep and Overall Health

Chronic sleep insufficiency is linked to increased risk of various health conditions including obesity, cardiovascular disease, and mental health challenges such as anxiety and depression. Sleep quality and quantity are modifiable factors that individuals can influence through behavioral practices, making sleep an accessible component of personal health promotion and disease prevention strategies.
