# Four Year Experiment

A four-year experiment is an extended research, development, or manufacturing project that requires sustained iterative work to validate and refine a complex process or product. Such projects are characterized by long timelines, repeated cycles of [[concepts/testing|testing]] and improvement, and significant resource commitment before achieving market viability or proof of concept.

## The Smarter Scrubber Case Study

[[entities/destin-sandlin|Destin Sandlin]], host of the YouTube channel [[entities/smarter-every-day|Smarter Every Day]], undertook a four-year experiment to design and manufacture a grill brush entirely in the [[entities/united-states|United States]]. The project, called the Smarter Scrubber, aimed to create a safer alternative to traditional wire bristle brushes, which can shed bristles into food. Sandlin's [[concepts/motivation|motivation]] for the project was rooted in his parents' background as [[entities/union-autoworkers|union autoworkers]], reflecting a commitment to [[concepts/domestic-manufacturing|domestic manufacturing]] and quality craftsmanship.

The experiment required Sandlin to navigate manufacturing processes, material selection, durability testing, and cost optimization while maintaining American production. The extended timeline allowed for [[concepts/iterative-refinement|iterative refinement]] of the product design and validation of manufacturing methods before commercial release, demonstrating the practical demands of bringing a physical product to market while adhering to specific constraints around sourcing and labor.
