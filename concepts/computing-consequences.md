# Computing Consequences

[[concepts/computation|Computing]] Consequences is the second stage in [[concepts/feynman|Feynman]]'s three-step [[concepts/scientific-method|scientific method]], serving as the critical bridge between formulating a hypothesis and conducting empirical testing. This [[concepts/phase|phase]] involves the rigorous mathematical and logical work required to translate a qualitative theoretical guess into precise, quantitative predictions. It ensures that the proposed [[concepts/theory|theory]] is not merely a [[concepts/storytelling|narrative]] but a framework capable of generating specific outcomes that can be verified or falsified.

The process requires deriving [[concepts/observable-consequences|testable predictions]] that logically follow from the initial assumptions of the theory. Scientists use established mathematical tools to calculate what observable phenomena should occur if the hypothesis is correct. This step transforms abstract concepts into concrete expectations, defining exactly what data needs to be collected and what results would constitute evidence for or against the proposed model.

By establishing these clear criteria for [[concepts/verification|verification]], Computing Consequences prevents [[concepts/ambiguity|ambiguity]] in the scientific process. It allows researchers to design experiments that directly challenge the theory, ensuring that the subsequent comparison with nature is meaningful. Without this computational derivation, a hypothesis remains untestable, and the scientific method cannot proceed to its final stage of validation against empirical reality.
