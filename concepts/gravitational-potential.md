# Gravitational Potential

**Gravitational potential** ($\Phi$) is a scalar field representing the potential energy per unit mass at a point in a gravitational field. It is defined such that the gravitational field strength $\vec{g}$ is the negative gradient of the potential: $\vec{g} = -\nabla \Phi$.

## Core Concepts

- **Definition**: For a point mass $M$, $\Phi(r) = -\frac{GM}{r}$.
- **Superposition**: The total potential of a system is the algebraic sum of potentials from individual masses.
- **Relation to Kinematics**: In stable circular orbits, the centripetal acceleration is provided by the gradient of the potential: $v^2/r = d\Phi/dr$.

## Galactic Dynamics & Dark Matter

The distribution of gravitational potential in galaxies is a primary probe for [[concepts/dark-matter]].

- **Rotation [[concepts/curves|Curves]]**: The orbital [[concepts/velocity|velocity]] $v(r)$ of stars/gas is derived from the enclosed mass $M(<r)$ via $v(r) = \sqrt{r \frac{d\Phi}{dr}}$.
- **Flat Rotation Curves**: Observations show $v(r)$ remains constant at large radii, implying $M(<r) \propto r$. This requires a massive dark matter halo since visible matter [[concepts/density|density]] drops off rapidly.
- **[[concepts/modified-gravity|Modified Gravity]] ([[concepts/modified-newtonian-dynamics-mond|MOND]])**: Alternative theories propose modifications to Newtonian dynamics at low accelerations to explain flat curves without dark matter.
