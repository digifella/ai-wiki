# High Quality Scans

High quality scans enable film photographers to digitize their negatives and [[concepts/google-slides|slides]] at home without relying on commercial lab services. This approach offers [[concepts/leftover-utilization|cost savings]] over time and allows greater control over the final image quality and digital workflow. Scanning at home requires basic equipment and understanding of technique, making it accessible to photographers at various [[concepts/skill|skill]] levels.

## Equipment and Setup

A dedicated film scanner designed for 35mm negatives or [[entities/google-slides|slides]] is the foundation of home scanning. Consumer-grade scanners typically offer resolutions between 2400 and 4000 DPI, which is sufficient for most photographic applications. Essential accessories include a dust-removal tool or compressed air, film holders or mounts designed for your scanner model, and a clean workspace. Proper lighting during the scanning setup helps ensure accurate color and [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] capture.

## Scanning Technique

Achieving high quality scans requires careful handling of film to minimize dust and scratches before scanning begins. Cleaning film gently with appropriate solvents and microfiber cloths removes particles that would otherwise be captured in the digital image. Scanner settings should be adjusted based on the film type—color negatives, [[concepts/black-and-white|black and white]] negatives, and slides each benefit from different software profiles. Taking time to adjust exposure, [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], and color balance within the scanner software before saving produces superior results compared to correcting scans later in post-processing.

## Workflow Considerations

Organizing scanned files systematically from the outset prevents confusion and makes future editing more efficient. Saving scans at the scanner's maximum quality setting preserves detail, with compression applied only to copies used for sharing or editing. Many home scanners include software for [[concepts/batch-processing|batch processing]] multiple frames, which reduces the time required to digitize an entire roll of film while maintaining consistent quality across images.
