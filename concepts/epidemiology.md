# Epidemiology

Epidemiology is the scientific study of the distribution and determinants of health-related states or events in specified populations, and the application of this study to control health problems. Unlike clinical medicine, which focuses on the diagnosis and treatment of individual patients, epidemiology examines health outcomes at the population level. This field investigates how diseases, injuries, and other health conditions are distributed across communities and how these patterns change over time, providing the foundational data for public [[concepts/healthcare-policy|health policy]] and [[concepts/vaccination|preventive medicine]].

The discipline relies on a combination of observational and experimental research designs to identify risk factors and causal [[concepts/relationships|relationships]]. Key methodological approaches include cohort studies, case-control studies, and randomized controlled trials, which allow researchers to track [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] to potential causes and subsequent health outcomes. By analyzing data on incidence, prevalence, and mortality, epidemiologists can map the spread of infectious diseases, identify environmental or behavioral risk factors for chronic conditions, and evaluate the effectiveness of interventions.

The primary goal of epidemiology is to inform [[concepts/population-health|public health]] practice by developing strategies for [[concepts/health-promotion|disease prevention]] and health promotion. Findings from epidemiological research guide vaccination [[concepts/software|programs]], screening guidelines, and health regulations aimed at reducing the burden of disease on society. As a core component of evidence-based medicine, it bridges the gap between biological [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] observed in laboratories and the complex realities of human health in diverse populations.
