# Fuel Injection

**Fuel injection** is the process of delivering fuel into an [[concepts/internal-combustion-engine|internal combustion engine]] in a controlled manner, replacing or augmenting traditional carburetor systems. Modern implementations are predominantly managed by [[concepts/electronic-engine-control]] units (ECUs), which optimize performance, efficiency, and emissions.

## Electronic Engine Control Integration

The transition from purely mechanical fuel delivery to computer-controlled systems has been fundamental to modern engine design. Contrary to the misconception that modern engines are "too computerized," electronic control has been a standard and beneficial evolution for decades.

### Key Benefits & History
*   **[[concepts/accuracy|Precision]] Control:** ECUs allow for real-time adjustment of fuel-air ratios based on sensor data (temperature, [[concepts/pressure|pressure]], throttle position), optimizing combustion efficiency fuel-air-ratio.
*   **Dispelling the "Too Computerized" Myth:** As noted in recent analyses, engines have not been fully mechanical in decades; electronic control is a proven, beneficial technology rather than a fragile novelty [[concepts/engine-management-systems|Electronic Engine Control]]: Benefits, History, and Dispelling "Too Computerized" Myth.
*   **Performance & Emissions:** Electronic management enables tighter control over emissions standards and improves engine responsiveness compared to mechanical carburetors.
*   **[[concepts/software-reliability|Reliability]]:** Modern electronic systems are robust and have replaced complex mechanical linkages that were prone to wear and calibration drift.
