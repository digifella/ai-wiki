# Nano Electronics

Nano electronics refers to the design, manufacture, and application of electronic devices and systems at the nanometer scale, typically involving structures smaller than 100 nanometers. At this scale, classical [[concepts/physics|physics]] gives way to quantum mechanical effects, which fundamentally alter how electrons behave and how devices function. The field encompasses transistors, [[concepts/memory|memory]] devices, sensors, and integrated circuits built from individual atoms or molecules.

## Current Applications and Development

Nano electronics already exists in practical form within modern semiconductor manufacturing, where transistor gate lengths have reached the low nanometer range. Current research focuses on extending Moore's Law as conventional [[concepts/silicon|silicon]]-based approaches approach physical limits, exploring alternatives such as carbon nanotubes, graphene, and other nanomaterials. These materials offer potential advantages in [[concepts/heat-dissipation|heat dissipation]], power efficiency, and performance [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]].

## Challenges and Future Directions

Manufacturing at the nanoscale presents significant technical challenges, including precise control during fabrication, managing quantum tunneling effects, and ensuring device [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] and [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]]. Researchers are investigating bottom-up assembly approaches, where structures are built from atomic or molecular components, alongside refinements to top-down lithographic techniques. The field remains largely experimental beyond conventional semiconductor applications, with commercialization of many proposed nano-electronic devices still years away.
