# Oil Formation

Oil formation is a geological process spanning millions of years, beginning with the accumulation of organic material in ancient marine and sedimentary environments. Plankton, algae, and other biological matter settled in oxygen-poor basins where decomposition was limited, allowing organic material to concentrate in the sedimentary record. As successive layers of sediment accumulated above this organic-rich material, the weight of overlying strata increased pressure while geothermal heat from Earth's interior rose with depth.

## Transformation and Maturation

Under high pressure and temperature conditions, the accumulated organic matter undergoes chemical changes, transforming into kerogen and eventually into hydrocarbons such as oil and natural gas. This maturation process requires specific geological conditions, including sufficient heat and time, to convert the precursor material into liquid and gaseous fuels. The resulting hydrocarbons migrate through porous rock layers until they are trapped by impermeable cap rocks, forming reservoirs.

## Geological Context and Oxidation

While oil formation relies on anoxic conditions for preservation, the broader geological history involves periods of high atmospheric oxygen that influenced global rock oxidation. These oxidative events contributed to the distinctive geological features of regions such as the red centre of Australia, where iron-rich rocks oxidized to form extensive red landscapes. Although coal formation is often associated with similar organic accumulation processes, the specific link between high-oxygen periods, rock oxidation, and the resulting geological features highlights the complex interplay between biological, chemical, and tectonic forces in Earth's history.
