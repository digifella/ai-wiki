# Service Delivery

Service delivery in the context of traumatic birth experiences refers to the support systems and interventions designed to assist fathers and partners during and after childbirth complications. Research indicates that approximately 30% of births are experienced as traumatic by attendant partners, highlighting a significant gap in current healthcare provision. This high prevalence suggests that many maternity services do not adequately account for the psychological and emotional needs of non-birthing partners during labor and delivery.

## Current Gaps

Most maternity services focus primarily on the birthing person and infant, often leaving partners without adequate preparation, information, or emotional support during labour. Partners may experience distress from witnessing complications, feeling helpless, or lacking understanding of clinical procedures and their necessity. The absence of structured support can lead to lasting psychological effects, including post-traumatic stress symptoms, anxiety, and relationship strain.

## Support Interventions

Effective service delivery in this area includes antenatal education that prepares partners for potential complications, clear communication from clinical staff during labour, and access to post-birth debriefing or counselling. Some maternity units have begun implementing partner support protocols, designated support persons during high-risk procedures, and follow-up mental health services. Training healthcare staff to recognize and address partners' distress as part of routine care has also shown promise in improving overall birth experiences.
