# Quantum Computing

## Overview
Quantum computing leverages quantum mechanical phenomena such as superposition and entanglement to process information. While theoretically capable of solving specific problems exponentially faster than classical computers, the field faces significant practical and theoretical hurdles.

## Critical Assessment & Practical Failures
Recent critical analyses highlight a disconnect between industry optimism and engineering reality. Key concerns include:

*   **Overhype vs. Reality:** Persistent optimism often obscures the severe limitations of current hardware, particularly regarding error rates and scalability Quantum Computing Overhype and Practical Failures: A Critical Assessment.
*   **Hardware Instability:** Qubits remain highly susceptible to decoherence, requiring extreme environmental controls that limit practical deployment.
*   **[[concepts/error-correction|Error Correction]] Bottlenecks:** The overhead required for quantum error correction currently demands more physical qubits than are practically available for useful [[concepts/computation|computation]].
*   **Limited Utility:** For most real-world applications, classical algorithms remain superior or competitive, with quantum advantage restricted to narrow, specific use cases.
