# Astronaut Safety

**Astronaut Safety** protocols and equipment are critical for survival during Extravehicular Activity (EVA) in the [[concepts/vacuum-of-space|vacuum of space]]. This domain includes mobility systems like [[concepts/man-maneuvering-units|Man-Maneuvering Units]] (MMUs) and protective life-support systems such as advanced [[concepts/space-suits|space suits]].

## Mobility and Retrieval Systems
**Man-Maneuvering Units** (MMUs), also known as [[concepts/astronaut-maneuvering-units]] (AMUs) or colloquially as [[concepts/space-jetpacks|space jetpacks]], are nitrogen-propelled devices used by astronauts to maneuver in the [[concepts/vacuum-of-space|vacuum of space]] during EVA.

### Technical Overview & Evolution
- **Functionality**: Uses compressed nitrogen gas expelled through nozzles to provide [[concepts/engine-thrust|thrust]] for translation and rotation in microgravity.
- **Design**: Typically backpack-mounted, allowing hands-free operation. Critical for safety and [[concepts/document-retrieval|retrieval]] operations.
- **[[concepts/historical-context|Historical Context]]**:
	- Evolution from earlier simpler restraint systems to fully autonomous maneuvering capabilities.
	- Designed to address the critical risk of an astronaut drifting away from the [[concepts/space-station]] or Space Shuttle.

## Protective Technology and Materials
Modern astronaut safety relies heavily on the intricate technology of space suits, which serve as miniature spacecraft protecting against extreme [[concepts/environmental-risks|environmental hazards]].

- **Extreme Environment [[concepts/secure|Protection]]**: Space suits are engineered to counteract the lethal conditions of outer space, including vacuum, radiation, and temperature extremes.
- **Material [[concepts/science|Science]]**: Advanced materials are utilized to ensure durability and flexibility while maintaining pressure [[concepts/honesty|integrity]].
