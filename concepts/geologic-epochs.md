# Geologic Epochs

The Carboniferous and Permian periods represent two critical intervals in [[entities/earth|Earth]]'s history, spanning approximately 360 to 250 million years ago. During the Carboniferous period (360–300 million years ago), vast swamp forests dominated continental interiors, accumulating immense quantities of plant material that eventually formed major coal deposits. This period was characterized by elevated [[concepts/oxygen-saturation|atmospheric oxygen levels]], reaching approximately 35 percent—significantly higher than today's 21 percent.

## Atmospheric Composition Changes

The high oxygen concentrations of the Carboniferous period had profound effects on both atmospheric chemistry and life on Earth. As massive quantities of plant material accumulated in swamps and coastal environments, carbon dioxide was sequestered from the [[concepts/earths-atmosphere|atmosphere]] and incorporated into what would become coal, oil, and other fossil fuel deposits. This process gradually altered atmospheric gas ratios throughout the period.

The subsequent Permian period (300–250 million years ago) continued these trends but with different outcomes. As swamp forests declined and continental configurations changed, atmospheric oxygen levels gradually decreased from their Carboniferous peaks. [[concepts/carbon-dioxide-levels|Carbon dioxide levels]] fluctuated as the vast accumulation of organic material continued to be buried and transformed, though at different rates than the previous period. The end of the Permian marked one of Earth's most severe extinction events, partly driven by these atmospheric and climatic shifts.

## Significance for Fossil Fuel Formation

Together, the Carboniferous and Permian periods account for much of the world's economically significant coal and petroleum reserves. The unique environmental conditions of these eras—particularly the abundance of vegetation, favorable burial conditions, and changing atmospheric chemistry—created ideal circumstances for [[concepts/oil-formation|fossil fuel formation]] that shaped energy resources available to humanity millions of years later.
