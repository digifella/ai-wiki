# Wide Angle Composition

Wide angle composition in photography extends beyond the simple goal of capturing expansive landscapes. While panoramic techniques are often associated with creating broad vistas, photographers employ [[concepts/panorama-stitching|panorama stitching]] for additional compositional and technical advantages that justify the method's more deliberate approach compared to standard single-frame photography.

## Extending Field of View

Panoramic stitching allows photographers to achieve effective wide-angle perspectives that may exceed the capabilities of their available lenses. By combining multiple overlapping frames, a photographer can create an image with a broader field of view than any single lens in their kit could produce, effectively functioning as an ultra-wide-angle or fish-eye equivalent without requiring expensive specialized equipment.

## Enhanced Image Quality and Resolution

Beyond capturing width, panorama stitching produces [[concepts/images|images]] with significantly higher resolution and detail than single frames. By merging multiple exposures, the resulting composite image contains substantially more pixel information, allowing for larger prints and greater flexibility in post-processing without quality degradation.

## Compositional Control

Shooting panoramas requires deliberate framing choices across multiple shots, which can encourage photographers to think more critically about composition. The sequential [[entities/nature|nature]] of panning across a scene allows for thoughtful consideration of element placement and balance across the full frame, potentially resulting in more intentional and refined compositions than quickly capturing a single wide-angle shot.
