# Prime Number

A prime number is a natural number greater than 1 that possesses exactly two distinct positive divisors: 1 and itself. By this definition, the number 1 is excluded from the set of primes. The sequence of prime numbers begins with 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, and 13. Notably, 2 is the only even prime number; all other primes are odd, as any other even number greater than 2 is divisible by 2.

## Mathematical Significance

The significance of prime numbers in mathematics is anchored by the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic. This theorem states that every integer greater than 1 is either a prime itself or can be represented as a unique product of prime numbers, regardless of the order of the factors. This property establishes primes as the fundamental building blocks of the natural numbers under multiplication, analogous to how atoms form molecules in chemistry.

## Applications in Cryptography

In the domain of logic and cryptography, prime numbers are critical for public-key encryption systems such as RSA. The security of these systems relies on the computational difficulty of factoring the product of two large prime numbers. While multiplying two large primes is computationally easy, reversing the process to determine the original primes from their product is extremely difficult for classical computers, providing a robust foundation for secure digital communication.
