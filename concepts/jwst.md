# Jwst

The [[concepts/james-webb-space-telescope|James Webb Space Telescope]] (JWST) is a space-based infrared observatory developed through international collaboration between [[concepts/nasa|NASA]], the [[entities/esa|European Space Agency]] (ESA), and the [[entities/canada|Canadian]] Space Agency (CSA). Launched on December 25, 2021, it succeeded the [[concepts/hubble-space-telescope|Hubble Space Telescope]] as humanity's primary space-based observatory for astronomical research. The telescope operates at the [[concepts/lagrange-point-2|Sun-Earth L2]] Lagrange point, approximately 1.5 million kilometers from Earth, where gravitational mechanics allow it to maintain a stable orbital position relative to both bodies.

## Design and Capabilities

JWST features a primary mirror composed of 18 hexagonal segments made of beryllium, coated with [[concepts/major-gold-deposit|gold]] to maximize infrared reflectivity. Its large [[concepts/aperture|aperture]] and sensitive instruments allow it to observe the universe in infrared wavelengths, enabling the study of objects obscured by dust or too distant for [[concepts/light|visible light]] telescopes. The observatory is equipped with a five-layer sunshield that protects its instruments from the heat and light of the Sun, Earth, and Moon, maintaining the cold operating temperatures required for infrared detection.

## Scientific Objectives

The primary scientific goals of JWST include observing the formation of the first galaxies in the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]], studying the physical development of galaxies from the early universe to the present day, and investigating the formation of [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]] and planetary systems. By analyzing the light from distant celestial objects, the telescope aims to understand the evolution of the cosmos and the conditions necessary for the [[concepts/emergent-behavior|emergence]] of life. Its capabilities also extend to the detailed characterization of exoplanet atmospheres, providing data on their [[concepts/writing|composition]] and potential [[concepts/habitability|habitability]].
