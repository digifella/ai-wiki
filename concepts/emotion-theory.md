# Emotion Theory

Emotion [[concepts/theory|theory]] comprises scientific frameworks for understanding emotions as complex psychological and physiological phenomena. These theories examine how emotions arise, what purposes they serve, and how they influence human behavior and [[concepts/cognition|cognition]]. Emotion theory integrates insights from [[concepts/psychology|psychology]], [[concepts/neuroscience|neuroscience]], physiology, and [[concepts/behavioral-science|behavioral science]] to explain emotional [[concepts/experience|experience]] and its role in human functioning.

## Major Theoretical Approaches

Several foundational approaches have shaped emotion research. The James-Lange theory proposes that emotional experience follows physiological arousal, suggesting that individuals feel emotion because they perceive bodily changes. In [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], the Cannon-Bard theory argues that physiological arousal and emotional experience occur simultaneously and independently. The Schachter-Singer two-factor theory posits that emotion results from the interaction of physiological arousal and cognitive labeling of that arousal based on environmental context.

Contemporary models often emphasize the role of the brain's limbic system, particularly the amygdala, in processing emotional stimuli. Cognitive appraisal theories further suggest that the interpretation of a situation, rather than the situation itself, determines the emotional response. These frameworks collectively highlight the bidirectional relationship between biological processes and mental states, providing a comprehensive view of how humans perceive and [[concepts/react-framework|react]] to their environment.
