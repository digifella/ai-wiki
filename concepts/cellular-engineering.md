# Cellular Engineering

**Cellular [[entities/national-academies|Engineering]]** is the discipline of modifying living cells to perform specific functions, often by introducing new genetic material or synthetic pathways. It bridges [[concepts/digital-gene-technology|Synthetic Biology]], Genetic Engineering, and Biomedical Engineering to create cells with novel capabilities for therapeutic, industrial, or research purposes.

## Core Principles
- **Genetic Modification**: Altering the genome to express new traits or silence existing ones.
- **Metabolic Reprogramming**: Redirecting cellular [[concepts/yeast-fermentation|metabolism]] to produce desired compounds or energy sources.
- **Synthetic Pathways**: Introducing non-native biochemical pathways into host organisms.

## Key Applications
- **Therapeutics**: Engineering immune cells (e.g., CAR-T therapy) or stem cells for tissue repair.
- **Bio-production**: Using engineered microbes to produce pharmaceuticals, biofuels, or materials.
- **Disease Modeling**: Creating cellular models to study disease [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] and drug responses.

## Recent Developments
- **2026 Breakthrough in Metabolic Integration**: Research has advanced toward introducing plant-like metabolic capabilities into animal cells. Specifically, efforts to enable Photosynthesis in [[concepts/mammalian-cells|mammalian cells]] for disease treatment have emerged as a significant development.
