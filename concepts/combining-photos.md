[[concepts/selective-reveal|Combining photos]] is a creative technique in which multiple images are merged or layered together to create a single composite photograph. This practice allows creators to blend distinct visual elements, whether by placing photographs side by side, overlaying them with varying degrees of transparency, or seamlessly merging them into a unified [[concepts/writing|composition]]. The resulting image can communicate [[concepts/ideas|ideas]] or effects that would be difficult or impossible to capture in a single photograph, making it a valuable tool for visual [[concepts/storytelling|storytelling]] and artistic expression.

## Historical Development

Photo combining has roots in traditional darkroom photography, where multiple negatives could be printed onto a single sheet of light-sensitive paper through [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] layering or [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] techniques. These methods required careful planning and technical [[concepts/skill|skill]], as [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] were difficult once the print process had begun. The practice emerged early in photography's history as artists sought to expand the [[entities/medium|medium]] beyond simple documentation.

## Digital Methods

Modern photo combining is predominantly accomplished through digital [[concepts/image-editing|image editing]] software, which offers precise control over [[concepts/layer-styles|layer blending]], [[concepts/opacity|opacity]], and positioning. Digital tools allow creators to combine images with far greater flexibility than traditional methods, enabling [[concepts/non-destructive-editing|non-destructive editing]], easy experimentation, and seamless blending through various [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]]. This [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]] has made photo combining a widespread practice across professional photography, [[concepts/design|graphic design]], and amateur creative work.

## Applications

Photo combining serves diverse purposes across multiple fields, from creating surreal artistic compositions to practical applications like [[concepts/long-lens-photography|panoramic photography]], [[concepts/blur-correction|focus stacking]], and architectural visualization. Marketing and advertising frequently employ composite imagery to convey concepts or product benefits, while fine art photographers use the technique to explore conceptual themes and push the boundaries of photographic representation.
