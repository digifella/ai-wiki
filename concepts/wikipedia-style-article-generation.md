# Wikipedia Style Article Generation

Wikipedia style article generation refers to the automated creation of encyclopedic articles through multi-agent AI systems designed to research topics comprehensively and synthesize information into structured, verified knowledge. This approach emerged from research into how AI agents can produce long-form, factually grounded content that mirrors the standards of human-written reference materials. Rather than relying solely on statistical patterns in training data, these systems incorporate systematic research workflows, source validation, and multi-perspective information gathering to construct articles that are both informative and reliable.

## STORM Framework

Stanford's STORM (Synthesis of Topic Outline through Retrieval and Multi-perspective question-asking) represents a notable implementation of this approach. STORM operates through a multi-stage workflow in which AI agents conduct research by formulating and answering questions about a given topic, consulting diverse information sources, and organizing findings into a coherent outline. The system explicitly separates the research phase from the writing phase, allowing agents to gather perspectives from multiple angles before synthesizing final content. This methodology addresses limitations in standard language model generation, where factual accuracy and source grounding remain persistent challenges.

## Key Characteristics

Systems for Wikipedia-style article generation typically feature distributed workflows where different agents handle specialized tasks—such as question generation, source retrieval, perspective gathering, and writing. The resulting articles include citations and maintain structural conventions associated with reference materials, such as clear section hierarchies and definitions. The emphasis on verifiability distinguishes this approach from general text generation, as the system must maintain traceable connections between claims and source materials throughout the writing process.
