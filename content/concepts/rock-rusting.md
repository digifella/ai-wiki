# Rock Rusting

[[concepts/coal-formation|Rock rusting]] is a geological process in which iron-bearing minerals in rocks oxidize due to [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] to atmospheric oxygen, producing iron oxide compounds that impart a distinctive reddish coloration. This process occurred prominently during the [[concepts/high-oxygen-period|high oxygen period]] of Earth's history, when [[concepts/oxygen-saturation|atmospheric oxygen levels]] rose significantly, creating conditions favorable for widespread oxidation of surface and near-surface rocks.

The red coloration visible across [[concepts/red-centre-of-australia|Australia's red centre]]—including regions like Uluru and the broader arid interior—is largely the result of ancient rock rusting. Iron oxides such as hematite and goethite accumulated in rocks and soils during this oxidation period, creating the characteristic red and rust-colored landscapes that define these regions today. This geological coloration process operated in tandem with other significant developments of the same era, including the formation of major coal and oil deposits.

The timing and extent of rock rusting reflects broader changes in Earth's atmospheric composition and oxidative capacity. As oxygen accumulated in the atmosphere, it chemically weathered iron-rich minerals that had previously been stable in a lower-oxygen environment, fundamentally altering the appearance and properties of exposed rock formations across continents.
