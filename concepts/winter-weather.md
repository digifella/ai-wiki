# Winter Weather

Winter weather encompasses the atmospheric conditions and [[concepts/precipitation-patterns|precipitation patterns]] that characterize the winter [[entities/season|season]] in temperate and polar regions. These conditions result from the seasonal shift in [[concepts/solar-energy|solar radiation]] caused by [[entities/earth|Earth]]'s axial tilt, which [[concepts/causes|causes]] the winter hemisphere to receive less direct sunlight. This fundamental change in solar energy input leads to lower temperatures, reduced daylight hours, and altered [[concepts/atmospheric-dynamics|atmospheric circulation]] patterns that persist throughout the winter months.

## Temperature and Precipitation

Winter temperatures vary significantly depending on latitude and proximity to ocean systems. Polar and high-altitude regions [[concepts/experience|experience]] extreme cold, while temperate zones may see moderate temperature fluctuations above and below freezing. Precipitation during winter often falls as snow in colder regions, though rain, sleet, and freezing rain also occur.
