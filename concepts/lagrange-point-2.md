# Lagrange Point 2

**[[entities/lagrange-point-2|Lagrange Point 2]]** (L2) is a gravitationally stable location in space approximately 1.5 million kilometers from Earth, situated on the line extending from the Sun through the Earth. It is a preferred orbital position for deep-space observatories due to its thermal stability and unobstructed view of the universe.

## Key Characteristics
- **Gravitational Balance**: At L2, the combined gravitational pull of the Sun and Earth allows a spacecraft to orbit the Sun with the same period as Earth, remaining in a fixed position relative to our planet.
- **Thermal Environment**: The location allows for consistent shading from the Sun, Earth, and Moon, facilitating passive cooling for sensitive infrared instruments.
- **Communication**: Proximity to Earth enables high-bandwidth data transmission with minimal latency compared to deep-space locations.

## Notable Missions at L2
- [[concepts/james-webb-space-telescope]] (JWST): Primary infrared observatory.
- Planck: [[concepts/cosmic-background-radiation|Cosmic microwave background]] mapping.
- Euclid: Dark energy and [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] investigation.
- [[concepts/falcon-heavy|Roman Space Telescope]]: Wide-field survey for cosmology.

## Roman Space Telescope Integration
The [[concepts/nancy-grace-roman-space-telescope]] is a major upcoming mission utilizing the L2 environment. Recent updates highlight its operational readiness and long-term potential.

- **Mission Profile**: Designed to conduct wide-field surveys to study dark energy, exoplanets, and infrared astrophysics.
- **Projected Lifespan**: Initial assessments indicate a robust operational window with a projected lifespan of 22 years, significantly extending the era of high-precision cosmological data collection at L2.
- **Initial Successes**: Early test images and system checks have demonstrated high fidelity in its wide-field imager and coronagraph instrument.
