# Prime Lens

A prime lens is a camera lens with a single, fixed [[concepts/focal-length|focal length]], meaning it cannot zoom in or out. Unlike zoom lenses, which offer variable focal lengths within a range, [[entities/prime-lenses|prime lenses]] maintain one perspective angle throughout use. This fundamental design difference makes prime lenses optically simpler, with fewer internal lens elements required to achieve focus at a single focal length.

## Optical Advantages

Prime lenses typically offer superior optical quality compared to zoom lenses of similar price and era. The fixed focal length allows for more precise optical design, resulting in sharper [[concepts/images|images]], wider maximum apertures, and better control of optical aberrations. Many prime lenses can achieve f/2.8 or wider apertures, allowing greater light gathering capability and more precise depth-of-field control—advantages particularly valued in low-light photography and portraiture.

## Practical Considerations

Using a prime lens requires the photographer to physically move closer to or farther from a subject to adjust composition, rather than adjusting the lens zoom. This constraint can encourage more intentional framing and creative [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]]. Prime lenses are generally lighter and more compact than equivalent zoom lenses, making them practical for travel and handheld work. They are available in many focal lengths—from ultra-wide to [[concepts/telephoto-lens|telephoto]]—allowing photographers to build a lens collection suited to different shooting [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]].
