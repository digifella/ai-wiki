# Photo Tonal Adjustments

Photo tonal adjustments involve modifying the brightness, contrast, and color balance of digital photographs to enhance visual impact or correct exposure issues. These modifications target specific segments of the tonal range, including shadows, midtones, and highlights, allowing for precise control over the image's luminance and color characteristics. This process is a fundamental component of post-processing workflows in both photography and digital image editing.

## Camera Raw Filter Implementation

The Adobe Photoshop Camera Raw Filter provides a comprehensive interface for applying these adjustments non-destructively. It allows users to manipulate global tonal curves, split toning, and color grading tools within a unified environment. By utilizing the filter, editors can apply complex tonal transformations that mimic traditional darkroom techniques while maintaining the original file's integrity.

## Workflow Integration

Integrating tonal adjustments into a creative workflow typically begins with raw data processing to establish a neutral baseline. Subsequent steps often involve local adjustments to specific areas of the image, followed by global color grading to achieve a cohesive aesthetic. This structured approach ensures that changes to shadows, midtones, and highlights are balanced, resulting in images with improved dynamic range and visual depth.
