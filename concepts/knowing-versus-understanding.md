# Knowing Versus Understanding

[[concepts/feynman|Richard Feynman]], the theoretical physicist and celebrated [[concepts/phil-moriarty|educator]], distinguished sharply between possessing factual information about something and achieving genuine understanding of it. In Feynman's view, knowing the name of a phenomenon or being able to recite its definition does not constitute true comprehension. This distinction became central to his [[concepts/philosophy-of-science|philosophy of science]] and education, influencing how he approached both research and teaching throughout his career.

## The Core Distinction

Feynman illustrated this principle through concrete examples, most notably the story of a bird in New York. He noted that one could know the name of the bird in many different languages, but that knowledge is entirely distinct from knowing how the bird actually behaves, why it migrates, or how it functions biologically. For Feynman, true understanding requires grasping the underlying [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] and [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] that govern a subject, rather than merely accumulating labels or isolated [[concepts/factual-knowledge|facts]]. He argued that without this deeper grasp, one remains vulnerable to misinformation and lacks the ability to apply knowledge in novel situations.

## Implications for Science and Education

This framework shaped Feynman’s approach to [[concepts/scientific-advancement|scientific progress]], emphasizing that the goal of science is not just to [[concepts/catalog|catalog]] the [[entities/earth|world]] but to understand its fundamental laws. In education, he advocated for teaching methods that prioritize conceptual [[concepts/clarity-slider|clarity]] over rote memorization. He believed that students who merely memorize definitions fail to engage with the material intellectually, leading to a fragile [[concepts/knowledge-base|knowledge base]] that collapses under scrutiny. Consequently, Feynman’s legacy in pedagogy continues to influence discussions on how to foster [[concepts/critical-thinking|critical thinking]] and genuine insight in learners across various disciplines.
