# Chicken Marinating

[[concepts/chicken-marination|Marinating chicken]] before [[concepts/bbq|grilling]] is a fundamental technique for preserving moisture and enhancing flavor. Acidic ingredients such as vinegar, citrus juice, or yogurt help break down muscle fibers and allow marinades to penetrate the meat, while oils and seasonings add both taste and a protective barrier during cooking. Marinating times typically range from 30 minutes to several hours, depending on the marinade's strength and the desired depth of flavor. Over-marinating—beyond 8-12 hours—can result in mushy [[concepts/texture|texture]] as the acid breaks down too much of the protein [[concepts/structure|structure]].

## Cut and Thickness

The size and cut of chicken pieces significantly affects cooking time and moisture retention. Thinner, more uniform cuts cook more quickly and evenly, reducing the risk of dry exterior sections while the interior remains undercooked. Pounding chicken breasts to an even thickness, using chicken thighs instead of breasts, or cutting larger pieces into smaller portions all help ensure consistent doneness and juiciness throughout the meat.

## Cooking Technique

Proper grilling temperature and timing are critical for maintaining juiciness. Cooking chicken at moderate heat rather than high temperatures prevents the exterior from drying out before the interior reaches safe internal temperatures. Using a meat thermometer to monitor doneness—targeting 165°F (74°C) internally—prevents overcooking. Allowing cooked chicken to rest for several minutes before serving enables juices to redistribute throughout the meat rather than [[concepts/running|running]] out when cut.
