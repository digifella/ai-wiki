# Saturn's Hexagonal North Pole

A persistent, six-sided jet stream pattern located at Saturn's north pole. First observed by [[concepts/voyager-2|Voyager 2]] in 1981 and extensively studied by the Cassini-Huygens mission, this massive cloud formation remains one of the most stable and enigmatic features in the solar system.

## Key Characteristics
- **Geometry:** A nearly perfect hexagon with each side approximately 13,800 km (8,600 mi) long, wider than [[entities/earth|Earth]].
- **Stability:** The structure has persisted for decades, suggesting a deep-seated atmospheric mechanism rather than a transient weather event.
- **Location:** Centered directly over the north pole, rotating with the planet's interior magnetic field period.

- **Core Mystery:** The geometric stability defies typical chaotic [[concepts/fluid-dynamics|fluid dynamics]] observed in other planetary atmospheres.
- **Proposed [[concepts/causes|Mechanisms]]:**
  - **Vertical Wind Shear:** Interactions between upper-level jet streams and deeper atmospheric layers may stabilize the hexagonal shape.
  - **Rossby Waves:** Standing planetary waves in the [[concepts/earths-atmosphere|atmosphere]] could lock the [[concepts/flow|flow]] into a six-sided pattern.
  - **Thermal Gradients:** Differences in heating between the pole and equator may [[concepts/ambition|drive]] the specific wave patterns required for hexagonal formation.
