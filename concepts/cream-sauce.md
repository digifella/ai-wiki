# Cream Sauce

A cream sauce is a foundational culinary [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] that combines cream with other ingredients to create a rich, flavorful accompaniment to proteins, vegetables, and pasta dishes. The sauce achieves its characteristic smooth [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]] through the [[concepts/food-emulsification|emulsification]] of fat and liquid, typically thickened using a roux, reduction, or binding agent such as cornstarch or egg yolk. This combination prevents the cream from separating during cooking, resulting in a stable, cohesive sauce.

## Preparation Methods

Cream sauces are commonly made using several techniques. The most traditional approach involves creating a roux by cooking flour and butter together before gradually whisking in milk or cream. This method, known as a [[concepts/white-sauce|béchamel]] base when milk is used, provides a stable structure that can be enriched with additional cream for a heavier [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]]. Alternatively, chefs may reduce heavy cream directly to thicken it, relying on the evaporation of [[concepts/moisture-content|water content]] to increase [[concepts/viscosity|viscosity]] and concentrate flavor.

Other variations utilize starches like cornstarch or arrowroot mixed with cold water to form a slurry, which is then incorporated into the heated cream to prevent lumps. Egg yolks are also employed in certain recipes, such as a liaison, where they are tempered with hot liquid before being stirred in to provide richness and body without the need for a roux. Each method offers distinct textural and flavor profiles, allowing for versatility in application across various cuisines.
