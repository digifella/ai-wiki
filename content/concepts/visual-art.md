# Visual Art

Visual [[concepts/art|art]] encompasses creative practices that communicate [[concepts/ideas|ideas]] and experiences through visual media, including painting, drawing, sculpture, photography, and digital media. Artists use visual elements such as color, form, composition, and perspective to express concepts, emotions, and observations about the world.

## Visual Art and Neurodiversity

Research and artistic practice have increasingly explored connections between different cognitive styles and visual art strengths. [[concepts/visual-arts|Kim Percy]]'s *[[entities/kim-percy|Visualising the Invisible]]* exhibition examines how dyslexic individuals often demonstrate particular strengths in visual-spatial [[concepts/human-cognition|thinking]], pattern recognition, and creative visualization. The exhibition explores visualization practices as a means of understanding and articulating these cognitive differences through artistic expression.

## Creative Practice and Visualization

Visualization practices in visual art—including sketching, conceptual mapping, and three-dimensional modeling—serve both as creative tools and as methods for developing and communicating complex ideas. These practices can be particularly valuable for individuals who think primarily in [[concepts/images|images]] and spatial [[concepts/relationships|relationships]], offering alternative pathways to conceptualization and expression alongside traditional linguistic methods.
