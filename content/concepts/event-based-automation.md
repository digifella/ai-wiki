# Event Based Automation

Event-based [[concepts/automation|automation]] refers to systems that trigger predefined actions or workflows in response to specific business events or user actions. Rather than [[concepts/running|running]] on fixed schedules, these systems activate when particular conditions occur—such as a customer purchase, form submission, incoming [[entities/email|email]], or system alert. This approach allows organizations to respond to business activities in real time without manual intervention.

## How It Works

Event-based automation operates by monitoring for designated triggers, then executing corresponding workflows automatically. When an event occurs, the system evaluates whether it matches configured criteria and, if so, initiates the associated automation sequence. This can include sending notifications, updating records, moving data between systems, or initiating secondary processes. The specificity of event detection determines how precisely automation can respond to business needs.

## Applications

Common [[concepts/scenarios|use cases]] include customer onboarding workflows triggered by new account creation, notification systems activated by order status changes, and [[concepts/data-synchronization|data synchronization]] initiated by form submissions. Sales teams might use event-based automation to log activities when deals reach certain stages, while support teams can automatically escalate tickets based on customer response times or priority [[concepts/indicators|indicators]]. The flexibility of [[concepts/event-driven-systems|event-based systems]] makes them applicable across departments for reducing manual work and improving response times.
