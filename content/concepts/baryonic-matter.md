# Baryonic Matter

Baryonic matter comprises all ordinary matter composed of atoms and molecules—including stars, planets, gas, and dust. In the universe's current mass-energy inventory, baryonic matter accounts for approximately 5% of the total content, while [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] comprises about 27% and dark energy about 68%. Measuring the [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] and quantity of baryonic matter is essential for [[concepts/testing|testing]] theories of cosmic [[concepts/structure|structure]] formation and validating dark matter models.

## Distribution in Galaxy Clusters

Galaxy clusters contain substantial quantities of baryonic matter, predominantly as hot ionized gas filling the intracluster [[entities/medium|medium]]. This hot gas, detectable through X-ray emissions, was expected to account for a specific fraction of cluster mass based on theoretical predictions. However, observations have revealed that some galaxy clusters contain more baryonic matter than standard cosmological models predict, suggesting either that measurement techniques require refinement or that current understanding of matter distribution in these systems is incomplete.

## Implications for Dark Matter Theory

The apparent discrepancy between predicted and observed baryonic matter in clusters presents a significant puzzle for [[concepts/cosmology|cosmology]]. If baryonic matter is genuinely more abundant in these systems than expected, it could indicate problems with assumed ratios between baryonic and dark matter, or it may point to previously unaccounted processes affecting gas distribution. Resolving this discrepancy is crucial for refining both dark matter models and our understanding of how matter assembles at cosmic [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]].
