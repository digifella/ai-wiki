# Superball Physics

**Superball [[concepts/physics|Physics]]** examines the mechanical behavior of highly elastic spheres, focusing on [[concepts/energy-conservation|energy conservation]], [[concepts/coefficient-of-restitution|coefficient of restitution]], and collision dynamics. Unlike standard rubber balls, superballs exhibit minimal [[concepts/energy-dissipation|energy loss]] during deformation, allowing for near-perfect elastic collisions under ideal conditions.

## Core Principles
- **High Coefficient of Restitution (COR):** Superballs typically possess a COR close to 0.9, meaning they retain ~81% of kinetic energy after impact with a rigid surface.
- **Elastic Deformation:** Energy is stored temporarily in the polymer structure during compression and released rapidly upon expansion, minimizing [[concepts/heat-dissipation|heat dissipation]].
- **Spin Dynamics:** [[concepts/friction|Friction]] between the ball and surface converts translational velocity into rotational velocity, often causing unexpected rebound angles or "kickback" effects.

## Anomalies and Complex Interactions
Standard models of elastic collisions assume isolated systems; however, multi-body interactions introduce counterintuitive results regarding energy transfer and momentum [[concepts/preservation|conservation]].

- **The Paradox of [[concepts/concept-of-nothingness|Zero]] Bounce:** Contrary to intuition, combining two highly elastic objects does not guarantee an optimal or even significant rebound. Specific configurations can result in negligible vertical displacement despite high individual elasticity.
	- See detailed analysis: [[lab-notes/2026-06-26-The-Paradox-of-Zero-Bounce-from-Similarly-Elastic-Object|The Paradox of Zero Bounce from Similarly Elastic Objects]]
	- This phenomenon highlights the complexity of energy distribution in coupled oscillatory systems, where [[concepts/phase|phase]] cancellation or [[concepts/material-damping|internal damping]] [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] may suppress macroscopic motion.
