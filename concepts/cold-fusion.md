# Cold Fusion

**Cold Fusion**, formally referred to as **[[concepts/low-energy-nuclear-reactions|Low Energy Nuclear Reactions]]** ([[concepts/lenr|LENR]]), denotes hypothetical nuclear reactions that [[concepts/deployment|release]] large amounts of energy at or near room temperature, contrasting with Hot Fusion which requires extreme temperatures and pressures. The field remains outside mainstream [[concepts/physics|physics]] due to a lack of reproducible [[concepts/empirical-evidence|experimental evidence]] and [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|theoretical frameworks]] consistent with standard nuclear physics.

## Core Concepts & History
- **Pons-Fleischmann [[concepts/scientific-experiment|Experiment]] (1989):** Initial claim of excess heat in electrolysis of heavy water using palladium cathodes. Subsequent failure of independent replication led to widespread [[concepts/scientific-skepticism|scientific skepticism]].
- **Mechanism:** Proposed [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] often involve lattice confinement fusion or anomalous neutron/proton emission, though no consistent model explains observed excess heat without violating known [[concepts/preservation|conservation]] laws.
- **Current Status:** Regarded as pseudoscience by most physicists; however, niche communities and private ventures continue research, often rebranding as "LENR" to distance from the stigma of "cold fusion."

## Recent Developments (2025-2026)
- **Investment Surge:** Notable increase in private funding and speculative investment in LENR startups, driven by media coverage of potential breakthroughs.
- **[[concepts/critical-thinking|Critical Analysis]]:**
    - See [[lab-notes/2026-05-25-Critical-Assessment-of-Cold-Fusion-LENR-Investments-and|Critical Assessment of Cold Fusion (LENR) Investments and Breakthrough Claims]] for detailed breakdown.
    - [[entities/sabine-hossenfelder|Sabine Hossenfelder]] (2026) highlights the disconnect between media hype and scientific rigor, noting that recent claims often lack peer-reviewed validation or controlled experimental conditions.
    - Many reported "breakthroughs" suffer from confirmation bias, poor calorimetry, or unexplained measurement errors.

## Key Challenges
- **Reproducibility:** No experiment has been successfully replicated across independent [[entities/labs|labs]] with rigorous controls.
- **Theoretical Inconsistency:** [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|Standard model of particle physics]] does not support low-energy [[concepts/fusion-power|nuclear fusion]] at room temperature without significant screening effects not observed in practice.
- **Detection Issues:** Expected byproducts (neutrons, [[concepts/gamma-rays|gamma rays]], tritium) are either absent or present at levels inconsistent with the claimed energy output.
