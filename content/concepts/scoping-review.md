# Scoping Review

A scoping review is a methodological approach used to systematically map the breadth of literature on a particular topic, concept, or field. Rather than seeking to answer a specific research question through rigorous [[concepts/quality-assessment|quality assessment]], scoping reviews identify and synthesize the types of evidence available, key concepts, themes, and knowledge gaps within a domain. This approach is particularly useful in areas that are broad, complex, or where evidence is heterogeneous in [[entities/nature|nature]] and sources.

## Scope and Methodology

Scoping reviews differ from systematic reviews in their approach and [[concepts/motivation|purpose]]. While systematic reviews critically appraise individual studies to answer focused questions, scoping reviews prioritize comprehensiveness and breadth over depth. They follow structured protocols that typically involve defining the research question, identifying relevant studies across multiple databases and sources, screening and extracting data from selected literature, and synthesizing findings through narrative or [[concepts/flow-management|thematic analysis]] rather than meta-analysis.

## Applications in Research and Practice

Scoping reviews serve several practical functions across health and social research. They are commonly used to clarify concepts and [[concepts/terminology|terminology]] within emerging fields, identify gaps in existing research to guide future investigations, and map the landscape of evidence available on topics relevant to policy and practice decisions. In [[concepts/health|healthcare]] contexts, they help stakeholders understand the current state of knowledge before investing in more intensive systematic reviews or developing [[concepts/adoption|implementation]] strategies. They are also valuable for synthesizing evidence across disciplines and study designs when exploring complex or multifaceted issues.
