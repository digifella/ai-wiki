# High-Speed Rail

**High-[[concepts/speed|speed]] rail** refers to railway systems that operate at significantly higher speeds than conventional rail, typically defined as 250 km/h (155 mph) or more. It serves as a critical component of sustainable [[concepts/intercity-travel|intercity transport]], reducing reliance on aviation and road networks for medium-distance travel.

## Global Context
High-speed rail networks are most prevalent in East Asia, [[entities/europe|Europe]], and increasingly in North [[entities/america|America]]. These systems prioritize efficiency, capacity, and reduced carbon emissions compared to air travel for routes under 800 km.

## Australia's High-Speed Rail Project
Australia is currently evaluating ambitious plans for a high-speed rail network, primarily focusing on the East Coast corridor connecting [[entities/sydney|Sydney]], Canberra, Newcastle, Brisbane, and potentially [[concepts/melbourne-region|Melbourne]].

### Key Developments
- **Political [[concepts/computer-vision|Vision]]:** The project is championed by [[concepts/prime-lens|Prime]] Minister [[entities/anthony-albanese|Anthony Albanese]], who views it as essential for national connectivity and economic growth.
- **Strategic [[concepts/value|Importance]]:** Aims to alleviate congestion on the Sydney–Melbourne rail corridor and reduce domestic flight demand.
- **Transformative Potential:** Expected to spur regional development, reduce travel times significantly, and lower the transport sector's carbon footprint.
- **Current Status:** Subject to ongoing feasibility studies and government reviews regarding funding and route alignment.
