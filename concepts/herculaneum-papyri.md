# Herculaneum Papyri

The **[[concepts/carbonized-papyrus|Herculaneum Papyri]]** are a collection of ancient scrolls discovered in the [[concepts/villa-of-the-papyri|Villa of the Papyri]] in Herculaneum, buried by the eruption of Vesuvius in 79 CE. Charred by volcanic heat, the scrolls are fragile and largely illegible to the naked eye, representing one of the most significant challenges in paleography and archaeology.

## Overview
- **Origin**: Library of the Villa of the Papyri, likely belonging to Lucius Calpurnius Piso Caesoninus, father-in-law of Julius Caesar.
- **Content**: Primarily [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]], including works by Philodemus, though other genres (poetry, rhetoric) are present.
- **Condition**: Carbonized rolls; unrolling physically destroys the material.
- **Significance**: Largest surviving collection of [[concepts/ancient-greek|ancient Greek]] literature from the Hellenistic period.

## Digital Decipherment & AI Integration
Recent advancements in digital humanities have shifted the paradigm from physical unrolling to virtual reconstruction.

- **[[concepts/virtual-unrolling|Virtual Unrolling]]**: [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] now utilize [[concepts/non-invasive-imaging|non-invasive imaging]] to virtually unroll carbonized scrolls, preserving their [[concepts/honesty|integrity]] while revealing text.
- **[[concepts/machine-learning|Machine Learning]] Applications**: [[concepts/algorithms|Algorithms]] are trained to distinguish between papyrus fibers and ink traces, enabling the decipherment of previously illegible sections.
- **Recent Breakthroughs**: Significant progress has been made in identifying complete texts within the scrolls, marking a shift in archaeological methodology.
