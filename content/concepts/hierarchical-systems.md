# Hierarchical Systems

Hierarchical systems represent a structured approach to organizing [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] context that prioritizes architectural design over raw data ingestion. Rather than directly uploading files or documents into language models, this methodology establishes a map-first framework that organizes information according to conceptual [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] and dependencies before AI processing occurs.

## Core Problem and Approach

Traditional Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems often struggle with context fragmentation, where individual documents or chunks are retrieved in isolation without clear understanding of how they relate to broader systems or structures. Hierarchical systems address this by first establishing the structural relationships between concepts, entities, and information domains, then allowing AI to navigate this pre-organized landscape. This reverses the typical workflow: instead of asking an AI to find and synthesize scattered information, the [[concepts/information-architecture|information architecture]] itself becomes explicitly structured.

## Practical Implementation

The map-first [[concepts/architecture|architecture]] typically involves creating explicit relationships between related concepts before querying the system. This might include defining how different domains connect, how entities relate to processes, or how various documents fit within larger [[concepts/conceptual-frameworks|conceptual frameworks]]. By establishing these connections upfront, AI systems can operate with clearer context about information [[concepts/hierarchy|hierarchy]] and interdependencies, reducing the need to upload and process large volumes of unstructured files.
