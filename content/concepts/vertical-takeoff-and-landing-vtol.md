# Vertical Takeoff And Landing (VTOL)

[[concepts/vertical-takeoff-and-landing|Vertical takeoff and landing]] ([[concepts/vertical-take-off-and-landing|VTOL]]) refers to aircraft capable of ascending, descending, and hovering without requiring a runway. This capability eliminates the need for extensive ground infrastructure and allows operations in confined spaces, making VTOL systems valuable for military, emergency response, and urban mobility [[concepts/software|applications]]. The technology represents a significant departure from conventional fixed-wing aircraft [[concepts/design|design]], requiring specialized aerodynamic and mechanical solutions.

## Tiltrotor Systems

[[concepts/tiltrotor-technology|Tiltrotor]] aircraft combine features of helicopters and fixed-wing planes by using rotors that can change orientation during flight. The [[entities/v-22-osprey|V-22 Osprey]], a military transport aircraft, exemplifies this approach with two large rotors mounted on rotating nacelles [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] the wingtips. During takeoff and landing, the rotors point upward like a helicopter; during forward flight, they rotate to point forward, functioning as propellers. This dual-mode capability allows tiltrotors to achieve vertical flight efficiency while maintaining the [[concepts/speed|speed]] and [[concepts/range|range]] advantages of fixed-wing aircraft.

## Other VTOL Approaches

Beyond tiltrotors, VTOL technology encompasses several alternative designs. Quadcopters and multirotor drones use multiple small rotors for lift and [[concepts/power|control]], offering simplicity and maneuverability at the [[concepts/cost|cost]] of limited payload and range. Vectored [[concepts/engine-thrust|thrust]] systems, such as those in the Harrier jet, redirect engine exhaust downward for vertical thrust. Emerging electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft designed for urban air mobility employ distributed electric propulsion systems. Each approach involves distinct trade-offs between complexity, performance, and operational requirements.
