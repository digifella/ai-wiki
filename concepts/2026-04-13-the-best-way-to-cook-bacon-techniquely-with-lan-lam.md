# 2026 04 13 The Best Way To Cook Bacon Techniquely With Lan Lam

Bacon can be prepared using several distinct cooking techniques, each producing different results in texture, crispness, and fat rendering. Traditional methods include oven-baking, pan-frying, and broiling, all of which rely on direct heat application. These conventional approaches vary in their consistency, cleanup requirements, and ability to cook large batches simultaneously.

## The Water Method

The water method begins with placing bacon strips in a cold skillet with a small amount of water. As the skillet heats, the water gradually evaporates while the bacon renders its fat slowly and evenly. Once the water has fully evaporated, the bacon continues cooking in its own rendered fat until it reaches the desired crispness. This technique produces consistent results with even browning and allows for precise control over the final texture. The gradual heating process results in less splattering than traditional pan-frying and reduces the risk of burning.

## Advantages and Considerations

The water method is particularly effective for cooking larger quantities of bacon simultaneously, as the even heat distribution prevents some strips from cooking faster than others. The technique also minimizes the need for active monitoring once the bacon begins cooking. However, the method requires slightly longer total cooking time compared to high-heat pan-frying, making it better suited for situations where time is less critical than consistency and safety.
