# Water Gardening

Water gardening, also known as hydroculture, is the practice of cultivating plants in water or inert media rather than traditional soil. This method is particularly suited to indoor environments, where plant roots are suspended in water or materials such as clay pellets, perlite, or rockwool. The water serves as the primary delivery system for both hydration and dissolved nutrients, effectively replacing the role of soil in the plant's growth cycle.

By eliminating soil, this cultivation technique removes common issues associated with earth-based gardening, such as pest infestations and fungal diseases that thrive in organic matter. It allows for greater control over nutrient intake and root health, making it a popular choice for maintaining indoor greenery without the mess or maintenance demands of conventional potting.

The system relies on the careful management of water quality and nutrient concentration to prevent root rot and ensure adequate oxygenation. While often associated with decorative aquatic plants, the principles of hydroculture apply to a wide variety of terrestrial species that can adapt to soil-less conditions, provided their specific light and humidity requirements are met.
