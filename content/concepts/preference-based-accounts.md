# Preference Based Accounts

Preference-based accounts of [[concepts/health|wellbeing]] define an individual's [[concepts/wellbeing|wellbeing]] in terms of the satisfaction of their preferences or desires. Under this framework, a person's life goes well to the extent that their actual experiences align with what they prefer, want, or value. This approach treats preference satisfaction as the primary measure of human flourishing, regardless of the objective content of those preferences.

## Philosophical Foundations

The preference satisfaction model offers an intuitive appeal: it respects individual autonomy by treating people as the best judges of their own interests, rather than imposing external [[concepts/open-standards|standards]] of what constitutes a good life. It avoids controversial claims about objective goods and instead grounds wellbeing in subjective mental states—specifically, the fulfillment of desires.

## Practical and Theoretical Challenges

Despite its theoretical elegance, preference-based accounts face significant practical difficulties. [[entities/kate-laffan|Kate Laffan]], discussing these frameworks in her [[entities/tedxlse|TEDxLSE]] talk, [[concepts/highlights|highlights]] how preferences can be adaptive, misinformed, or shaped by limited circumstances. A person may come to prefer conditions that harm them, or their preferences may reflect social conditioning rather than authentic values. Additionally, measuring preference satisfaction requires both identifying what people genuinely prefer and determining when those preferences are actually met—both empirically challenging tasks.
