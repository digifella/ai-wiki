# Digital Health

Digital health encompasses the use of digital technologies, tools, and platforms to improve health outcomes and [[concepts/health|healthcare]] delivery. This includes electronic health records, telemedicine, health monitoring [[concepts/software|applications]], [[concepts/wearable-devices|wearable devices]], and data analytics systems that support [[concepts/clinical-reasoning|clinical decision-making]] and patient engagement. The field represents a convergence of healthcare practice, information technology, and [[concepts/population-health|public health]] initiatives aimed [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] making healthcare more accessible, efficient, and personalized.

## Current Developments

Recent developments in digital health include the [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of [[concepts/ai-agentic-applications|agentic AI systems]] by regulatory bodies such as the [[concepts/fda|FDA]]. These [[concepts/agentic-ai|autonomous AI systems]] are being deployed in-house to support various regulatory and [[concepts/quality-assurance|quality assurance]] functions within healthcare oversight. Such implementations reflect growing confidence in [[concepts/ai-technologies|AI technologies]] to handle complex, domain-specific tasks in medical and pharmaceutical contexts.

## Knowledge and Familiarity

Understanding of digital health concepts varies across populations and professional groups. [[concepts/survey|Survey]] research has been conducted to assess public and professional familiarity with digital health [[concepts/terminology|terminology]] and applications, using standardized measurement [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]]. These assessments help identify knowledge gaps and inform educational efforts around digital health adoption and literacy.
