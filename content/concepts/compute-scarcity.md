# Compute Scarcity

Compute scarcity refers to the insufficient [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]] available to meet demand for AI [[concepts/inference|model inference]] and [[concepts/training|training]], particularly in the context of [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]] like [[concepts/claude-ai|Claude]]. This constraint becomes a critical business and technical challenge when demand for model access exceeds available GPU/TPU capacity, forcing companies to make difficult decisions about resource allocation and service availability.

## The Anthropic Case

Anthropic faced a notable [[concepts/compute|compute]] miscalculation regarding Claude's demand trajectory. The company underestimated both the volume of API requests and the computational requirements needed to serve them reliably. This gap between expected and actual demand created operational pressures that affected service quality, [[concepts/pricing|pricing structures]], and the company's strategic roadmap for [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]] infrastructure.

## Strategic Implications

Compute scarcity has broader implications for AI companies beyond immediate operational challenges. It influences pricing power, shapes competitive positioning, affects research capabilities, and determines which features and [[concepts/scenarios|use cases]] can be supported. Companies must balance investing in expensive infrastructure against uncertainty about future demand patterns and technological improvements that might reduce computational costs.
