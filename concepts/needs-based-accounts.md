# Needs Based Accounts

Needs-based accounts are philosophical frameworks for understanding wellbeing that ground human flourishing in the satisfaction of fundamental needs rather than subjective preferences or external measures. This approach diverges from purely subjective theories of wellbeing, which define it as the satisfaction of whatever an individual desires, by proposing that certain needs, when met, objectively contribute to genuine wellbeing regardless of whether a person recognizes or values them. The core distinction lies in the objective nature of these needs, suggesting that well-being is not merely a matter of personal opinion or cultural conditioning, but is tied to universal human requirements for flourishing.

This framework is notably associated with the work of Kate Laffan, who explores how identifying these fundamental needs provides a more robust basis for evaluating health and wellbeing. By shifting the focus from what people want to what they fundamentally require, needs-based accounts offer a normative standard for assessing quality of life. This allows for critical evaluation of situations where individuals may desire things that are detrimental to their long-term flourishing or fail to desire things that are essential for their well-being.

The implications of this theory extend into ethical and political philosophy, particularly in discussions regarding social justice and public policy. If wellbeing is determined by the satisfaction of objective needs, then societies have a moral obligation to ensure these needs are met for all individuals. This perspective challenges purely libertarian or preference-satisfaction models of welfare, arguing that the state or community must intervene to secure the conditions necessary for human flourishing, independent of individual consent or immediate desire.
