# Scale Effect

The **scale effect** refers to how the performance and capabilities of [[concepts/artificial-intelligence-models|artificial intelligence models]], particularly transformer-based architectures, are heavily influenced by [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]]. Larger datasets, more powerful hardware (such as GPUs), and increased [[concepts/model-size|model size]] generally lead to better results due to an improvement in [[concepts/abstraction|generalization]] ability, capacity for capturing complex patterns, and [[concepts/parallel-processing|parallel processing]] [[concepts/speed|speed]].

### Key Points
- **Larger Models**: The performance of [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] increases with scale up until a certain point where [[concepts/diminishing-returns|diminishing returns]] are observed.
- **Computational Power**: Modern systems rely on [[entities/high-performance|high-performance]] GPUs and [[entities/big-data|large datasets]] to train complex [[concepts/neural-networks|neural networks]] efficiently.
- **[[concepts/limited-resources|Resource Constraints]]**: Limited [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]] can significantly impact the training time, efficiency, and overall effectiveness of an AI model.

### Summary
The video, presented by [[entities/dave|Dave]], aims to demystify the [[concepts/training-process|training process]] of a [[concepts/neural-network|neural network]] by running a transformer on a vintage 1979 [[concepts/pdp-1144|PDP-11/44]] computer. Unlike modern cloud clusters with thousands of GPUs, this system operates with a single 6MHz CPU and a mere 64KB of RAM (though later upgraded to 4MB). The core idea, [[entities/dave-plummer|Dave]] argues, is not magical or new; it highlights the scale effect in AI training, showing that even small-scale hardware can train models but at an extremely slow pace compared to modern standards.

### Bullet Points
- Demonstrates transformer model training on a [[concepts/pdp-11|PDP-11]] with [[concepts/limited-resources|limited resources]].
