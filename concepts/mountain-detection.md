# Mountain Detection

Mountain Detection is a [[concepts/masking-technique|selective editing]] technique within [[concepts/landscape-photography|landscape photography]] that leverages the [[concepts/ai-masking|automated masking]] capabilities of [[entities/adobe-lightroom|Adobe Lightroom]] and [[concepts/adobe-camera-raw|Adobe Camera Raw]]. This method allows photographers to isolate specific geographic features, such as mountain ranges, from the rest of the image. By targeting these distinct areas, users can apply [[concepts/masking-tools|localized adjustments]] to [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], saturation, or [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] without affecting the foreground or sky. The process relies on the software's ability to recognize tonal and textural differences between the sky, mid-tones, and [[concepts/shadows|shadows]].

## Implementation

The technique is primarily demonstrated through tutorials by [[entities/colin-smith-channel|Colin Smith]], who highlights the efficiency of using [[concepts/landscape-masks|Landscape Masks]] for complex terrain. Users initiate the process by selecting the Landscape Mask tool, which automatically generates masks for the sky, foreground, and subject. In scenes with prominent peaks, the software often identifies the mountain range as a distinct subject or mid-tone layer.

## Application and Adjustment

Once the mountain region is isolated, photographers can apply specific adjustments to enhance detail and depth. Common edits include increasing [[concepts/clarity-slider|clarity]] to emphasize rock textures, adjusting exposure to balance lighting on the peaks, or modifying saturation to enrich natural colors. Because the mask is generated algorithmically, it may require manual refinement using the brush or [[concepts/gradient-tools|gradient tools]] to ensure precise boundaries around irregular ridgelines.

## Limitations and Refinement

While effective for clear, high-contrast scenes, the automated detection may struggle with low-light conditions or complex overlapping elements. In such cases, the initial mask may include unwanted areas of the sky or foreground. Photographers are advised to review the mask overlay and manually adjust the edges to prevent [[concepts/haloing|haloing]] or unintended color shifts in adjacent regions. Regular [[concepts/software-updates|updates]] to Adobe software often improve the accuracy of these automated detection [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]].
