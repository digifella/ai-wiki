# The Bitters

Bitters are concentrated flavoring compounds utilized in cocktail preparation, produced by steeping herbs, spices, roots, and other botanical materials in alcohol. Applied sparingly, typically in quantities of a few dashes per drink, they serve to enhance flavor complexity, balance sweetness, and add aromatic depth to mixed beverages. The most widely recognized commercial varieties include Angostura, Peychaud's, and orange bitters, each possessing distinct flavor profiles suited to specific cocktail styles.

## Historical Origins

The concept of bitters traces its origins to the 19th century, evolving from medicinal tonics used by pharmacists to treat digestive ailments. These early formulations were often sold in apothecaries as digestive aids before being adopted by bartenders for their ability to modify the taste of spirits. The practice of adding bitters to cocktails became standardized in the late 1800s, with figures like Dr. John G. Siegler in Cincinnati and Antoine Peychaud in New Orleans playing pivotal roles in popularizing specific brands that remain staples in modern mixology.

## Modern Context and Regional Variations

In contemporary mixology, bitters are categorized by their primary flavor notes, such as aromatic, orange, chocolate, or celery, allowing for precise customization of drink profiles. The domain of food and nutrition recognizes their role not only in culinary arts but also in the historical intersection of herbal medicine and beverage culture. Notably, the craft of bitters making has seen a resurgence in specialized regions, such as Heraklion, where former pharmacists have applied traditional botanical knowledge to create artisanal variations that emphasize local ingredients and historical accuracy.
