# Electromagnetic Interaction

Electromagnetic interaction refers to the fundamental force by which charged particles exert forces on one another through electric and magnetic fields. This force is one of the four fundamental interactions in [[concepts/physics|physics]] and is responsible for most phenomena observable in everyday experience, from chemical bonding to light itself.

## Role in Gravitational Collapse

[[concepts/dark-matter|Dark matter]] presents a puzzling [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] to [[concepts/baryonic-matter|ordinary matter]] when considering gravitational collapse. Ordinary matter, composed of atoms and [[concepts/subatomic-particles|subatomic particles]], can radiate energy through electromagnetic processes—including light emission, heat radiation, and [[concepts/friction|friction]]. This energy loss allows ordinary matter to cool and compress under [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]], eventually forming dense objects like stars and potentially [[concepts/black-holes|black holes]]. Dark matter, by definition, does not interact electromagnetically. It neither emits nor absorbs light and cannot dissipate energy through [[concepts/light|electromagnetic radiation]].

Without the ability to shed energy electromagnetically, dark matter cannot cool efficiently as it collapses gravitationally. This fundamental constraint severely limits how densely dark matter particles can accumulate in any given region. While dark matter can certainly form structures through gravity—such as the halos surrounding galaxies—the absence of electromagnetic interaction prevents the runaway compression that leads to black hole formation. The particles can approach one another only so far before quantum mechanical effects or other forces become significant, establishing a natural limit to concentration that gravitational collapse alone cannot overcome.
