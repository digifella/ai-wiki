# Camera Angle Manipulation

Camera [[concepts/perspective-adjustment|angle manipulation]] refers to techniques for controlling and adjusting camera perspectives in AI-generated imagery. Rather than relying solely on [[concepts/natural-language-descriptions|natural language descriptions]], modern [[concepts/ai-image-editing|AI image editing]] systems employ structured control [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] to achieve precise adjustments to framing, viewpoint, and compositional elements. This approach enables creators to specify exact parameters for how a scene should be viewed—such as camera height, rotation, and field of view—with greater [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] and accuracy than [[concepts/text-prompts|text prompts]] alone.

## Structured Control Methods

The implementation of these controls often involves [[concepts/json-structuring|structured data]] formats that allow for granular instruction. For instance, the [[concepts/camera-angle-control|Nano Banana 2]] system utilizes JSON control to enable precise AI image editing within [[concepts/gemini|Gemini]]. This method allows users to define specific geometric and optical properties directly, bypassing the [[concepts/ambiguity|ambiguity]] often associated with descriptive prompts. By [[concepts/encoding|encoding]] camera parameters into a machine-readable format, the system can interpret and apply changes to the image generation process with higher fidelity.

This technique is particularly valuable in creative pursuits where specific visual outcomes are required. It supports workflows that demand repeatability and exactitude, such as architectural visualization or character design. The ability to manipulate the virtual camera independently of the subject matter allows for [[concepts/iterative-learning|iterative refinement]] of the [[concepts/writing|composition]] without altering the core content of the generated image.
