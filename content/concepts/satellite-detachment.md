# Satellite Detachment

Satellite detachment refers to the unintended separation of a payload from its launch vehicle during the powered flight [[concepts/phase|phase]], specifically when [[concepts/payload-fairing-separation|payload fairing separation]] occurs while the first stage engines are still burning. This phenomenon represents a critical failure mode in [[concepts/satellite-launch|launch vehicle operations]], as the payload can be exposed to extreme aerodynamic forces, vibration, and structural loads before reaching a safe orbital velocity or altitude.

The primary risk occurs in the brief window between fairing jettison and first stage burnout. During this period, the payload experiences rapid changes in aerodynamic pressure and may be subjected to mechanical shock from separation mechanisms. If structural [[concepts/integrity|integrity]] is compromised during this sensitive phase, the payload can become unstable or separate prematurely, resulting in loss of the mission and potential debris hazards.

Various engineering approaches exist to mitigate this risk, including improved separation system [[concepts/design|design]], enhanced payload [[concepts/attachment-theory|attachment]] mechanisms, and refined flight profiles that optimize the timing of fairing release. The complexity and [[concepts/cost|cost]] of implementing robust solutions—such as advanced clamping systems—must be weighed against mission requirements and launch vehicle [[concepts/architecture|architecture]].
