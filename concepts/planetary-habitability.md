# Planetary Habitability

Planetary habitability refers to the capacity of a celestial body to sustain life, primarily evaluated through the lens of Earth-like conditions. Scientists assess this potential by analyzing physical and chemical parameters necessary for biological processes. The primary criteria include the presence of liquid water, an atmospheric composition that supports stability, temperature ranges compatible with biochemistry, and sufficient protection from harmful cosmic radiation. The concept is central to the search for extraterrestrial life and guides exploration missions to nearby worlds, particularly Mars, where evidence of past aqueous activity suggests previous habitable environments.

## Key Determinants

The assessment of habitability relies on several interconnected factors. The most critical is the existence of liquid water, which serves as a solvent for biochemical reactions. This requirement defines the "habitable zone" or "Goldilocks zone" around a star, where temperatures allow water to remain in liquid form rather than freezing or evaporating. Atmospheric pressure and composition are equally vital, as they regulate surface temperature through the greenhouse effect and shield the surface from ionizing radiation. Additionally, geological activity may play a role by recycling nutrients and maintaining atmospheric pressure over geological timescales.

## Research and Exploration

Evidence from Mars research provides a terrestrial analog for studying habitability limits. Data from orbiters and rovers have identified mineralogical signatures of past water flow, such as hydrated sulfates and clay minerals, indicating that Mars once possessed conditions capable of supporting microbial life. Current missions focus on identifying subsurface water ice and organic molecules, which are the building blocks of life. These findings help refine models of planetary evolution and inform the selection of targets for future missions to icy moons like Europa and Enceladus, where subsurface oceans may exist beneath frozen crusts.
