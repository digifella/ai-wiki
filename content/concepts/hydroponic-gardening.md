# Hydroponic Gardening

[[concepts/soil-less-gardening|Hydroponic gardening]] is a method of cultivating plants without soil, using nutrient-rich water solutions instead. In hydroponic systems, plant roots are suspended in water or an inert growing [[entities/medium|medium]] and receive essential nutrients directly from the water supply. This approach eliminates the need for traditional soil-based cultivation and allows plants to be grown indoors year-[[concepts/rounding|round]], regardless of climate or season.

## Indoor Plant Cultivation

Several common houseplants thrive in hydroponic environments when provided with adequate [[concepts/light|light]] and nutrient solutions. Species suitable for water-based cultivation include [[entities/pothos|pothos]], philodendron, lucky bamboo, coleus, begonias, mint, and spider plants. These plants adapt well to having their roots in water and can develop strong growth when given consistent lighting and regular nutrient replenishment.

## Practical Advantages

Hydroponic systems offer practical benefits for [[concepts/indoor-gardening|indoor gardening]]. Plants often grow faster than in soil due to direct nutrient availability, and the method uses less water overall than traditional gardening since water is recycled rather than absorbed into soil. Indoor hydroponic setups also reduce pest and disease problems associated with soil-based growing, and eliminate concerns about soil contamination or quality variation.
