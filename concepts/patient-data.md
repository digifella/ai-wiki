# Patient Data

[[concepts/patient-information|Patient data]] refers to any information collected, stored, or processed about individuals within [[concepts/health-care|healthcare]] and [[concepts/digital-health|digital health]] contexts. This encompasses personal identifiers, medical histories, diagnostic results, treatment plans, and ongoing [[concepts/health-metrics|health metrics]]. In digital [[concepts/health|health]] environments, patient data exists in electronic formats and becomes subject to specific [[concepts/security|security]], [[concepts/privacy|privacy]], and regulatory requirements designed to protect sensitive personal information.

## Types and Scope

Patient data ranges from basic demographic information to complex clinical records. It includes [[concepts/json-structuring|structured data]] such as test results and vital signs, as well as [[concepts/unstructured-data|unstructured data]] like clinical [[concepts/notes|notes]] and imaging files. The scope of patient data has expanded with [[concepts/healthcare-informatization|digital health systems]] to include data generated by [[concepts/wearable-devices|wearable devices]], patient-reported outcomes, and activity tracking, creating comprehensive longitudinal records of individual health.

## Regulatory and Ethical Considerations

The handling of patient data is governed by regulations such as [[concepts/hipaa|HIPAA]] in the [[entities/united-states|United States]], [[concepts/gdpr|GDPR]] in [[entities/europe|Europe]], and comparable frameworks in other jurisdictions. These regulations establish requirements for data access controls, encryption, breach notification, and patient consent. [[concepts/health-sector|Healthcare organizations]] and [[concepts/telehealth-systems|digital health platforms]] must implement safeguards to maintain [[concepts/data-integrity|data integrity]], ensure appropriate access, and enable patients to understand and control how their information is used.

## Clinical and Research Applications

Patient data supports both direct [[concepts/clinical-care|clinical care]] and broader [[concepts/health-research|health research]]. In clinical settings, organized patient data improves [[concepts/continuity|continuity]] of care and enables [[concepts/evidence-based-decision-making|evidence-based decision-making]]. In research contexts, de-identified or aggregated patient data contributes to epidemiological studies, drug development, and quality improvement initiatives, though these uses require careful consideration of privacy protections and ethical oversight.
