# Personal Knowledge Management (PKM)

Personal Knowledge Management refers to the practice and systems individuals use to capture, organize, store, and retrieve information they encounter across their digital and physical environments. Rather than allowing information to scatter across multiple applications, emails, and documents, PKM provides a structured approach to managing personal information assets. This becomes increasingly important as people accumulate knowledge from various sources—research, reading, conversations, projects, and professional work—that would otherwise become difficult to locate or leverage.

## Core Functions

A PKM system typically addresses several interconnected needs: capturing information from diverse sources, organizing it in a retrievable manner, connecting related ideas, and surfacing relevant knowledge when needed. Implementation varies widely depending on individual workflows and preferences, ranging from simple note-taking systems to more elaborate digital gardens or interconnected knowledge bases. Common tools include note-taking applications, document management systems, tagging and linking systems, and specialized knowledge management platforms.

## Relationship to Knowledge Work

PKM has become particularly relevant for knowledge workers who must manage substantial information flows as part of their daily work. Effective PKM systems can improve decision-making, reduce time spent searching for information, support creative thinking through connections between ideas, and provide a personal reference resource for ongoing projects and learning. The approach complements broader organizational knowledge management practices, though it focuses on individual rather than institutional information needs.
