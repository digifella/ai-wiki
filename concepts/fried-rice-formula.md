# Fried Rice Formula

Fried rice is a versatile dish whose [[concepts/success|success]] depends primarily on two critical factors: proper rice [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] and thoughtful [[concepts/ingredient-selection|ingredient selection]]. This formula-based approach allows cooks to produce successful fried rice consistently, regardless of which specific ingredients are available or preferred. The foundation of quality fried rice relies on using the correct type of cooked rice and preparing it correctly before frying.

## Rice Treatment

The most important step in making fried rice is proper rice preparation. Day-old cooked rice that has been refrigerated overnight is ideal because the cooling process [[concepts/causes|causes]] the starches to retrograde, resulting in firmer [[concepts/granular-materials|grains]] that are less likely to become mushy during the high-heat frying process. If fresh rice must be used, it should be spread out on a baking sheet and cooled in the refrigerator for at least an hour to reduce surface moisture. The rice should be broken up into individual grains before being added to the wok or pan to ensure even cooking and prevent clumping.

## Ingredient Selection

Thoughtful ingredient selection enhances the [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]] and flavor profile of the final dish. Aromatics such as garlic, ginger, and scallions are typically sautéed first to build a flavor base. Proteins, including eggs, meat, or seafood, should be cooked separately or pushed to the side of the pan to avoid overcooking while the rice finishes. Vegetables are added based on their [[concepts/cooking-time|cooking time]], with harder varieties like carrots or peas requiring more heat than delicate greens. Seasonings such as soy sauce, fish sauce, or sesame oil are added toward the end to coat the grains evenly without making them soggy.
