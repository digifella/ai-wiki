# PMI Frameworks

The Project Management Institute (PMI) frameworks represent a collection of standardized methodologies for planning, executing, and delivering projects across industries. These frameworks provide structured approaches to managing core project dimensions including scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, communications, risk, and stakeholder engagement. PMI frameworks are grounded in documented best practices and have been refined through extensive organizational experience, making them widely adopted in enterprise settings.

## Core Framework Categories

PMI's primary frameworks include PMBOK (Project Management Body of Knowledge), which serves as the foundational guide covering five process groups and ten knowledge areas; PRINCE2, an alternative structured methodology emphasizing stage-based project management; Agile frameworks such as Scrum and Kanban, which prioritize iterative delivery and flexibility; and specialized approaches including Program Management (PgMP) for managing related projects as coordinated programs, and Portfolio Management for aligning projects with organizational strategy. Additionally, PMI recognizes frameworks addressing specific contexts such as construction project management, systems development, and product delivery.

## Application and Adoption

Organizations typically select PMI frameworks based on project characteristics, industry requirements, and organizational maturity. Traditional waterfall-oriented frameworks suit projects with well-defined scope and stable requirements, while Agile frameworks accommodate changing requirements and rapid iteration cycles. Many organizations adopt hybrid approaches, combining elements from multiple frameworks to address specific project needs. PMI certification programs, including PMP (Project Management Professional) and CAPM (Certified Associate in Project Management), validate practitioner competency in applying these frameworks and have become common credentials in project-intensive industries.
