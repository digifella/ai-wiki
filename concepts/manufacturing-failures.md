# Manufacturing Failures

**Manufacturing failures** refer to defects, deviations, or catastrophic breakdowns occurring during the production, assembly, or quality assurance phases of industrial goods. These failures often stem from inadequate [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] processes, material selection errors, or bypassing standard regulatory protocols.

## Key Characteristics
- **Process Deviation**: Departure from established engineering standards or safety protocols.
- **Material [[concepts/honesty|Integrity]]**: Issues arising from improper material testing, selection, or fabrication techniques.
- **Systemic Oversight**: Lack of independent [[concepts/verification|verification]] or [[concepts/document-review|peer review]] in critical design phases.

## Case Study: OceanGate Titan
A prominent example of manufacturing and engineering failure is the implosion of the **[[concepts/oceangate-titan|OceanGate Titan]]** submersible. Investigations concluded that the disaster was not caused by the inherent properties of the carbon fiber material, but by an **inadequate [[concepts/engineering-process|engineering process]]** and failure to adhere to standard safety certifications.
