## Artemis II Simulated Mission Report: Crew Operations, Orion Systems, Starship Updates
**Clip title:** We Just Watched Artemis II Come Back… That Was Intense
**Author / channel:** Marcus House
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CrzP6naZGKs

### Summary
This video provides a comprehensive update on recent developments in [[concepts/space-exploration|space exploration]], primarily focusing on the simulated [[concepts/artemis-ii-mission|Artemis II mission]] and ongoing progress with [[entities/spacex|SpaceX]]'s Starship. The main topic revolves around humanity's return to the Moon after decades, detailing the journey of the Artemis II crew, their experiences, the technological advancements, and the challenges faced. The video also covers significant milestones and construction updates at SpaceX's Starbase facility.

The Artemis II segment begins by recounting the Trans-Lunar Injection (TLI) burn on the second day, marking the first time humans in over five decades were heading around the Moon with modern technology. Viewers are treated to never-before-seen footage of the [Inter-Cryogenic Propulsion Stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Cryogenic_Propulsion_Stage) (ICPS) separation, a beautiful display of debris, likely ice. Inside the Orion capsule, the crew's day-to-day activities like exercising, meals, and waste water dumps are shown, alongside a unique demonstration of CPR and choking response in [microgravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microgravity), highlighting the complexities of medical procedures in space. A notable, albeit minor, issue with the toilet's waste water venting system led the crew to utilize Collapsible Contingency Urinals (CCUs), a reusable [[concepts/solution|solution]] for such malfunctions.

As the mission progressed, the Orion spacecraft successfully performed only one of the three planned outbound trajectory correction burns due to the precision of the initial TLI. The crew conducted manual control tests of the spacecraft and successfully tested the new [Optical Communications System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_Communications_System), utilizing lasers to transmit over 100 gigabytes of high-resolution data back to Earth at speeds up to 260 megabytes per second. During the Moon flyby, the crew observed and named two new lunar craters—[[concepts/integrity|Integrity]], after their spacecraft, and Carroll, after astronaut [[entities/jeremy|Jeremy]] Hansen's late wife. They broke Apollo 13's record for the farthest humans have traveled from Earth, witnessed a lunar eclipse by the Sun, and observed meteoroid impact flashes. The mission culminated with a challenging lofted [re-entry profile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Re-entry_profile) designed to manage [heat shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_shield) temperatures, followed by a successful splashdown in the Pacific Ocean and recovery.

The video also dedicates a portion to the rapid advancements at SpaceX's Starbase. It showcases dual static fire tests for Starship Flight 12, booster 18.3 being lifted into the Super Crusher stand for [[concepts/testing|testing]], and the installation of grid fin hardware. Significant construction progress on the flame trench and [water deluge system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_deluge_system) for the new Pad 2 is highlighted, including sac testing with large water bags to reinforce the launch mount. Furthermore, the video introduces the Starcloud project, an initiative for [[concepts/orbital-computing|orbital data centers]] that recently secured $170 million in funding and aims to launch satellites with NVIDIA H100 GPUs, connecting them via laser links to form a massive constellation.

In conclusion, the video paints a vivid picture of humanity's ongoing push into space, showcasing both the awe-inspiring feats of the Artemis II mission and the relentless pace of [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] at SpaceX. From the meticulous planning and execution of lunar trajectories to the development of advanced in-space communication and ambitious orbital data centers, these endeavors underscore a collective commitment to expanding our presence beyond Earth, with future missions promising even more groundbreaking achievements.
