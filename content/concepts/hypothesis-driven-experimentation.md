# Hypothesis Driven Experimentation

Hypothesis Driven Experimentation is a systematic methodology for iterative improvement based on forming testable predictions, conducting controlled experiments, and using results to inform subsequent iterations. Rather than making changes based on intuition or broad exploration, this approach requires explicitly stating what outcome is expected, measuring whether that outcome occurs, and using the data to refine the next cycle. The methodology is applicable across domains including [[concepts/coding|software development]], product design, scientific research, and machine [[concepts/learning|learning]].

## Core Process

The typical cycle involves four steps: formulating a specific hypothesis about what change will produce a measurable improvement, designing an experiment to test that hypothesis, executing the experiment and collecting data, and analyzing results to validate or refute the hypothesis. Failed hypotheses are as informative as successful ones, as they eliminate possibilities and guide future investigation. This structured approach reduces wasted effort on untested assumptions and creates a clear record of what has and hasn't been tried.

## Application in AI Agent Development

[[concepts/action-oriented-ai|Autonomous AI agents]] can be particularly well-suited to this methodology, as they can propose code modifications based on observed performance gaps, run experiments to test whether those modifications improve outcomes, and automatically iterate through cycles without manual intervention between tests. This allows for rapid hypothesis testing at [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] difficult to achieve through manual experimentation, while maintaining the same principle of evidence-based refinement that characterizes the broader methodology.
