# Oil Formation

Oil formation is a geological process spanning millions of years, beginning with the accumulation of organic material in ancient marine and sedimentary environments. Plankton, algae, and other biological matter settled in oxygen-poor basins where decomposition was limited, allowing organic material to concentrate in the sedimentary record. As successive layers of sediment accumulated above this organic-rich material, the weight of overlying strata increased pressure while geothermal heat from Earth's interior rose with depth.

## Transformation Through Burial

Under conditions of high pressure and temperature, the accumulated organic matter undergoes chemical changes known as catagenesis. This process transforms the initial organic sludge, or kerogen, into liquid hydrocarbons. The resulting oil migrates through porous rock layers until it is trapped by impermeable caprock, forming reservoirs that can be extracted.

## Geological Context and Oxidation

While oil formation requires anoxic conditions for preservation, the broader geological history involves periods of high atmospheric oxygen that influenced global rock chemistry. These oxidative periods contributed to the formation of oxidized rock layers, such as the red beds found in the Australian interior. Although coal and oil share organic origins, their preservation and the resulting geological features are dictated by the specific balance of oxygen levels, burial depth, and thermal history during their respective formation eras.
