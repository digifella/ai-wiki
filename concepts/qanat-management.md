# Qanat Management

[[concepts/qanat|Qanat]] management refers to the maintenance, operation, and [[concepts/governance|governance]] systems for underground water conveyance networks that have been central to water supply in [[concepts/iran|Iran]] and surrounding regions for over two millennia. These gravity-fed [[concepts/tunnel|tunnel]] systems transport groundwater from aquifers to surface outlets for irrigation and domestic consumption, engineered to minimize evaporation losses in arid climates. The systems require ongoing maintenance of intake structures, tunnel sections, and outlet points to function effectively.

## Historical Development and Decline

Qanats proliferated throughout [[entities/iran|Iran]] from ancient times through the medieval period, with skilled [[entities/employees|workers]] (known as muqanni) maintaining elaborate networks across the country. However, the 20th century saw significant decline in [[concepts/qanats|qanat systems]] due to several converging factors: the introduction of motorized pumps and deep wells, shifts toward centralized [[concepts/water-management|water management]] infrastructure, rural-to-urban migration, and loss of traditional knowledge among younger generations. This deterioration has contributed substantially to Iran's contemporary water [[concepts/security|security]] challenges.

## Contemporary Water Crisis

The abandonment and degradation of qanat networks has coincided with intensifying [[concepts/water-scarcity|water scarcity]] across Iran. Many remaining qanats suffer from reduced [[concepts/flow|flow]], neglected maintenance, and contamination, while extraction from deep aquifers and surface sources has accelerated groundwater depletion. The loss of these historically sustainable systems has left Iran increasingly dependent on unsustainable water extraction methods and vulnerable to prolonged droughts, exacerbating both agricultural and urban water shortages.

## Restoration Efforts

Recent decades have witnessed growing recognition of qanats' potential as sustainable water management solutions. UNESCO designation of Persian qanats as Intangible [[concepts/cultural-heritage|Cultural Heritage]] and various government initiatives have aimed to document, restore, and preserve functional systems. These efforts balance water security needs with cultural [[concepts/adaptive-reuse|heritage conservation]], though challenges remain in funding [[concepts/preservation|restoration]] work and reviving the [[concepts/expertise|expertise]] required for system maintenance.
