# Cooking Dried Pasta

The fundamental method for cooking dried pasta begins with bringing a large pot of salted water to a rolling boil. The standard ratio is approximately four parts water to one part pasta by weight, providing sufficient space for even heat distribution and preventing the pasta from sticking together. Salt should be added to the water before the pasta enters, as this is when the starch structure most readily absorbs seasoning. The generous water volume maintains boiling temperature when pasta is added, ensuring consistent cooking without significant temperature drops that could lead to uneven texture.

## Texture and Timing

Pasta is typically cooked until it reaches *al dente*, a state where the exterior is tender but the center retains a slight firmness. This texture is crucial for sauce adhesion, as the porous surface of properly cooked pasta grips sauce more effectively than overcooked, mushy strands. Cooking time varies by shape and thickness, so following package instructions as a baseline while tasting frequently is recommended to achieve the desired consistency.

## Sauce Integration

Reserving a portion of the starchy cooking water before draining is a critical step for flavor integration. The starch in the water acts as an emulsifier, helping to bind oil-based sauces to the pasta and creating a cohesive, glossy coating. Adding this liquid to the sauce while tossing the pasta ensures that the flavors meld together, preventing the sauce from sliding off the noodles and enhancing the overall mouthfeel of the dish.
