# English Alphabet

## Overview
The standard 26-character sequence used in Modern English orthography, functioning as a phonemic [[concepts/writing|writing]] system derived from the Latin script. Serves as the foundational framework for lexicography, phonics, dictionary sequencing, and computational [[concepts/text|text]] [[concepts/encoding|encoding]] (ASCII/Unicode).

## Historical Evolution
- Traces developmental lineage from Egyptian hieroglyphs through Proto-Sinaitic, Phoenician, and Greek alphabet [[concepts/adaptations|adaptations]] before Etruscan and Roman standardization.
- Contemporary alphabetical ordering preserves ancient phonetic categorization principles rather than arbitrary arrangement, reflecting historical consonant-to-vowel classification.
- Fundamentally contrasts with Logographic systems and Syllabaries by utilizing discrete phoneme representation for efficient sound-to-symbol mapping.
- Structural stabilization occurred via medieval Latinization, early printing press standardization, and global colonial linguistic expansion.

## Structural Properties
- Composition: 5 primary vowels (A, E, I, O, U), 1 semi-vowel (Y), 21 consonants.
- Functions as the canonical reference for alphabetization, computational base mappings, and educational phonics instruction.
- Exhibits regional orthographic variations (e.g., British vs. American English spellings) without altering the core sequential order.
