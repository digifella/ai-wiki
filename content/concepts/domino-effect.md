# Domino Effect

## Definition
A chain reaction where an initial event triggers a subsequent series of events. Originates from the game Dominoes, where one piece tipping causes the next to fall due to kinetic energy transfer.

## Core Principles
- **Cascading Failure**: In systems engineering, the failure of one component causes failure in connected components.
- **Non-linearity**: Small initial changes can result in disproportionately large outcomes.
- **Interconnectivity**: Requires a coupled system where entities are physically or logically linked.

## Applications & Context

### Systems & Engineering
- Supply Chain Management: Disruptions [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] one [[entities/nodejs|node]] propagate to upstream/downstream partners.
- Financial Crisis: Contagion effects in banking systems; failure of one institution impacts market confidence globally.

### History & Sociology
- [[concepts/geopolitics]]: The original Domino [[concepts/theory|Theory]] applied to Cold War containment strategy.
- Pandemics: Historical events often display cascading triggers rather than single causes.
	- Example: [[lab-notes/2026-05-19-2022-Study-Volcanic-Eruptions-Triggering-the-Black-Death|2022 Study: Volcanic Eruptions Triggering the Black Death]] suggests [[concepts/volcanic-eruptions|volcanic eruptions]] triggered climatic shifts that exacerbated the [[entities/black-death|Black Death]], illustrating a natural domino effect from [[concepts/geology|geology]] to climate to [[concepts/epidemiology|epidemiology]].

### Biology
- Signal Transduction: Molecular cascades where one protein activates another in a sequence.
