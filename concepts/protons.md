# Protons

Protons are positively charged subatomic particles that serve as fundamental constituents of atomic nuclei. Each proton carries an elementary positive charge of approximately $1.602 \times 10^{-19}$ coulombs and possesses a mass of roughly $1.67 \times 10^{-27}$ kilograms, which is approximately 1,836 times the mass of an electron. Along with neutrons, protons form the dense core of every atom, and their stability is a key factor in the structural integrity of matter.

The number of protons within an atom's nucleus defines its atomic number, thereby identifying the specific chemical element and determining its position in the periodic table. This count remains constant for a given element regardless of its isotopic variation, which is instead determined by the number of neutrons. Consequently, the proton count is the primary determinant of an atom's chemical identity and its placement within the periodic framework.

In the context of high-energy physics and astrophysics, protons are significant components of cosmic rays. Observatories such as the Large High Altitude Air Shower Observatory (LHAASO) detect these particles to study their origins and propagation through space. Research into cosmic ray protons provides critical insights into high-energy processes in the universe, including supernova remnants and active galactic nuclei, contributing to the broader understanding of particle physics and cosmic phenomena.
