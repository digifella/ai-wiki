# Magnetic Traces

**Magnetic traces** (remanent magnetization) are permanent magnetic signatures recorded in rocks during their formation or cooling, aligning with [[concepts/earths-magnetic-field|Earth's magnetic field]] at that specific geological time. They serve as the primary proxy for reconstructing paleomagnetism and validating [[concepts/tectonic-plate-shifts|plate tectonics]].

## Key Findings & Applications

- **Paleolatitude Reconstruction**: Inclination of magnetic minerals indicates the latitude of [[concepts/geological-formation|rock formation]], allowing reconstruction of [[concepts/continental-drift|continental drift]].
- **Seafloor Spreading Evidence**: Symmetrical magnetic stripes on the ocean floor provide direct [[concepts/proof|proof]] of seafloor spreading and polarity reversals.
- **Early [[concepts/crustal-movement|Tectonic Activity]]**: Magnetic traces in ancient crustal fragments provide the oldest direct evidence for [[entities/earth|Earth]]’s [[concepts/crust-movement|tectonic motion]], suggesting plate tectonics began ~3.48 billion years ago.
	- Published in *[[concepts/science|Science]]* (Brenner et al., Harvard/Yale; cited in [[entities/scientific-american|Scientific American]] June 2026).
	- See also: [[lab-notes/2026-05-28-Earliest-evidence-of-plate-tectonics|Earliest evidence of plate tectonics]]
	- This early onset implies tectonic processes influenced early atmospheric [[concepts/writing|composition]], the [[concepts/earths-magnetic-field|planetary magnetic field]], and potentially the flourishing of life by shaping global geochemical cycles.
