# Ultra Wide Lens

An ultra wide lens is defined by a field of view typically exceeding 100 degrees, achieved through short focal lengths. On full-frame sensors, these lenses generally range from 14mm to 24mm, while crop-sensor formats require proportionally shorter focal lengths to achieve equivalent angles of view. This optical design captures a substantially larger portion of a scene than standard lenses, providing critical spatial context for landscape, architectural, and environmental photography.

The extreme perspective inherent to ultra wide lenses introduces significant geometric distortion, particularly at the edges of the frame. Straight lines near the periphery often appear curved, a characteristic that requires careful composition to manage. Photographers utilize this distortion intentionally to emphasize foreground elements and create a sense of depth, or they correct it during post-processing to maintain architectural integrity.

These lenses are essential tools for panoramic photography and stitching techniques. By capturing a wider swath of the environment in a single frame, they reduce the number of images required for high-resolution panoramas, minimizing alignment errors and exposure inconsistencies. This capability makes them particularly valuable for capturing vast interiors, expansive landscapes, and dynamic urban environments where a standard lens would fail to encompass the full scope of the subject.
