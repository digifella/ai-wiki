# Mars

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun in Earth's solar system, orbiting between Earth and the asteroid belt. As a terrestrial planet, it possesses a thin atmosphere composed primarily of carbon dioxide, with surface temperatures ranging from approximately −125 °C at the poles to 20 °C at the equator during summer. The planet's distinctive reddish appearance results from iron oxide present in its surface rocks and dust. Mars has a diameter roughly half that of Earth and a mass approximately one-tenth that of Earth.

## Physical Characteristics

The planet features a solid, cratered surface characterized by vast plains, deep valleys, and the largest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons. Its geology includes evidence of past water activity, such as dried riverbeds and mineral deposits that form in the presence of liquid water. The surface is covered in fine dust and regolith, which contributes to the planet's global dust storms that can occasionally envelop the entire globe.

## Exploration and Scientific Interest

Mars is a primary subject of scientific study, particularly regarding the investigation of potential extremophile environments. Numerous robotic missions have been sent to the planet to analyze its geology, climate, and potential for past or present life. These efforts focus on understanding the planet's history of water flow and assessing the habitability of its subsurface environments, where liquid water may still exist beneath the frozen crust.
