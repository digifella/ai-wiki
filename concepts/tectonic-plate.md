# Tectonic Plate

A tectonic plate is a large, rigid segment of [[entities/earth|Earth]]'s lithosphere that floats on the semi-fluid asthenosphere beneath it. The lithosphere is divided into several major plates and numerous smaller ones, with boundaries where plates meet marking zones of intense geological activity. These plates are in constant motion, moving at rates typically between 2 and 10 centimeters per year, driven by convection currents in Earth's mantle.

## Chronology and Origins
* [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Earliest-evidence-of-plate-tectonics|Earliest evidence of plate tectonics]]: Recent studies utilizing [[concepts/magnetic-traces|magnetic traces]] from ancient crust indicate that some form of [[concepts/tectonic-plate-shifts|plate tectonics]] may have initiated as early as 3.48 billion years ago.
* [[lab-notes/2026-05-27-Earliest-evidence-of-plate-tectonics|Earliest evidence of plate tectonics]]: A June 2026 study in *[[concepts/science|Science]]* provides compelling evidence for the oldest direct signs of Earth’s [[concepts/crust-movement|tectonic motion]]. Researchers used magnetic traces from ancient crust to confirm that plate tectonics likely began ~3.48 billion years ago.
* These early movements were critical in shaping Earth’s atmospheric [[concepts/writing|composition]], the planet’s [[concepts/protective-magnetic-field|protective magnetic field]], and potentially the flourishing of life.

## Plate Interactions and Boundaries

The geological consequences of plate motion depend on the relative [[concepts/exercise|movement]] of the plates at their boundaries.
