# Poaching Eggs

[[concepts/seafood-poaching|Poaching]] is a cooking method in which eggs are gently simmered in water without their shells, typically until the [[concepts/whites|whites]] are set while the yolk remains soft. The technique is valued in cooking for producing eggs with a delicate [[concepts/texture|texture]] and is commonly served on toast, salads, or as part of dishes like eggs Benedict.

## The Vinegar Pre-Soak Method

A technique demonstrated by cooking educator [[entities/papamau|PapaMau]] involves pre-soaking eggs in vinegar before poaching. The acidity of the vinegar is thought to help coagulate the egg whites more quickly and evenly, reducing the likelihood of the whites dispersing into wispy strands in the water. This method represents one approach to achieving a more compact, neatly formed poached egg with minimal waste.

## Basic Poaching Process

The standard poaching process requires simmering water in a shallow pan, typically around 160–180°F (70–80°C), though some methods use boiling water followed by removal from heat. The egg is cracked into the water where it cooks until the whites become opaque and set while the yolk ideally remains runny. The poached egg is then removed with a slotted spoon and drained before serving.
