# Texture

Texture in visual media refers to the surface detail and tactile quality apparent in an image. It describes how a surface appears to feel—whether rough, smooth, bumpy, or fine-grained—and encompasses both the actual surface characteristics of photographed objects and the perceived visual qualities created through image composition and processing. Texture adds depth and visual interest by engaging the viewer's tactile perception, even though the image itself is flat.

## In Photography

Texture becomes particularly prominent in photography through the interplay of light and shadow across a subject's surface. Side lighting, often referred to as raking light, accentuates micro-reliefs and irregularities, making texture more visible to the camera sensor. Conversely, flat, diffuse lighting tends to minimize textural details, resulting in a smoother, more uniform appearance.

## Digital Manipulation

In post-processing workflows, such as those in Adobe Lightroom, texture is often manipulated to enhance or reduce surface detail without affecting the overall clarity or sharpness of the image. Tools like the Dehaze slider can increase the perception of texture by boosting local contrast, while texture-specific sliders allow for the subtle enhancement of fine details or the smoothing of skin and surfaces. This distinction allows editors to adjust the tactile quality of an image independently from its edge definition.
