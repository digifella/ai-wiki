# Domestication Of Sugar

Sugar cane, native to New Guinea and Southeast Asia, was first domesticated approximately 8000 BCE. Early Indian civilizations developed foundational techniques for sugar production, [[concepts/learning|learning]] to extract juice from cane stalks and crystallize it into granulated sugar through repeated boiling and cooling cycles. This labor-intensive process transformed sugar from a minor flavoring agent into a refined commodity with substantial commercial value, gradually displacing honey as the primary sweetener in many regions.

## Medieval and Early Modern Expansion

During the medieval period, Arab traders facilitated the spread of sugar [[concepts/agriculture|cultivation]] across the Mediterranean and North Africa, introducing refined [[concepts/crystal-formation|crystallization]] methods to the Islamic [[entities/earth|world]]. By the early modern era, European powers, particularly Portugal and Spain, established large-scale plantations in Atlantic islands such as Madeira and the Canary Islands. This expansion laid the groundwork for the transatlantic [[concepts/sugar|sugar trade]], shifting production centers to the [[concepts/new-world|Americas]] and [[concepts/linking|linking]] the commodity directly to the rise of [[concepts/slavery|chattel slavery]] and colonial economic systems.
