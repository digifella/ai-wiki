# Uta Frith

**[[concepts/uta-frith|Uta Frith]]** FBA is a prominent British developmental psychologist and cognitive [[entities/edward-taub|neuroscientist]]. She is widely regarded as a foundational figure in the study of [[concepts/autism|Autism]] and Dyslexia, having pioneered the cognitive approach to understanding these conditions. Currently an Emeritus Professor of [[concepts/cognitive-evolution|Cognitive Development]] at [[entities/mit|the Institute]] of Cognitive [[concepts/neuroscience|Neuroscience]], University College London, her work has shaped diagnostic frameworks and theoretical models for decades.

## Key Contributions & Research

### Autism Spectrum Research
- Established early cognitive models of autism, emphasizing deficits in [[concepts/theory|Theory]] of [[concepts/the-mind|Mind]] and executive function.
- Authited seminal texts such as *Autism: Understanding the Disorder* (1989), which bridged behavioral observation with cognitive [[concepts/psychology|psychology]].
- Contributed significantly to the DSM-III-R criteria for autism, influencing how the disorder is clinically identified.
- **Critical Reflections on Diagnostic Expansion:**
	- In recent commentary, Frith has critically examined the broadening of autism [[concepts/diagnostic-criteria|diagnostic criteria]].
	- See: [[lab-notes/2026-05-28-Uta-Friths-Critical-Look-at-Autism-Spectrums-Widening-Di|Uta Frith's Critical Look at Autism Spectrum's Widening Diagnostic Criteria]]

### Dyslexia & Reading Development
- Pioneered the "double-deficit hypothesis" in dyslexia research.
- Investigated the phonological processing deficits underlying reading disorders.
- Co-authored *[[concepts/learning|Learning]] to Read and Write: A Cognitive Approach* (2001) with Margaret Snowling.

### Theoretical Frameworks
- Advocated for the distinction between "mindblindness" (lack of ToM) and general social [[concepts/motivation|motivation]] in autistic individuals.
- Emphasized the heterogeneity of autism, arguing against a single unified causal model.
