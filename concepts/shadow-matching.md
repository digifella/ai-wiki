# Shadow Matching

Shadow matching is the process of ensuring shadows in a compositing workflow align with the environmental light source, color temperature, and intensity. When combining visual elements from different sources—such as placing a photographed object into a new background—the shadows cast by that element must correspond to the same lighting conditions as the scene. This alignment is essential for maintaining visual consistency, as viewers intuitively recognize how light behaves in physical space.

## Technical Considerations

Effective shadow matching requires analyzing several interconnected factors. The direction and angle of shadows must correspond to the light source's position relative to the composited element. Color temperature is equally important; shadows in cool daylight differ significantly from those under warm indoor lighting. Shadow density, softness, and falloff should also match the original scene's atmospheric conditions and distance from surfaces. Compositors typically use reference photography or 3D lighting information from the original shoot to guide these adjustments.

## Practical Application

In practice, shadow matching often involves adjusting shadows through layers, masks, and color correction tools. Compositors may need to reposition, resize, or recolor existing shadows, or create new ones if the source material lacks sufficient shadow information. The process becomes more complex when working with multiple light sources or when the composited element must interact with the environment—for instance, when shadows need to fall across uneven terrain or reflect the ambient light bounce from surrounding surfaces.
