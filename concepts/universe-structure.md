# Universe Structure

Universe structure refers to the large-scale arrangement and composition of matter and energy throughout the cosmos. The observable universe exhibits a hierarchical organization, progressing from individual galaxies to galaxy groups and clusters, then to superclusters connected by vast filaments of matter. These structures are separated by cosmic voids—relatively empty regions of space. This large-scale structure formed through gravitational processes acting on primordial density fluctuations established during the early universe.

## Dark Matter and Cosmic Composition

The distribution of visible matter does not account for the gravitational forces required to hold these large-scale structures together, leading to the inference of dark matter. Recent analyses of gamma-ray emissions from the Milky Way have detected signals consistent with WIMP (Weakly Interacting Massive Particle) annihilation. These observations provide potential evidence for the nature of dark matter, which constitutes a significant portion of the universe's total mass-energy density. Understanding these components is essential for modeling the evolution and ultimate fate of the cosmic web.
