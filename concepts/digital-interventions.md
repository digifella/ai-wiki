# Digital Interventions

Digital interventions refer to the use of digital technologies—including [[concepts/portable-devices|mobile devices]], sensors, and telecommunication infrastructure—to support health systems and improve [[concepts/public-health]] outcomes. These interventions span a spectrum from basic [[concepts/healthcare-informatization|health information systems]] to complex clinical decision support tools.

## Key References & Guidelines

- [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-WHO-guideline|WHO guideline]]: The [[entities/earth|World]] Health Organization published specific [[concepts/recommendations|recommendations]] on digital interventions for [[concepts/health-system|health system]] strengthening (2019). This guideline outlines evidence-based strategies for integrating digital tools into broader health system architectures, focusing on coverage, efficacy, and [[concepts/adoption|organizational integration]].
	- **Scope**: Focuses on strengthening health systems through digital means rather than isolated clinical applications.
	- **Key Themes**: [[concepts/health]] [[concepts/robustness|system resilience]], workforce capacity, digital coverage, and regulatory frameworks.
	- **Source Context**: While the primary publisher is the WHO, related [[concepts/consulting|consulting]] analyses (e.g., EY, 2019) often contextualize these guidelines for implementation in varied economic settings, including the [[entities/uk|United Kingdom]].

## Core Characteristics

- **Systemic Integration**: Must align with existing health infrastructure to avoid fragmentation.
- **Evidence-Based**: Interventions should be grounded in robust clinical and operational data.
- **Scalability**: Designed to expand coverage without proportional increases in cost or complexity.
