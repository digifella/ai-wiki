# US-China AI Competition

## Overview
The strategic rivalry between the [[entities/united-states]] and [[entities/china]] for supremacy in [[entities/ai|Artificial Intelligence]] (AI) development, encompassing [[concepts/hardware|hardware]], [[concepts/software|software]], talent, and ecosystem influence.

## Key Pillars of Competition
- **[[concepts/compute|Compute]] & [[concepts/hardware|Hardware]]:** The struggle for control over the [[concepts/semiconductor-supply-chain|Semiconductor supply chain]], specifically high-end GPUs and [[concepts/neural-processing-units|AI Accelerators]] (e.g., [[entities/nvidia]]), and the impact of US-led Export Controls.
- **Algorithmic [[concepts/innovation|Innovation]]:** The tension between the US-led [[concepts/proprietary-ai]] models and the rise of [[entities/high-performance|high-performance]] [[concepts/open-source]] architectures developed in [[entities/china]].
- **[[concepts/data-sovereignty|Data Sovereignty]]:** Competition over large-scale [[concepts/training-data|datasets]], [[concepts/ai-security]] regulations, and the ability to leverage massive-scale human-generated data for [[concepts/llm]] [[concepts/training|training]].
- **Infrastructure & Energy:** The [[concepts/deployment|deployment]] of Data Centers and the critical requirement for scalable energy [[concepts/grids|grids]] to support massive [[concepts/compute]] demands.

## Recent Developments
- **Rise of Cost-Efficient Chinese Models (2026-04-26):**
    - [[entities/deepseek-v4]] has emerged as a significant [[concepts/open-source]] challenger to US-based [[concepts/proprietary-ai]] [[concepts/leadership|leadership]].
    - The model demonstrates high [[concepts/cost-optimization]], leveraging architectural optimizations to mitigate the impact of [[concepts/compute]] limitations.
    - Represents a [[concepts/strategic-shift|strategic shift]] in [[entities/china]]'s ability to challenge US dominance through efficient, accessible AI technology.
