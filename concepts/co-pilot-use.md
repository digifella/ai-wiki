# Co Pilot Use

Co Pilot Use refers to the deployment of AI-powered assistant tools and [[concepts/agentic-frameworks|agentic frameworks]] within organizational environments, often without formal [[concepts/governance|governance]] structures or [[concepts/security|security]] oversight. These systems—such as AI copilots integrated into [[concepts/productivity|productivity]] suites—enable rapid automation and decision support but introduce significant risks when deployed outside established IT and security controls. The widespread [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of such tools by individual users and departments can create [[concepts/shadow-it|shadow IT]] [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]] where organizations lack visibility into data flows, model capabilities, and potential vulnerabilities.

## Security and Governance Risks

Ungoverned [[concepts/copilot-chat|copilot]] implementations present several interconnected risks. These tools may process sensitive corporate data without encryption or data loss [[concepts/preventive-care|prevention]] [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]], expose [[concepts/intellectual-property-rights|intellectual property]] to third-party AI vendors, and operate with unclear data [[concepts/storing|retention]] [[concepts/policies|policies]]. Agentic frameworks that make autonomous decisions compound these concerns by reducing human oversight and [[concepts/accountability|accountability]]. Additionally, the rapid evolution of these systems means organizational security baselines may not address their specific threat vectors, leaving gaps in [[concepts/vulnerability|vulnerability]] detection and [[concepts/incident-response|incident response]].

## Shadow AI Impact

When [[entities/copilot|copilot]] adoption occurs outside formal procurement and governance channels, it creates what is termed [[concepts/shadow-ai|Shadow AI]]—unvetted [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] operating within organizational infrastructure. [[entities/employees|Employees]] may adopt these tools to increase productivity without [[concepts/conscious-thought|awareness]] of [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] implications, creating distributed risk across the organization. This fragmentation makes it difficult for security and governance teams to maintain consistent policies, audit trails, or control over [[concepts/model-behavior|model behavior]] and outputs.

Organizations addressing co pilot use effectively establish centralized governance frameworks that balance enablement with control, including vendor security assessments, data handling policies, and user training on appropriate [[concepts/use-cases|use cases]].
