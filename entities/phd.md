# Phd

A PhD (Doctor of Philosophy) is a terminal research degree awarded by universities across numerous academic disciplines, including sciences, engineering, humanities, and medicine. The degree represents the highest level of academic qualification in most fields and signifies expertise and original contribution to a discipline. PhD programs exist in virtually every major academic field, from traditional domains like physics and literature to contemporary fields such as computer science and public health.

## Academic Requirements

PhD programs typically require several years of study beyond the bachelor's or master's level. Candidates must complete advanced coursework, pass comprehensive examinations, and conduct original research that results in a dissertation or thesis. This work must demonstrate a significant and novel contribution to the field, which is then evaluated by a committee of experts.

## Professional Context

Holders of a PhD are often referred to as doctors, particularly in academic and medical contexts. The degree is a prerequisite for many careers in higher education, research institutions, and specialized professional roles. In fields such as medicine, the PhD is frequently pursued alongside or after other professional degrees to focus on scientific research and academic inquiry rather than clinical practice.
