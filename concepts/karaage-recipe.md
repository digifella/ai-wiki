# Karaage Recipe

Karaage (唐揚げ) is a Japanese style of fried chicken consisting of bite-sized, boneless pieces marinated in a savory liquid before frying. The marinade typically includes soy sauce, sake, ginger, and garlic, which infuse the meat with umami and aromatic flavors. This preparation method distinguishes karaage from Western fried chicken traditions, as the seasoning penetrates the meat rather than relying primarily on a seasoned coating. The result is tender chicken with well-developed flavor throughout.

## Preparation

To prepare karaage, chicken thighs or breasts are cut into bite-sized pieces and submerged in the marinade for at least 30 minutes, though longer marinating times intensify the flavor. The marinade ratio is typically two parts soy sauce to one part sake, with grated ginger and garlic to taste. After marinating, the chicken pieces are lightly coated in potato starch or cornstarch and fried in oil at around 170–180°C until golden and cooked through. The starch coating creates a crispy exterior while the marinated interior remains moist.

## Serving

Karaage is traditionally served as an appetizer or casual snack, often accompanied by lemon wedges, mayo-based dipping sauce, or simply on its own. It is commonly found in Japanese restaurants, food courts, and casual dining establishments, and has become popular in other cuisines as well. The dish can be prepared in advance and served at room temperature or reheated briefly before serving.
