# Lhc Cms Experiment

The Compact Muon Solenoid (CMS) Experiment is one of two major general-purpose particle detectors operating at CERN's Large Hadron Collider (LHC) in Switzerland. Built to observe high-energy proton-proton collisions, the experiment is designed to detect and analyze the fundamental particles and forces that constitute matter. Its distinctive engineering feature is a compact design integrated with a large superconducting solenoid magnet, which bends the paths of charged particles to enable precise measurement of their momentum and energy.

## Research Focus

The experiment conducts tests for quark substructure, investigating whether quarks are elementary particles or composed of smaller constituents. By analyzing collision data at high energies, CMS seeks to identify deviations from the Standard Model that might indicate new physics or composite structures within quarks. This research complements its broader mandate to study the Higgs boson, supersymmetry, and other phenomena arising from high-energy particle interactions.
