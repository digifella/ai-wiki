# Gravitational Pull

Gravitational pull is the fundamental attractive force that acts between all objects possessing mass. Described mathematically by Newton's law of universal gravitation and refined by Einstein's general relativity, this force operates across all distances and cannot be shielded or eliminated. The strength of gravitational attraction between two masses depends on the magnitude of those masses and decreases with the square of the distance between them.

## Gravitational Effects on Dark Matter

Dark matter, which comprises approximately 85% of the matter in the universe, experiences gravitational attraction just as [[concepts/baryonic-matter|ordinary matter]] does. However, dark matter does not undergo electromagnetic interactions—it neither emits nor absorbs light and does not interact with the electromagnetic force. This absence of [[concepts/electromagnetic-interaction|electromagnetic interaction]] means dark matter cannot lose energy through radiation in the way that ordinary matter can. Consequently, dark matter does not collapse into increasingly dense structures like [[concepts/neutron-stars|neutron stars]] or [[concepts/black-holes|black holes]], despite being subject to gravitational forces. Instead, dark matter tends to form diffuse halos around galaxies, with its [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] governed primarily by gravitational dynamics and the initial conditions of the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]].

The [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] between dark matter and ordinary matter illustrates how multiple forces shape the [[concepts/structure|structure]] of the cosmos. While ordinary matter can radiate energy and collapse into compact objects, dark matter's lack of electromagnetic properties allows it to remain distributed across larger [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]], playing a crucial role in the gravitational scaffolding that holds galaxies together.
