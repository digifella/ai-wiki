# Particle Physics Experiments

Particle physics experiments are systematic investigations designed to test theories and discover fundamental properties of matter and energy. These experiments typically involve high-energy particle collisions, precise measurements of particle properties, or detection of rare particle interactions. Major experiments operate at dedicated facilities such as the [[entities/lhc|Large Hadron Collider]] (LHC) at CERN and Fermilab in the [[entities/united-states|United States]], where scientists use sophisticated detectors to observe and analyze the products of particle collisions.

## The Muon g-2 Experiment

The Muon g-2 experiment at Fermilab measures the magnetic properties of muons, [[concepts/elementary-particles|elementary particles]] similar to electrons but with greater mass. The experiment determines the muon's [[concepts/anomalous-magnetic-moment|anomalous magnetic moment]], a value that tests predictions from the [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|Standard Model of particle physics]]. By comparing experimental measurements with theoretical predictions, physicists can identify discrepancies that might reveal new, undiscovered particles or forces. Results from the latest measurement phase were expected in 2025 and represent one of the most precise tests of fundamental [[concepts/physics|physics]] available.

## Significance and Methods

Modern particle physics experiments employ advanced detection technologies and analysis methods to achieve unprecedented precision. The data gathered from these experiments contributes to our understanding of fundamental forces, the [[entities/nature|nature]] of matter, and the [[concepts/structure|structure]] of the universe. Results from experiments like Muon g-2 either confirm existing [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|theoretical frameworks]] or hint at physics beyond the Standard Model that may require new theoretical developments and future experimental investigation.
