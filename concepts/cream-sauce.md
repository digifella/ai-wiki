# Cream Sauce

A cream sauce is a foundational culinary preparation that combines cream with other ingredients to create a rich, flavorful accompaniment to proteins, vegetables, and pasta dishes. The sauce achieves its characteristic smooth texture through the emulsification of fat and liquid, typically thickened using a roux, reduction, or binding agent such as cornstarch or egg yolk. This combination prevents the cream from separating during cooking, resulting in a stable, cohesive sauce.

## Preparation Methods

The most common method for creating a stable cream sauce involves the use of a roux, which consists of equal parts fat and flour cooked together before the addition of milk or cream. Alternatively, chefs may employ a liaison of egg yolks and cream, known as a crème anglaise base, or thicken the sauce through simple reduction. Each method requires careful temperature control to avoid curdling or breaking the emulsion, ensuring the final product remains smooth and uniform.

## Variations and Applications

Cream sauces serve as a versatile base for numerous classic dishes, ranging from béchamel-based preparations to lighter reductions. Common variations include adding cheese for a Mornay sauce, incorporating mustard or herbs for flavor complexity, or using stock instead of milk for a lighter consistency. These sauces are frequently paired with delicate proteins like fish and chicken, as well as starchy components such as pasta, potatoes, and gratins, to enhance mouthfeel and depth of flavor.
