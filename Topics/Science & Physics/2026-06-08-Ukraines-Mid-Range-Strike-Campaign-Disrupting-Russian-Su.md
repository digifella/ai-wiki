### Summary
The video from [[entities/perun|Perun]] provides a comprehensive overview of the "[[concepts/mid-range-strike|mid-range strike]]" in the Ukrainian [[concepts/conflict|conflict]], an increasingly crucial operational layer positioned between [[concepts/tactical-battlefield-engagements|tactical battlefield engagements]] and [[concepts/strategic-long-range-attacks|strategic long-range attacks]]. While short-range [[concepts/fpv-drones|FPV drones]] dominate the immediate front lines and long-range [[concepts/cruise-missiles|cruise missiles]] target distant strategic assets, the mid-range strike focuses on disrupting enemy logistics and [[concepts/supply-lines|supply lines]] [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] operational depths, often hundreds of kilometers from the front. The [[entities/speaker|speaker]] highlights that this domain of warfare has seen a significant and rapid escalation in 2024, indicating a pivotal evolution in the [[concepts/conflict|conflict]]'s dynamics.

The mid-range battle employs a diverse and evolving set of tools and tactics. Initially, [[entities/ukraine|Ukraine]] utilized guided multiple rocket launchers like HIMARS for precision strikes against static operational infrastructure, such as ammunition depots and command posts, proving effective in degrading Russian fire rates. However, to address moving targets and deeper objectives, Ukraine has increasingly deployed purpose-built Unmanned Aerial Systems (UAS). These include lighter, fixed-wing loitering munitions like the Hornet, designed as "truck hunters" due to their cost-effectiveness, autonomous targeting, and quiet operation, with ranges up to 160 km. Larger, adapted one-way attack drones, such as the Ukrainian FP-2, carry heavier payloads (e.g., 44kg to 200kg explosives) up to 200 km, targeting more valuable static infrastructure like buildings or air defense systems. Further innovations involve range-extended rotor drones and drone "motherships," leveraging advanced technologies like Starlink for resilient [[concepts/communication|communication]] and AI for enhanced target recognition. This growth is supported by increased [[concepts/domestic-production|domestic production]], synergistic effects with SEAD/DEAD (Suppression/Destruction of Enemy Air Defenses) and long-range strike campaigns, and a multi-agency approach within the Ukrainian military.

The video uses the Southern Ukraine front (Zaporizhzhia, Kherson, and Crimea) as a case study to illustrate the strategic impact of the mid-range strike. The region's geography, characterized by water bodies to the west and south, makes Russian supply lines particularly vulnerable once land-based routes become the primary option. Ukraine has systematically disrupted alternative logistical pathways: direct shipping to Crimean ports was made precarious by naval drone attacks; cross-Kerch Strait ferries and the Crimean Bridge faced significant damage and restrictions, largely barring military-sensitive cargo. This has funnelled Russian military supplies, particularly fuel and ammunition, onto the northern rail and road routes through occupied Ukrainian territory, placing them directly within the mid-range strike zone. The scale of this disruption is evidenced by official Ukrainian claims of vastly increased soft-skin vehicle losses (trucks and cars) from March to May 2024, supported by geolocated strike data. While the sheer volume of Russian vehicles means complete logistical paralysis is unlikely, these strikes dramatically increase inefficiency, risk, and operational costs for [[entities/russia|Russia]]. This, in turn, diverts critical manpower and air defense assets away from the front line to protect logistics, impacts civilian life in occupied territories through fuel rationing and restrictions, and potentially constrains Russia's ability to sustain large-scale offensives, or even sets conditions for future Ukrainian counter-offensives. The continuous expansion of the drone "kill zone" further into operational depth signifies a critical shift in the nature of warfare that militaries worldwide should closely observe.
