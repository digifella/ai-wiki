# Upper Stage Failure

Upper stage failure refers to a malfunction or loss of function in the upper stage of a multi-stage launch vehicle. The upper stage serves as the final propulsive component, responsible for achieving the necessary orbital velocity and performing precise payload insertion after the lower stages have separated. Because this component operates in the vacuum of space and must execute critical maneuvers to reach target orbits, failures at this phase of flight typically result in the total loss of both the vehicle and its payload.

In the context of Blue Origin's New Glenn heavy-lift launch vehicle, upper stage failure represents a critical risk factor in mission success. The New Glenn's upper stage is designed to deliver heavy payloads to geostationary transfer orbit and beyond, requiring high reliability in its propulsion and guidance systems. A failure during this phase would prevent the delivery of commercial or government satellites to their intended destinations, resulting in significant financial and operational setbacks.

The consequences of such a failure extend beyond the immediate loss of hardware. It may trigger extensive investigations into the root cause, potentially leading to delays in the launch schedule and a temporary grounding of the fleet until corrective actions are implemented. For the New Glenn program, maintaining upper stage reliability is essential for establishing a reputation for consistent performance in the competitive commercial launch market.
