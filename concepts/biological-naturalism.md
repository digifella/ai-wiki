# Biological Naturalism

**Biological Naturalism** is the philosophical position, primarily associated with [[entities/chef-john|John]] Searle, that mental states and [[concepts/conscious-thought|consciousness]] are higher-level features of the brain, causally reduced to lower-level neuronal computations, but not reducible to them in a way that eliminates their subjective character. It asserts that consciousness is a biological phenomenon, akin to digestion or photosynthesis, requiring specific biological machinery to exist.

## Core Tenets

*   **Causal Reductionism:** Mental states are caused by sub-neuronal processes and are identical to them, yet they possess irreducible subjective qualities (qualia).
*   **Biological Specificity:** Consciousness is not substrate-independent; it requires specific biological causal powers found in brains, not just computational structures.
*   **Anti-Computationalism:** The brain is not a computer in the classical sense; [[concepts/physics-simulation|computational simulation]] of a process is not the same as the process itself.

## AI and Consciousness

Recent analyses reinforce the biological naturalist view that current AI architectures lack the necessary biological constraints for genuine consciousness.

*   **Substrate Dependence:** As argued in [[lab-notes/2026-10-12-AI-Consciousness-Biological-Constraints-and-Current-Limi|AI Consciousness: Biological Constraints and Current Limitations]], [[concepts/demystifying-llms|large language models]] (LLMs) operate on syntactic manipulation without [[concepts/semantic-understanding|semantic understanding]] or biological grounding.
*   **Current Limitations:** Scientists identify that achieving consciousness requires more than scale or complexity; it demands specific biological [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] that current silicon-based systems do not possess.
*   **Philosophical Context:** This aligns with Searle's Chinese Room Argument, which posits that syntax (code) is insufficient for semantics (meaning/consciousness).
