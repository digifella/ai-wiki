# Wearable Devices

Wearable devices are electronic instruments designed to be worn on the body, incorporating embedded sensors that continuously collect data on physiological parameters, movement, or environmental factors. These devices function through integrated hardware and software systems that monitor, record, and often transmit data in real time or at intervals. They have become established tools in both consumer health applications and clinical allied health practice.

## Clinical and Allied Health Applications

In allied health settings, wearable devices are used for patient monitoring, assessment, and rehabilitation. Common applications include accelerometers and motion sensors for gait analysis and movement disorders, chest-worn or wrist-based devices for cardiac monitoring, and skin patches for continuous measurement of vital signs such as temperature and hydration levels. Physical therapists, occupational therapists, and other allied health professionals use these devices to quantify functional outcomes, track recovery progress, and detect changes in patient status that may warrant intervention.

## Device Types and Data Collection

Wearable devices range from smartwatches and fitness trackers to specialized medical-grade equipment. Data collected can include heart rate, respiratory rate, sleep patterns, activity levels, posture, and location. The continuous or frequent sampling provided by wearables offers advantages over periodic clinical assessment, enabling detection of patterns and trends that might not be apparent during standard appointments. However, data accuracy and reliability vary depending on device design, calibration, and individual factors affecting sensor performance.
