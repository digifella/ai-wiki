# Expansion of the Universe

The **expansion of the universe** is the increase in distance between gravitationally unbound parts of the [[concepts/observable-universe|observable universe]] over time. This fundamental concept resolves several [[concepts/cosmological-paradox|cosmological paradoxes]], including the darkness of the [[concepts/night-sky|night sky]].

## Key Concepts

*   **[[concepts/olbers-paradox|Olbers' Paradox]]**: The question of why the night sky is dark if the universe is infinite and static. The expansion of the universe (and its finite age) provides the primary [[concepts/solution|resolution]] by redshifting light and limiting the observable horizon.
    *   For detailed analysis, see [[lab-notes/2026-08-10-Olbers-Paradox-Why-the-Night-Sky-is-Dark|Olbers' Paradox: Why the Night Sky is Dark]].
*   **[[concepts/galaxy-redshift|Cosmological Redshift]]**: As space expands, the wavelength of light traveling through it stretches, shifting towards the red end of the spectrum. This reduces the energy of [[concepts/light|photons]] from [[concepts/distant-galaxies|distant galaxies]].
*   **Finite Age**: The universe has a finite age (~13.8 billion years). Light from [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]] beyond a certain distance has not had time to reach us, preventing the sky from being uniformly bright.
*   **Observable Universe**: The spherical region of the universe comprising all matter that can be observed from [[entities/earth|Earth]] at the present time, limited by the [[concepts/finite-speed-of-light|speed of light]] and the [[concepts/universe-age|age of the universe]].
