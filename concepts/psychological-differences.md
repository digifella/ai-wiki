# Psychological Differences

**Definition:** The study of how different psychological concepts or frameworks can influence our perception and interpretation of the same phenomena, even when these phenomena are mathematically identical.

### Related Concepts
- cognitive-[[concepts/dissonance|dissonance]]

### Key Points
- [[concepts/scientific-theories|Scientific theories]] that are mathematically equivalent may differ psychologically due to distinct underlying concepts and frameworks.
- Understanding involves more than just knowing; it requires grasping the deeper philosophical [[concepts/ideas|ideas]] behind [[concepts/scientific-principles|scientific principles]].

### New Insights from Feynman's Lecture

- **[[concepts/mathematical-equivalence|Equivalent Theories]]:** Two theories, A and B, can be mathematically identical yet psychologically different in their conceptualization.
- **Progress Through Understanding:** True progress is not solely about [[concepts/experimental-validation|empirical validation]] but also involves deepening understanding of the philosophical underpinnings that shape our theories.
