# Model Ownership

**Model Ownership** refers to the legal, technical, and ethical rights associated with the creation, distribution, and usage of [[concepts/ai-models|artificial intelligence models]]. It encompasses [[concepts/intellectual-property-rights|intellectual property rights]], [[concepts/licensing|licensing]] terms, and the distinction between proprietary closed-source systems and open-weight/open-source alternatives.

## Key Dimensions

- **Intellectual Property**: Determination of who holds the copyright or patent rights to [[concepts/model-weights|model weights]], architecture, and [[concepts/custom-dataset|training data]].
- **Licensing**: Constraints on commercial use, modification, and redistribution (e.g., [[concepts/apache-2-license|Apache 2.0]], MIT, or restrictive proprietary licenses).
- **[[concepts/opacity|Transparency]]**: The degree to which model internals, [[concepts/language-data|training data]], and [[concepts/ai-performance-evaluation|performance metrics]] are disclosed to the public.

## Recent Developments

- **Shift Toward [[concepts/open-source-weights|Open Weights]]**: The [[concepts/deployment|release]] of [[entities/inkling|Inkling]]: [[entities/thinking-machines-lab|Thinking Machines Lab]]'s Open [[concepts/multimodal-ai|Multimodal AI]] Breakthrough by Thinking Machines Lab marks a significant shift in the landscape. Inkling is an open-weights [[concepts/multimodal-language-models|multimodal AI]] model that challenges the dominance of proprietary frameworks by providing accessible, [[entities/high-performance|high-performance]] capabilities without restrictive licensing barriers.
- **Impact on Proprietary Models**: The availability of robust open models like Inkling pressures commercial [[concepts/nodes|entities]] to justify their closed-source approaches through superior performance or unique proprietary features, rather than mere access control.
