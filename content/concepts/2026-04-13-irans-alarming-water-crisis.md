# 2026 04 13 Iran's Alarming Water Crisis

Iran faces a critical water shortage driven by a combination of geographical and climatic factors. The country's location in an arid region of the Middle East, coupled with decades of declining precipitation and rising temperatures, has severely depleted both surface water and groundwater resources. Major rivers including the Tigris and Euphrates have reduced flows, while aquifers that supply much of Iran's drinking water and agricultural needs are being drawn down faster than they can be naturally replenished.

## Agricultural and Economic Impact

Agriculture accounts for the majority of Iran's water consumption, making the crisis particularly acute for rural communities and the national economy. Falling water tables have forced farmers to drill deeper wells or abandon agricultural land entirely, contributing to rural migration toward cities. Competition for limited water resources between agricultural, industrial, and residential sectors has intensified, creating social and economic tensions across the country.

## Urban and Regional Challenges

Major cities including Tehran, Isfahan, and other population centers face acute water supply pressures. Some regions have experienced water rationing and infrastructure stress as demand outpaces available supply. The crisis also has regional dimensions, as Iran's water stress intersects with transboundary water-sharing agreements and disputes with neighboring countries over shared river systems.
