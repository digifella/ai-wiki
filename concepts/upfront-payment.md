# Upfront Payment

**Upfront Payment** refers to a transaction model where funds are transferred before the delivery of goods, services, or the [[concepts/happiness|fulfillment]] of a contractual obligation. While standard in many commercial contexts (e.g., Prepaid Services, Deposits), it is the defining mechanic of Advance Fee Fraud.

## Mechanism & Risk
- **Cash [[concepts/flow|Flow]] Priority:** Seller receives capital immediately, shifting risk entirely to the buyer.
- **[[concepts/trust|Trust]] Dependency:** Requires high trust or strong legal recourse [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] to prevent [[concepts/fraud]].
- **Liquidity:** Provides immediate liquidity to the recipient, often used in Supply Chain Financing.

## Fraudulent Context: Advance Fee Scams
In illicit contexts, upfront payments are the core of Advance Fee Fraud (419 scams). The perpetrator requests an initial payment under the guise of fees, taxes, or bribes to [[concepts/deployment|release]] a larger sum or asset.

### Historical Evolution
The structure of these scams has evolved significantly, adapting to geopolitical changes and communication technologies:

- **[[concepts/spanish-prisoner-swindle|Spanish Prisoner Swindle]]:** The earliest recorded [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] (late 18th century), involving a fictional prisoner needing funds for release and subsequent reward.
- **[[concepts/nigerian-prince-scam|Nigerian Prince Scam]]:** A modern adaptation emerging in the late 20th century, leveraging [[entities/email|email]] and international banking complexities.
- **Modern Variants:** Includes Romance Scams, Business Email Compromise, and cryptocurrency-based Pig Butchering schemes.
