# Cream Sauce

A cream sauce is a foundational culinary preparation that combines cream with other ingredients to create a rich, flavorful accompaniment to proteins, vegetables, and pasta dishes. The sauce achieves its characteristic smooth texture through the emulsification of fat and liquid, typically thickened using a roux, reduction, or binding agent such as cornstarch or egg yolk. This combination prevents the cream from separating during cooking, resulting in a stable, cohesive sauce.

## Preparation Methods

Cream sauces are commonly made using several techniques. The most traditional approach involves creating a roux—a mixture of equal parts fat (usually butter) and flour—which is then combined with stock or broth before cream is added. Alternatively, cream can be reduced directly over heat to concentrate flavors and achieve desired thickness, or a liaison of egg yolk and cream can be used to thicken and enrich sauces near the end of cooking. The choice of method depends on the desired consistency and the specific dish being prepared.

## Common Applications

Cream sauces serve as accompaniments to a wide range of dishes across numerous cuisines. They are frequently paired with pasta, grilled or pan-seared meats, and roasted vegetables. Classic examples include béchamel, a foundational French sauce made from roux and milk, and Alfredo sauce, which combines butter, cream, and cheese. Cream sauces can be flavored with herbs, mushrooms, wine, or other ingredients to complement specific dishes.
