# Purpose

Purpose refers to the fundamental reason or intention that drives an action, decision, organization, or individual existence. It represents the "why" behind what we do, distinct from the "what" (the action itself) or the "how" (the method of execution). Purpose operates as a guiding principle across multiple levels—personal, organizational, and societal—shaping priorities, values, and long-term direction. It serves as a foundation for [[concepts/motivation|motivation]], meaning-making, and sustained engagement with goals.

## Individual Purpose

At the individual level, purpose represents a person's core reason for existing and acting in the world. This may encompass career direction, personal values, relationships, creative expression, or contribution to causes larger than oneself. Psychological research suggests that individuals with a clear sense of purpose tend to experience greater well-being, resilience, and life satisfaction. Purpose often develops through reflection on personal strengths, values, and how one wishes to impact others or the world.

## Organizational Purpose

Organizations similarly function with explicit or implicit purposes that guide decision-making and strategy. An organization's purpose extends beyond profit or output to address why the organization exists and what problem it solves or value it creates. This concept has gained prominence through frameworks such as Simon Sinek's [[concepts/golden-circle|Golden Circle]], which emphasizes starting with "why" as a means of building stronger organizational culture, employee engagement, and customer loyalty. Clear organizational purpose can differentiate institutions in competitive markets and attract stakeholders aligned with their values.

## Purpose and Action

Purpose differs from abstract meaning by being intimately connected to deliberate action. While related to concepts like [[concepts/meaning|meaning]] and [[concepts/values|values]], purpose is specifically oriented toward intentional doing. The relationship between identifying one's purpose and achieving goals remains subject to ongoing discussion—some evidence suggests purpose precedes motivation and achievement, while other perspectives suggest the relationship is reciprocal, with purpose clarified through meaningful action and accomplishment.
