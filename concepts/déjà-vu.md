# Déjà Vu

Déjà vu is a psychological phenomenon characterized by the strong, fleeting sense of familiarity experienced when encountering a new situation, place, or conversation for the first time. The term originates from French, literally translating to "already seen." Although the [[concepts/experience|experience]] typically lasts only seconds to minutes, it often leaves a striking impression on the individual, [[concepts/prompting|prompting]] questions about its underlying causes and [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]].

Research indicates that déjà vu is a common occurrence across human populations, with studies suggesting that between 60 and 80 percent of people report having experienced it at least once. The phenomenon is generally considered benign and is not inherently linked to pathology, though it can occasionally be associated with neurological conditions such as epilepsy. Its prevalence peaks in young adulthood and tends to decrease with age.

## Theoretical Explanations

Several theories attempt to explain the mechanism behind déjà vu. One prominent hypothesis suggests it results from a minor delay in neural processing, where the brain processes sensory information twice, creating a false [[concepts/memory|memory]] of prior experience. Another [[concepts/theory|theory]] posits that it arises from a mismatch between the brain's familiarity and recollection systems, where a situation feels familiar due to subtle similarities with past events, even though the specific context is new. These explanations highlight the complex interplay between [[concepts/storing|memory formation]] and perception in the human brain.
