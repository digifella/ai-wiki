# Meat Tenderization

Meat tenderization refers to techniques that improve the texture, moisture retention, and cooking properties of meat. These methods work by breaking down tough muscle fibers and connective tissue, making meat easier to chew and more palatable. Tenderization can be achieved through mechanical means, enzymatic processes, or chemical treatments.

## Baking Soda Method

Baking soda is an effective and accessible tenderizing agent for ground meat. When applied to ground meat before cooking, baking soda raises the pH of the meat's surface, allowing the muscle fibers to relax and retain more moisture during heating. This results in improved texture and juiciness in the cooked product. Additionally, the alkaline environment created by baking soda promotes better browning through the Maillard reaction, enhancing flavor development. Typically, ¼ teaspoon of baking soda per pound of ground meat is sufficient; the meat should rest for 15 minutes after application before cooking.

## Other Tenderization Methods

Mechanical tenderization involves physically breaking down muscle fibers using mallets or specialized equipment. Enzymatic tenderization employs natural enzymes from fruits like pineapple, papaya, and kiwi, which contain proteases that digest muscle proteins. Salt and acidic ingredients such as vinegar, citrus juice, and yogurt also tenderize meat by denaturing proteins, though these methods require longer marinating times. The choice of method depends on the cut of meat and desired cooking application.
