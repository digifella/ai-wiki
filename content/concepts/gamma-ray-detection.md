# Gamma Ray Detection

Gamma ray detection is an observational technique used to identify high-energy [[concepts/light|photons]] produced by various astrophysical phenomena. In the context of [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] research, gamma ray detection serves as a method for searching for signatures of [[concepts/wimp-particles|Weakly Interacting Massive Particles]] (WIMPs) when they annihilate or decay in regions of high dark matter density, such as the galactic center and halo of the [[concepts/milky-way|Milky Way]].

## Detection Method

[[concepts/gamma-rays|Gamma rays]] are detected using specialized space-based and ground-based instruments that measure the energy and direction of incoming high-energy photons. Space telescopes like Fermi observe gamma rays from orbit, while ground-based Cherenkov telescopes detect secondary particles produced when gamma rays interact with Earth's atmosphere. These instruments can map the spatial [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] and energy spectrum of gamma ray sources across the sky.

## WIMP Annihilation Signatures

When WIMPs annihilate, they can produce gamma rays either directly or through the decay of secondary particles like electrons and positrons. This creates a distinctive signature characterized by specific energy distributions and spatial patterns concentrated in high dark matter density regions. The detection of such signals would provide evidence for dark matter particle interactions and help constrain the properties of WIMPs, including their mass and interaction cross-section.
