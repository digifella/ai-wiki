# Scale Effect

The **scale effect** refers to how the performance and [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] of [[concepts/artificial-intelligence-models|artificial intelligence models]], particularly transformer-based architectures, are heavily influenced by [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]]. Larger datasets, more powerful [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] (such as GPUs), and increased [[concepts/model-size|model size]] generally lead to better results due to an improvement in generalization ability, capacity for capturing complex patterns, and [[concepts/parallel-processing|parallel processing]] [[concepts/speed|speed]].

### Key Points
- **Larger [[concepts/models|Models]]**: The performance of [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] increases with scale up until a certain point where [[concepts/diminishing-returns|diminishing returns]] are observed.
- **Computational [[concepts/power|Power]]**: Modern systems rely on [[entities/high-performance|high-performance]] GPUs and large datasets to train complex [[concepts/neural-networks|neural networks]] efficiently.
- **[[concepts/limited-resources|Resource Constraints]]**: Limited [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]] can significantly impact the [[concepts/training|training]] time, efficiency, and overall effectiveness of an AI model.

### Summary
The video, presented by [[entities/dave|Dave]], aims to demystify the [[concepts/training-process|training process]] of a [[concepts/neural-network|neural network]] by [[concepts/running|running]] a transformer on a vintage 1979 [[concepts/pdp-1144|PDP-11/44]] computer. Unlike modern cloud clusters with thousands of GPUs, this system operates with a single 6MHz [[concepts/cpu|CPU]] and a mere 64KB of [[concepts/ram|RAM]] (though later upgraded to 4MB). The core idea, Dave argues, is not magical or new; it [[concepts/highlights|highlights]] the scale effect in AI [[concepts/training|training]], showing that even small-scale hardware can train [[concepts/models|models]] but [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] an extremely slow pace compared to modern [[concepts/open-standards|standards]].

### Bullet Points
- Demonstrates transformer model training on a PDP-11 with [[concepts/limited-resources|limited resources]].
- Contrasts modern AI training practices with those possible using 1970s technology.
- Emphasizes the role of computational power in [[concepts/scaling|scaling]] AI performance.
- Addresses misconceptions about AI's complexity and necessity for high-end hardware.
