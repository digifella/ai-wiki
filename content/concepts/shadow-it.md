# Shadow It

Shadow IT refers to the use of ungoverned [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] solutions and [[concepts/agentic-frameworks|agentic frameworks]] within organizations without formal oversight, [[concepts/governance|governance]], or [[concepts/security|security]] controls. Similar to traditional shadow IT practices where employees use unauthorized [[concepts/software|software]] systems, [[concepts/shadow-ai|shadow AI]] emerges when teams deploy [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]], autonomous [[concepts/agents|agents]], or AI frameworks outside established IT and security approval processes. This practice creates significant organizational risk through unmonitored data flows, inconsistent security practices, and [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] of sensitive information to third-party AI services.

## Governance and Cybersecurity Risks

The [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of [[concepts/co-pilot-use|ungoverned AI solutions]] introduces multiple overlapping risks. Organizations lose visibility into which AI systems process proprietary data, how that data is retained or used by external providers, and what safeguards are implemented. Agentic frameworks—AI systems capable of autonomous [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] and action—pose particular governance challenges, as their behavior can be difficult to predict or audit. Without centralized oversight, organizations may face [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] violations, data breaches, [[concepts/intellectual-property-rights|intellectual property]] leakage, and operational failures resulting from unvetted AI [[concepts/deployment|deployment]].

## Organizational Response

Addressing shadow AI requires establishing formal [[concepts/ai-governance|AI governance frameworks]] that provide [[concepts/secure|secure]], approved alternatives while maintaining organizational awareness of [[concepts/ai-adoption-trends|AI tool adoption]]. This involves creating clear [[concepts/policies|policies]] around acceptable AI use, implementing discovery mechanisms to identify ungoverned deployments, and developing [[concepts/open-standards|standards]] for evaluation and approval of AI solutions. The challenge lies in balancing security and governance requirements with the legitimate business need for [[concepts/rapid-experimentation|rapid experimentation]] and [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] using AI technologies.
