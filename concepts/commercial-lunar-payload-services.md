# Commercial Lunar Payload Services

Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) is a NASA initiative established in the late 2010s to contract private companies for the delivery of scientific instruments and equipment to the lunar surface. The program represents a strategic shift toward leveraging commercial spaceflight providers for lunar exploration, aiming to reduce costs and increase the frequency of surface deliveries compared to relying solely on government-operated systems. Under the CLPS framework, NASA issues task orders to qualified vendors, allowing for a more agile and cost-effective approach to lunar logistics.

The initiative supports the broader Artemis program by providing critical infrastructure for the Artemis 3 mission and subsequent lunar landings. By utilizing commercial Heavy Lunar Landers (HLS) alongside the Space Launch System (SLS), NASA aims to ensure readiness for crewed missions while simultaneously advancing scientific discovery. This dual-use strategy allows for the transport of both astronauts and scientific payloads, facilitating a sustained presence on the Moon.

CLPS vendors are responsible for designing, building, and operating lunar landers capable of navigating the complex terrain of the lunar surface. These commercial partners operate under a task-order contract model, which enables NASA to procure specific services as needed rather than maintaining a large, permanent government-operated fleet. This structure encourages innovation and competition within the commercial space sector, fostering the development of reusable technologies and sustainable lunar operations.
