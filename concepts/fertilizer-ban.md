# Fertilizer Ban

A **Fertilizer Ban** refers to government-imposed restrictions or total prohibitions on the production, import, or use of synthetic chemical fertilizers. These [[concepts/policies|policies]] are typically driven by environmental concerns regarding water pollution, soil degradation, and the push for [[concepts/organic-farming|organic farming]] standards, though they often carry significant risks for food [[concepts/security|security]] and economic stability if executed without transitional support.

## Key Examples & Case Studies

### Sri Lanka (2021)
The most prominent modern case of a radical fertilizer prohibition occurred in [[entities/sri-lanka|Sri Lanka]], serving as a cautionary example of policy execution failure.

*   **Policy Implementation:** In April 2021, the government abruptly banned the import of all chemical fertilizers, insecticides, and herbicides, aiming to transition the nation entirely to organic [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]] [[lab-notes/2026-06-15-Sri-Lankas-Abrupt-Fertilizer-Ban-Policy-Intent-Execution|Sri Lanka's Abrupt Fertilizer Ban: Policy Intent, Execution Failures, and Agricultural Crisis]].
*   **Context & Intent:** The move was framed as an effort to reduce import dependency and improve [[concepts/population-health|public health]], making Sri Lanka the first country to attempt such a comprehensive ban.
*   **Outcome:** The abrupt nature of the ban led to immediate supply shocks, drastic reductions in crop yields (particularly for tea and rice), and contributed significantly to the broader economic [[concepts/crisis|crisis]] that destabilized the country.
*   **Source Analysis:** Detailed analysis by Asianometry highlights the disconnect between policy intent and on-the-ground agricultural realities, noting severe [[concepts/execution-failures|execution failures]] [[Sri Lanka's Abrupt Fertilizer Ban: Policy Intent, Execution Failures, and Agricultural Crisis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1S2wwbX_p_E)].

## Implications

*   **Economic:** Sudden bans can disrupt [[concepts/supply-chains]] for staple crops, leading to inflation and trade deficits.
*   **Social:** Loss of livelihoods for farmers dependent on high-yield chemical inputs.
*   **Environmental:** While intended to reduce nitrogen runoff, poor transition strategies can lead to increased [[concepts/land-use|land use]] as yields drop, potentially offsetting environmental benefits.
