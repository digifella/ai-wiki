# Sam Altman

**Sam Altman** is an American entrepreneur and technology executive, best known as the CEO of [[entities/openai]]. He is a central figure in the modern [[concepts/artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] revolution, having co-founded OpenAI in 2015 and led its development of [[concepts/large-language-models|large language models]] including [[entities/chatgpt]] and the GPT-4 series.

## Key Roles & Affiliations
- **CEO, OpenAI**: Led the organization through its transition to a "capped-profit" structure and subsequent governance changes.
- **Former President, Y Combinator**: Served as president from 2014 to 2019, overseeing the accelerator's growth.
- **Co-founder, Lenny**: Early-stage investment firm.
- **Board Member**: Served on the board of Twitter (now X) during its acquisition by [[entities/elon-musk]].

## AI Philosophy & Industry Context
Altman has consistently advocated for the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence, arguing that the benefits of AGI ([[concepts/artificial-general-intelligence|Artificial General Intelligence]]) outweigh the risks if managed correctly. His stance often contrasts with more cautious voices in the industry.

- **Pacing Debate**: Altman's approach to AI development speed has been a subject of intense debate. In September 2026, [[entities/dario-amodei]] (CEO of Anthropic) publicly called for an industry slowdown, citing existential risks and [[concepts/power-concentration|power concentration]] [[lab-notes/2026-09-16-Dario-Amodeis-AI-Warning-Pacing-Existential-Risks-and-Po|Dario Amodei's AI Warning: Pacing, Existential Risks, and Power Concentration]].
- **Regulatory Stance**: Altman has engaged with global policymakers, advocating for proactive regulation rather than reactive bans, emphasizing the need for international cooperation on [[concepts/model-safety|AI safety]] standards.
- **Compute & [[concepts/infrastructure|Infrastructure]]**: Under his leadership, OpenAI has pursued massive investments in compute infrastructure, partnering with major cloud providers and energy firms to support training of frontier models.
