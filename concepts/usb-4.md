# Usb 4

USB 4 is a high-speed connector standard finalized by the USB Implementers Forum in 2019. It builds upon the physical capabilities of USB-C and is based on Intel's Thunderbolt 3 protocol, which Intel made available for licensing to other manufacturers. This standard represents a significant advancement in data transfer capabilities and power delivery compared to previous USB generations.

The specification supports data transfer speeds of up to 40 gigabits per second, effectively doubling the bandwidth of USB 3.2. To achieve this performance, USB 4 utilizes tunneling protocols to carry multiple types of data, including PCIe and DisplayPort, over a single connection. The standard maintains backward compatibility with USB 3.2, USB 2.0, and Thunderbolt 3 devices, ensuring that existing peripherals can continue to function while allowing for higher performance when supported by both the host and the device.
