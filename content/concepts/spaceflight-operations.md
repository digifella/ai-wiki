# Spaceflight Operations

Spaceflight operations refer to the integrated systems, procedures, and management protocols required to execute crewed space missions safely and effectively. These operations encompass pre-launch [[concepts/preparation|preparation]], in-flight management, and mission-critical [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] across multiple phases of a mission. For crewed lunar missions such as [[entities/artemis-ii|Artemis II]], spaceflight operations involve coordination between ground [[concepts/power|control]], onboard systems, and crew to maintain vehicle performance and crew safety throughout the journey.

## Orion Spacecraft Systems

The [[entities/orion|Orion spacecraft]] represents the primary vehicle for [[concepts/nasa|NASA]]'s deep space [[concepts/crewed-missions|crewed missions]]. Its operational systems include life support, power generation, propulsion, and thermal management—each requiring constant monitoring and adjustment during flight. Successful spaceflight operations depend on the [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] of these systems and the crew's ability to respond to anomalies or degraded performance in the deep [[concepts/vacuum-of-space|space environment]], where communication delays and limited resupply options create distinct operational constraints compared to low Earth orbit missions.

## Mission Challenges

Crewed missions to the Moon and beyond present operational challenges that differ significantly from previous spaceflight [[concepts/software|programs]]. Extended mission duration, greater distance from Earth, and the requirement to maintain life support systems over weeks of flight demand robust operational procedures and contingency planning. Real-time [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] by both flight crews and ground teams, combined with thorough pre-mission [[concepts/training|training]] and [[concepts/simulation|simulation]], forms the foundation of managing these complex operational environments.
