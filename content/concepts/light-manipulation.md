# Light Manipulation

Light manipulation in [[concepts/portrait-photography|portrait photography]] refers to techniques for controlling, adjusting, and enhancing how light appears in [[concepts/images|images]]. In digital post-processing, this involves using [[concepts/software|software]] tools to selectively brighten, darken, or redirect light across different areas of a portrait to improve overall appearance and visual impact.

## Photoshop Techniques

Common light manipulation approaches in [[concepts/photoshop|Photoshop]] include dodging and burning, which lighten and darken specific regions respectively, and using [[concepts/adjustment-layers|adjustment layers]] to control [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] and [[concepts/contrast|contrast]]. These tools allow photographers to enhance facial features by subtly adjusting highlights and [[concepts/shadows|shadows]], correct uneven lighting from the original photograph, and create more dimensionality in flat-lit portraits. Effective light manipulation requires careful calibration to maintain natural-looking results.

## Practical Considerations

Successful light manipulation depends on working environment conditions, including monitor calibration, which affects how accurately edits can be assessed during the editing process. The goal is typically to enhance the portrait's existing lighting [[concepts/structure|structure]] rather than fundamentally alter it, preserving the photograph's authenticity while improving visual clarity and subject presentation.
