# TV Broadcasting

TV broadcasting refers to the transmission of television and film content to audiences through various [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] channels and platforms. Historically centered on over-the-air transmission via radio frequencies to household receivers, the field has evolved significantly with technological advancement. Modern TV broadcasting encompasses traditional terrestrial transmission, cable networks, satellite delivery, and increasingly, internet-based [[concepts/streaming-services|streaming services]]. This expanded landscape has fundamentally altered how content reaches viewers and how audiences consume entertainment.

## Traditional and Modern Distribution

Conventional TV broadcasting relied on scheduled programming transmitted to passive audiences with limited choice over what and when to watch. This model remains operational in many regions through free-to-air networks funded by advertising or public fees. The industry has progressively shifted toward on-demand distribution, where viewers access content through subscription services, ad-supported platforms, or rental [[concepts/models|models]]. This transition has created competition between established broadcasters and new digital platforms, reshaping content production, [[concepts/licensing-agreements|licensing agreements]], and audience measurement.

## Industry Structure

TV broadcasting involves multiple stakeholders including content creators, production companies, distributors, and broadcasters. Rights management—determining who can broadcast content in specific regions and formats—remains a central commercial concern. Regulatory frameworks vary by country, governing [[concepts/licensing|licensing]], content [[concepts/open-standards|standards]], and spectrum allocation. The economics of broadcasting continue to evolve as [[concepts/ad-revenue|advertising revenue]] models compete with subscription-based income and as audiences fragment across multiple platforms rather than concentrating on traditional broadcast schedules.
