# 3D Editing

3D editing is the digital practice of creating, modifying, and refining three-dimensional models and assets for use in various media and applications. This process involves using specialized software to construct geometry, apply materials and textures, and position objects in virtual three-dimensional space. 3D editors serve as fundamental tools across industries including visual effects, animation, [[concepts/product-design|product design]], architecture, game development, and scientific visualization.

## Core Functions

The primary functions of 3D editing software include geometry modeling—building the basic shapes and structures of objects—along with material application, texturing, lighting, and [[concepts/visual-rendering|rendering]]. Most 3D editors allow users to work with different modeling approaches, such as polygonal modeling for creating defined surfaces, sculpting tools for organic shapes, or parametric modeling for precision-based design. Users can also animate objects, apply physical simulations, and prepare assets for export to other platforms or applications.

## Common Applications

Different industries rely on 3D editing for distinct purposes. Film and television production use it for visual effects and character animation. Product designers employ [[concepts/3d-modeling|3D modeling]] to prototype and visualize designs before manufacturing. Architects create detailed building models and spatial visualizations. Game developers build game worlds, characters, and interactive assets. Scientific researchers use 3D editing to model molecular structures, anatomical systems, and geological formations.

## Software and Workflow

Numerous software options exist at varying levels of complexity and [[concepts/specialization|specialization]], from general-purpose tools like Blender, Maya, and 3ds Max to industry-specific solutions. The 3D [[concepts/editing-workflow|editing workflow]] typically involves initial concept modeling, refinement of detail, material and [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]] work, lighting setup, and final [[concepts/fat-rendering|rendering]] or export. Collaborative workflows often require [[concepts/asset-management|asset management]] systems and [[concepts/app-updates|version control]] to coordinate work across teams.
