# Supercomputer Architecture

Supercomputer architecture refers to the structural design and operational principles of High-Performance [[concepts/computation|Computing]] systems designed to execute complex calculations at extreme speeds. Key architectural components include:

- **Processing Units**: Utilization of specialized CPUs and GPUs for parallel processing.
- **Interconnects**: High-bandwidth, low-latency networks (e.g., Slingshot interconnect) to facilitate data transfer between nodes.
- **[[concepts/memory|Memory]] Hierarchy**: Complex Memory architecture strategies including NUMA and distributed memory models.
- **Cooling Systems**: Advanced thermal management such as Liquid cooling or Immersion cooling to handle high power densities.
- **Power [[concepts/infrastructure|Infrastructure]]**: Redundant power supplies and efficient energy distribution systems.

## Recent Developments: El Capitan

The landscape of supercomputer architecture is currently defined by the deployment of exascale systems. A primary example is the **El Capitan** supercomputer at [[concepts/lawrence-livermore-national-laboratory]].

- **Scale and Engineering**: El Capitan represents a significant leap in computational scale, integrating advanced engineering solutions to manage its massive power and thermal requirements [[lab-notes/2026-09-27-El-Capitan-Supercomputer-Scale-Engineering-and-Mission-a|El Capitan Supercomputer: Scale, Engineering, and Mission at LLNL]].
- **Mission Profile**: Designed for national security simulations, climate modeling, and [[concepts/scientific-discovery|scientific discovery]], it pushes the boundaries of [[concepts/exascale-computing|Exascale computing]].
- **Architectural Insights**: Detailed tours and analyses of its hardware configuration and operational efficiency provide critical case studies for future Supercomputer design efforts [[El Capitan Supercomputer: Scale, Engineering, and Mission at LLNL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HlsyZAWnOu8)].
