# Meat Texture

The texture of ground meat during cooking is primarily determined by the behavior of its proteins and the retention of moisture. As the meat is heated, proteins denature and contract, a process that can squeeze out internal water and result in a dense, dry final product. Additionally, the surface condition of the meat plays a critical role in the quality of the crust; excess moisture on the surface inhibits the Maillard reaction, the chemical process responsible for developing desirable flavor and color through browning.

Baking soda improves ground meat texture through a process known as alkaline hydrolysis. When mixed with ground meat prior to cooking, baking soda raises the pH level of the meat's surface. This increase in alkalinity prevents proteins from bonding too tightly during the heating process, allowing them to retain more water. Consequently, the meat remains juicier and more tender.

The altered pH also accelerates the Maillard reaction by promoting faster browning at lower temperatures. This allows for a more efficient development of crust and flavor without the need for excessive cooking times that might dry out the meat. The combination of improved moisture retention and enhanced browning results in a ground meat product that is both more flavorful and has a superior mouthfeel.
