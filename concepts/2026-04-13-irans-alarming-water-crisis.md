# 2026 04 13 Irans Alarming Water Crisis

[[concepts/iran|Iran]] confronts a severe [[concepts/water-scarcity|water shortage]] driven by its inherently [[concepts/arid-geography|arid geography]] combined with climate pressures. The country receives approximately 250 millimeters of annual rainfall on average, which is less than one-quarter of the global mean of 990 millimeters. This low precipitation baseline creates a structural deficit in [[concepts/renewable-water-resources|freshwater resources]], necessitating reliance on non-renewable groundwater reserves and complex water transfer projects to sustain [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]] and urban populations.

## Climate and Environmental Factors

Precipitation in Iran is distributed unevenly across seasons and regions, with winter months accounting for the majority of rainfall. This precipitation concentrates primarily in mountainous areas, particularly along the Zagros and Alborz ranges, leaving vast portions of the central plateau and eastern deserts with limited direct freshwater availability. The geographic reality of high evaporation rates in these arid zones exacerbates the scarcity, as much of the limited moisture is lost to the [[concepts/earths-atmosphere|atmosphere]] before it can replenish surface water bodies or aquifers.

## Hydrological Implications

The disparity between water availability and demand has led to the depletion of major lakes and wetlands, such as Lake Urmia and the Hamun wetlands, which serve as critical ecological buffers. Groundwater levels in key agricultural regions have dropped significantly due to over-extraction for irrigation, reducing the land's capacity to recover during wetter periods. This hydrological [[concepts/stress|stress]] contributes to land subsidence and soil salinization, further degrading the arable land and threatening long-term food [[concepts/security|security]] within the region.
