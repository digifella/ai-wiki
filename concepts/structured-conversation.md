# Structured Conversation

**Structured Conversation** refers to intentional [[concepts/dialogue|dialogue]] frameworks designed to extract specific insights, manage [[concepts/group-dynamics|group dynamics]], or achieve predefined communication goals. Unlike open-ended chatter, these interactions follow a defined schema for [[concepts/flow|flow]], participant roles, and [[concepts/information-extraction|information extraction]].

## Core Characteristics
- **Agenda-Driven:** Follows a pre-defined sequence of topics or questions.
- **[[concepts/system-card|Role Definition]]:** Clear distinction between facilitator, participants, and observers.
- **Goal-Oriented:** Targets specific outcomes (e.g., data collection, [[concepts/ses-family|conflict resolution]], [[concepts/decision-making|decision making]]).
- **Standardization:** Uses consistent protocols to allow for comparison across sessions.

## Methodological Implementations

### Focus Group Interviewing
A primary application of structured conversation in qualitative research. It involves guided group discussions to explore attitudes, perceptions, and [[concepts/ideas|ideas]] on specific topics.

- **Methodology:** Utilizes a facilitator to guide a small group (typically 6-10 participants) through a series of [[concepts/open-ended-questions|open-ended questions]] while managing [[concepts/team-dynamics|group dynamics]].
- **Advantages:** Leverages group interaction to stimulate ideas that might not emerge in one-on-one interviews; efficient for gathering diverse perspectives.
- **Challenges:** Risk of groupthink, dominance by vocal participants, and difficulty in isolating individual viewpoints.
- **Reference:** Detailed methodologies and [[concepts/best-practices|best practices]] are outlined in [[concepts/peer-reviewed-research|peer-reviewed literature]], such as [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Focus-Group-Interviewing|Focus Group Interviewing]].

### Other Variants
- **In-Depth Interviews (IDI):** One-on-one structured conversations allowing for deep probing.
- **Delphi Method:** Structured communication technique relying on an [[concepts/iterative-refinement|iterative process]] of questionnaires and controlled [[concepts/feedback|feedback]].
- **User Testing:** Structured observation of users interacting with a product to identify usability issues.
