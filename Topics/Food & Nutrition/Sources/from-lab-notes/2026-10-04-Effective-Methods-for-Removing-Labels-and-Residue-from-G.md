## Effective Methods for Removing Labels and Residue from Glass and Plastic
**Clip title:** How to Remove Sticky Labels & Sticker Residue from Glass and Plastic (Easy Cleaning Hack)
**[[entities/tasia-custode|Author]] / channel:** Marlene Cardenas
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e98XGZl4MYM

### Summary
This video demonstrates effective methods for removing labels and [[concepts/adhesive-residue|adhesive residue]] from various types of [[concepts/containerization-technology|containers]], both plastic and glass, for purposes of reuse or recycling. The presenter begins by showcasing an assortment of empty food [[concepts/containerization-technology|containers]], including plastic tubs, bottles, and glass jars, highlighting the common frustration of sticky remnants left behind after removing labels. She categorizes labels into two main types: silkier, weather-resistant ones often found on refrigerated items, and paper-based labels typical for pantry items, noting that paper labels tend to be more challenging to remove.

The primary method introduced involves filling the cleaned-out containers with hot water and allowing them to soak for several minutes. This heat helps to melt the adhesive, making the labels easier to peel off. For plastic containers that cannot hold water directly on the label surface, they are flipped upside down into a basin of hot water. The presenter demonstrates that the silkier, weather-resistant labels often peel away cleanly with minimal effort after soaking, leaving little to no sticky residue, as seen with the Simply Lemonade and Mesquite Smoked Ham containers. However, paper-based labels, especially if they inadvertently get wet during initial cleaning, tend to tear and leave significant paper and glue remnants.

For stubborn adhesive and paper residue left after the initial peeling, a simple homemade paste is introduced: equal parts [[concepts/baking-soda|baking soda]] and cooking oil (such as olive or vegetable oil). This paste is applied generously to the sticky areas and rubbed in to exfoliate the surface. The oil works to break down the adhesive while the [[concepts/baking-soda|baking soda]] provides a gentle abrasive action. After scrubbing, the containers are rinsed, revealing clean, residue-free surfaces. Additionally, the video provides a quick tip for removing printed date stamps directly on plastic containers using rubbing alcohol or hand sanitizer and a paper towel, which effectively wipes away the ink.

In conclusion, the video successfully illustrates how common household items like hot water, baking soda, cooking oil, and rubbing alcohol can be used to efficiently prepare used containers for repurposing. The presenter emphasizes that while weather-resistant labels are generally easier to remove, the baking soda and oil paste effectively tackles even the most tenacious paper residue. This practical guide promotes sustainable practices by making it easier to clean and reuse containers, thereby reducing waste.
