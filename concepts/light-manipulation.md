# Light Manipulation

Light manipulation in portrait photography encompasses techniques for controlling and adjusting the appearance of illumination during both capture and post-processing. In the context of digital post-processing, photographers utilize software tools to selectively brighten, darken, or redirect light across specific areas of an image. This process aims to enhance visual impact, correct lighting imbalances, and refine the original lighting conditions established during the shoot.

## Post-Processing Techniques

Standard methods in digital editing include dodging and burning, which involve selectively lightening or darkening specific regions to create depth and guide the viewer's attention. These techniques allow for the simulation of directional lighting that may not have been present in the original capture. By adjusting tonal values, editors can emphasize facial features, smooth out harsh shadows, and balance exposure across the subject.

## Optical and Physical Methods

Prior to digital intervention, light manipulation is achieved through physical modifiers such as reflectors, diffusers, and flags. Reflectors bounce existing light onto shadowed areas to reduce contrast, while diffusers soften harsh light sources to create more flattering illumination. Flags and gobos are used to block or shape light, preventing unwanted spill and ensuring that illumination falls precisely where intended on the subject.
