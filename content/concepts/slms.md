# Slms

Slms, or Small Language Models, refer to language models with significantly fewer [[concepts/parameters|parameters]] than [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]] (LLMs). While there is no strict threshold, Slms typically contain fewer than several billion parameters, compared to [[concepts/foundation-model|foundation models]] that may have tens or hundreds of billions. These models are designed to maintain functional capability while reducing computational requirements, [[concepts/memory|memory]] footprint, and latency.

## Purpose and Applications

Small language models serve several practical functions within [[concepts/ai-productivity-agents|AI agent systems]]. They enable [[concepts/deployment|deployment]] on edge devices, mobile platforms, and resource-constrained environments where full-scale LLMs are impractical. In agent architectures, Slms can handle specific subtasks—such as [[concepts/text-classification|text classification]], [[concepts/entity-extraction|entity extraction]], or routing decisions—without requiring the overhead of larger models. They also reduce [[concepts/inference|inference]] costs and response times, which is important for real-time agent interactions.

## Techniques and Development

Slms are typically created through [[concepts/model-quantization|model compression]] techniques applied to larger models, or trained from scratch on curated datasets. Common approaches include knowledge distillation, where a smaller model learns to replicate a larger model's behavior; [[concepts/parameter-reduction|quantization]], which reduces numerical precision; and pruning, which removes less important parameters. Some Slms are also trained on [[concepts/domain-specific-data|domain-specific data]] or synthetic data generated by larger models, optimizing them for particular agent tasks without requiring general-purpose capabilities.
