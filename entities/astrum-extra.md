# Astrum Extra

Astrum Extra is a reference designation for NASA's Project Gemini, the human spaceflight program that operated between 1961 and 1966. The program served as a critical developmental bridge between the earlier Mercury program and the subsequent Apollo program, allowing NASA to test and refine essential techniques required for lunar missions.

## Program Objectives

Project Gemini focused on mastering fundamental spaceflight challenges that would be necessary for the Apollo moon landings. Key objectives included developing procedures for crewed spacewalks, perfecting orbital rendezvous and docking maneuvers, conducting long-duration spaceflights, and evaluating life support systems in extended missions. The program consisted of 10 crewed missions and 2 uncrewed test flights.

## Legacy

The techniques and knowledge gained during Gemini directly enabled the success of the Apollo program. By the time Apollo 11 launched in 1969, astronauts and mission control had already practiced the complex procedures required for lunar orbit rendezvous and had demonstrated human capability to work effectively in the space environment. Project Gemini thus represents a crucial phase in the development of human spaceflight capabilities.
