# Visa Expiration

Visa expiration refers to the final date on which a visa remains valid for travel and entry purposes. Once a visa expires, it can no longer be used to enter a destination country, and travelers must obtain a new visa if they wish to travel. The expiration date is a critical piece of information printed on all visa documents, whether in physical or digital form, and travelers are responsible for checking this date before attempting to cross a border.

## Electronic Visas

Electronic visas, or e-visas, operate under the same expiration principles as traditional paper visas. An e-visa has a specific validity period from the date of issuance, after which it becomes invalid for entry. Unlike paper visas that can be physically stamped in a passport, e-visas exist as digital records in government databases and must be verified electronically at border control. Travelers should retain confirmation emails or digital copies of their e-visa and verify the expiration date well in advance of their intended travel date.

## Planning Travel Around Expiration Dates

Travelers should check visa expiration dates at least several weeks before departure to allow time for renewal if necessary. Some countries impose entry restrictions if a visa will expire within a certain timeframe after arrival, typically requiring visitors to have valid documentation for the entire planned stay. Additionally, some visas have validity periods measured from the date of issue rather than from entry, so the timing of application submission can affect when a visa can no longer be used.
