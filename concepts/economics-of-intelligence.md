# Economics of Intelligence

## Core Concept
The **Economics of Intelligence** examines the cost structures, utility metrics, and market dynamics surrounding [[concepts/agi|Artificial General Intelligence]] (AGI) and narrow [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]]. It challenges the assumption that intelligence is asymptotically approaching [[concepts/concept-of-nothingness|zero]] marginal cost, analyzing instead how [[concepts/product-utility|product utility]] drives valuation in an environment where [[concepts/computational-resources|compute]] and [[concepts/inference|inference]] costs fluctuate.

## Key Themes
- **Marginal Cost of Inference**: The relationship between model complexity, [[concepts/token-optimization|token efficiency]], and real-[[entities/earth|world]] deployment costs.
- **Utility over Capability**: Shifting focus from benchmark scores to actionable product utility that justifies expenditure on [[concepts/compute]] resources.
- **Market Segmentation**: How intelligence is commoditized versus specialized, affecting [[concepts/pricing|pricing]] models for enterprises vs. consumers.

- Analysis of [[concepts/google-search|Google]]'s [[concepts/strategic-pivot|strategic pivot]] highlights a counter-intuitive trend: intelligence may be getting more expensive relative to utility in certain product verticals.
- The [[Google I/O AI Strategy: Product Utility and the Economics of Intelligence|Google I/O AI Strategy: Product Utility and the Economics of Intelligence]] note details how [[entities/google-io|Google I/O]] 2026 announcements reflect a maturation [[concepts/phase|phase]] where raw [[concepts/scale-effect|model scaling]] yields [[concepts/diminishing-returns|diminishing returns]] without corresponding utility gains.
