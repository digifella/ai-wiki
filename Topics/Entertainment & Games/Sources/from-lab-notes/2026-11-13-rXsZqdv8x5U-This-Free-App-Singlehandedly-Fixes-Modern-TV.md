## This Free App Singlehandedly Fixes Modern TV
**Clip title:** This Free App Singlehandedly Fixes Modern TV
**Author / channel:** Switch and Click
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rXsZqdv8x5U
**Credibility:** Commentary (tier 1 of 5). YouTube video from Switch and Click: not edited or peer reviewed, so its claims are the speaker's own. Summary written by Gemini.

### Summary
The video discusses **Plasma Bigscreen**, an open-source TV interface for Linux created by the KDE team. The speaker shares their experience using Plasma Bigscreen as an alternative to traditional streaming boxes, exploring its features, usability, and how it handles various applications and peripherals.

The main points discussed include:
- **Installation and Compatibility:** Plasma Bigscreen works with specific Linux distros (Fedora and Arch Linux are highlighted as the best options). Installing it involves a simple command-line process through the terminal, turning a Linux setup into a streaming box interface.
- **Interface and Usability:** The speaker praises the layout, which is divided into Favorites, Recent, Games, and Applications. It automatically groups emulators and Steam games into an easy-to-access section, though the general apps row can feel cluttered and requires some manual organization.
- **Web Apps Feature:** To bypass the lack of native Linux apps for services like Disney+, Plasma Bigscreen includes a web apps feature. Users can add a web app by entering its URL, though opening them requires navigating a small browser window before making it fullscreen.
- **Controller and Remote Support:** The video details controller integration, noting that using a gamepad (like an Xbox controller) makes navigation much easier, effectively replacing a traditional remote. However, Consumer Electronics Control (CEC) support can be finicky depending on the hardware and firmware used.

The conclusion highlights that Plasma Bigscreen is a surprisingly usable and effective alternative to commercial streaming boxes. Despite some minor quirks with web apps and hardware compatibility, the speaker is impressed by the KDE team's work, suggesting that Plasma Bigscreen has the potential to truly replace traditional streaming devices for Linux users.
