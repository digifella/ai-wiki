# Usb C Connectivity

USB-C is a universal connector standard that supports bidirectional power delivery, high-speed data transfer, and multiple protocols through a single reversible port. Its design eliminates orientation alignment issues common in earlier USB standards, allowing the connector to be inserted in either direction. This standard has become prevalent across consumer electronics, including tablets, smartphones, and laptops, due to its versatility and efficiency.

Apple integrated USB-C connectivity into its iPad lineup starting with the iPad Pro in 2018. The transition expanded to the iPad Air and iPad mini in subsequent years, replacing the proprietary Lightning connector. This shift enables iPads to connect to a wider range of external devices, including displays, storage drives, and peripherals, without the need for additional adapters.

The adoption of USB-C enhances iPad productivity by supporting faster data transfer rates and more robust power delivery capabilities. Users can connect to external monitors for extended desktop-like workflows, attach professional audio interfaces, and charge the device via standard power banks. This connectivity model aligns iPad hardware more closely with the infrastructure standards used in laptops and other computing devices.
