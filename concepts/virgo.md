# Virgo

Virgo is a gravitational wave detector located in Cascina, Italy, operated by the European Gravitational-Wave Observatory (EGO). As one of the primary instruments in the global network of gravitational wave observatories alongside LIGO's facilities in the United States, Virgo uses laser interferometry to detect ripples in spacetime caused by violent cosmic events. The detector became operational in 2007 and has been integral to confirming and localizing gravitational wave sources since the first detection in 2015.

## Technical Design

Virgo employs a Michelson interferometer with 3-kilometer arms to measure the minute distortions in spacetime caused by passing gravitational waves. The instrument uses high-power lasers, suspended mirrors, and sophisticated isolation systems to achieve the sensitivity necessary to detect signals from distant cosmic sources. Its sensitivity has been progressively improved through upgrades, including the Advanced Virgo configuration that began observations in 2017, bringing its detection capabilities closer to those of LIGO.

## Scientific Contributions

Virgo's participation in the international gravitational wave detector network has enabled precise localization of gravitational wave sources in the sky, crucial for coordinating electromagnetic follow-up observations. The detector has contributed to the discovery and characterization of binary neutron star mergers, binary black hole coalescences, and other energetic phenomena. Data from Virgo combined with LIGO observations has improved understanding of compact object populations and tested predictions of general relativity.
