# Astronomy

Astronomy is the natural science concerned with the study of celestial objects and phenomena beyond Earth's atmosphere. It encompasses the observation and analysis of stars, planets, galaxies, [[concepts/black-holes|black holes]], and other cosmic structures, as well as the physical processes that govern their behavior. As one of the oldest scientific disciplines, astronomy combines [[concepts/empirical-evidence|observational data]] with [[concepts/theoretical-physics|theoretical physics]] to develop models explaining the origin, evolution, and [[concepts/structure|structure]] of the universe.

## Observational Methods

Astronomers employ a range of observational tools to gather data about the cosmos. Ground-based optical telescopes have been fundamental to the discipline since the 17th century, while radio telescopes detect [[concepts/light|electromagnetic radiation]] at longer wavelengths. Space-based instruments such as the Hubble Space Telescope and other observatories operate above Earth's atmosphere, avoiding distortion from atmospheric interference. These instruments generate vast datasets that astronomers analyze to test theoretical models and make new discoveries about celestial phenomena.

## Major Fields of Study

Astronomy encompasses several interconnected fields of inquiry. Observational astronomy focuses on gathering and interpreting data from celestial objects. [[concepts/astrophysics|Astrophysics]] applies physical laws to explain stellar processes, [[concepts/galaxy-formation|galaxy formation]], and the behavior of matter under extreme conditions. [[concepts/cosmology|Cosmology]] addresses fundamental questions about the universe's origin, structure, and fate. Additional specializations include [[concepts/planetary-science|planetary science]], which studies planets and moons within and beyond our solar system, and astrometry, which measures the precise positions and movements of celestial bodies.
