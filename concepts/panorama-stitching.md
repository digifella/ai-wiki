# Panorama Stitching

Panorama stitching is a photographic technique that combines multiple overlapping images into a single, wider composition than a standard camera frame allows. Instead of relying on in-camera panorama modes or ultra-wide-angle lenses, photographers capture a series of distinct shots and merge them using specialized software. This method provides greater creative control over the final image and enables precise composition planning during the shooting phase.

The technical process involves capturing a sequence of photographs with significant overlap to ensure seamless alignment. By using a longer focal length lens for individual frames, photographers can achieve long lens compression, which flattens perspective and enhances detail in wide-angle scenes. This approach often results in higher resolution and better dynamic range compared to single-shot wide-angle photography, as it avoids the optical distortions and vignetting common with ultra-wide lenses.

Specialized stitching software analyzes the overlapping areas to align the images accurately, correcting for perspective and exposure differences. The software then blends the edges to create a cohesive final image. This technique is particularly valuable for architectural, landscape, and real estate photography, where capturing extensive detail and maintaining geometric accuracy are critical. It allows photographers to exceed the field of view limitations of their equipment while retaining high image quality.
