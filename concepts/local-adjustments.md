# Local Adjustments

Local adjustments refer to edits applied to specific regions of a photograph rather than to the entire image. This technique allows photographers to selectively enhance or modify particular areas—such as brightening a subject's face, adjusting color in a specific region, or correcting exposure in isolated zones—while leaving the rest of the image unchanged. Local adjustments are fundamental to non-destructive digital editing workflows, as they preserve the original image data while enabling targeted refinements.

## Common Methods

The primary tools for making local adjustments in modern photo editing software include masks, brushes, and graduated filters. Masks define the boundaries of an adjustment, either through freehand painting, geometric selections, or automated detection based on subject properties like color or luminance. Adobe Lightroom's intersect masking feature combines multiple mask types, allowing photographers to create complex selections by layering conditions—for example, selecting only the sky within a particular tonal range, or isolating skin tones within a specific area of the frame.

## Practical Applications

Local adjustments are widely used to correct common photographic challenges. Photographers frequently use them to balance exposure across different zones of an image, enhance specific subjects while maintaining natural-looking surroundings, or apply selective color corrections. The non-destructive nature of these edits means adjustments can be refined or removed at any point during the editing process without degrading image quality.
