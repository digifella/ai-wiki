# Disengagement

Disengagement in [[concepts/qualitative-research|qualitative research]] refers to a failure of researchers to maintain active analytical involvement during the [[concepts/findings-chapter|findings chapter]] of [[concepts/flow-management|thematic analysis]] studies. This concept is addressed in [[concepts/educational-resources|educational resources]] by qualitative research educators who identify common pitfalls that weaken research outputs.

## Common Issues in Thematic Analysis

Several mistakes commonly occur when researchers present their findings from [[concepts/thematic-analysis|thematic analysis]]. These include insufficient depth in theme development, inadequate [[concepts/connection|connection]] between data extracts and analytical claims, and a loss of researcher perspective in the interpretation [[concepts/phase|phase]]. Such disengagement from the analytical process results in findings chapters that lack rigor and fail to demonstrate genuine engagement with the data.

## Implications for Research Quality

When disengagement occurs, the gap between raw data and meaningful findings widens. This leads to findings chapters that may be marked down in academic assessment or fail peer review. Maintaining active engagement throughout the analytical process—particularly when moving from coded data to organized themes to interpreted findings—is essential for producing credible qualitative research that demonstrates thorough analytical work.
