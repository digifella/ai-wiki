# Engram

An [[entities/deepseek-engram|engram]] is a physical or chemical trace in the brain theorized to underlie the [[entities/storage|storage]] and retrieval of memories. The term was first introduced by German biologist [[concepts/feynman|Richard]] Semon in 1904 to describe the neural substrate of [[concepts/memory|memory]]—the biological changes that occur when information is encoded. While the precise mechanism remains incompletely understood, modern [[concepts/neuroscience|neuroscience]] has identified various candidates for engrams, including synaptic strengthening, structural changes in neurons, and patterns of neural activity across distributed networks.

## Neuroscientific Research

Contemporary memory research treats engrams as a foundational concept, though debate continues regarding their exact [[entities/nature|nature]] and localization. Studies using optogenetics and other advanced techniques have demonstrated that specific patterns of neural activity can be associated with particular memories, and that reactivating these patterns can trigger [[concepts/recall|recall]]. The field recognizes that engrams likely involve multiple levels of biological organization, from molecular changes within synapses to large-scale network reorganization.

## Modern Technological Usage

In recent years, the term "engram" has been adopted in [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]], most notably by [[entities/deepseek|DeepSeek]], to describe aspects of their language [[concepts/architecturetechnique|model architecture]]. In this context, it refers to a system for [[concepts/context-aware-knowledge-retrieval|context-aware knowledge retrieval]] that aims to improve efficiency by dynamically accessing relevant information rather than processing all context redundantly. This technological usage represents a metaphorical extension of the neuroscientific concept, drawing an analogy between biological memory traces and computational representations of knowledge.
