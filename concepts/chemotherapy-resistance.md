# Chemotherapy Resistance

**Chemotherapy resistance** refers to the phenomenon where cancer cells initially respond to treatment but subsequently develop [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] to survive and proliferate despite continued [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] to cytotoxic agents. This leads to disease recurrence and limits therapeutic options.

## Mechanisms of Resistance
*   **Efflux Pumps:** Upregulation of ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporters (e.g., P-glycoprotein) that expel drugs from cells.
*   **DNA Repair Enhancement:** Increased capacity to repair chemotherapy-induced DNA damage.
*   **Apoptosis Evasion:** Mutations in apoptotic pathways (e.g., p53) preventing cell death.
*   **Tumor Microenvironment:** Physical barriers and hypoxic conditions reducing drug efficacy.

## Emerging Therapeutic Alternatives
To overcome traditional chemotherapy limitations, novel approaches are being explored, including:

*   **[[concepts/oncolytic-virotherapy|Oncolytic Virotherapy]]:** Use of genetically modified viruses to selectively infect and lyse tumor cells while stimulating an [[concepts/immune-system-function|immune response]].

## Clinical Implications
*   Need for [[concepts/personalized-medicine|personalized medicine]] strategies to identify resistance markers early.
*   Integration of non-cytotoxic therapies (e.g., immunotherapy, virotherapy) into treatment [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]].
*   [[concepts/value|Importance]] of monitoring tumor microenvironment dynamics during treatment.
