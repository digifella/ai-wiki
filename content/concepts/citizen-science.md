# Citizen Science

Citizen science is a model of scientific research in which members of the general public participate in data collection, analysis, or other research activities alongside professional scientists. Rather than science being conducted exclusively by credentialed researchers in institutional settings, citizen science distributes certain aspects of the research process to volunteers and community members. This approach has been applied across numerous scientific disciplines, from [[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]] and biology to archaeology and environmental monitoring.

## Historical Context and Growth

The participation of non-professionals in [[concepts/scientific-method|scientific inquiry]] is not new—naturalists and amateur astronomers have contributed to knowledge for centuries. However, the systematic [[concepts/organization|organization]] of public participation in research has expanded significantly with digital technology, which enables easier coordination of large numbers of contributors and rapid data sharing. Contemporary citizen science projects often leverage crowdsourcing to tackle research questions that benefit from distributed observation or labor-intensive data processing.

## Practical Applications

One notable example comes from Denmark, where the government has engaged private metal detectorists in archaeological fieldwork to uncover buried artifacts in farmland. This program has yielded significant archaeological finds while distributing the labor of [[concepts/artifact-discovery|artifact discovery]] across a trained volunteer workforce. Similar models exist in other fields—bird watchers contribute to ornithological surveys, volunteers monitor water quality in local ecosystems, and online participants assist with image classification and pattern recognition tasks in large datasets.

## Value and Considerations

Citizen science projects can accelerate research timelines, reduce costs, and generate public engagement with the scientific process. However, such programs require careful design to ensure [[concepts/data-conceptsintegrityintegrity|data quality]], provide appropriate [[concepts/training|training]], and maintain ethical [[concepts/open-standards|standards]]. The success of citizen science depends on clear protocols, effective communication between professionals and volunteers, and realistic expectations about the scope of public contribution to research outcomes.
