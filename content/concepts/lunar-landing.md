# Lunar Landing

Lunar landing represents a critical objective of [[concepts/nasa|NASA]]'s [[concepts/artemis-program|Artemis program]], which aims to return humans to the Moon. The success of this goal depends on the readiness of two primary systems: the Space Launch System (SLS) and the [[concepts/human-landing-system|Human Landing System]] (HLS). Artemis 3, scheduled as the first crewed lunar landing mission of the program, [[concepts/faces|faces]] significant technical and developmental challenges that affect its launch timeline and operational feasibility.

## SLS and HLS Development Status

The SLS, NASA's heavy-lift launch vehicle, and the HLS, which includes the lunar lander and ascent systems, remain under active development. Both systems must demonstrate [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] and readiness before [[concepts/crewed-missions|crewed missions]] can proceed. Technical challenges, manufacturing complexities, and [[concepts/integration|integration]] issues have historically affected program timelines, requiring careful evaluation of when both systems [[entities/will|will]] be sufficiently mature for human flight operations.

## Mission Readiness Outlook

The timeline for Artemis 3 depends on resolving outstanding technical issues and completing necessary ground and flight [[concepts/testing|testing]]. [[entities/artemis-ii|Artemis II]], planned as an uncrewed [[concepts/lunar-flyby|lunar flyby]], serves as a critical validation step for [[concepts/orion-systems|Orion systems]] and in-flight operations before attempting a landing. The program's success requires coordinated progress across multiple subsystems and rigorous [[concepts/verification|verification]] of all safety-critical components.
