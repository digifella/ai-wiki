# Canadian Aerospace Industry

The **Canadian Aerospace Industry** encompasses the design, manufacturing, and maintenance of aircraft and spacecraft within Canada. Historically characterized by state-supported [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] and private enterprise, the sector has evolved from early military contracts to a global leader in business jets and regional aircraft.

## Historical Context & Key Entities

- **[[concepts/avro-canada|Avro Canada]]**: A pivotal mid-20th-century manufacturer known for advanced jet technology.
	- **CF-105 Arrow**: The most famous Canadian aerospace project, cancelled in 1959, leading to a brain drain of engineers.
	- **Avro C102 Jetliner**: A proposed commercial jet airliner that represented a significant, albeit unrealized, potential for Canada in the global commercial aviation market. See [[lab-notes/2026-07-15-Avro-C102-Jetliner-Canadas-Unfulfilled-Promise-in-Commer|Avro C102 Jetliner: Canada's Unfulfilled Promise in Commercial Jet Aviation History]] for detailed analysis of this unfulfilled promise.
- **Bombardier Aerospace**: Dominated the regional jet market (CRJ, CSeries) before selling its commercial aircraft division to Airbus.
- **Piaggio Aerospace**: Acquired Bombardier's business jet division (Challenger, Global series).
- **De Havilland Canada**: Specializes in turboprop aircraft (Dash 8 series).

## Recent Developments & Analysis

- **Commercial Jet Ambitions**:
	- The **Avro C102 Jetliner** project highlights early Canadian attempts to compete in the transatlantic jet market.
	- As detailed in recent historical reviews, the C102 was overshadowed by the CF-105 Arrow but remains a critical case study in Canadian industrial policy and technological capability during the 1950s.
	- The cancellation of such projects often redirected Canadian aerospace talent toward military applications or export markets rather than domestic commercial carriers.
