# Product Strategy

## Core Definition
Product strategy is the high-level plan that defines the target market, [[concepts/business-model|value proposition]], and roadmap for a product to achieve business goals. It bridges the gap between vision and execution.

## Key Components
- **Market Analysis**: Understanding customer needs and competitive landscape.
- **Value Proposition**: Defining unique benefits delivered to the user.
- **Roadmap**: Prioritizing features and initiatives over time.
- **Metrics**: Defining success criteria (e.g., KPIs, OKRs).

## Strategic Frameworks
- Jobs-to-be-Done
- RICE Scoring
- MoSCoW Prioritization
- Product-Market-Fit

## Case Study: Apple's 2026 Mobile Strategy
[[entities/apple|Apple]]'s recent product moves illustrate a shift towards form-factor innovation and ecosystem lock-in.

- **Event Context**: Apple's Fall 2026 event highlighted a dual-pronged approach to the smartphone market.
- **Incremental Innovation**: Release of the [[concepts/iphone-18-pro]] and Pro Max, focusing on iterative hardware improvements.
- **Disruptive Innovation**: Introduction of the [[concepts/iphone-duo]], Apple's first [[concepts/folding-smartphone|folding smartphone]], signaling a strategic pivot to capture the emerging foldable market segment.
- **Strategic Implication**: This move addresses the need for larger screen real estate without abandoning the compact form factor, potentially challenging Samsung and Huawei in the foldable category.
- **Community Reception**: Early impressions suggest mixed reactions to the design and pricing, with significant focus on the durability and software adaptation for the folding mechanism.
