# Panorama Stitching

Panorama stitching is a photographic technique in which multiple overlapping images are digitally combined to create a single, wider composition than any individual camera frame. Rather than relying on in-camera panorama modes or ultra-wide-angle lenses, photographers deliberately capture a series of overlapping shots and merge them using specialized software. This approach offers greater creative control over the final image and allows for precise composition planning during the shooting phase.

## Technical Process

The technique involves capturing a sequence of photographs with consistent overlap, typically 20-40 percent between adjacent frames. The photographer maintains a fixed focal length and exposure settings while panning across the scene. Specialized stitching software then automatically aligns the overlapping areas, blending the images together while correcting for perspective distortion and exposure inconsistencies. The result is a high-resolution image with expanded dimensions in one direction.

## Advantages and Applications

Panorama stitching offers distinct advantages over alternative wide-angle approaches. Using longer focal lengths during capture preserves the visual compression and perspective characteristics of telephoto lenses while extending the horizontal field of view. This allows photographers to achieve wide compositions while maintaining subject detail and minimizing distortion. The technique is commonly used for landscapes, architecture, and environmental portraits where a broader context enhances the visual narrative.
