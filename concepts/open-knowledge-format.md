# Open Knowledge Format

**[[concepts/data-management|Open Knowledge Format]]** (OKF) is a proposed standard for structuring and managing personal [[concepts/knowledge-bases|knowledge bases]], specifically targeting the interoperability of [[concepts/large-language-model]] (LLM)-generated wikis. It addresses the fragmentation of individual, unstructured "LLM Wikis" by establishing a unified schema for data exchange and integration.

## Core Concepts

- **Standardization of LLM Wikis**: Moves beyond isolated, proprietary note structures to a common format that allows different [[concepts/llm-based-agents|LLM agents]] or tools to read, write, and update knowledge [[concepts/number-systems|bases]] seamlessly.
- **Interoperability**: Enables cross-[[concepts/platform-compatibility|platform compatibility]] for [[concepts/knowledge-management|personal knowledge management]] systems, reducing [[concepts/vendor-lock-in|vendor lock-in]] and data silos.
- **Structured Evolution**: Represents a shift from ad-hoc note-taking to a systematic approach.
- **[[concepts/trust|Trust]] Signals (v0.2)**: Recent [[concepts/software-updates|updates]] focus on establishing "trust" in knowledge representation for [[concepts/ai-agents|AI agents]], ensuring [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] and provenance of [[concepts/ai-content-creation|AI-generated content]].

## Google OKF v0.2 Update

The latest [[concepts/iteration|iteration]], OKF v0.2, emphasizes trust [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] within AI knowledge representation. This update addresses the need for verifiable sources and confidence scores in AI-generated wikis.

- **Focus on Trust**: Establishes frameworks for AI agents to evaluate and represent the reliability of knowledge sources.
