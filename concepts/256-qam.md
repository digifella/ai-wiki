# 256 Qam

256 [[concepts/quadrature-amplitude-modulation-qam|Quadrature Amplitude Modulation]] (256 QAM) is a digital modulation technique employed in radio frequency communications to transmit data over electromagnetic carrier waves. The method encodes information by simultaneously varying both the amplitude and [[concepts/phase|phase]] of a carrier signal. This dual modulation creates 256 distinct signal states, typically represented as points in a constellation diagram. Each state corresponds to a unique 8-bit binary sequence, allowing the transmission of 8 [[concepts/classical-bits|bits]] of information per symbol.

## Spectral Efficiency and Performance

The primary advantage of 256 QAM is its high spectral efficiency, which enables greater data throughput within a given [[concepts/network-speed|bandwidth]] compared to lower-order modulation schemes. By packing more bits into each symbol, the technique maximizes the utility of the available spectrum. However, this increased [[concepts/density|density]] requires a higher [[concepts/camera-raw|signal-to-noise ratio]] (SNR) to maintain signal [[concepts/honesty|integrity]], making the system more susceptible to interference and attenuation than simpler modulation methods like QPSK or 16-QAM.

## Application in Starlink v3 Architecture

In the context of [[entities/spacex-starlink|SpaceX Starlink]] v3 architecture, 256 QAM is utilized to enhance the capacity of satellite-to-ground and inter-satellite links. The implementation of this modulation technique supports the high-bandwidth requirements of the next-generation constellation, allowing for more efficient data transmission between user terminals and the satellite network. The use of 256 QAM in this domain reflects a balance between maximizing data rates and managing the physical constraints of space-based communication systems.
