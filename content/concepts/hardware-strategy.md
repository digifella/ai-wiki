# Hardware Strategy

[[entities/apple|Apple]]'s [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] strategy represents a fundamental shift toward integrating [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] capabilities directly into devices rather than relying on cloud-based processing. This transition reflects changing economics around [[concepts/cloud-computing|cloud computing]], where the costs of processing, [[entities/storage|storage]], and data transmission have created incentives for companies to move computational workloads to the edge. By embedding [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] into iPhones, Macs, and other products, Apple aims to reduce latency, improve [[concepts/privacy|privacy]] by keeping user data local, and decrease dependency on external server infrastructure.

## Economic Drivers

The economics of [[concepts/cloud-ai|cloud-based AI]] have become increasingly challenging for hardware manufacturers. Continuous data transmission to remote servers incurs bandwidth costs, while maintaining cloud infrastructure [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] scale requires significant capital investment. [[concepts/on-device-processing|On-device processing]] offers a more sustainable model for Apple's hardware ecosystem, allowing the company to deliver AI features without proportional increases in operational expenses. This approach also differentiates Apple's products in a competitive market where processing [[concepts/power|power]] and privacy have become key consumer concerns.

## Implementation and Product Implications

The shift requires substantial changes to hardware [[concepts/design|design]], including more powerful [[concepts/central-processing-units|processors]] and specialized neural accelerators to handle complex models locally. This strategy affects product development across Apple's lineup, from flagship devices to more modest offerings, as the company determines which [[concepts/capabilities|AI capabilities]] require on-device processing versus selective cloud assistance. The approach necessarily constrains which features can be delivered on older hardware, creating a natural upgrade cycle tied to computational capability rather than traditional [[concepts/performance-data-gathering|performance metrics]].
