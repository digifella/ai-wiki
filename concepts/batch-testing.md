# Batch Testing

Batch testing is a [[concepts/quality-control|quality control]] methodology employed in pharmaceutical and chemical manufacturing to analyze multiple samples from a single production lot simultaneously. Instead of evaluating individual units in [[concepts/disconnection|isolation]], this approach examines representative samples together to assess variations in substance properties, [[concepts/writing|composition]], and behavior. By processing material as a collective group, manufacturers can identify deviations from expected specifications across the entire production run more efficiently than through unit-by-unit testing.

The primary advantage of this method lies in its scalability and [[concepts/speed|speed]] for large-scale operations. It enables the rapid detection of anomalies that might be missed or cost-prohibitive to find through exhaustive individual testing. This efficiency is critical in maintaining consistent product quality while managing the logistical demands of industrial manufacturing.

A notable application of batch testing occurred during the investigation of the Ritonavir [[concepts/crystalline-structure|polymorph crisis]]. In this instance, the methodology was used to analyze batches of the drug substance to identify unexpected changes in crystal structure. The ability to test multiple samples from the same batch allowed investigators to pinpoint the [[concepts/emergent-behavior|emergence]] of a new, less soluble polymorph that had compromised the drug's efficacy, highlighting the method's role in resolving complex stability issues.
