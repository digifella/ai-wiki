# Program Evaluation And Review Technique Pert

Program Evaluation and Review Technique (PERT) is a statistical project management methodology developed in the 1950s by the U.S. Navy for managing the Polaris submarine missile program. It was designed to address the challenges of large-scale, complex projects where task durations and outcomes cannot be predicted with certainty. Unlike traditional project management approaches that rely on single-point time estimates, PERT uses probabilistic estimates to account for inherent uncertainty in novel or research-intensive work.

The core methodology relies on three time estimates for each activity: optimistic, most likely, and pessimistic. These values are combined using a weighted average formula to calculate the expected duration of tasks. This approach allows project managers to model the probability of completing the project within a specific timeframe, providing a more realistic view of schedule risks compared to deterministic methods.

PERT is typically visualized using a network diagram that maps out the sequence of activities and their dependencies. By identifying the critical path—the longest sequence of dependent tasks that determines the minimum project duration—managers can focus resources on activities that directly impact the final deadline. This structure facilitates better monitoring of progress and helps in adjusting schedules when delays occur in critical tasks.

While originally developed for defense and engineering projects, PERT has been adapted for various industries involving high uncertainty, including software development and research initiatives. In the context of AI agents and complex system integration, PERT provides a framework for managing the unpredictable nature of task completion times, allowing for more robust planning and risk mitigation in dynamic environments.
