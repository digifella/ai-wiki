# Small Form Factor Pc

A small form factor (SFF) PC is a compact personal computer housed in a chassis significantly smaller than a standard desktop tower, typically occupying 10–20 liters of volume. These systems utilize mini-ITX or smaller motherboard form factors paired with space-efficient components, such as low-power processors and compact cooling solutions, to deliver full desktop computing capabilities within tight physical constraints. The engineering required to balance performance with reduced size has made SFF PCs increasingly viable for both consumer and specialized professional applications where space is at a premium.

## Hardware Architecture and Components

SFF designs rely on specialized motherboards, most commonly the mini-ITX standard, which feature fewer expansion slots and integrated peripherals to reduce footprint. To accommodate the limited internal volume, manufacturers employ low-profile expansion cards, slimline power supplies, and specialized case designs that optimize airflow in a confined space. Processors in these systems often prioritize thermal design power (TDP) efficiency over raw peak performance, utilizing mobile or low-voltage desktop chips to minimize heat generation and power consumption.

## Use Cases and Self-Hosting

Due to their reduced physical size and energy efficiency, SFF PCs are frequently selected as the hardware foundation for self-hosting personal cloud servers and home labs. Their compact nature allows them to fit discreetly in living spaces or office desks, while their lower power draw compared to full-sized towers reduces operational costs for 24/7 uptime. This makes them a practical alternative to dedicated network-attached storage (NAS) devices for users requiring the flexibility of a general-purpose operating system alongside storage management.
