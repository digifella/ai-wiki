# Editing Workflow

An [[concepts/photography-workflow|editing workflow]] is a systematic approach to processing [[concepts/digital-images|digital images]] that emphasizes efficiency and [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]]. Rather than treating each photograph as an isolated project, editors establish standardized procedures and repeatable processes to handle multiple images with similar requirements. This structured methodology reduces the time spent on routine [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] and minimizes variation across image batches, which is particularly valuable when processing large collections of photographs from the same shoot or [[concepts/session|session]].

## Automation and Presets

Automation forms the core of modern editing workflows. Software-based tools like adjustment presets, [[concepts/batch-processing|batch processing]], and recorded action sequences allow editors to apply predetermined settings across multiple images simultaneously. These presets encode common adjustments—[[concepts/exposure|exposure]] correction, white balance, [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] curves, and [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|color grading]]—that can be applied as a starting point, then refined individually as needed. Many editing platforms also support scripting and plugin systems that further extend automation capabilities.

## AI-Assisted Tools

[[concepts/ai-technologies|Artificial intelligence]] has introduced new possibilities for [[concepts/workflow-optimization|workflow optimization]], particularly through intelligent [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] and [[concepts/selective-adjustment|selective adjustment]] tools. AI-powered mask generation can automatically isolate specific elements like skies, subjects, or backgrounds without manual selection, allowing targeted adjustments without affecting the entire image. These tools reduce the technical [[concepts/friction|friction]] of complex edits while maintaining quality and control, making advanced techniques accessible within faster workflows.

## Practical Implementation

Effective workflows balance automation with flexibility. Rather than creating one-size-fits-all processes, experienced editors typically develop modular systems with multiple preset profiles for different shooting conditions, subjects, or creative intentions. Regular workflow refinement—identifying repetitive steps and consolidating them into new presets or [[concepts/automations|automations]]—allows the process itself to evolve and improve over time.
