# Ashokan Edicts

The Ashokan Edicts are a collection of inscriptions commissioned by the Mauryan Emperor Ashoka during the 3rd century BCE. Carved onto stone pillars, rocks, and cave walls across the Indian subcontinent, these texts serve as primary historical sources for understanding Ashoka’s administrative policies and his philosophical transformation following the Kalinga War. The edicts mark a significant shift in imperial governance, emphasizing *Dhamma* (Dharma), a code of social and moral conduct that promoted non-violence, tolerance, and welfare for all subjects.

The inscriptions were composed in multiple languages and scripts to ensure accessibility across the diverse empire. The majority are written in Prakrit using the Brahmi and Kharosthi scripts, while a smaller subset appears in Greek and Aramaic, reflecting the empire's reach into Hellenistic and Central Asian territories. These linguistic choices indicate an intentional effort to communicate directly with both local populations and foreign dignitaries, reinforcing the emperor’s authority and ethical directives.

The content of the edicts covers a wide range of topics, including the abolition of certain forms of animal sacrifice, the establishment of medical facilities for humans and animals, the planting of trees along roads, and the appointment of officials known as *Dhamma Mahamatras* to oversee the implementation of moral principles. They also record Ashoka’s visits to Buddhist holy sites and his patronage of the Sangha, providing crucial evidence for the spread of Buddhism during this period.

Historically, the Ashokan Edicts are significant not only for their religious and ethical content but also for their role in the development of Indian epigraphy and linguistics. They provide the earliest clear evidence of the use of Brahmi script and offer insights into the political geography and social structure of the Mauryan Empire. The stones themselves have become important archaeological sites, helping scholars reconstruct the timeline and extent of Ashoka’s reign.
