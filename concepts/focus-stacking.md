# Focus Stacking

Focus stacking is a photography technique that combines multiple images taken at different focal distances to produce a single photograph with greater overall sharpness than any individual shot. The method addresses a fundamental optical challenge in photography: the inverse relationship between depth of field and image quality. When photographers stop down their aperture to increase depth of field, they reduce light entering the camera and may introduce diffraction, which degrades sharpness. Focus stacking provides an alternative by capturing the scene in multiple exposures, each focused at a different distance, then blending them computationally to create a final image with extended depth of field without these optical penalties.

## Process and Execution

The basic workflow involves mounting the camera on a tripod and taking a series of photographs while incrementally adjusting the focus point from the nearest subject element to the farthest. The number of images required depends on the magnification level and desired depth of field. Modern cameras with autofocus motors or manual focus adjustments allow photographers to control focus precisely between shots. Specialized software then analyzes the sharp regions of each image and seamlessly combines them, using algorithms to blend overlapping areas and create a unified result.

## Applications and Limitations

Focus stacking is particularly valuable in landscape photography, macro photography, and product photography, where maximizing sharpness across the entire frame is important. However, the technique is not suitable for subjects with moving elements, as the required multiple exposures demand a static scene. Additionally, the post-processing stage can be time-consuming and may require manual intervention when automated blending produces artifacts or misalignments.
