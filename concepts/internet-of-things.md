# Internet Of Things

The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to a network of physical devices embedded with sensors, software, and connectivity that enable them to collect, exchange, and act on data. In [[concepts/health|health]] and [[concepts/wellbeing|wellbeing]] contexts, IoT applications focus particularly on [[concepts/wearable-devices|wearable devices]] worn directly on the body that continuously monitor physiological metrics and behavioral patterns.

## Wearable Devices in Health

Wearable [[entities/iot-devices|IoT devices]] collect real-time data on parameters such as heart rate, [[concepts/sleep|sleep]] patterns, [[concepts/exercise|physical activity]], and body temperature. These devices transmit information to connected platforms where data can be analyzed and accessed by users and healthcare providers. The continuous nature of this monitoring provides insights that traditional periodic health assessments cannot capture, supporting both individual health [[concepts/conscious-thought|awareness]] and [[concepts/clinical-reasoning|clinical decision-making]].

Key findings from systematic reviews, such as [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-2-Thilakarathne-NN-The-Role-of-the-Internet-of-Things-in|[2] Thilakarathne, NN, The Role of the Internet of Things in Health Care A Systematic and Comprehensive Study]], highlight the comprehensive role of IoT in healthcare:

- **Systematic Integration**: IoT facilitates a holistic approach to healthcare by integrating data from diverse sources, improving the accuracy of health assessments.
- **[[concepts/remote-health-monitoring|Remote Patient Monitoring]]**: Enables continuous surveillance of chronic conditions, reducing the need for frequent hospital visits and enhancing patient autonomy.
- **Data-Driven Insights**: The aggregation of large-scale health data supports [[concepts/artificial-intelligence-in-healthcare|predictive analytics]], allowing for [[concepts/secondary-prevention|early detection]] of potential health issues before they become critical.
- **Interoperability Challenges**: Successful implementation requires robust data standards to ensure seamless communication between different IoT devices and [[concepts/electronic-health-record|electronic health record]] (EHR) systems.
- **[[concepts/privacy|Privacy]] and [[concepts/security|Security]]**: The extensive collection of personal health data necessitates stringent security protocols to protect patient privacy and prevent data breaches.

## Qualitative Data Integration

While IoT provides quantitative physiological data, qualitative methods are essential for understanding user behavior and context:

- **Focus [[concepts/group-dynamics|Group Dynamics]]**: Utilizes group interaction to uncover shared experiences and social norms regarding [[concepts/adoption|technology adoption]], as detailed in [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Focus-Group-Interviewing|Focus Group Interviewing]].
- **Mixed-Methods Approach**: Combining sensor data with [[concepts/semi-structured-interviews|qualitative interviews]] enhances the validity of health interventions by correlating [[concepts/purpose|objective]] metrics with subjective user perceptions.
- **Source Credibility**: Insights derived from [[concepts/peer-reviewed-research|peer-reviewed literature]] (e.g., NCBI PMC articles) ensure robust methodological standards in interpreting patient [[concepts/feedback|feedback]].
