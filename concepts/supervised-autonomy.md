# Supervised Autonomy

Supervised autonomy is a regulatory classification for autonomous vehicles that permits operation under human oversight rather than requiring continuous manual control. Under this framework, the vehicle autonomously manages routine driving tasks including acceleration, braking, steering, and lane maintenance, while a human operator remains attentive and capable of intervention. This approach represents an intermediate regulatory position between fully manual driving and complete vehicle autonomy, enabling manufacturers to deploy advanced driving systems while maintaining human responsibility for safety-critical decisions.

## Regulatory Implementation

The framework emerged from the need to test and deploy autonomous technologies in real-world conditions before full automation becomes viable. Various jurisdictions have adopted different supervised autonomy standards. Tesla's Full Self-Driving (FSD) system received approval for supervised autonomous operation in the Netherlands, marking a significant precedent for European Union regulatory acceptance. This approval established conditions under which drivers could engage autonomous features while remaining responsible for monitoring vehicle behavior and intervening when necessary.

## Operational Requirements and Liability

Supervised autonomy typically requires drivers to maintain situational awareness and keep hands on or near controls, though engagement levels vary by jurisdiction and system design. The regulatory framework places liability considerations on both manufacturers and operators, with the specific allocation depending on local laws and the circumstances of any incident. This shared responsibility model reflects the transitional nature of the technology, where neither fully autonomous nor fully manual operation applies.
