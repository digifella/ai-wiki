# Open Source Hardware

[[concepts/open-source|Open source]] hardware refers to physical devices, machines, and equipment whose design specifications, schematics, and documentation are publicly available under licenses that permit modification, distribution, and study. Similar to open source software, this approach emphasizes [[concepts/opacity|transparency]] and collaborative development, allowing engineers and makers to inspect, improve, and adapt hardware designs. The [[concepts/exercise|movement]] enables cost reduction through shared development efforts and facilitates rapid [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] across distributed communities of contributors.

## Design and Documentation

Open source hardware projects typically include complete [[concepts/technical-documentation|technical documentation]] such as circuit [[concepts/diagrams|diagrams]], bills of materials, CAD files, and assembly [[concepts/instructions|instructions]]. This transparency allows users to understand how devices function at a fundamental level and to manufacture or repair them independently. The quality and completeness of documentation varies significantly across projects and directly influences their [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] and community engagement.

## Applications and Examples

Open source hardware spans numerous domains, from single-board computers like Arduino and [[entities/raspberry-pi|Raspberry Pi]] to scientific instruments, laboratory equipment, and networking devices. In research contexts, open source hardware reduces equipment costs and permits [[concepts/customization|customization]] for specific experimental needs. The [[entities/nano-banana|nano banana]] represents an example of open source hardware [[concepts/design|design principles]] applied to microscale systems.

## Licensing and Practical Considerations

Open source hardware employs various [[concepts/licensing|licensing]] models, including [[entities/cern|CERN]] Open Hardware [[concepts/license|License]] and Creative Commons variants, which define permissible uses and attribution requirements. While the concept promotes [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]] and [[concepts/innovation|innovation]], practical challenges include manufacturing complexity, component sourcing, and liability considerations. [[concepts/success|Success]] of open source hardware projects often depends on active communities willing to maintain documentation and provide technical support.
