# Masking Tool

A masking tool is a feature in image editing software that allows photographers and designers to selectively apply adjustments to specific areas of an image while leaving other areas unaffected. By isolating portions of a photograph, masking enables precise control over edits such as exposure, color, contrast, and other effects. This selective editing capability is fundamental to non-destructive image processing, as it allows adjustments to be made and modified without permanently altering the original image data.

## Applications in Landscape Photography

Masking tools are particularly valuable in landscape photography, where different areas of a scene often require different adjustments. A photographer might use a landscape mask to brighten the sky while keeping the foreground at its original exposure, or to enhance color saturation in specific regions. Software like Adobe Lightroom and Camera Raw offer dedicated masking features designed for this purpose, allowing users to define areas based on luminance ranges, color ranges, or manual selections.

## Non-Destructive Editing

The primary advantage of masking is that it maintains the original image data while applying targeted edits. This non-destructive approach means adjustments can be refined, removed, or modified at any point in the editing workflow without degradation. Masking layers in image editing software store instructions for where and how adjustments should be applied, rather than permanently modifying pixels.
