# Verifiable Facts

Verifiable facts are claims or pieces of information that can be objectively confirmed through evidence, observation, testing, or logical reasoning. They form the foundation of reliable communication and decision-making by establishing a clear distinction between what can be reliably established and what remains uncertain, speculative, or opinion-based. A verifiable fact is typically supported by multiple independent sources, reproducible methods, or logical demonstration that others can examine and validate.

## Role in AI Systems

In contexts involving AI agents and advisory systems, the distinction between verifiable facts and other forms of information becomes particularly important. AI systems that function as advisors or decision-support tools benefit from explicit identification of which claims rest on established evidence versus those that involve interpretation, prediction, or uncertainty. This transparency allows users to assess the reliability of recommendations and understand the confidence levels appropriate to different types of claims. Agents that clearly separate factual foundations from reasoning based on those facts tend to produce more trustworthy outputs and reduce misrepresentation of uncertain information as settled knowledge.

## Practical Application

Verifiable facts enable more effective step-by-step reasoning and prompt clarification in advisory contexts. When an AI system grounds its analysis in demonstrable facts before proceeding to inference or recommendation, it provides checkpoints where assumptions can be examined. This approach also supports iterative refinement: clarifying which elements of a question rest on verifiable facts and which require further investigation or judgement allows both the system and its user to identify gaps in knowledge and adjust their approach accordingly.
