# Application Customization

Application [[concepts/customization|customization]] involves modifying and adapting [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] and tools to meet specific use case requirements. Within the [[entities/hugging-face|Hugging Face]] [[concepts/open-source-machine-learning|open-source machine learning]] platform, this process allows developers and organizations to tailor [[concepts/pre-trained-models|pre-trained models]] to their particular domains, datasets, and performance objectives. This approach enables the creation of specialized solutions without the need to train models from scratch, leveraging existing foundational architectures as a starting point.

The customization process typically relies on techniques such as [[concepts/fine-tuning|fine-tuning]], where a [[concepts/pre-trained-model|pre-trained model]] is further trained on a smaller, domain-specific dataset. This method adjusts the model's [[concepts/parameters|weights]] to better understand the nuances of the target application while retaining the general knowledge acquired during initial training. Hugging Face provides libraries and tools that facilitate this adaptation, making it accessible for users to integrate custom [[concepts/open-source-philosophy|logic]] and data into [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|standard model]] frameworks.

By utilizing pre-trained models as a base, customization significantly reduces the [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]] and time required to [[concepts/deployment|deploy]] effective [[concepts/ai-powered-applications|AI applications]]. It allows for rapid [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] and deployment of models that are optimized for specific tasks, such as [[concepts/language-processing|natural language processing]] in legal or medical fields, or [[concepts/computer-vision|computer vision]] in industrial inspection. This strategy balances the need for specialized performance with the efficiency of leveraging community-contributed, open-source foundational models.
