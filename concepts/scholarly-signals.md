# Scholarly Signals

Scholarly signals are [[concepts/metadata|metadata]] [[concepts/indicators|indicators]] and structural features used to assess the **credibility**, **authority**, and **[[concepts/document-review|peer-review]] status** of information sources. These signals help distinguish rigorous [[entities/tomasz-janowski|academic]] work from low-[[concepts/integrity|integrity]] or unverified content.

## Key Indicators

- **Peer-Review Status**: The strongest signal of scholarly rigor. Indicates that content has undergone [[concepts/scientific-skepticism|critical evaluation]] by experts.
- **Source [[concepts/honesty|Integrity]] [[concepts/flags|Flags]]**: Markers like `unverified` or `verified` that track the trustworthiness of the source's metadata.
- **Credibility Tiers**: Hierarchical ratings (e.g., Tier 5: Peer-Reviewed) that quantify source [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]].
- **Journal Metrics**: Data points such as SJR (SCImago Journal Rank) and ISSN that provide quantitative measures of journal impact and [[concepts/data-indexing|indexing]] status.
