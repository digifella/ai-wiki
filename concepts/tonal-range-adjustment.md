# Tonal Range Adjustment

Tonal range adjustment is a fundamental technique in digital photo editing that involves modifying the distribution of tones—from shadows through midtones to highlights—across an image. This process allows photographers and editors to enhance contrast, recover detail in overexposed or underexposed areas, and establish the overall luminosity and mood of a photograph. The tonal range refers to the span between the darkest and lightest values in an image, and adjusting this range is essential for achieving properly exposed and visually compelling results.

## Methods and Tools

Common tools for tonal range adjustment include curves, levels, and exposure controls available in most photo editing software. The Photoshop Camera Raw Filter provides a comprehensive interface for these adjustments, allowing editors to modify shadows, highlights, whites, and blacks independently. These tools enable precise control over specific tonal regions without affecting the entire image uniformly, making it possible to selectively brighten dark areas while preserving highlight detail, or vice versa.

## Practical Applications

Tonal range adjustment is particularly valuable when correcting images with poor initial exposure or when preparing photographs for different output media. Expanding the tonal range can increase perceived contrast and visual impact, while compressing it may be useful for protecting shadow or highlight detail. Proper tonal adjustment is often one of the first steps in a complete color grading workflow, as it establishes a foundation for subsequent color correction and creative adjustments.
