# Internal Geometry

**Internal Geometry** refers to the implicit, high-dimensional spatial structures and topological mappings that [[concepts/large-language-model]]s (LLMs) develop within their latent spaces to represent semantic [[concepts/relationships|relationships]], logical constraints, and [[concepts/material-properties|physical properties]]. Unlike explicit geometric [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]], internal geometry emerges as a byproduct of optimizing for predictive accuracy, allowing models to encode complex structural priors without explicit programming.

## Core Characteristics

- **[[concepts/embedding-spaces|Latent Space]] Topology**: [[concepts/semantic-similarity|Semantic similarity]] often correlates with geometric proximity in embedding space, forming clusters that reflect conceptual hierarchies.
- **Emergent Structure**: Complex [[concepts/reasoning-capabilities|reasoning capabilities]], such as counting or spatial navigation, arise from the alignment of internal representations with external structural regularities.
- **Implicit Priors**: Models internalize [[concepts/fundamental-laws-of-physics|physical laws]] (e.g., [[concepts/preservation|conservation]] of mass, spatial [[concepts/continuity|continuity]]) as geometric constraints within their activation patterns.
