# Human Cognition

Human cognition refers to the mental processes through which humans acquire, process, and apply knowledge. These processes include perception, [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]], [[concepts/memory|memory]], [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]], [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]], and language comprehension. [[concepts/cognition|Cognition]] operates through both conscious deliberation and unconscious automatic processes, allowing humans to navigate complex environments, learn from experience, and adapt behavior accordingly.

## Machine Intelligence and Human Cognition

Physicist [[concepts/feynman|Richard Feynman]] addressed comparisons between machine intelligence and human cognition in a 1985 Q&A [[concepts/session|session]]. Rather than treating them as equivalent phenomena, Feynman emphasized fundamental differences in how machines and humans process information and solve problems. His perspective highlighted that human cognition involves qualities—such as intuition, creativity, and [[concepts/contextual-understanding|contextual understanding]]—that differ substantively from the rule-based, computational approaches of machines as understood at that time.

Feynman's analysis remained grounded in observable differences rather than philosophical speculation, reflecting his characteristic emphasis on clarity and empirical observation. His discussion contributed to mid-1980s thinking about [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] limitations and the distinctive character of human thought.
