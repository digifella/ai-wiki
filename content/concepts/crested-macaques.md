# Crested Macaques

Crested macaques (*Macaca nigra*) are large primates native to the Indonesian island of Sulawesi. They are characterized by their dark fur and distinctive crest of hair on their heads, which gives the species its common name. These highly social animals live in multi-female, multi-male groups and are known for their complex social behaviors, including tool use and cultural transmission of behaviors within populations.

## Relationship to the Infinite Monkey Theorem

The infinite monkey theorem—a thought experiment proposing that a monkey randomly typing at a keyboard for infinite time would eventually produce the complete works of Shakespeare—has become a cultural shorthand for probability and randomness. While the theorem is mathematical rather than biological, crested macaques have occasionally featured in popular discussions of this concept due to their cognitive abilities and capacity for [[concepts/learning|learning]], though no actual experiment has demonstrated that monkeys can produce coherent text through [[concepts/random-typing|random typing]].

## Conservation and Research

Crested macaques are endangered, with their population threatened by habitat loss and hunting. They have become subjects of significant primatological research, particularly regarding social learning and cultural behavior. Their intelligence and social complexity make them valuable subjects for understanding primate [[concepts/cognition|cognition]], though such research focuses on their actual behavioral and cognitive capacities rather than literary production.
