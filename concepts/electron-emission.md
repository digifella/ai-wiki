# Electron Emission

**Electron emission** is the process by which electrons are released from a material or atomic system. This phenomenon occurs through various [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]], including thermionic emission, field emission, photoelectric effect, and radioactive decay.

## Mechanisms

### Radioactive Decay
In the context of nuclear [[concepts/physics|physics]], electron emission is primarily associated with **beta decay** ($\beta^-$ decay). During this process, a neutron within an unstable nucleus transforms into a proton, emitting an electron (beta particle) and an electron antineutrino to conserve lepton number and energy.

*   **Beta Decay Dynamics**: The emission of the electron is accompanied by the [[concepts/deployment|release]] of a Neutrino, a nearly massless, chargeless particle that interacts weakly with matter.
*   **[[concepts/preservation|Conservation]] Laws**: The simultaneous emission of the electron and the antineutrino ensures the conservation of energy, momentum, and angular momentum.

### Other Emission Types
*   **Thermionic Emission**: Ejection of electrons from a heated material due to thermal energy overcoming the work function.
*   **Photoelectric Effect**: Emission of electrons when [[concepts/light|electromagnetic radiation]] (light) strikes a material.
*   **Field Emission**: Ejection of electrons from a material under the influence of a strong electric field.

## Related Concepts

*   Neutrino: The neutral lepton emitted alongside electrons in [[concepts/beta-decay|beta decay]].
*   Work Function: The minimum energy needed to remove an electron from a solid.
