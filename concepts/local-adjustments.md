# Local Adjustments

Local adjustments refer to edits applied to specific regions of a photograph rather than to the entire image. This technique allows photographers to selectively enhance or modify particular areas—such as brightening a subject's face, adjusting color in a specific region, or correcting exposure in isolated zones—while leaving the rest of the image unchanged. Local adjustments are fundamental to non-destructive digital editing workflows, as they preserve the original image data while enabling targeted refinements.

## Common Methods

The primary tools for making local adjustments include gradient filters, radial filters, and brush-based selections. Gradient filters create smooth transitions between adjusted and unadjusted areas, making them suitable for sky replacements or horizon corrections. Radial filters affect circular or elliptical regions, often used to draw attention to a central subject by darkening the surroundings. Brush-based tools allow for manual painting of adjustments with varying hardness and flow settings, offering the highest degree of precision for complex shapes.

## Intersect Masking

Modern editing software, such as Adobe Lightroom, has introduced intersect masking features to streamline the creation of complex selections. This functionality allows users to combine multiple masks, such as a brush selection and an AI-generated subject mask, to isolate specific areas with greater accuracy. By intersecting these masks, editors can apply adjustments only where the selections overlap, reducing the need for manual erasing or refining of mask edges. This approach significantly improves efficiency when working on detailed portraits or landscapes with intricate foreground elements.
