# Computational Functionalism

**Computational Functionalism** is the philosophical position that mental states are defined by their functional roles—i.e., their causal relations to sensory inputs, behavioral outputs, and other mental states—rather than by their physical [[concepts/writing|composition]]. It posits that if a system implements the right computational structure, it instantiates the corresponding mental state, regardless of substrate.

## Core Tenets
- **Multiple Realizability:** Mental states can be realized in diverse physical substrates ([[concepts/silicon|silicon]], carbon, etc.) provided the functional organization is preserved.
- **Software/Hardware Distinction:** [[concepts/the-mind|The mind]] is to the brain as software is to hardware; the mind is the abstract computational program running on the physical brain.
- **Anti-Biological Essentialism:** Consciousness and [[concepts/cognition|cognition]] do not require specific biological properties, only the correct causal/functional dynamics.

## Critiques and Limitations
Recent discourse highlights significant challenges to the applicability of functionalism to current and future [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]], particularly regarding the necessity of biological constraints for genuine consciousness.

- **Biological Constraints:** Arguments suggest that current [[concepts/ai-consciousness|AI Consciousness]]: Biological Constraints and Current Limitations cannot achieve true consciousness due to a lack of biological embodiment and specific physiological constraints necessary for [[concepts/conscious-thought|subjective experience]].
- **LLM Limitations:** [[concepts/demystifying-llms|Large Language Models]] (LLMs) are often cited as lacking the causal power or intrinsic intentionality required for functionalist accounts of mind, as they merely manipulate symbols without understanding.
- **Substrate Independence Debate:** While functionalism asserts substrate independence, critics point out that certain biological properties (e.g., neurochemical [[concepts/systems|feedback loops]]) may be functionally indispensable for consciousness, challenging the "pure [[concepts/computation|computation]]" view.
