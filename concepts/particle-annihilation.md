# Particle Annihilation

Particle annihilation is a fundamental process in particle physics where a subatomic particle and its corresponding antiparticle collide and convert their combined mass entirely into energy. This transformation is governed by Einstein’s mass-energy equivalence principle ($E=mc^2$), resulting in the emission of high-energy photons or other particle-antiparticle pairs. The phenomenon is well-established and routinely observed in controlled laboratory environments, most notably in positron-electron annihilation, which serves as the physical basis for Positron Emission Tomography (PET) medical imaging technology.

## Dark Matter Annihilation

In the context of astrophysics, dark matter annihilation refers to the theoretical process where Weakly Interacting Massive Particles (WIMPs), a leading candidate for dark matter, collide and annihilate each other. This process is hypothesized to produce standard model particles, including gamma rays, which can be detected by space-based observatories. Recent research has identified a potential signal of WIMP annihilation within the gamma-ray emissions from the Milky Way, suggesting that dark matter may interact with itself through mechanisms consistent with particle physics models.
