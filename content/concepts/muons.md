# Muons

Muons are [[concepts/elementary-particles|fundamental particles]] belonging to the lepton family, similar to electrons but approximately 207 times more massive. They are produced when [[concepts/cosmic-rays|cosmic rays]] collide with Earth's atmosphere and decay into electrons and neutrinos with a mean lifetime of about 2.2 microseconds. Despite their brief existence, muons penetrate deep into the Earth's atmosphere and reach the surface, making them detectable in [[concepts/particle-physics-experiments|particle physics experiments]].

## The Muon g-2 Experiment

The Muon g-2 experiment, conducted at Fermilab, measures a precise property of muons known as the [[concepts/anomalous-magnetic-moment|anomalous magnetic moment]], or "g-2." This value describes how strongly a muon responds to magnetic fields and is predicted by the [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|Standard Model of particle physics]]. By comparing experimental measurements with theoretical predictions, physicists can detect signs of [[concepts/physics|physics]] beyond the Standard Model, such as undiscovered particles or forces.

## Methodology and Significance

The experiment works by injecting polarized muons into a [[entities/storage|storage]] ring where they precess in a carefully controlled magnetic field. Sensitive detectors track the spin precession of the muons, allowing researchers to measure g-2 with extraordinary precision. The anticipated 2025 results aim to provide more refined measurements that may reveal discrepancies with Standard Model calculations, potentially indicating new physics. These findings could have profound implications for understanding the fundamental [[entities/nature|nature]] of matter and the universe.
