# Astrobiology

Astrobiology is an interdisciplinary scientific field that examines the conditions necessary for life to exist beyond Earth and investigates where such life might be found in the universe. The discipline combines methods and knowledge from biology, chemistry, geology, and astronomy to address fundamental questions about life's origins, distribution, and the environmental requirements for habitability. Rather than engaging in speculation, astrobiology grounds its inquiries in empirical science, using our understanding of life on Earth as a foundation for exploring possibilities elsewhere.

## Key Research Areas

Astrobiologists study extremophiles—organisms on Earth that thrive in extreme environments such as deep-sea hydrothermal vents, acidic hot springs, and Antarctic ice—as models for understanding what forms extraterrestrial life might take. These organisms demonstrate that life can persist under conditions previously thought incompatible with biology, expanding the range of potentially habitable environments throughout the cosmos. Research focuses on identifying biosignatures, chemical or physical evidence that could indicate past or present life, particularly on Mars, Venus, and the moons of Jupiter and Saturn.

The field also investigates the chemical and geological conditions that enable habitability, including the presence of liquid water, appropriate chemical composition, and energy sources. Astrobiologists contribute to space exploration missions designed to search for evidence of microbial life or its remnants, and they inform the search for potentially habitable exoplanets orbiting distant stars. Through this work, astrobiology connects questions about life's place in the universe to concrete scientific investigation.
