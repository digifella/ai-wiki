# Restaurant Techniques

Restaurant-[[concepts/style|style]] blanching and steaming are professional [[concepts/cooking-methods|cooking methods]] that can be adapted for efficient home vegetable [[concepts/preparation|preparation]]. These techniques involve briefly cooking vegetables in boiling water or steam, then rapidly cooling them to halt the cooking process. By preparing vegetables this way in advance, home cooks can significantly reduce meal preparation time during the week while maintaining quality and nutritional value.

## Blanching and Steaming

Blanching involves submerging vegetables in boiling salted water for a short period—typically one to three minutes depending on the vegetable—then immediately transferring them to ice water to stop cooking. Steaming accomplishes similar results by exposing vegetables to steam rather than direct [[entities/contact|contact]] with boiling water, often preserving more nutrients and color. Both methods partially cook vegetables to the point where they are tender but retain a slight firmness, making them ideal for quick reheating or incorporation into various dishes throughout the week.

## Practical Applications

Pre-blanched or steamed vegetables can be stored in the refrigerator for several days, allowing cooks to quickly assemble meals by reheating or combining them with proteins, grains, and sauces. This approach streamlines weekday cooking by eliminating the time spent on vegetable preparation during meal assembly, while the controlled cooking ensures consistent results and reduces the risk of overcooking during final preparation.
