# Milky Way

The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy approximately 100,000 light-years in diameter, containing an estimated 100-400 billion [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]]. Our solar system is located in one of its spiral arms, roughly 26,000 light-years from the [[concepts/galactic-center|galactic center]]. The galaxy's [[concepts/structure|structure]] consists of a central bar, spiral arms, a thick disk, a thin disk, and an extended halo. It is composed of stars, gas, dust, and a significant concentration of [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]], which comprises approximately 85% of the galaxy's total mass.

## Composition and Structure

The visible matter in the Milky Way includes Population I and Population II stars, along with interstellar gas and dust clouds. Dark matter forms an invisible halo surrounding the galaxy and contributes substantially to its gravitational dynamics and rotation curves. The galactic center is believed to contain a supermassive [[concepts/black-holes|black hole]] with a mass approximately four million times that of the Sun, around which stars and gas orbit.

## Observational Studies

Observations of the Milky Way have been conducted across multiple wavelengths of the electromagnetic spectrum, from radio waves to [[concepts/gamma-rays|gamma rays]]. Recent studies have investigated signatures consistent with dark matter interactions, including potential weakly interacting massive particle ([[concepts/wimp|WIMP]]) [[concepts/annihilation-signals|annihilation signals]] detected through gamma-ray emissions. These observations continue to refine our understanding of the galaxy's composition and the properties of dark matter within it.
