# Temperature Adjustment

Temperature adjustment is the process of modifying the color temperature of digital images to correct for lighting conditions or to establish a specific aesthetic mood. In photography and image editing, this parameter is measured on the Kelvin scale, where lower values produce warmer tones characterized by orange and red hues, while higher values yield cooler tones with blue characteristics. This fundamental post-processing task allows editors to neutralize unwanted color casts introduced by mixed lighting sources or white balance errors, ensuring that whites appear neutral and colors are rendered accurately.

The technique serves two primary functions: corrective and creative. Corrective adjustments address technical inaccuracies, such as the yellow cast from incandescent bulbs or the blue tint from shade, restoring the image to a naturalistic representation of the scene. Conversely, creative adjustments manipulate temperature to evoke specific emotional responses, utilizing warm palettes for intimacy or nostalgia and cool palettes for detachment or clinical sterility.

In modern software ecosystems, such as Adobe Photoshop 2025, temperature control is often integrated into broader color management tools. Tutorials by experts like Blake Rudis highlight the evolution of these features, demonstrating how new interfaces streamline the adjustment of color and vibrance alongside temperature. These updates aim to provide more intuitive access to white balance controls, allowing for precise fine-tuning without the need for complex manual curve adjustments or separate white balance tools.
