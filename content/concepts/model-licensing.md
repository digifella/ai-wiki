# Model Licensing

Model licensing refers to the legal frameworks and commercial terms under which AI language [[concepts/models|models]] are distributed and used. These approaches vary significantly across vendors, ranging from proprietary closed models to [[concepts/open-source|open-source]] releases with permissive licenses. The [[concepts/licensing|licensing]] [[concepts/structure|structure]] determines who can access a model, how it can be modified, and what restrictions apply to commercial or research use.

## Open-Weight Model Licensing

[[concepts/model-customization|Open-weight models]] have become increasingly common in the [[concepts/ai-industry|AI industry]], with major providers releasing models under permissive licenses. [[concepts/google-search|Google]]'s [[concepts/23b-parameter-models|Gemma 4]] series, for example, was released under the [[concepts/apache-2-0|Apache 2.0 license]], enabling broad use including commercial [[concepts/software|applications]] and local device [[concepts/deployment|deployment]]. This licensing approach contrasts with fully proprietary models and reflects a competitive strategy to [[concepts/ambition|drive]] [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] and reduce barriers to entry for developers and researchers.

## Proprietary and Commercial Models

Proprietary models maintained by companies like [[entities/openai|OpenAI]] remain subject to commercial [[concepts/licensing-agreements|licensing agreements]] that restrict access and usage rights. These models typically operate under terms-of-service agreements rather than open-source licenses, allowing vendors to maintain greater [[concepts/power|control]] over model [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]], safety measures, and commercial deployment. The [[concepts/cost|cost]] structures for proprietary models can be substantial for enterprise applications, as noted in assessments of competing products in the market.
