# Buddhism

Buddhism is a philosophical and spiritual tradition that emerged in [[concepts/ancient-india|ancient India]] around the 5th century BCE, founded on the teachings of Siddhartha Gautama, known as the Buddha or "the Awakened One." Rather than centering on worship of deities, Buddhism offers a practical path for understanding the nature of human suffering and achieving liberation, known as nirvana. The tradition has developed into a major [[entities/earth|world]] religion with hundreds of millions of adherents across Asia and increasingly worldwide.

## Core Teachings

The Buddha's fundamental insights are encapsulated in the Four Noble Truths: that suffering exists, that suffering arises from craving and [[concepts/attachment-theory|attachment]], that suffering can cease, and that a path exists to end suffering. This path, called the Eightfold Path, provides ethical and mental disciplines for practitioners. Central to Buddhist philosophy is the concept of non-self (anatta), the interconnectedness of all phenomena, and the impermanent nature of existence. Meditation and [[concepts/mindfulness|mindfulness]] practice serve as primary methods for cultivating insight and transforming one's relationship to [[concepts/experience|experience]].

## Major Traditions

Buddhism has evolved into several major traditions. Theravada Buddhism, found primarily in Southeast Asia, emphasizes individual practice and monastic communities. Mahayana Buddhism, dominant in East Asia, developed a broader conception of enlightenment accessible to all beings and incorporates veneration of bodhisattvas—enlightened beings who delay their own final liberation to help others. Vajrayana Buddhism, practiced in Tibet and surrounding regions, incorporates tantric practices and ritual elements alongside philosophical study.

## Historical Development and Spread

From its origins in [[entities/india|India]], Buddhism spread along trade routes to Central Asia, [[entities/china|China]], [[entities/japan|Japan]], Southeast Asia, and Tibet, adapting to local cultures while maintaining core teachings. Though Buddhism largely declined in India by the 12th century, it became deeply embedded across Asia. In the modern era, Buddhism has expanded to Western countries, where it has influenced [[concepts/psychology|psychology]], [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]], and secular mindfulness practices while maintaining its traditional monastic and devotional forms.
