# GCC Countries (Gulf Cooperation Council)

The [[concepts/gulf-cooperation-council|Gulf Cooperation Council]] (GCC) is a political and economic union of six Middle Eastern countries comprising [[entities/saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], [[entities/kuwait|Kuwait]], the United Arab Emirates ([[entities/uae|UAE]]), Qatar, Bahrain, and Oman. Established in 1981, the GCC aims to promote intergovernmental cooperation in various fields including economy, infrastructure, education, [[concepts/health|health]] services, and defense.

### Key Features
- **Economic Integration:** Free trade agreements, common markets, and currency unification initiatives.
- **[[concepts/large-scale-infrastructure|Infrastructure Projects]]:** Large-scale construction projects such as the GCC rail network and joint military bases.
- **Energy Resources:** Dominance in global oil and gas production.
- **[[concepts/water-management|Water Management]] Challenges:** Severe [[concepts/water-scarcity|water scarcity]] issues due to arid climates.

### Desalination
Desalination plays a crucial role in addressing water scarcity within GCC countries:
- **[[concepts/msf-technology|MSF Technology]]:** Multi-Stage Flash (MSF) is widely used for desalination, converting seawater into fresh drinking water.
- **[[concepts/energy-demand|Energy Demand]]:** High energy consumption required for the operation of large-scale [[concepts/desalination-plants|desalination plants]].
