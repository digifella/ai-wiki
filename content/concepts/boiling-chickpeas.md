# Boiling Chickpeas

Boiling chickpeas is a foundational cooking technique for preparing dried chickpeas into a tender, edible form. The process begins with soaking dried chickpeas in water for [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] least 8 hours or overnight, which rehydrates them and reduces [[concepts/cooking-time|cooking time]]. After draining the soaked chickpeas, they are simmered in fresh water for 1–2 hours until tender, though cooking time varies depending on the chickpea variety and age. The goal is to achieve a consistent [[concepts/texture|texture]] throughout without splitting the skins.

## Nutritional Profile

Chickpeas are legumes rich in plant-based protein, dietary fiber, and complex carbohydrates, making them a nutritionally dense food. A cooked cup of chickpeas contains approximately 15 grams of protein and 12 grams of fiber. This combination of protein and fiber contributes to sustained satiety, helping regulate appetite and potentially supporting [[concepts/weight-loss|weight management]] when incorporated into a balanced [[concepts/diet|diet]]. Chickpeas also provide essential minerals including iron, magnesium, and folate.

## Applications in Salads

Boiled chickpeas serve as a versatile base for salads and other dishes. A simple [[concepts/chickpea-salad|chickpea salad]] can be prepared by combining cooled boiled chickpeas with fresh vegetables such as cucumber, tomatoes, and bell peppers, then dressing with lemon juice and olive oil. This [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] preserves the nutritional benefits of both the chickpeas and fresh produce while creating a satisfying, nutrient-complete meal that supports sustained energy and [[concepts/satiety|fullness]].
