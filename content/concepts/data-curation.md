# Data Curation

Data curation is the process of selecting, organizing, and maintaining digital and physical information resources to ensure their quality, [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]], and long-term [[concepts/preservation|preservation]]. Within security-infrastructure contexts, data curation involves establishing systematic approaches to managing sensitive information throughout its lifecycle, from creation and classification through [[entities/storage|storage]], access [[concepts/power|control]], and eventual archival or deletion. Effective data curation requires clear [[concepts/governance|governance]] frameworks, documented procedures, and ongoing [[concepts/quality-assurance|quality assurance]] to meet both operational and [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] requirements.

## Preservation and Access

A key function of data curation is balancing preservation with controlled access. Institutions must establish [[concepts/open-standards|standards]] for how information is stored, what [[concepts/metadata|metadata]] is recorded, and how records can be discovered and retrieved by authorized users. This includes maintaining documentation about data provenance, handling practices, and any modifications made over time. Proper curation ensures that information remains usable and trustworthy even as technologies and organizational needs change.

## Integration with Institutional Practice

Data curation operates as part of broader information management systems within organizations. Libraries, archives, and institutional repositories use curation practices to maintain collections—whether digitized historical materials or contemporary records—ensuring they remain discoverable and contextually understood. For security-sensitive environments, curation practices must incorporate access restrictions, audit trails, and periodic reviews to verify that stored information continues to meet organizational needs and regulatory obligations.
