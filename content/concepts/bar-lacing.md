# Bar Lacing

Bar lacing is a method of fastening shoes in which the lace runs horizontally across the shoe from one eyelet to the opposite eyelet, creating a series of parallel bars across the instep. This technique differs from cross-lacing, the predominant lacing method, where the lace passes diagonally across the shoe's surface. In bar lacing, the horizontal segments run straight across [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] each eyelet level, with vertical segments connecting successive rows along the sides of the shoe.

## Technical Characteristics

The bar lacing pattern creates distinct visual and functional differences compared to cross-lacing. The horizontal orientation of the lace segments distributes pressure more evenly across the width of the foot rather than concentrating tension along diagonal lines. This configuration may reduce pressure points on the instep and provides a different tension [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] profile across the shoe's surface.

## Applications and Claims

Bar lacing has been promoted primarily within [[concepts/running|running]] communities as an alternative to traditional cross-lacing, with proponents suggesting it may offer improved comfort and reduced foot strain during athletic activity. However, the extent to which bar lacing provides biomechanical advantages remains primarily anecdotal, and scientific validation of superior performance is limited. The technique remains uncommon in mainstream shoe manufacturing and consumer practice, with cross-lacing continuing as the dominant fastening method across most shoe types.
