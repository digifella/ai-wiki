# Natural Ground Detection

Natural Ground Detection is a post-processing technique utilized within Adobe Lightroom and Adobe Camera Raw to identify and isolate terrestrial surfaces in landscape photography. By leveraging AI-powered masking tools, the software automatically distinguishes ground elements—such as soil, dirt, grass, and rock—from other image components like the sky, water, and vegetation. This selective isolation allows photographers to apply targeted adjustments to the ground area without inadvertently affecting the rest of the composition.

The workflow typically begins with the selection of the "Ground" mask option within the Masking panel. The algorithm analyzes the image to create a precise selection of the earthy elements, which can then be refined using brush or gradient tools for greater accuracy. Once the ground is isolated, users can adjust exposure, contrast, color balance, and clarity specifically for the terrain. This is particularly useful for enhancing the texture of soil or rocks, correcting color casts caused by ambient light, or balancing the tonal relationship between the ground and the sky.

This technique is distinct from global adjustments or manual masking because it relies on semantic understanding of the scene's content. It is most effective in landscape and nature photography where the ground constitutes a significant portion of the frame and requires specific tonal or color treatment. The method supports non-destructive editing, allowing the mask to be edited or deleted at any stage of the workflow without altering the original image data.
