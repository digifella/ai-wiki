# Harness Engineering

[[concepts/execution-failures|Harness engineering]] represents a conceptual shift in [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] development methodology, emphasizing the infrastructure and orchestration systems surrounding [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] over the models themselves. Rather than focusing primarily on model selection and [[concepts/prompt-based-modeling|prompt engineering]] as the primary levers of AI system performance, this approach prioritizes the design of the execution environment—the "harness"—that manages how models are deployed, chained, monitored, and refined in production contexts.

## Core Premise

The fundamental claim underlying harness engineering is that system [[concepts/performance-gains|performance gains]] are increasingly derived from architectural decisions about how models are integrated and executed, rather than from incremental improvements to the models or prompts alone. This suggests that as [[concepts/foundational-models|foundational models]] become commoditized, competitive advantages and performance improvements will emerge from superior orchestration, error handling, [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] loops, and systematic refinement of the overall pipeline rather than from [[concepts/fine-tuning|fine-tuning]] individual components.

## Relationship to Existing Practices

Harness engineering builds upon and extends concepts from [[concepts/execution-orchestration|execution orchestration]] and systems engineering, repositioning them as primary concerns in [[concepts/ai-development|AI development]]. While prompt engineering focuses on optimizing inputs to a single model, harness engineering addresses the broader question of how multiple models, tools, human feedback, and monitoring systems work together as an integrated whole to produce reliable, adaptable [[concepts/ai-powered-applications|AI applications]].
