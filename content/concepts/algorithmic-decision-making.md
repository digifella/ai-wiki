# Algorithmic decision-making

The use of Automated Decision Systems (ADS) and mathematical [[concepts/models|models]] to automate conclusions, recommendations, or actions that affect human lives, often reducing the role of human oversight.

## Critical Dimensions
- **Algorithmic Bias**: Systematic and unfair discrimination resulting from biased [[concepts/training-data|training data]] or model [[concepts/design|design]].
- **Transparency and Explainability**: The "Black Box" problem, where the logic behind a decision is inaccessible to users or regulators.
- **Algorithmic [[concepts/accountability|Accountability]]**: The difficulty in assigning legal or ethical responsibility for errors or harms caused by automated processes.
- **[[concepts/automation|Automation]] Bias**: The human tendency to over-rely on or trust automated suggestions, even when they are erroneous.

## Case Studies & Failures
- **[[concepts/robodebt-scheme|Robodebt Scheme]]: Australia's Unlawful Algorithm Causing Deaths**
    - An automated system implemented by the Australian government in 2016 to identify and recover alleged welfare overpayments.
    - Utilized unlawful automated processes to issue debt notices.
    - Linked to significant human harm and documented deaths.
