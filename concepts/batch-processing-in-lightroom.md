# Batch Processing In Lightroom

[[concepts/batch-processing|Batch processing]] in [[concepts/lightroom|Lightroom]] refers to applying edits, [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]], and [[concepts/metadata|metadata]] changes to multiple photographs simultaneously rather than editing each image individually. This workflow capability is essential for photographers managing large shoots, product photography, or curated collections that require consistent styling or technical corrections across many images.

## Core Functionality

Lightroom enables batch processing through several methods. The most common approach uses the [[concepts/adobe-lightroom-develop-module|Develop module]]'s "Copy Settings" and "Paste Settings" features, allowing photographers to copy adjustments from one image and apply them to selected images. The "Auto [[concepts/tone|Tone]]" and "Auto [[concepts/exposure|Exposure]]" functions can also be applied to multiple selected photos at once. Additionally, the Sync Settings panel in the Develop module provides [[concepts/granular-control|granular control]] over which specific adjustments—such as exposure, white balance, or vibrance—are synchronized across selected images.

## Practical Applications

Batch processing streamlines workflows by reducing repetitive manual adjustments. It ensures visual [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] across image series, which is particularly important for editorial shoots, event photography, or product catalogs. Photographers can apply white balance corrections to entire shoots taken under the same lighting conditions, adjust exposure across similar scenes, or apply identical cropping and [[concepts/straightening|straightening]] to multiple images simultaneously. Batch operations also extend to metadata, enabling photographers to add [[concepts/keywords|keywords]], copyright information, or ratings to many images at once.

## Limitations and Considerations

While batch processing significantly improves efficiency, it works best when images share similar lighting and [[concepts/writing|composition]]. Highly varied shots may still require individual [[concepts/fine-tuning|fine-tuning]] after initial batch adjustments. The approach typically functions as a starting point for consistent styling rather than a complete replacement for selective editing, particularly for professional or gallery-quality work.
