# Polygonal Jointing

**Polygonal jointing** refers to the natural fracturing of rock masses into polygonal shapes, most commonly observed in basalt flows. This phenomenon is driven by [[concepts/thermal-contraction|thermal contraction]] during the cooling process of volcanic lava.

## Mechanism of Formation

The formation of these structures is a direct result of physical processes occurring as hot [[concepts/volcanic-rock|volcanic rock]] cools:

*   **Thermal Contraction:** As basaltic [[concepts/lava-flows|lava flows]] cool, they undergo significant volume reduction due to temperature drop.
*   **[[concepts/tensile-stress|Tensile Stress]]:** The contraction generates tensile stresses within the rock mass.
*   **Fracture Propagation:** To relieve this [[concepts/stress|stress]], the rock fractures. The geometry of these fractures tends toward hexagonal or polygonal patterns because this shape allows for the most efficient packing and stress distribution in a contracting plane.
*   **Column Development:** These fractures propagate vertically and horizontally, creating the characteristic columnar structures seen in nature.

## Key Factors

*   **Cooling Rate:** Uniform cooling promotes regular polygonal shapes; rapid or uneven cooling may result in irregular jointing.
*   **Lava [[concepts/writing|Composition]]:** Basalt is the most common host rock due to its [[concepts/viscosity|viscosity]] and cooling properties, though similar jointing can occur in other igneous rocks.
*   **[[concepts/flow|Flow]] Thickness:** Thicker lava flows typically produce taller and more distinct columns.
