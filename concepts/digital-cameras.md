# Digital Cameras

Digital cameras are electronic devices that capture and store images in digital format, replacing traditional film-based photography. They use image sensors to convert [[concepts/light|light]] into electrical signals, which are then processed and saved as digital files. Unlike film cameras, digital cameras allow photographers to view images immediately, adjust settings in real-time, and store thousands of images on reusable [[concepts/memory|memory]] cards.

## How Digital Cameras Work

At the core of every digital camera is an image sensor—either a CCD (Charge-Coupled Device) or CMOS (Complementary Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor) chip—that captures light through the camera's lens. When light strikes the sensor, it generates electrical charges proportional to the brightness and color of each pixel. These signals are processed by the camera's [[concepts/cpu|processor]], converted to [[concepts/digital-information|digital data]], and saved to the memory card in formats such as JPEG or RAW. This process happens almost instantaneously, enabling the photographer to review and delete images on the spot.

## Types and Applications

Digital cameras range from smartphones and compact point-and-shoot models to advanced DSLRs and mirrorless systems. Smartphone cameras have become the most commonly used type, offering convenience and computational photography features. Dedicated digital cameras are preferred by professionals and enthusiasts for their superior image quality, manual control options, and interchangeable lenses. Digital cameras are used across photography, filmmaking, scientific documentation, surveillance, and countless other fields where visual recording is essential.

## Advantages Over Film

The shift to [[concepts/smartphone-camera-apps|digital photography]] eliminated the need for film stock and darkroom processing, reducing both time and cost. [[concepts/digital-images|Digital images]] can be instantly transmitted, edited with software, and backed up across multiple devices. The ability to take unlimited shots without material costs has democratized photography, allowing people to [[concepts/scientific-experiment|experiment]] freely and develop their [[concepts/skills|skills]] more rapidly than was possible with film.
