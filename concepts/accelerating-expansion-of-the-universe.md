# Accelerating Expansion of the Universe

The **[[concepts/accelerating-universe|accelerating expansion]] of the universe** is the observation that the rate at which the universe is expanding is increasing over time. This phenomenon is attributed to **[[concepts/the-composition-of-the-universe-extends-beyond-what-is-directly-observable|Dark Energy]]**, a hypothetical form of energy that permeates all of space and exerts negative pressure.

## Current Status & Recent Findings (2026)

Recent data from the **Dark Energy [[concepts/survey|Survey]] (DES)** and the **Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI)** have prompted a reassessment of the standard cosmological model ($\Lambda$CDM).

- **Reassessment of Dark Energy:** New findings suggest that dark energy may not be a constant [[concepts/type-ia-supernovae|cosmological constant]] ($\Lambda$) but could be dynamic, evolving over time [[lab-notes/2026-07-22-Dark-Energy-DESDESI-Reassess-Universes-Accelerating-Expa|Dark Energy: DES/DESI Reassess Universe's Accelerating Expansion]].
- **Deviation from [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|Standard Model]]:** The data indicates potential deviations from the assumption that the universe has been expanding uniformly since the Big Bang, challenging previous consensus on the nature of cosmic acceleration.
- **Implications:** These results require updated models of **[[concepts/cosmology]]** to account for possible time-varying properties of dark energy.

## Key Concepts

- **Dark Energy**: The mysterious force driving the acceleration.
- **[[concepts/big-bang]]**: The initial event from which expansion began.
- **Cosmological Constant**: The traditional explanation for dark energy, now under scrutiny.
