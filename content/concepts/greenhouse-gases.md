# Greenhouse Gases

Greenhouse gases are atmospheric compounds that absorb and re-emit infrared radiation, trapping heat in the Earth's atmosphere and causing global warming. The primary greenhouse gases include carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), nitrous oxide (N₂O), and fluorinated gases. While some greenhouse gases occur naturally and maintain conditions suitable for life, concentrations have increased significantly since industrialization due to human activities such as fossil fuel combustion, [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]], and industrial processes.

## Atmospheric Effects and Air Quality

The accumulation of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere contributes to both global [[concepts/climate-change|climate change]] and local air quality degradation. High concentrations of these gases, particularly when combined with other pollutants, can reduce air quality and create hazardous conditions for human [[concepts/health|health]] and ecosystems. Greenhouse gas emissions often occur alongside the release of particulate matter and other air pollutants, compounding air quality impacts in industrialized and densely populated regions.

## Sources and Distribution

Major anthropogenic sources of greenhouse gases include energy production, transportation, manufacturing, and agriculture. The [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] of these emissions varies geographically, with developed nations historically responsible for larger cumulative emissions, though rapidly industrializing regions now contribute significantly to global totals. Understanding source attribution is essential for developing effective [[concepts/emissions-reduction|emissions reduction]] strategies [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] local, regional, and global [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]].
