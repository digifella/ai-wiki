## Evernote New Pricing Structure: User Reactions and Migration Guidance
**Clip title:** Reactions To The Price Increase  - Plus, How To Remove
[[concepts/files|Files]] from Evernote
**Author / channel:** [[entities/dave-edwards|Dave Edwards]]
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D04Z5s16QnE

### Summary
This video, presented by certified [[entities/evernote|Evernote]] expert [[entities/dave-edwards|Dave Edwards]], addresses
the widespread concern among Evernote users regarding its new tiered
[[concepts/pricing-structure|pricing structure]] and offers advice for those considering migrating their
data. Edwards, who is not affiliated with Evernote, provides an unbiased
perspective on the recent changes and offers practical guidance for users
evaluating their options.

The core of the issue revolves around Evernote's newly introduced "Starter"
and "Advanced" plans. The [[concepts/starter-plan|Starter plan]], priced at $8.25/month ($99/year),
is described as basic, with limitations on notes, notebooks, spaces,
attachments, and devices, notably lacking access to new [[concepts/ai-features|AI features]]. The
[[concepts/advanced-plan|Advanced plan]], at $20.83/month ($249.99/year), offers unlimited
capabilities and includes the [[concepts/ai-features|AI features]]. Edwards highlights that many
[[concepts/power-users|power users]], and even casual users who appreciate the new AI
functionalities, will likely find the [[entities/starter-plan|Starter plan]] insufficient and feel
compelled to upgrade to the significantly more expensive Advanced tier.

Edwards categorizes [[concepts/user-feedback|user feedback]] into four groups: casual users who like
AI but can't justify the cost; power users who face price concerns,
including retirees; "mad" users frustrated by successive price hikes since
Bending Spoons acquired Evernote; and a hypothetical group who value the
app and its new features and are accepting of the increased price. For
those contemplating a move, Edwards stresses key considerations: the
potential cost of a new app, whether it offers comparable features and ease
of use, and the "juice being worth the squeeze" regarding the effort of
migration. He shares his personal experience of attempting to use [[concepts/obsidian|Obsidian]]
as an alternative, finding its learning curve too high, which led to less
actual work and more setup time.

For users who decide to leave, Edwards provides detailed [[concepts/instructions|instructions]] on
how to export data from Evernote without hassle. He clarifies that while
exporting individual notes has a 100-note limit, the trick is to export
entire notebooks, which circumvents this restriction. Users should select
the ENEX (Evernote's proprietary format) option, as many other productivity
apps are capable of importing these files, along with various note
attributes like creation date, tags, and source URLs. He advises [[concepts/testing|testing]] a
new app with a small set of notes for a few months before committing to a
full migration, and to consider the new app's long-term viability and ease
of [[concepts/data-export|data export]].

Ultimately, [[entities/dave|Dave]] Edwards concludes that for his own [[concepts/workflow|workflow]], Evernote's
features, ease of use, and robust system justify the continued investment,
and he plans to remain an Evernote user. He encourages viewers to carefully
weigh their individual needs, budget, and the potential effort of switching
before making a decision. He also invites viewers to share their thoughts
and experiences and offers one-on-one coaching to help users optimize their
Evernote setup for maximum productivity.
