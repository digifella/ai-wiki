# Habits

Habits are recurring behaviors and practices that become automatic through repetition, playing a fundamental role in personal [[concepts/health|wellbeing]] and daily functioning. They represent the bridge between intention and sustained action, forming the foundation of how individuals [[concepts/structure|structure]] their lives and make progress toward their goals. Understanding how habits form, persist, and can be modified is central to improving health, productivity, and overall [[concepts/quality-of-life|quality of life]].

## Formation and Automation

Habits develop through repeated cycles of cue, routine, and reward, gradually becoming ingrained in neural pathways until they require minimal conscious effort to execute. This automaticity is both advantageous and challenging: established habits can support wellbeing by removing decision fatigue from beneficial behaviors like [[concepts/exercise|exercise]] or meditation, but entrenched habits can also perpetuate harmful patterns. The process of habit formation typically requires consistent practice over weeks or months, though the timeline varies significantly based on individual differences, behavior complexity, and environmental factors.

## Habit-Based Wellbeing Frameworks

Researchers and practitioners like [[entities/gretchen-rubin|Gretchen Rubin]] have developed frameworks for understanding how habits contribute to [[concepts/happiness|happiness]] and wellbeing. Rubin's work emphasizes that small, consistent practices—from maintaining [[concepts/daily-routines|daily routines]] to implementing simple rules like the One-Minute Rule for managing clutter—create cumulative improvements in life satisfaction. This approach focuses on practical, habit-based interventions rather than major overhauls, recognizing that sustainable wellbeing emerges from the daily choices and routines people maintain over time.
