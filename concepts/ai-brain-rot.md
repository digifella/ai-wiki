# AI Brain Rot

**AI Brain Rot** refers to the hypothesized degradation of human cognitive faculties, critical [[concepts/human-cognition|thinking]], and foundational [[concepts/skills|skills]] resulting from over-reliance on [[concepts/ai-technologies|Artificial Intelligence]] systems. This phenomenon parallels concerns regarding [[concepts/algorithmic-decision-making|automation bias]] and [[concepts/skill|skill]] atrophy in other domains.

## Core Mechanisms

- **[[concepts/human-responsibility|Cognitive Offloading]]**: Shifting mental workload from human [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] to AI generation, potentially weakening neural pathways associated with [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]].
- **Illusion of Competence**: Users may perceive AI-generated outputs as their own understanding, [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] gaps in actual knowledge.
- **Dependency [[concepts/loops|Loops]]**: Reduced practice leads to decreased [[concepts/excellence|proficiency]], which in turn increases reliance on AI assistance.

## Domain-Specific Impacts

### Software Development
Recent analysis highlights a tension between efficiency and skill [[concepts/storing|retention]] in [[concepts/coding|coding]] workflows.

- **[[concepts/speed|Speed]] vs. [[concepts/skill-erosion|Skill Erosion]]**: While [[concepts/ai-tools|AI tools]] accelerate [[concepts/code-generation|code generation]], there is a risk of diminishing fundamental programming intuition and [[concepts/debugging|debugging]] capabilities.
