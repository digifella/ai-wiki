# Pressure Vessel Design

**Pressure vessel design** involves the [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] of closed [[concepts/containerization-technology|containers]] capable of holding gases or liquids at a pressure significantly different from the ambient pressure. Key considerations include material selection, [[concepts/stress|stress]] analysis, fatigue life, and safety factors.

## Core Principles
- **Stress Analysis**: Calculation of hoop stress, longitudinal stress, and radial stress to ensure [[concepts/payload-structure|structural integrity]] under internal or external pressure.
- **Material Selection**: Choice of materials based on strength, ductility, corrosion resistance, and fatigue properties (e.g., steel, titanium, carbon fiber composites).
- **Safety Factors**: Application of multipliers to theoretical failure loads to account for uncertainties in manufacturing, material defects, and operational conditions.
- **Regulatory [[concepts/compliance|Compliance]]**: Adherence to standards such as ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code, ISO 11495, or specific industry regulations.

## Failure Modes
- **Buckling**: Instability failure common in thin-walled vessels under external pressure.
- **Fatigue**: Progressive structural damage due to cyclic loading.
- **Brittle Fracture**: Sudden failure with little plastic deformation, often temperature-dependent.
- **Corrosion**: Material degradation leading to wall thinning or stress corrosion cracking.

## Case Study: OceanGate Titan
Recent investigations into the [[concepts/oceangate-titan]] [[concepts/submersible-implosion|submersible implosion]] highlight critical failures in engineering processes rather than inherent material flaws.

- **[[concepts/engineering-process|Engineering Process]] Failures**: The primary cause was identified as an inadequate engineering process, lacking rigorous validation and [[concepts/document-review|peer review]] typical of certified pressure vessel design [[lab-notes/2026-07-09-OceanGate-Titan-Implosion-Inadequate-Engineering-and-Man|OceanGate Titan Implosion: Inadequate Engineering and Manufacturing Failures]].
- **Manufacturing Deficiencies**: Issues were found in the manufacturing [[concepts/quality-control|quality control]], specifically regarding the [[concepts/honesty|integrity]] of the carbon fiber composite hull.
- **Material Misconception**: The failure was not due to the carbon fiber material itself being unsuitable, but rather how it was engineered and manufactured without sufficient safety margins or testing protocols.
- **Regulatory Bypass**: The vessel operated outside standard regulatory frameworks, avoiding mandatory third-party certification and inspection.
