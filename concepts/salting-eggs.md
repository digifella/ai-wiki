# Salting Eggs

Salting eggs is a fundamental cooking technique that affects the texture and tenderness of scrambled eggs and other egg dishes. When salt is added to raw eggs, the sodium chloride dissolves and begins to denature the proteins in the egg white and yolk. This chemical process alters how the proteins coagulate during cooking, directly influencing the final dish's texture—from tender and custard-like to firm and separated.

## Timing of Salt Addition

The timing of salt addition significantly impacts the results. Adding salt immediately after cracking eggs initiates early protein denaturation, which can lead to a creamier, more tender texture in scrambled eggs. Conversely, adding salt just before cooking produces a different result. Delaying salt addition until the eggs are already in the pan may yield slightly different textural qualities. America's Test Kitchen research has demonstrated that early salting—mixing salt into the raw egg mixture before cooking—produces consistently tender scrambled eggs by allowing the proteins to denature gradually rather than suddenly contracting under heat.

## Practical Application

For optimal results in scrambled eggs, salt the eggs at least five to ten minutes before cooking, or add it immediately when cracking them into a bowl. This allows sufficient time for the salt to dissolve and begin the protein-denaturing process. The amount of salt should be moderate, as excessive salt can cause unwanted effects on texture. Understanding this relationship between salt, protein structure, and heat is essential for achieving the desired consistency in scrambled eggs and similar preparations.
