# Mammoths

Mammoths were large terrestrial mammals of the genus *Mammuthus* that became extinct thousands of years ago. The woolly mammoth, the most well-known species, inhabited cold regions during the Pleistocene epoch and was adapted to ice age climates with long hair and layers of insulating fat. [[concepts/fossil-evidence|Fossil evidence]] and preserved remains found in permafrost have provided extensive knowledge about their anatomy, diet, and behavior.

## Cloning Research

In recent decades, mammoths have attracted significant scientific interest as potential subjects for [[concepts/de-extinction|de-extinction]] through cloning. A notable Japanese research program spanning approximately 20 years investigated serial cloning as a method to recreate mammoths using preserved genetic material. The study concluded in 2026 that serial cloning of mammoths was genetically unviable, indicating that the repeated cloning process introduced insurmountable genetic complications that made successful resurrection of the species impractical through this approach.
