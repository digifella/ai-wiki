## Changing Photo Mood: Strategic Two-Color Grading Techniques
**Clip title:** The 2 Color Trick That Changes the Whole Mood of a Photo

### Summary
This video provides a clear and practical guide on how to use [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|color grading]] to evoke emotion and add depth to your photographs. The presenter emphasizes that impactful [[concepts/photo-enhancement|photo editing]] isn't just about using presets or applying generic adjustments, but rather about consciously deciding how a photo should "feel" and then applying color strategically to achieve that mood. The [[concepts/tutorial|tutorial]] focuses on two primary tools available in [[concepts/on-device-editing|Adobe Camera Raw]] (and [[concepts/lightroom|Lightroom]]) – the [[concepts/color-grading-panel|Color Grading panel]] and [[concepts/curves|Curves]] – demonstrating how simple techniques can profoundly alter an image's perception.

The first technique demonstrated is the Color Grading panel, which offers three distinct [[concepts/color-wheels|color wheels]] for midtones, [[concepts/shadows|shadows]], and highlights. Each wheel allows precise control over Hue, Saturation, and Lightness for its respective tonal range. The presenter illustrates a popular "split toning" method, applying cool blue tones to the shadows while introducing warm yellow-orange tones to the highlights. This not only adds visual depth, with cool tones receding and warm tones advancing, but also creates a more emotionally resonant image. The key is to apply these adjustments subtly to maintain a natural look, avoiding over-saturation.

Following this, the video introduces using Curves for color grading, a versatile tool available in virtually any [[concepts/photo-editing|photo editing]] software. Unlike global adjustments, Curves allow manipulation of individual RGB color channels (Red, Green, Blue). By adjusting the curve for each channel, one can add or subtract its complementary color in specific tonal areas (e.g., raising the blue curve to add blue to shadows, or lowering it to add yellow to highlights). To further enhance [[concepts/accuracy|precision]] and isolate adjustments, the presenter then introduces Luminance Range masks. These masks allow color grading, whether from the Color Grading panel or Curves, to be applied only to specific [[concepts/light|light]] or dark areas of the image, preventing unwanted spills and significantly increasing the separation and depth within the photograph.

In conclusion, the video underscores that understanding [[concepts/color-theory|color theory]] and intentionally applying color adjustments are paramount for compelling photo editing. By utilizing tools like the Color Grading panel, Curves, and Luminance Range masks, photographers can move beyond merely "fixing" an image to actively shaping its emotional impact and visual depth. The techniques shown empower users to make deliberate color decisions, transforming ordinary shots into evocative visual stories, ultimately resulting in photos that truly resonate with the viewer.
