# Galaxy Formation

Galaxy formation is the process by which galaxies assemble and evolve in the universe, beginning shortly after the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]] and continuing to the present day. The formation and early development of galaxies remains a central question in [[concepts/cosmology|cosmology]], as observations reveal galaxies existed when the universe was only a few hundred million years old—earlier than many theoretical models predicted. Understanding how these first galaxies formed, what composed them, and how they grew over [[concepts/cosmic-time|cosmic time]] requires both [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|theoretical frameworks]] and high-[[concepts/solution|resolution]] [[concepts/empirical-evidence|observational data]].

## Early Universe and Population III Stars

The earliest galaxies are believed to have formed from the [[concepts/gravitational-collapse|gravitational collapse]] of [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] [[concepts/halos|halos]], which provided the potential wells necessary for [[concepts/baryonic-matter|baryonic matter]] to accumulate. Recent observations by the [[concepts/james-webb-space-telescope|James Webb Space Telescope]] (JWST) of the galaxy [[concepts/gn-z11-galaxy|GN-z11]] have provided critical evidence regarding the [[concepts/writing|composition]] of these early structures. Specifically, data from GN-z11 suggests the presence of [[concepts/first-generation-of-stars|Population III stars]], which are hypothetical [[concepts/population-iii-stars|first-generation stars]] composed almost entirely of hydrogen and helium, lacking heavier elements.

## Evolution and Growth

Galaxies did not form in [[concepts/disconnection|isolation]] but through a hierarchical process of mergers and accretion. As the universe expanded and cooled, gas flows along cosmic web filaments fed these nascent structures, triggering star formation and driving chemical enrichment. Over [[concepts/cosmic-time|cosmic time]], interactions between galaxies led to the diverse morphological types observed today, from ellipticals to spirals, shaped by both internal dynamics and external environmental factors.
