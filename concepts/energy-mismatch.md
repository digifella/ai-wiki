# Energy Mismatch

**Energy Mismatch** refers to the temporal and spatial discrepancy between energy generation and consumption. In renewable-heavy [[concepts/grids|grids]], this manifests as surplus production during peak generation periods (e.g., midday solar, windy nights) and deficits during peak demand or low-generation periods. This mismatch necessitates robust Energy [[entities/storage|Storage]] solutions to maintain grid stability and maximize renewable utilization.

## Key Challenges
- **Intermittency:** Solar and wind sources are non-dispatchable, leading to volatility in supply.
- **Seasonal Variance:** Annual generation patterns often do not align with heating/cooling demand cycles.
- **Grid [[concepts/inertia|Inertia]]:** Reduced rotational inertia from fossil fuel displacement affects frequency [[concepts/regulation|regulation]].

## Mitigation Strategies
- **Short-term Storage:** Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) for frequency regulation and daily shifting.
- **Long-term Storage:** Solutions capable of [[concepts/storing|storing]] energy for weeks or months to address seasonal mismatches.
- **[[concepts/demand-response|Demand Response]]:** Shifting consumption to match generation peaks.

## Recent Developments: Thermochemical Storage
Emerging technologies aim to solve the seasonal storage gap with high efficiency and minimal loss.

- **[[entities/season|Season]]'s Technology:** A Swiss-developed [[concepts/solution|solution]] utilizing [[concepts/thermochemical-heat-storage|thermochemical heat storage]] to store surplus renewable energy for months.
- **Mechanism:** Uses reversible chemical reactions to store heat, offering higher [[concepts/density|density]] and lower self-discharge rates compared to sensible heat storage.
- **Relevance:** Directly addresses the Energy Mismatch by enabling long-duration storage of excess summer solar/wind energy for winter heating demands.
