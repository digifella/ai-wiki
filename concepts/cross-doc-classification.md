# Cross Doc Classification

Cross Doc Classification is a feature within the [[concepts/ollama-first-model|Portal Intelligence Layer]] designed to enable the systematic categorization of documents across multiple sources. The system analyzes document content and [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] to establish appropriate categories, allowing users to apply consistent classification schemes regardless of the document's origin or format. This capability facilitates better organization and [[concepts/document-retrieval|retrieval]] of information from diverse document sets by standardizing how documents are tagged and indexed.

The implementation involved the integration of ten specific tasks into the Portal Intelligence Layer. These tasks were merged to ensure the system could effectively process and classify documents from various inputs. The resulting architecture supports the analysis of content and structural relationships to determine accurate categorization, ensuring that the classification process is robust and scalable across different data types.

By standardizing the tagging and [[concepts/data-indexing|indexing]] processes, Cross Doc Classification improves the [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] of [[concepts/knowledge-bases|information retrieval]]. Users can rely on a unified classification scheme that transcends individual document formats or source systems. This integration enhances the overall utility of the Portal Intelligence Layer by providing a reliable method for organizing complex, multi-source document collections.
