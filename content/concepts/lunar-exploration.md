# Lunar Exploration

Lunar exploration refers to scientific and [[concepts/crewed-missions|crewed missions]] undertaken to study the Moon and establish human presence in lunar orbit and on the lunar surface. Modern lunar exploration [[concepts/software|programs]] build upon decades of robotic missions and Apollo-era achievements, integrating advanced [[concepts/spacecraft-systems|spacecraft systems]] and updated operational procedures. These missions serve both scientific objectives—such as studying lunar [[concepts/geology|geology]], water ice deposits, and radiation environments—and programmatic goals of developing technologies for sustained [[concepts/human-spaceflight|human spaceflight]] beyond Earth orbit.

## Contemporary Programs

The [[concepts/artemis-program|Artemis program]] represents the current focal point of crewed lunar exploration efforts. [[entities/artemis-ii|Artemis II]] serves as a crewed test mission of the Artemis [[concepts/architecture|architecture]], conducting a [[concepts/human-lunar-flyby|human lunar flyby]] to validate spacecraft systems and operational procedures before subsequent landing missions. The mission tests the [[entities/orion|Orion spacecraft]], which carries the crew, alongside supporting launch and deep space infrastructure. Through simulated operations and in-flight activities, Artemis II gathers critical data on crew performance, vehicle systems behavior, and mission procedures in the actual deep [[concepts/vacuum-of-space|space environment]] rather than relying solely on ground-based [[concepts/testing|testing]].

## Mission Architecture

Lunar exploration missions require integrated systems spanning launch vehicles, crew capsules, and in some cases, lunar landers. The Artemis architecture incorporates the Space Launch System for initial launch capability and planned [[concepts/integration|integration]] with additional elements for surface operations. Crews conduct extensive [[concepts/training|training]] on spacecraft systems and emergency procedures before flight, with missions generating operational data that informs refinements to [[concepts/vehicle-design|vehicle design]] and mission planning. This iterative approach—combining ground [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] with actual spaceflight experience—remains essential to developing reliable systems for human lunar operations.
