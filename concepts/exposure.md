Exposure refers to the total amount of light captured by a camera's sensor or film, which determines the overall brightness and visibility of an image. It is a fundamental concept in photography, as proper exposure is essential for creating clear, well-balanced visuals. Depending on the quantity of light that reaches the recording medium during capture, an image may be correctly exposed, underexposed (too dark), or overexposed (too bright).

## The Exposure Triangle

The exposure of a photograph is controlled through three interdependent variables known as the exposure triangle: aperture, shutter speed, and ISO sensitivity. Aperture controls the size of the lens opening, regulating the volume of light entering the camera while also affecting depth of field. Shutter speed dictates the duration for which the sensor is exposed to light, influencing motion blur and clarity. ISO measures the sensor's sensitivity to light, where higher values allow for shooting in darker conditions but may introduce digital noise. Adjusting one variable typically requires compensating with another to maintain a consistent exposure level.

## Digital Adjustment

In photo editing software, exposure adjustments allow for the correction or creative manipulation of an image's brightness after capture. This process involves altering the luminance values of pixels to recover details in shadows or highlights that were lost during the initial capture. While digital tools can simulate the effects of the exposure triangle, they cannot fully recover data from severely underexposed or overexposed areas due to the limitations of dynamic range. Proper exposure at the time of capture remains the most effective method for preserving image quality and detail.
