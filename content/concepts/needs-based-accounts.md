# Needs Based Accounts

Needs Based Accounts represent a philosophical framework for understanding and [[concepts/accounts-of-how-you-feel|defining wellbeing]] by grounding it in human needs rather than subjective preferences or external measures. This approach asks what constitutes genuine [[concepts/health|wellbeing]] by examining which needs, when satisfied, contribute meaningfully to human flourishing. Rather than treating [[concepts/wellbeing|wellbeing]] as purely subjective or dependent on individual desires, needs-based accounts attempt to identify objective or semi-objective criteria that apply across different contexts and individuals.

The framework addresses a practical challenge in wellbeing research and policy: how to distinguish between what people say makes them happy or satisfied in the moment and what actually contributes to their genuine wellbeing over time. A needs-based approach suggests that some needs—such as autonomy, competence, relatedness, or basic material [[concepts/security|security]]—have intrinsic importance to wellbeing, regardless of whether individuals consciously prioritize them or feel satisfied without them.

[[entities/kate-laffan|Kate Laffan]]'s work in this area explores how [[concepts/philosophical-accounts-of-wellbeing|philosophical accounts of wellbeing]] can inform practical [[concepts/software|applications]], including policy decisions and wellbeing measurement. Her research examines both the theoretical foundations of needs-based frameworks and their real-world implications, investigating how different conceptions of human needs shape our understanding of what societies should prioritize to genuinely improve people's lives.
