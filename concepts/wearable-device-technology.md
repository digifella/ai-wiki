# Wearable Device Technology

## Overview
Wearable Device Technology refers to electronic devices worn on the body that collect, process, and transmit [[concepts/health|health]], fitness, or environmental data. These devices serve as critical [[concepts/nodes|nodes]] in the broader [[concepts/digital-health]] ecosystem, enabling continuous data streams for [[concepts/remote-health-monitoring|Remote Patient Monitoring]] (RPM) and personal health management.

## Key Components
- **Sensors**: Biometric (heart rate, SpO2, ECG), environmental (GPS, ambient [[concepts/light|light]]), and activity trackers (accelerometers).
- **Connectivity**: [[concepts/bluetooth-technology|Bluetooth Low Energy]] (BLE), Wi-Fi, and Cellular (LTE-M/NB-IoT) for Data Transmission.
- **Power Management**: Battery [[concepts/optimization-guide|optimization strategies]] for continuous wear.

## Integration with Clinical Ecosystems
Recent analysis from 2020 highlights the critical role of [[concepts/internet-of-things|wearables]] in Remote Patient Monitoring and [[concepts/telehealth|Virtual Care]]  A Deep. Key integration points include:

- **EMR Integration**: Wearables bridge the gap between point-of-care data and [[concepts/electronic-health-records|Electronic Medical Records]], ensuring Data [[concepts/flow|Flow]] [[concepts/continuity|continuity]].
- **[[concepts/technical-infrastructure|Technical Infrastructure]]**: Relies on robust Core Technologies for Data Collection and [[concepts/secure|secure]] [[entities/storage|storage]].
- **[[concepts/allied-health|Allied Health]] Perspective**: Enhances monitoring capabilities for non-physician providers, allowing for early intervention based on real-time metrics.
- **Challenges**:
	- **[[concepts/data-integrity|Data Quality]]**: Variability in sensor accuracy affects [[concepts/clinical-reasoning|clinical decision-making]].
	- **[[concepts/security|Security]] Considerations**: Protection of sensitive health data during transmission and storage.
	- **Current [[concepts/adoption|Adoption]]**: While growing, integration barriers remain significant in many [[concepts/health-care|healthcare]] settings.
