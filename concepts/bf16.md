# Bf16

Bf16 (Brain Float 16) is a 16-bit floating-point format developed by Google Brain that balances [[concepts/digit-precision|numerical precision]] with [[concepts/algorithm-efficiency|computational efficiency]]. It utilizes a structure of one sign bit, eight exponent bits, and seven mantissa bits. This design preserves the same [[concepts/dynamic-range|dynamic range]] as the standard IEEE 754 float32 format while significantly reducing the [[concepts/4gb-memory|memory footprint]] and computational cost required for processing.

The format achieves this efficiency by truncating the mantissa of float32 rather than rounding it. This approach maintains the exponent range necessary for stable training and [[concepts/ai-inference|inference]] in large [[concepts/ai-models|neural networks]], preventing issues such as overflow or underflow that can occur with lower-precision formats like FP16. Consequently, Bf16 allows for faster matrix operations without the need for complex loss scaling techniques often required in other mixed-precision strategies.

In the context of [[concepts/demystifying-llms|large language models]], Bf16 has become a standard for both training and deployment. Its ability to maintain numerical stability while halving the [[concepts/storage-bandwidth|memory bandwidth]] requirements makes it particularly effective for accelerating [[concepts/ai-model-optimization|model compression]] and inference tasks. This efficiency enables the handling of larger batch sizes and more complex architectures on hardware optimized for 16-bit operations.
