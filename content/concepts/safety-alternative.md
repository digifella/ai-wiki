# Safety Alternative

A safety alternative is a product, process, or method designed to replace a hazardous or unsafe option with a safer one while maintaining or improving the original functionality. Safety alternatives address risks inherent to existing solutions—whether those risks involve injury, contamination, environmental damage, or other harms—by reimagining how a task or need can be fulfilled without compromising performance or usability.

## Characteristics

Effective safety alternatives balance multiple priorities. They must eliminate or substantially reduce the identified hazard, perform as well as or better than the unsafe option they replace, and be feasible to implement [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] scale. The transition from an unsafe standard to a safer alternative typically requires engineering work, [[concepts/testing|testing]], and often changes to manufacturing or [[concepts/consumer-behavior|consumer behavior]].

## Example: Grill Brush Innovation

The [[entities/smarter-scrubber|Smarter Scrubber]] exemplifies a safety alternative developed to address hazards from wire bristle grill brushes, which can shed bristles into food. Researcher [[entities/destin-sandlin|Destin Sandlin]] spent four years developing and manufacturing a safer alternative in the [[entities/united-states|United States]], demonstrating that a safety-focused redesign could be produced competitively while eliminating the contamination risk. This project combined product [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] with practical manufacturing considerations.
