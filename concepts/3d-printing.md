# 3D Printing

## Overview
3D printing, also known as additive manufacturing, is the construction of a three-dimensional object from a CAD model or a digital 3D model. It involves layering material to build objects with high [[concepts/accuracy|precision]], enabling the fabrication of complex geometries that are difficult or impossible to achieve with traditional subtractive manufacturing methods.

## Applications in Horology
The field of [[concepts/watch-mechanism|Watch-Mechanism]] design has increasingly adopted 3D printing for prototyping and functional models, particularly for complex timekeeping components.

### Tourbillon Mechanism
A Tourbillon is a complication designed to improve the accuracy of mechanical watches by counteracting the effects of [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]] on the Escapement and Balance-Wheel.

*   **Purpose**: The mechanism rotates the balance wheel and pallet fork assembly to average out positional errors caused by gravity, ensuring consistent timekeeping.
*   **[[concepts/gravity-compensation|Gravity Compensation]]**: By continuously changing the orientation of the oscillating system, the tourbillon mitigates the variance in rate that occurs when a watch is in different positions (e.g., dial up vs. crown down).
*   **3D-Printed Models**: Recent developments include the creation of functional or demonstrative [[concepts/gyro-tourbillon]] models using additive manufacturing. These models serve as educational tools to visualize the "heart of a watch" and demonstrate the intricate interplay of gears and springs.
*   **Complexity**: The tourbillon is considered one of the most complex [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] in watchmaking, requiring precise alignment and miniaturization that 3D printing can now simulate effectively.
