# Supersonic Travel

**Supersonic travel** refers to transportation moving at speeds exceeding the speed of sound (Mach 1). While historically dominated by military applications, recent efforts focus on reviving commercial passenger travel to reduce transcontinental flight times.

## Commercial Supersonic Revival

The industry is currently undergoing a renaissance driven by private aerospace companies aiming to overcome the economic and environmental hurdles that grounded the Concorde and Tupolev Tu-144.

### Key Players and Projects

*   **[[entities/boom-supersonic|Boom Supersonic]]**: The leading contender in the current market, developing the **[[concepts/overture-airliner|Overture]]** airliner.
    *   **Engine Development**: Focus on the "SuperPower" jet engine, designed to meet strict noise and emissions regulations.
    *   **Commercial Ambitions**: Aiming to launch commercial supersonic passenger flights, leveraging advancements in aerodynamics and propulsion.
    *   **Technical Progress**: Recent developments in engine symmetry and performance metrics are critical to the viability of the Overture program.
    *   For detailed technical analysis, see [[lab-notes/2026-09-05-Boom-Supersonic-Overture-Symphony-Engine-Development-and|Boom Supersonic Overture: Symphony Engine Development and Commercial Supersonic Flight Ambitions]].

### Challenges

*   **[[concepts/sonic-boom|Sonic Boom]]**: Mitigating the acoustic shockwave over land remains a primary regulatory and engineering hurdle.
*   **Fuel Efficiency**: Supersonic flight typically consumes significantly more fuel per passenger than subsonic alternatives.
*   **Noise Regulations**: Strict airport noise limits require advanced engine technology to ensure community acceptance.
