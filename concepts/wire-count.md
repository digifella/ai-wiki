# Wire Count

Wire count refers to the number of individual electrical conductors present within a [[concepts/usb-c-connector|USB-C cable]]. While [[concepts/usb-30|USB-C]] connectors feature 24 pins, manufacturers do not uniformly implement all available connections. The actual number of wires inside a cable—typically ranging from 4 to 24 depending on the cable's intended function—determines what operations that cable can perform. This variation exists because different [[concepts/scenarios|use cases]] require different pin configurations, and full implementation adds manufacturing cost.

## Functional Implications

The wire count directly affects a cable's capabilities. A minimal-wire cable might carry only power delivery or basic data signals, while fully-wired cables support simultaneous high-[[concepts/speed|speed]] data transmission and power delivery. Cables with incomplete wiring cannot access certain pins, which can prevent them from functioning with specific devices or adapters. Poorly designed cables with inadequate wire counts may also lack proper grounding or shielding, compromising safety and signal [[concepts/honesty|integrity]].
