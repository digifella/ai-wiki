# Photo Culling

Photo culling is the process of reviewing and selecting photographs from a larger set to identify the best or most usable images for a project. It is a standard workflow step in photography that occurs after a shoot and before editing or final delivery. During culling, photographers evaluate images based on technical criteria such as sharpness and exposure, as well as subjective factors including composition, subject matter, and alignment with artistic intent.

## Manual and Assisted Culling

Traditionally, culling has been performed manually, with photographers reviewing each image individually and making selection decisions. This approach is time-intensive, particularly when dealing with large shoots that may contain hundreds or thousands of images. Modern software tools, notably Adobe Lightroom, have introduced AI-assisted culling features that can accelerate this process. These tools analyze images for technical quality and sometimes flagged issues, helping photographers prioritize which images warrant closer review. The photographer retains final selection authority, but the automation reduces the initial sorting burden.

## Workflow Context

Photo culling fits into the broader photography workflow as a critical intermediary step. By identifying and removing technically flawed or redundant images early, photographers reduce the volume of images requiring detailed editing work. This efficiency gain allows more time and attention to be spent on post-processing the selected keepers, ultimately improving the quality of the final deliverables.
