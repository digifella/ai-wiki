# Spaceflight Simulation

Spaceflight simulation encompasses the computational and operational recreation of spacecraft systems and crew procedures to evaluate mission performance and readiness. In NASA's Artemis program, simulations serve as critical validation tools for crew operations protocols, equipment responses, and emergency procedures before actual crewed missions. These simulations combine hardware-in-the-loop testing, software modeling, and human factors analysis to ensure crew familiarity with vehicle systems and procedural accuracy under nominal and off-nominal conditions.

## Artemis II Application

For Artemis II, simulations focus on validating crew operations procedures specific to the Orion spacecraft. These include ascent sequences, on-orbit operations, abort scenarios, and return entry procedures. Simulation environments replicate the crew cabin layout, control interface responses, and system state changes that astronauts will encounter during the mission. This training establishes baseline competency and identifies procedural gaps or design issues before flight.

## Integration with Mission Architecture

Simulations are integrated throughout the mission development lifecycle, from early design phases through final crew certification. They support verification of spacecraft systems performance, validation of crew procedures, and assessment of human-system interaction. Results from simulation campaigns inform design modifications, procedure refinement, and crew training content, reducing both technical and operational risk for crewed lunar missions.
