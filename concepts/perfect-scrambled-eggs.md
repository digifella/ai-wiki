# Perfect Scrambled Eggs

Perfect scrambled eggs refer to a culinary technique designed to produce a creamy, custard-like texture characterized by small, delicate curds. This outcome stands in contrast to the rubbery, dry, or watery results often caused by improper heat management or excessive cooking times. The quality of the final dish depends heavily on controlling protein coagulation, a process that initiates at relatively low temperatures: egg whites begin to set at approximately 63°C (145°F), while yolks start to coagulate around 65°C (149°F).

## Temperature and Timing

The primary method for achieving the desired consistency involves cooking at low to medium-low heat. By maintaining a gentle temperature, the proteins denature and coagulate slowly, allowing moisture to remain trapped within the curds rather than being squeezed out. Rapid heating causes proteins to tighten quickly, expelling water and resulting in a dry, tough texture. Continuous, gentle stirring during the cooking process ensures even heat distribution and prevents the formation of large, rubbery clumps.

## Finishing and Resting

The cooking process should be halted just before the eggs appear fully set, as residual heat will continue to cook them after removal from the heat source. Adding a small amount of cold butter or cream at the end can help lower the temperature slightly and enrich the mouthfeel. Allowing the eggs to rest for a brief moment before serving ensures that the texture remains stable and the desired creamy consistency is preserved.
