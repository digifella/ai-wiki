# Quantum Computing

## Overview
[[entities/quantum-computing|Quantum computing]] leverages quantum mechanical phenomena such as superposition and entanglement to process information. While [[entities/theoretically-media|theoretically]] capable of solving specific problems exponentially faster than [[concepts/classical-computers|classical computers]], the field [[concepts/faces|faces]] significant practical and theoretical hurdles.

## Critical Assessment & Practical Failures
Recent critical analyses highlight a disconnect between industry optimism and [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] reality. Key concerns include:

*   **Overhype vs. Reality:** Persistent optimism often obscures the severe limitations of current hardware, particularly regarding error rates and scalability Quantum Computing Overhype and Practical Failures: A Critical Assessment.
*   **Hardware Instability:** [[concepts/qubits|Qubits]] remain highly susceptible to decoherence, requiring extreme environmental controls that limit practical deployment.
*   **[[concepts/error-correction|Error Correction]] Bottlenecks:** The overhead required for [[concepts/quantum-error-correction|quantum error correction]] currently demands more physical qubits than are practically available for useful [[concepts/computation|computation]].
*   **Limited Utility:** For most real-[[entities/earth|world]] applications, classical [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]] remain superior or competitive, with [[concepts/quantum-advantage|quantum advantage]] restricted to narrow, specific [[concepts/scenarios|use cases]].
