# Sugar

Sugar has been consumed by humans for thousands of years, with evidence of sugarcane cultivation dating back to ancient India and Papua New Guinea. Early civilizations extracted juice from sugarcane and boiled it to create crystalline sugar through a labor-intensive process that remained localized to specific regions for centuries. The substance was initially rare and expensive in Europe and the Middle East, where it was treated as a luxury good and medicinal compound rather than an everyday foodstuff.

## Medieval Trade and Early Colonization

Arab traders and later Venetian merchants played crucial roles in introducing sugar to European markets during the medieval period. Production gradually expanded to Mediterranean islands and North Africa, but remained limited in scale. The real transformation began in the 15th and 16th centuries when European colonizers established sugarcane plantations in the Caribbean and Brazil, exploiting newly "discovered" territories for large-scale production.

## Sugar and Enslavement

The dramatic increase in sugar production became inseparable from the transatlantic slave trade. European colonizers, seeking cheap labor to work vast plantations under brutal conditions, forcibly enslaved millions of Africans. Sugar became one of the most profitable commodities of the colonial economy, generating enormous wealth for European merchants and planters while directly funding and perpetuating systems of human enslavement. By the 17th and 18th centuries, sugar had transformed from a rare luxury into a widely consumed commodity in Europe and North America, built fundamentally on the exploitation and suffering of enslaved peoples.
