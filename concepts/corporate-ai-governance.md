# Corporate Ai Governance

Corporate AI Governance encompasses the frameworks, policies, and oversight mechanisms organizations implement to manage artificial intelligence systems within enterprise environments. These structures are designed to address risks associated with both officially sanctioned AI deployments and the informal, unauthorized use of AI tools by employees, a phenomenon known as Shadow AI. Effective governance ensures that AI adoption aligns with organizational goals while mitigating potential security, compliance, and ethical liabilities.

A primary challenge in this domain is the lack of visibility into unapproved AI usage. Employees often utilize external generative AI services for productivity, creating data privacy vulnerabilities and intellectual property risks that bypass traditional IT security controls. Governance strategies must therefore extend beyond managing internal models to include monitoring and regulating the broader ecosystem of AI tools accessed by the workforce.

Organizations typically address these issues by establishing clear usage policies, implementing technical controls such as data loss prevention systems, and conducting regular audits of AI interactions. Frameworks like those promoted by IBM emphasize the need for a centralized governance body that can standardize risk assessments and ensure that all AI applications, whether sanctioned or shadow, adhere to regulatory requirements and corporate ethics standards.
