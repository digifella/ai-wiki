# Image Masking

Image [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] is a digital editing technique used to isolate specific areas of an image for [[concepts/selective-adjustment|selective adjustment]] or manipulation. In photography and [[concepts/design|graphic design]], [[concepts/masking|masking]] allows editors to apply effects, color corrections, or filters to precise regions while leaving the rest of the image untouched. The technique is particularly valuable for complex selections where traditional rectangular or geometric selection tools prove inadequate.

## Traditional Manual Masking

[[concepts/manual-masking|Manual masking]] involves using selection tools and brush-based methods to create masks by hand. Editors use feathering, gradients, and careful brush work to define mask boundaries with [[concepts/accuracy|precision]]. This approach requires [[concepts/skill|skill]] and patience but offers complete [[concepts/creative-control|creative control]] over which areas are affected by [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]]. Traditional masking remains widely used for fine detail work and situations where the editor's artistic judgment is essential.

## AI-Powered Masking

Modern [[concepts/image-editing|image editing]] software increasingly incorporates [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] to automate masking tasks. [[concepts/ai-powered-masking|AI-powered masking]] tools can automatically detect and isolate complex subjects—such as hair, foliage, or intricate edges—without manual selection work. These [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]] analyze pixel patterns and boundaries to generate masks more quickly than manual methods, significantly reducing editing time for tasks like background removal or [[concepts/foregroundbackground-separation|subject isolation]]. [[concepts/ai-based-selection|AI masking]] works best on subjects with clear visual distinction from their backgrounds.

## Practical Applications

Masking is commonly used in [[concepts/portrait-retouching|portrait retouching]] to selectively adjust [[concepts/skin-tones|skin tones]], refine hair edges, or modify backgrounds independently of the subject. Commercial product photography relies on masking to isolate items for [[concepts/background-relocation|background replacement]] or specialized lighting adjustments. The technique is also essential in composite work, where multiple images are combined and individual elements require precise boundary control.
