# Digital Preservation

Digital preservation refers to the active maintenance and safeguarding of digital records, documents, and cultural materials to ensure their long-term [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]] and [[concepts/integrity|integrity]]. As institutions increasingly digitize their collections, the practice has become essential for archival work, [[concepts/cultural-heritage|cultural heritage]] management, and historical research. Digital preservation addresses the unique challenges posed by technological obsolescence, format degradation, and the fragility of digital [[entities/storage|storage]] media over extended periods.

## Core Objectives

The primary goals of digital preservation include preventing data loss, maintaining file authenticity, ensuring continued accessibility across changing technological platforms, and documenting the provenance and context of digital materials. Organizations such as university archives implement [[concepts/preservation|preservation]] strategies to protect everything from scanned historical documents to born-digital records. This requires ongoing monitoring, periodic migration to newer formats, and comprehensive [[concepts/metadata|metadata]] documentation.

## Institutional Practice

Universities and cultural institutions like the [[concepts/digital-repository|University of Melbourne Archives]] apply digital preservation principles to their digitized collections, making historical materials accessible to researchers while protecting the originals from handling damage. These institutions develop [[concepts/open-standards|standards]] and workflows for ingesting, storing, and providing access to digital materials, recognizing that preservation is not a one-time action but an ongoing institutional commitment.
