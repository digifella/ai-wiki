# 3D Depth

**3D Depth** refers to the perceptual illusion of three-dimensionality within a two-dimensional image plane. It is achieved through visual cues that trick the brain into interpreting spatial [[concepts/relationships|relationships]], volume, and distance. Key techniques include chiaroscuro, atmospheric perspective, and selective [[concepts/local-adjustments|local adjustments]].

## Techniques for Enhancing Depth

### Selective Light & Shadow Manipulation
Local [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] are critical for defining form and separating subjects from backgrounds.

- **[[concepts/dodging|Dodging]] and [[concepts/burning|Burning]]**: Traditional darkroom techniques adapted for digital workflows to selectively brighten (dodge) or darken (burn) specific areas.
- **The "Three Brushes" Workflow**: As detailed in [[lab-notes/2026-05-29-Lightrooms-Three-Brushes-Dodging-Burning-and-Enhancing-P|Lightroom's Three Brushes: Dodging, Burning, and Enhancing Photo Depth]], [[entities/colin-smith|Colin Smith]] ([[entities/colin-smith-channel|PhotoshopCAFE]]) outlines a specific three-brush methodology in [[entities/adobe|Adobe]] [[concepts/lightroom|Lightroom]] to maximize subject separation.
	- Uses [[concepts/color-and-masking-adjustments|selective adjustments]] to make subjects "pop" by enhancing three-dimensional depth.
	- Focuses on precise [[concepts/local-control|local control]] rather than global [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] changes.

### Visual Cues
- **Atmospheric Perspective**: Distant objects appear less saturated and lower in contrast due to [[concepts/light|light]] scattering.
- **Linear Perspective**: Converging lines guide the eye into the scene.
- **Overlapping**: Closer objects obscure distant ones, establishing [[concepts/hierarchy|hierarchy]].
