## TreeDux USB Cable Tester: Demystifying USB Standards and Performance
**Clip title:** I Finally Sorted Out My Box of Mystery USB Cables
**[[entities/tasia-custode|Author]] / channel:** KTZ Systems
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyexqtQBpDI

### Summary
The video addresses the pervasive frustration of USB [[concepts/cable-compatibility|cable compatibility]] by introducing the [[concepts/treedux-usb-cable-tester|TreeDux USB Cable Tester]], a $50 device designed to demystify the true capabilities of various USB cables. The presenter highlights the growing "mess" of USB standards, particularly with the ubiquitous [[entities/usb-c-port|USB-C]] connector, which often hides vastly different [[concepts/performance-specifications|performance specifications]] beneath an identical physical form factor.

A significant portion of the video is dedicated to illustrating the convoluted history of USB standards, from [[concepts/usb-20|USB 2.0]]'s 480 Mbps in 2000 to the current Thunderbolt 5 offering 80 Gbps (and even 120 Gbps boost) by 2023. The timeline reveals how new specifications like [[concepts/usb-30|USB 3.0]], 3.1, 3.2, and USB4 were repeatedly renamed and absorbed, creating a confusing landscape where a single [[concepts/usb-c-connector|USB-C connector]] can support anything from basic USB 2.0 data and 100W power delivery to full 40Gbps Thunderbolt 4 with display and PCIe data, or even advanced 240W Power Delivery 3.1. This inconsistency means cables that look identical can perform drastically differently.

The TreeDux tester itself is a compact device featuring a clear display, an action button for navigation, and a USB-C input for power (also supports AAA batteries). Crucially, it boasts a comprehensive array of ports, including USB-A, Micro-USB, Mini-USB, USB-B, USB-C, and even Lightning, allowing users to test almost any modern cable. Upon connecting a cable, the tester quickly displays its data transmission capabilities (e.g., USB 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, Thunderbolt), power delivery ratings (PD wattage), pin-out connections, and even internal resistance to detect shorts, providing an "E-marker" chip readout for intelligent cables.

Practical demonstrations vividly showcase the tester's utility. For instance, a OnePlus cable, despite its [[concepts/presence|appearance]], was revealed to only support USB 2.0 data and proprietary fast charging, not standard USB PD. Conversely, a premium Anker cable, while also limited to USB 2.0 data, fully supported 240W USB PD 3.1. A high-end Thunderbolt 5 cable was confirmed to deliver 80 Gbps data and 240W PD, illustrating the vast range of performance. The presenter concludes that manufacturers often include the "bare minimum" cable needed for their product to function, rather than a [[concepts/excellence|high-quality]], versatile one, making it challenging for consumers to know what they're truly getting. This leads to wasted time and suboptimal performance when using an unsuitable cable. The TreeDux tester offers a straightforward way to cut through this [[concepts/ambiguity|ambiguity]] and ensure the right cable is used for the job.

### Video Description & Links
#### Description
[[concepts/usb-20|USB-C cables]] can look identical while supporting completely different speeds, charging standards and features. In this video, I use a $50 Treedix USB cable tester to find out what my collection of mystery cables can actually do.

We test USB-A, USB-C and Lightning cables, including a charge-only cable, an expensive Anker cable limited to USB 2.0 and a Thunderbolt 5 cable capable of 80Gbps and 240W charging. The tester can check pin connections, resistance, Power Delivery support and eMarker data.

I also take a quick trip through the confusing history of USB-C, USB4, Thunderbolt and USB Power Delivery.
