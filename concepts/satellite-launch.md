# Satellite Launch

Satellite launch is the process of deploying artificial satellites into orbit using specialized launch vehicles. The operation encompasses the entire sequence from pre-flight preparation through orbital insertion, including vehicle assembly, fueling, countdown procedures, and ascent to the target orbital altitude. Launch operations require coordination across multiple systems, including ground infrastructure, flight control centers, and tracking stations. Success depends on precise timing, vehicle performance, and adherence to safety protocols established through decades of spaceflight experience.

## Launch Infrastructure and Operations

Satellite launches require substantial ground-based infrastructure, including launch pads, vehicle assembly facilities, propellant storage systems, and mission control centers. Launch providers operate from dedicated spaceports, which house the equipment needed to prepare vehicles and payloads for flight. During the countdown phase, extensive checks verify all vehicle systems and ground support equipment. Ground tracking stations monitor the vehicle's trajectory and telemetry data throughout ascent, providing real-time feedback to flight controllers managing the mission.

## Launch Vehicles

Modern launch vehicles range from small-lift carriers designed for cubesats and microsatellites to heavy-lift systems capable of delivering substantial payloads to high-energy orbits. The New Glenn, currently in development, represents a heavy-lift vehicle designed to compete in the commercial launch market. Launch vehicle selection depends on the satellite's mass, target orbit, and mission requirements. Expendable vehicles remain common for single-use missions, while reusable systems offer potential cost advantages for frequent launch operations.
