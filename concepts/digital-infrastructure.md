# Digital Infrastructure

Digital infrastructure refers to the underlying systems and networks that support digital technology, including hardware, software, data [[entities/storage|storage]], communication protocols, and network services. It is essential for modern computing and information exchange. In the context of [[concepts/health-care|healthcare]], this manifests as Digital [[concepts/health|Health]] Infrastructure (DHI), enabling data-driven care models and post-[[concepts/crisis|crisis]] [[concepts/resilience|resilience]].

## Key Components
- **Internet**: The backbone of global digital communication.
- **[[concepts/techno-economics|Data Centers]]**: Facilities used to house computer systems and associated components, such as telecommunications and storage systems.
- **Networks**: Wired and wireless networks that facilitate data transmission between devices.
- **Software**: [[concepts/software|Programs]] running on various platforms to provide services and applications.
- **Health-Specific Protocols**: Standards for interoperable health data exchange, critical for [[concepts/digital-health]] ecosystems.

### Digital Health Infrastructure (DHI) Trends
Post-pandemic analysis indicates a shift in DHI towards greater integration and resilience. [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Morrison-etal-DHI-2022-Emerging-trends-in-digital-health|Morrison etal DHI 2022 Emerging trends in digital health and care a refresh post covid]] (2022) outlines key developments:
- **Conceptual Models**: Evolution of digital health visions focusing on interoperable care models rather than siloed solutions.
- **Post-[[concepts/covid-19|COVID]] Refresh**: Accelerated [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of remote care and digital triage tools, requiring robust underlying infrastructure to support sudden load spikes.
- **Stakeholder Integration**: Emphasis on collaborative frameworks involving clinicians, patients, and tech providers to reduce implementation errors.
- **Governance in Health**: Specific application of [[concepts/data-politics|data governance]] models to ensure patient [[concepts/privacy|privacy]] while enabling large-scale [[entities/big-data]] analysis for [[concepts/population-health|public health]] outcomes.
