# Skin Tone Editing

Skin [[concepts/tone|tone]] editing in [[concepts/lightroom|Lightroom]] involves using the [[concepts/lightroom-calibration-panel|Calibration Panel]] to perform [[concepts/foundational-color|foundational color]] correction on [[concepts/images|images]]. This panel allows photographers to adjust the color response of their camera's sensor, providing a non-destructive way to [[concepts/power|influence]] how [[concepts/skin-tones|skin tones]] appear across an entire image or set of images. By working [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] the calibration level rather than on individual adjustment sliders, editors can establish a consistent color foundation that affects all subsequent edits.

## Calibration Panel Approach

The Calibration Panel offers controls for [[concepts/shadows|shadows]], midtones, and [[concepts/highlights|highlights]] across different color channels. When applied thoughtfully to skin tones, these [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] can correct [[concepts/color-casts|color casts]] inherent to specific camera sensors or lighting conditions. This foundational adjustment serves as a baseline, allowing subsequent [[concepts/local-adjustments|local adjustments]] and other corrections to build upon a more accurate color starting point. The non-destructive [[entities/nature|nature]] of these edits means changes can be refined or reverted without quality loss.

## Cinematic Applications

Beyond basic color correction, skin tone editing through calibration can achieve cinematic styling. By shifting the [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|color grading]] at the sensor level, photographers can create cohesive mood and aesthetic across images. This approach is particularly useful for establishing consistent looks across image series or projects, as the calibration settings can be applied as a preset to multiple photos while still allowing individual adjustments where needed.
