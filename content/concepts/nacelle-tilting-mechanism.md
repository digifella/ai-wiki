# Nacelle Tilting Mechanism

The nacelle tilting mechanism is a crucial mechanical system in [[concepts/tiltrotor-technology|tiltrotor]] aircraft that allows the engine nacelles (housing the rotors and engines) to rotate between vertical and horizontal orientations. This pivoting capability enables the aircraft to transition between helicopter-like [[concepts/vertical-takeoff-and-landing-vtol|vertical takeoff and landing (VTOL)]] and conventional airplane flight modes. The [[entities/v-22-osprey|V-22 Osprey]], a military transport aircraft, is the most prominent example of this technology in operational use.

## Design and Function

The mechanism consists of a series of actuators, bearings, and structural components that allow each nacelle to tilt through approximately 90 degrees. During vertical flight, the nacelles point upward, with the rotors functioning as lifting propellers. For forward flight, the nacelles gradually tilt forward until the rotors act as propellers, while the aircraft's wings provide aerodynamic lift. This transition occurs smoothly during flight, allowing the aircraft to optimize its configuration for different phases of operation.

## Engineering Challenges

Implementing nacelle tilting requires sophisticated control systems to manage the structural loads and maintain stability during transition phases. The mechanism must be extremely reliable, as failures could compromise aircraft safety. Designers must account for the dynamic forces generated by rotating masses, aerodynamic pressures, and the mechanical [[concepts/stress|stress]] of repeated tilting cycles. Weight considerations also [[concepts/power|influence]] the [[concepts/design|design]], as the actuators and supporting [[concepts/structure|structure]] add significant mass to the aircraft.
