# Planetary Science

Planetary science is the scientific study of planets, moons, and planetary systems throughout the universe. The discipline integrates knowledge from geology, atmospheric physics, chemistry, physics, and biology to investigate the composition, structure, evolution, and potential habitability of planetary bodies. Researchers employ multiple methodologies including direct observation through telescopes, analysis of data collected by spacecraft and rovers, laboratory experimentation, and computational modeling to understand how planets form, develop, and change over time.

A significant area of focus within the field is the investigation of Mars, particularly regarding soil composition and its implications for potential microbial life. By analyzing the chemical and mineralogical properties of Martian regolith, scientists seek to determine past environmental conditions and assess the planet's capacity to support life. This research often involves comparing terrestrial analogs with extraterrestrial samples to refine models of planetary habitability and biosignature detection.

The scope of planetary science extends beyond the solar system to include the study of exoplanets and their host stars. Advances in observational technology have enabled the detection of thousands of planets orbiting other stars, allowing researchers to classify them by size, mass, and orbital characteristics. Comparative planetology remains a core approach, using the diverse environments of our solar system as a baseline to interpret the formation and dynamics of distant planetary systems.
