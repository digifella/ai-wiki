# Trust

**Trust** is the willingness of a party to be vulnerable to the actions of another party based on the expectation that the other [[entities/will|will]] perform a particular action important to the trustor, irrespective of the ability to monitor or control that other party. In organizational and social contexts, trust serves as the foundational glue for Cooperation, [[concepts/communication-strategies]], and [[concepts/psychological-safety]].

## Core Dimensions
- **Cognitive Trust**: Based on competence, [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]], and predictability.
- **Affective Trust**: Based on emotional bonds, care, and interpersonal [[concepts/relationships|relationships]].
- **Institutional Trust**: Confidence in systems, procedures, and organizational [[concepts/integrity|integrity]].

## Relationship with Psychological Safety
Trust is a prerequisite for [[concepts/psychological-safety]]. Without a baseline of interpersonal and systemic trust, individuals fear negative consequences of taking interpersonal risks, such as speaking up with [[concepts/ideas|ideas]], questions, concerns, or mistakes.

## Key Research & Applications
- **[[entities/amy-edmondson|Amy Edmondson]]’s Framework**: Edmondson argues that [[concepts/psychological-safety]] is distinct from trust but relies on it.
	- Trust is the willingness to be vulnerable; [[concepts/vulnerability|psychological safety]] is the shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking.
	- High trust can exist without psychological safety (e.g., in [[entities/high-performance|high-performance]] teams where failure is not tolerated despite mutual [[concepts/respect|respect]]).
	- Refer to: [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Psychological-Safety-Amy-C.-Edmondson|Psychological Safety – Amy C. Edmondson]] for detailed commentary on this distinction.
- **[[concepts/project-aristotle|Project Aristotle]]**: [[concepts/google-search|Google]]’s research identified psychological safety (rooted in trust) as the #1 factor in successful teams.
- **[[entities/amy-edmondson|Amy Edmondson]]’s Framework**: Edmondson argues that trust enables the interpersonal risk-taking required for [[concepts/learning|learning]] behaviors.
- **[[concepts/servant-leadership|Servant Leadership]] Integration**: [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Improving-Team-Effectiveness-Through-Servant-Leadership|Improving Team Effectiveness Through Servant Leadership Training\" by Jason R. Weber]] identifies servant [[concepts/leadership|leadership]] training as a mechanism to enhance team effectiveness by fostering trust. This approach prioritizes the growth and [[concepts/well-being|well-being]] of team members, thereby strengthening both **Affective Trust** (through care) and **Cognitive Trust** (through consistent support), which directly correlates with improved [[concepts/team-effectiveness]].
