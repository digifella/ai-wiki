# Value Perception

Value perception describes the cognitive and physiological processes through which individuals assess the significance of information, experiences, or objects. This assessment is not a purely rational calculation but a complex integration of sensory data, emotional responses, memory associations, and social context. The brain determines what is considered meaningful or worthwhile by balancing conscious evaluation with unconscious neurochemical signaling, effectively filtering external stimuli through a lens of personal and biological relevance.

## Neurochemical Mechanisms in Storytelling

Narrative engagement triggers specific neurochemical pathways that directly influence emotional engagement and perceived value. During storytelling, the brain releases oxytocin, which enhances empathy and social connection, while dopamine regulates attention and reward anticipation. Simultaneously, cortisol levels may rise to create tension, and mirror neurons activate to simulate the experiences of characters. These chemical interactions synchronize the listener’s physiological state with the narrative, transforming abstract information into a visceral, emotionally resonant experience that heightens the perceived importance of the story’s content.
