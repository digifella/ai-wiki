# Anomalous Magnetic Moment

The anomalous magnetic moment is the difference between the measured magnetic moment of a particle and the value predicted by the Dirac equation. For [[concepts/elementary-particles|fundamental particles]] like the electron and muon, this deviation arises from quantum corrections to their electromagnetic interactions. In quantum field [[concepts/theory|theory]], virtual particle-antiparticle pairs briefly exist and interact with the particle, causing its magnetic moment to differ slightly from classical predictions. The anomalous magnetic moment is expressed as *a* = (*g* − 2)/2, where *g* is the gyromagnetic ratio of the particle.

## Measurement and Significance

The anomalous magnetic moment of the muon has been measured with exceptional precision through the [[concepts/muon-g-2-experiment|Muon g-2 experiment]], first conducted at Brookhaven National Laboratory and later continued at Fermilab. These measurements represent some of the most precise tests of quantum electrodynamics and the [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|Standard Model of particle physics]]. Discrepancies between experimental measurements and theoretical predictions could indicate [[concepts/physics|physics]] beyond the Standard Model, such as previously unknown particles or forces.

## Theoretical Context

The theoretical prediction of the anomalous magnetic moment involves calculating contributions from virtual particle loops in quantum field theory. Electrons, muons, and other charged particles all possess anomalous magnetic moments, though the effect is most precisely measured in muons due to their longer lifetime compared to other unstable particles. The calculation of these quantum corrections requires sophisticated techniques in quantum field theory and continues to be refined as experimental precision improves.
