# Market competitiveness

The ability of a business or product to effectively compete in its market through factors like cost efficiency, quality, [[concepts/innovation|innovation]], and speed to market.

## Case studies

- [[entities/destin-sandlin|Destin Sandlin]]'s [[entities/smarter-every-day|Smarter Every Day]] [[concepts/four-year-experiment|four-year experiment]] (2026) to manufacture the [[entities/smarter-scrubber|Smarter Scrubber]] entirely in [[entities/america|America]], [[concepts/testing|testing]] if [[concepts/domestic-production|domestic production]] can achieve market competitiveness. Motivated by his parents' history as [[entities/union-autoworkers|Union autoworkers]], the project documented end-to-end U.S. manufacturing challenges including material sourcing, production costs, and [[concepts/quality-control|quality control]].
- The experiment highlighted key competitiveness factors: U.S. labor costs vs. overseas alternatives, supply chain [[concepts/resilience|resilience]], and consumer willingness to pay premium for domestically made products.
- Video documentation: [[entities/smarter-every-day|Smarter Every Day]]'s "Destin - manufacturing in America" (2026-04-14), featuring the full process of creating a safer, durable grill brush alternative to traditional wire bristle brushes.
