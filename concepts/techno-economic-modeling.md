# Techno Economic Modeling

Techno-economic modeling is an analytical framework that integrates technical and economic analysis to evaluate the feasibility and viability of large-scale infrastructure projects. Rather than treating engineering design and financial assessment as separate disciplines, this approach produces unified evaluations that demonstrate how technical decisions directly influence capital costs, operational expenses, and revenue generation. The methodology is particularly valuable for projects requiring substantial upfront investment where technical choices have cascading financial consequences.

## Core Components

The approach systematically models relationships between physical system design, resource requirements, and economic outcomes. This includes analyzing capital expenditures (equipment, construction, installation), operational costs (energy, maintenance, labor), technical performance parameters (efficiency, throughput, reliability), and revenue models or value streams. By linking these elements in integrated models, practitioners can identify cost drivers, evaluate trade-offs between competing design options, and stress-test assumptions about performance and market conditions.

## Applications

Techno-economic modeling is commonly applied to infrastructure projects with high capital intensity and long operational timelines, such as energy systems, telecommunications networks, and data centers. The method enables stakeholders to compare design alternatives, assess sensitivity to key variables, and determine breakeven points or required performance targets. Results typically inform investment decisions, regulatory evaluation, and project financing structures.
