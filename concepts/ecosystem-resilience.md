# Ecosystem Resilience

**Ecosystem [[concepts/resilience|Resilience]]** is the capacity of an ecological system to absorb disturbance, reorganize, and retain essentially the same function, structure, identity, and feedbacks. It is a measure of the magnitude of disturbance that can be absorbed before the system changes its controlling variables and state.

## Core Principles

- **Resistance vs. Resilience**: Resistance is the ability to remain unchanged during disturbance; resilience is the ability to recover after change.
- **Adaptive Capacity**: The scope for an ecosystem to adjust to [[concepts/climate-change|climate change]], taking advantage of opportunities, or responding to or [[concepts/stress-management|coping]] with adverse effects.
- **[[concepts/biodiversity|Biodiversity]] as Insurance**: High species richness often correlates with functional redundancy, ensuring that if one species fails, others can maintain ecosystem processes.
- **Thresholds and Tipping Points**: Non-linear changes where small perturbations lead to large-scale regime shifts.

## Case Study: Ancient Stability and Geological Survival

The concept of resilience is not limited to rapid recovery from acute shocks but also encompasses long-term [[concepts/data-persistence|persistence]] through geological time [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]].

- **[[concepts/daintree-rainforest|Daintree Rainforest]] Example**:
	- The Daintree Rainforest: Oldest Living Tropical Ecosystem, [[concepts/ancient-biodiversity|Ancient Biodiversity]], and [[concepts/geological-survival|Geological Survival]] serves as a critical example of deep-time resilience.
	- As one of the oldest living tropical ecosystems, it has persisted through significant climatic and geological shifts, demonstrating how ancient biodiversity contributes to long-term [[concepts/ecological-balance|ecosystem stability]].
	- Its survival highlights the role of geological context in maintaining habitat [[concepts/continuity|continuity]], allowing for the [[concepts/preservation|preservation]] of lineages that might otherwise be lost in younger, more volatile ecosystems.

## Mechanisms of Resilience

1. **Functional Redundancy**: Multiple species performing similar roles.
2. **Modularity**: Compartmentalization of interactions to prevent cascade failures.
3. **[[concepts/systems|Feedback Loops]]**: Positive feedbacks that reinforce stability (e.g., nutrient cycling).
4. **[[concepts/memory|Memory]]**: Ecological memory via seed banks, soil microbiomes, or long-lived species.
