# Hierarchical Systems

Hierarchical Systems constitute a structured methodology for organizing [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] context through a [[concepts/map-first-architecture|map-first architecture]]. This approach prioritizes the establishment of a conceptual framework that maps information [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] and dependencies prior to AI processing, rather than directly ingesting raw documents into language models. By structuring data hierarchically, the system aims to provide a more coherent context for downstream tasks, distinguishing itself from traditional flat [[concepts/data-indexing|indexing]] methods.

The primary [[concepts/purpose|objective]] of this architecture is to address inherent limitations in [[concepts/answer-generation|retrieval-augmented generation]] (RAG), particularly issues related to context fragmentation and [[concepts/search-relevance|relevance ranking]] across large document collections. [[concepts/traditional-rag|Traditional RAG]] systems often struggle to maintain semantic [[concepts/coherence|coherence]] when [[concepts/retrieving|retrieving]] disjointed chunks of text. Hierarchical Systems mitigate this by ensuring that retrieved information is pre-organized according to logical connections, thereby improving the accuracy and relevance of the generated output.

Core to this methodology is the creation of a top-down structural map that defines the scope and relationships of the data domain. This map serves as a navigational guide for the AI, allowing it to traverse information based on conceptual proximity rather than mere keyword similarity. This [[concepts/payload-structure|structural integrity]] helps preserve the logical [[concepts/flow|flow]] of [[concepts/complex-information|complex information]], making it particularly suitable for domains requiring deep [[concepts/contextual-understanding|contextual understanding]] and precise [[concepts/dependency-tracking|dependency tracking]].
