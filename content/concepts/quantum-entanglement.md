# Quantum Entanglement

Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon in [[concepts/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]] where two or more particles become correlated in such a way that measuring a property of one particle instantaneously determines the corresponding property of the other, regardless of the distance separating them. This correlation persists even when the particles are spatially separated by arbitrary distances. The entangled particles share a single quantum state, meaning their properties remain undefined until measurement occurs, [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] which point the measurement of one particle's state immediately constrains the possible states of its entangled partner.

## Physical Basis and Measurement

The phenomenon arises from the [[entities/nature|nature]] of quantum superposition. Before measurement, an entangled particle exists in a superposition of multiple possible states simultaneously. When a measurement is performed on one particle, its quantum state "collapses" to a definite value, and the state of the entangled partner collapses accordingly. This instantaneous correlation troubled early quantum theorists, as it appeared to violate the principle that no [[concepts/power|influence]] can travel faster than [[concepts/light|light]]. However, entanglement does not permit faster-than-light communication because the measurement outcomes appear random to individual observers until classical information about both measurements is exchanged.

## Applications and Implications

Quantum entanglement forms the theoretical foundation for emerging quantum technologies, particularly [[concepts/quantum-computing|quantum computing]] and quantum [[concepts/cryptography|cryptography]]. In [[entities/quantum-computing|quantum computing]], entanglement allows [[concepts/qubits|qubits]] to exist in correlated superpositions, potentially enabling exponential speedup for certain [[concepts/computational-problems|computational problems]]. In quantum cryptography, entanglement enables [[entities/theoretically-media|theoretically]] [[concepts/secure|secure]] communication protocols by detecting eavesdropping attempts. The phenomenon continues to be central to fundamental questions about the nature of quantum reality and the relationship between locality and determinism in [[concepts/physics|physics]].
