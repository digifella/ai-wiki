# Thomas Edison

## Overview
Thomas Edison was an American inventor and businessman who is best known for his pioneering work in developing practical commercial electrical systems and technologies. He played a pivotal role in the transformation of how [[concepts/power|power]] was distributed and utilized in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

## Key Contributions
*   **Practical Electric Lighting:** Edison's most significant contribution was the development of a commercially viable system for electric lighting, which revolutionized domestic and commercial use.
*   **The Phonograph:** He developed the phonograph, an early recording device, which laid the groundwork for modern sound recording technology.
*   **Electrical Systems:** Edison was instrumental in creating the infrastructure necessary for widespread electrical [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] in the [[entities/united-states|United States]].

## Context in Electrical History
Edison's work occurred during a critical period of intense technological competition regarding power transmission methods.

*   The history of electrical power transmission involves a rivalry between Direct Current (DC) and [[concepts/alternating-current-ac|Alternating Current (AC)]].
*   The shift toward AC transmission eventually led to the dominance of AC systems in large-scale power [[concepts/grids|grids]].
*   Recent innovations are seeing a specialized resurgence of DC transmission methods, particularly in high-efficiency [[concepts/software|applications]], as highlighted by developments in other regions, such as the focus on Sweden's HVDC [[concepts/innovation|Innovation]]: Resurgence of Direct Current Transmission.
