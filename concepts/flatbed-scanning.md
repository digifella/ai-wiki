# Flatbed Scanning

Flatbed scanning is a method of digitizing film negatives and slides using a standard flatbed scanner equipped with transparency scanning capabilities. While flatbed scanners are primarily designed for documents and reflective materials, many models include transparency adapters or backlit scanning modules that enable them to read transmitted light through film. This approach allows individuals to convert analog film materials into digital format at home without investing in specialized dedicated film scanners.

The process typically involves placing the film directly on the scanner glass, often using a specialized holder to keep the film flat and aligned. The scanner’s light source illuminates the film from below, capturing the image data as it passes through the emulsion. Users must configure the scanner software to recognize the input as a transparency rather than a reflective document, which adjusts the exposure and color balance algorithms accordingly.

Image quality depends heavily on the scanner’s optical resolution and dynamic range. Higher resolution settings capture finer grain and detail, while a greater dynamic range preserves information in both the darkest shadows and brightest highlights of the negative. Post-processing is usually required to invert the colors, correct white balance, and adjust contrast to produce a final positive image suitable for digital use.
