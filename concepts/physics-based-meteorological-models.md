# Physics-based Meteorological Models

**Physics-based meteorological models** are computational systems that simulate atmospheric dynamics using fundamental laws of physics, such as [[concepts/fluid-motion|fluid dynamics]] and thermodynamics. While traditionally dominated by numerical weather prediction (NWP), the field is evolving with the integration of Machine Learning and Deep Learning to enhance resolution, speed, and accuracy.

## Key Developments

*   **Hybrid Approaches:** Modern forecasting increasingly blends traditional physics-based grids with AI-driven corrections to mitigate computational costs and improve local accuracy.
*   **High-Resolution Global Forecasting:** Recent advancements focus on overcoming the trade-off between global coverage and local detail, enabling more timely and precise predictions.
*   **[[concepts/ai-integration|AI Integration]]:** [[concepts/artificial-intelligence|Artificial intelligence]] is being leveraged to process vast datasets and identify patterns that pure physics models might miss or compute too slowly.

*   **[[entities/google-deepmind|DeepMind]]'s [[concepts/weathernext-3|WeatherNext 3]]:** A significant breakthrough in AI-driven [[concepts/global-weather-forecasting|global weather forecasting]], offering higher accuracy and resolution [[lab-notes/2026-09-04-DeepMinds-WeatherNext-3-AI-Driven-High-Resolution-Global|DeepMind's WeatherNext 3: AI-Driven High-Resolution Global Weather Forecasting]].
