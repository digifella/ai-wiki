# Habits

Habits are recurring behaviors and practices that become automatic through repetition, requiring minimal conscious effort to perform. They form a crucial component of daily life, enabling individuals to accomplish routine tasks efficiently while freeing cognitive resources for more demanding activities. The neurological basis of habit formation involves the brain's basal ganglia, which gradually assumes behavioral control from the prefrontal cortex as actions repeat, making them increasingly automatic over time.

## Formation and Change

Habits develop through a cyclical process involving a cue, a routine, and a reward. This loop reinforces the neural pathways associated with the behavior, making it more likely to be triggered by the same cue in the future. Changing established habits often requires identifying the existing cue and reward to modify the routine, or deliberately introducing new cues to break the automatic cycle.

## Impact on Wellbeing

Recurring behaviors significantly influence overall health and happiness. Positive habits, such as regular exercise or mindful eating, contribute to physical and mental stability, while negative habits can hinder progress toward personal goals. Frameworks like Gretchen Rubin’s *The Happiness Project* explore how intentional habit formation can enhance life satisfaction by aligning daily actions with broader values and objectives.
