# Standard Model

The Standard Model is the foundational theoretical framework in particle physics that describes elementary particles and their interactions through three of the four known fundamental forces: the electromagnetic, weak nuclear, and strong nuclear forces. Developed incrementally from the 1960s onward through contributions by numerous physicists, the model successfully unifies quantum mechanics with special relativity. It classifies matter into fermions, which include quarks and leptons, and force carriers into bosons, such as photons and gluons. The Higgs mechanism, confirmed by the discovery of the Higgs boson in 2012, explains how these particles acquire mass.

Despite its predictive success, the Standard Model is incomplete as it does not incorporate gravity, nor does it account for dark matter or dark energy. Current research focuses on precision tests of the model, such as measurements of the muon g-2 experiment, which have shown potential deviations from theoretical predictions. Additionally, investigations into quark substructure and the search for physics beyond the Standard Model continue to drive experimental efforts at high-energy colliders.
