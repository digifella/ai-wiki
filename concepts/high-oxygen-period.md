# High Oxygen Period

The High Oxygen Period denotes geological intervals characterized by atmospheric oxygen concentrations significantly exceeding the modern baseline of approximately 21 percent. The most prominent instance occurred during the Late Paleozoic Era, specifically within the Carboniferous period (roughly 359 to 299 million years ago). During this time, oxygen levels potentially surpassed 30 percent, creating an environment distinct from subsequent geological [[concepts/epochs|epochs]].

These elevated levels were sustained by extensive coal-forming forests and high marine [[concepts/productivity|productivity]], which sequestered vast amounts of organic carbon. This burial prevented the oxidation of organic matter, thereby allowing oxygen to accumulate in the [[concepts/earths-atmosphere|atmosphere]]. The resulting oxidative weathering processes contributed to the formation of extensive red bed deposits, influencing the geological features observed in regions such as the [[concepts/red-centre-of-australia|red centre of Australia]].
