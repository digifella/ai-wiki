# Gravitational Mass

Gravitational mass is the intrinsic property of matter that determines the strength of the gravitational attraction it exerts on other objects. In classical physics, this quantity is equivalent to inertial mass, which measures an object's resistance to acceleration. This equivalence is formalized in Einstein's equivalence principle and serves as a foundational pillar of general relativity. Experimental verification has confirmed this relationship to extraordinary precision.

The concept becomes critical in astrophysics when analyzing the dynamics of large-scale structures, particularly galaxy clusters. Observations of these systems reveal significant discrepancies between the visible baryonic matter and the total mass required to explain their gravitational behavior. This mismatch highlights the presence of dark matter, which contributes substantially to the gravitational mass of the cluster without emitting electromagnetic radiation.

Understanding the distribution of gravitational mass allows researchers to map the underlying structure of the universe. By studying the rotation curves of galaxies and the velocity dispersions of clusters, scientists infer the presence of non-luminous matter. These analyses provide essential constraints on cosmological models and the nature of dark matter, distinguishing it from ordinary baryonic matter through its gravitational influence rather than direct detection.
