# Engine Oil

Engine oil serves as the lifeblood of internal combustion engines, performing multiple critical functions that enable reliable engine operation. It lubricates moving parts, reducing friction between metal surfaces, and helps dissipate heat generated during combustion. Engine oil also carries away combustion byproducts and contaminants, transporting them to the oil filter where they are removed. Without adequate oil circulation and pressure, engine components experience rapid wear, overheating, and failure.

## Pressure and Circulation

Engine oil is distributed throughout the engine via a pressurized circulation system powered by an oil pump. This pressure maintains a protective film between moving parts, preventing metal-to-metal contact. The oil pump draws oil from the pan at the engine's base and forces it through galleries and passages to critical areas including the crankshaft, camshaft, and valve lifters. A pressure relief valve regulates system pressure to prevent damage from excessive force.

## Monitoring and Maintenance

Modern vehicles monitor oil pressure through electronic sensors that trigger a warning light on the dashboard when pressure drops below safe levels. This indicates either insufficient oil volume or a problem with the circulation system. Regular oil changes are necessary because oil degrades over time through oxidation and contamination accumulation, reducing its effectiveness at lubrication and heat transfer. Maintaining proper oil level and following manufacturer-recommended service intervals is essential for engine longevity.
