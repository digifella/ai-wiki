# Mars

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun in Earth's solar system, orbiting between Earth and the asteroid belt. As a terrestrial planet, it possesses a thin atmosphere composed primarily of carbon dioxide, with surface temperatures ranging from approximately −125 °C at the poles to 20 °C at the equator during summer. The planet's distinctive reddish appearance results from iron oxide present in its surface rocks and dust. Mars has a diameter roughly half that of Earth and a mass approximately one-tenth that of Earth.

## Physical Characteristics

Mars orbits the Sun at an average distance of about 228 million kilometers, completing one revolution every 687 Earth days. Its rotation period is similar to Earth's, resulting in a day length of approximately 24 hours and 37 minutes. The planet features a solid surface characterized by vast plains, deep valleys, large volcanoes, and polar ice caps composed of water ice and frozen carbon dioxide. Notable geological features include Olympus Mons, the largest volcano in the solar system, and Valles Marineris, a massive canyon system.

## Exploration and Scientific Study

Mars has been a primary subject of scientific investigation due to its potential for past habitability and the possibility of current extremophile environments. Numerous robotic missions, including orbiters, landers, and rovers, have been deployed by various space agencies to analyze the planet's geology, climate, and search for signs of water. These studies aim to understand the planet's history, assess its potential for future human exploration, and determine whether microbial life ever existed or persists in subsurface niches.
