# Telephoto Lens

A telephoto lens is a camera lens with an extended [[concepts/focal-length|focal length]], typically 70mm or greater, that magnifies distant subjects and brings them closer in the photographic frame. By increasing focal length, these lenses allow photographers to capture fine detail from subjects that are far away without physically moving closer to them. The magnification effect is directly proportional to focal length—a 200mm lens provides roughly twice the magnification of a 100mm lens.

## Optical Characteristics

Telephoto lenses are distinguished by their narrow field of view, which concentrates the image on a smaller area of the camera sensor. This narrow perspective compresses the apparent distance between objects in a scene, making background elements appear closer to the subject than they actually are. The longer the focal length, the more pronounced this compression effect becomes. This characteristic is useful in portraiture, wildlife photography, and sports photography, where isolating subjects and flattening depth creates visually compelling results.

## Common Applications and Trade-offs

Photographers use telephoto lenses to photograph wildlife, distant sports action, and architectural details without requiring proximity to their subjects. These lenses are also popular for portrait work, where their focal lengths naturally produce flattering facial proportions and soft backgrounds. The primary trade-offs include reduced light-gathering ability compared to wider lenses, increased susceptibility to camera shake that may require faster shutter speeds or stabilization, and reduced depth of field that demands more precise focusing.
