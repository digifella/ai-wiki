# Corporate AI Governance

Corporate [[concepts/ai-governance|AI Governance]] refers to the frameworks and [[concepts/policies|policies]] organizations establish to manage [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems and their associated risks within enterprise environments. This includes oversight of both official, sanctioned AI deployments and informal or unauthorized uses of [[entities/ai-tools|AI tools]] by employees—a phenomenon known as [[concepts/shadow-ai|Shadow AI]].

## Shadow AI and Organizational Risk

[[concepts/shadow-it|Shadow AI]] describes the use of AI systems and tools within a corporation that occur outside formal [[concepts/governance|governance]] structures and IT oversight. Employees may adopt third-party AI services, [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]], or [[concepts/automation-tools|automation tools]] to improve productivity without formal approval or [[concepts/security|security]] review. This creates organizational risk including data [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] violations, inconsistent quality [[concepts/open-standards|standards]], and loss of visibility into how AI systems are being deployed and what data they access.

## Governance Strategies

Effective corporate AI governance balances [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] with [[concepts/power|control]] by establishing clear policies for [[concepts/ai-adoption-trends|AI tool adoption]], data handling protocols, and security requirements. Rather than prohibiting unauthorized AI use, leading organizations develop frameworks that bring Shadow AI into formal processes through approval workflows, [[concepts/training|training]] [[concepts/software|programs]], and [[concepts/integration|integration]] with existing IT infrastructure. This approach acknowledges the practical reality that employees [[entities/will|will]] use [[concepts/ai-tools|AI tools]] while maintaining necessary oversight and compliance.
