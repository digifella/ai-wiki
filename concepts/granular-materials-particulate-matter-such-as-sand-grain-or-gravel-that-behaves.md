# Granular Materials: Particulate Matter Such As Sand Grain Or Gravel That Behaves

Granular materials are collections of discrete solid particles that interact through mechanical contact rather than chemical bonding. Common examples include sand, gravel, soil, powders, and industrial materials like coal and cereals. The defining characteristic of granular systems is that individual particles can move independently while the collection as a whole exhibits emergent physical properties distinct from those of its constituent parts.

## Physical Properties

Unlike conventional solids with fixed internal structures, granular materials display properties intermediate between solids and liquids. They can be poured and flow like fluids, yet they also support static stress and maintain angles of repose. This dual nature means they do not conform to standard models of solid or liquid behavior. Friction between particles and the geometry of particle packing significantly influence how granular systems respond to applied forces, vibration, and compaction.

## Practical Significance

Granular materials are ubiquitous in engineering, agriculture, pharmaceuticals, and natural systems. Understanding their behavior is essential for designing storage silos, optimizing material handling, predicting avalanches and landslides, and developing industrial processes. Despite their common occurrence, the mechanics of granular flow and stress distribution remain areas of active research, as their behavior depends sensitively on factors including particle shape, size distribution, moisture content, and packing arrangements.
