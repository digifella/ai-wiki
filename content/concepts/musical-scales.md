# Musical Scales

A musical scale is an ordered sequence of pitches or [[concepts/notes|notes]] arranged in ascending or descending order. Scales serve as the foundational framework for [[concepts/melody|melody]], [[concepts/musical-harmony|harmony]], and composition across nearly all musical traditions. They define which notes sound consonant or natural together within a given key, constraining the [[entities/pitch|pitch]] choices available to musicians and composers. The most familiar scale in Western music is the major scale, which divides an octave into twelve semitones using a specific pattern of whole and half steps.

## Common Scale Types

Different scales produce distinct emotional and [[entities/sonic|sonic]] qualities. The major scale conveys brightness and stability, while the natural minor scale carries a darker or more introspective character. Other widely used scales include the harmonic minor, melodic minor, pentatonic, and modal scales (derived from the major scale but starting from different degrees). Non-Western musical traditions employ their own scale systems—such as ragas in Indian classical music or maqam in Arabic music—each with unique intervals and cultural significance.

## Function in Music

Scales provide the palette from which chords and melodies are built. A chord typically consists of multiple notes taken from a single scale, and the [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] between those notes determine the chord's harmonic function. Understanding scale degrees and how they relate to one another enables musicians to predict harmonic progressions, create tension and resolution, and develop a sense of musical coherence. Scales also form the basis of ear [[concepts/training|training]] and [[concepts/music-conceptstheorytheory|music theory]] education.
