# Tiered Pricing Models

Tiered pricing models divide a product or service offering into multiple levels, each with distinct features, capacities, or support options available at different price points. This [[concepts/structure|structure]] allows companies to serve different customer segments simultaneously—from price-sensitive users seeking basic functionality to premium customers willing to pay for advanced capabilities. By offering multiple entry points, tiered models can expand addressable [[concepts/market-size|market size]] while maximizing revenue from customers with higher willingness to pay.

## Common Structure

Most tiered systems follow a hierarchical approach, typically labeled as Basic, Standard, and Premium (or similar variants), where higher tiers include all lower-tier features plus additional ones. Differentiation between tiers may involve feature access, [[concepts/rate-limits|usage limits]], [[entities/storage|storage]] capacity, priority support, or service level agreements. The goal is to create meaningful distinctions that justify price differences while ensuring the lowest tier remains attractive to potential customers.

## Implementation Considerations

When introducing or adjusting [[concepts/tiered-pricing|tiered pricing]], companies must carefully position each level to avoid cannibalizing higher-tier sales or alienating existing customers. Price increases or restructuring—such as raising tier costs or removing features from lower levels—can trigger user migration to competitors or increased churn. The success of tiered models depends partly on transparent communication about what changes and why, as well as realistic assessment of how existing user bases will respond to new tier assignments.
