# Fertility Rate

**Fertility Rate** quantifies the average number of children born per woman, a fundamental driver of Population Dynamics and Long-term Economic Planning. The standard metric is Total Fertility Rate (TFR).

## Metrics & Thresholds
*   **Replacement Level:** ~2.1 children/woman in low-mortality societies; sustained rates below this cause Population Decline absent net Migration.
*   **Determinants:** Strongly correlated with Women's Education, [[entities/employees|Labor Force]] Participation, Childcare Infrastructure, Housing Affordability, and Social [[concepts/security|Security]] [[concepts/robustness|robustness]].
*   **Global Trends:** Widespread transition to Low Fertility Regimes, often persisting despite Pro-natalist [[concepts/policies|Policies]], suggesting structural rather than temporary shifts.

## Systemic Impacts
*   **Demographic Structure:** Declining fertility accelerates Aging Population and worsens the Dependency Ratio, increasing fiscal pressure on Pension Systems and [[concepts/health|Healthcare]].
*   **Economic Consequences:** Linked to Labor Shortages, [[concepts/productivity|Productivity]] stagnation, Housing Market deflation, and Fiscal [[concepts/stress|Stress]] as the tax base shrinks relative to elder-care expenditures.
*   **Policy Efficacy:** Financial incentives show [[concepts/diminishing-returns|diminishing returns]]; sustainable support requires integrated structural reforms addressing work-life balance and cost of living.
