# Pathogen Exposure

Pathogen exposure refers to the [[entities/contact|contact]] between a person (or other organism) and disease-causing microorganisms such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, or parasites. This contact may occur through various routes including inhalation, ingestion, skin contact, or through bodily fluids. [[concepts/exposure|Exposure]] does not necessarily result in infection; whether infection develops depends on factors including the pathogen's virulence, the route of transmission, and the immune status of the exposed individual.

## Exposure and Infection

The distinction between exposure and infection is clinically significant. An individual may be exposed to a pathogen without becoming infected, particularly if their immune system successfully prevents the microorganism from establishing itself. Conversely, exposure is a prerequisite for infection to occur. Understanding exposure patterns helps epidemiologists track disease transmission and identify at-risk populations.

## Factors Influencing Outcomes

Several variables determine whether pathogen exposure leads to clinical disease. These include the dose or number of pathogens encountered, the specific characteristics of the pathogen strain, the individual's age and overall [[concepts/health|health]] status, nutritional state, and prior exposure history. [[concepts/vaccination|Vaccination]] or previous infection can provide varying degrees of protection against subsequent exposures to the same pathogen.
