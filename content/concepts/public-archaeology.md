# Public Archaeology

Public archaeology is the practice of conducting archaeological work with direct participation from non-professional members of the public. Rather than restricting excavation and [[concepts/artifact-recovery|artifact recovery]] to credentialed archaeologists, public archaeology [[concepts/software|programs]] involve community members, volunteers, and amateur enthusiasts in fieldwork, analysis, and interpretation. This approach aims to democratize archaeological practice, build public interest in [[concepts/cultural-heritage|heritage]], and expand the workforce available for archaeological projects.

## Models and Implementation

Public archaeology takes various forms depending on local context and objectives. Some programs train and supervise volunteers to assist with systematic excavations led by professional archaeologists. Others, such as Denmark's [[concepts/metal-detecting|metal detecting]] initiative, grant private individuals permits to search for artifacts on farmland under agreed-upon protocols, with discoveries reported to authorities and documented within official records. These approaches balance public access and engagement with the need to maintain archaeological [[concepts/open-standards|standards]] and preserve [[concepts/contextual-information|contextual information]] about finds.

## Outcomes and Considerations

Public archaeology programs can uncover artifacts and sites that might otherwise remain undocumented, particularly in rural areas with limited archaeological resources. They also serve educational purposes, increasing public awareness of local history and archaeological methods. However, such programs require careful management to ensure that artifacts are properly recorded, that sites are not damaged through careless excavation, and that finds are appropriately preserved and studied rather than lost to private collections.
