# Bmj Review

The BMJ Review addresses a critical gap in [[concepts/health|healthcare]]'s approach to [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] [[concepts/adoption|adoption]]. While AI technologies are being rapidly integrated into [[entities/clinical-practice|clinical practice]] and [[concepts/health-care|healthcare]] systems, the organisational frameworks necessary to govern their implementation have not kept pace. This mismatch creates significant risks around patient safety, data [[concepts/security|security]], and the responsible use of these powerful tools.

## Governance and Ethics Challenges

Existing [[concepts/organisational-governance|organisational governance]] structures in healthcare institutions often lack the specific processes and oversight [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] required to ensure [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] are deployed safely and ethically. Previous attempts at establishing [[concepts/ai-governance|AI governance frameworks]] have primarily focused on high-level [[concepts/core-principles-of-ethics|ethical principles]] rather than practical implementation [[concepts/recommendations|guidance]]. The BMJ Review identifies this disconnect as a pressing concern that requires urgent [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] from healthcare administrators, clinicians, and policymakers.

The review emphasises that [[concepts/healthcare-organisations|healthcare organisations]] need to develop comprehensive [[concepts/governance|governance]] systems that address not only the ethical dimensions of AI use but also [[concepts/safety-protocol|operational safeguards]], [[concepts/opacity|transparency]] mechanisms, and [[concepts/accountability|accountability]] structures. Without such frameworks in place, healthcare institutions risk deploying [[entities/ai-tools|AI tools]] without adequate scrutiny of their clinical validity, potential [[concepts/biases|biases]], or unintended consequences for [[concepts/roles-in-community-settings|patient care]].
