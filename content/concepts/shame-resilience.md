# Shame Resilience

[[concepts/shame-resiliency|Shame resilience]] is the ability to acknowledge and work through feelings of shame in a constructive manner. This concept was popularized by researcher [[entities/brené-brown|Brené Brown]] and has since become an integral part of discussions on [[concepts/mental-health|mental health]], [[concepts/relationships|relationships]], and organizational behavior.

### Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/vulnerability|Vulnerability]]**: The willingness to show up authentically and expose oneself to potential judgment or rejection.
- **[[concepts/courage|Courage]]**: The practice of living out one's values in the face of fear, often in the context of vulnerability and shame.
- **[[concepts/connection|Connection]]**: The fundamental human need for belonging and [[concepts/love|love]].

### Brené Brown's Contributions

Brené Brown is a research professor known for her work on vulnerability and [[concepts/resilience|resilience]]. Her research has shown that the ability to face shame constructively can lead to greater self-awareness, healthier relationships, and more authentic [[concepts/leadership|leadership]].
