# Climate Resilience

Climate [[concepts/resilience|resilience]] describes the capacity of [[entities/earth|Earth]]'s physical systems—including the [[concepts/earths-atmosphere|atmosphere]], hydrosphere, biosphere, and geosphere—to withstand, adapt to, and recover from climate disturbances and variability. These interconnected systems maintain their essential functions when subjected to shocks such as [[concepts/extreme-weather-events|extreme weather events]], prolonged droughts, or rapid temperature shifts. The concept encompasses both the inherent stability of natural environments and their ability to return to previous states or shift to new equilibrium conditions following disruption.

In the context of human health and [[concepts/wellbeing|wellbeing]], climate resilience extends beyond ecological stability to include the [[concepts/robustness|robustness]] of social and economic structures. It involves the ability of communities and individuals to anticipate, cope with, and recover from climate-related health risks, such as [[concepts/heat-stress|heat stress]], vector-borne diseases, and food insecurity. This dimension emphasizes the [[concepts/value|importance]] of adaptive capacity, where [[concepts/health|healthcare]] systems, [[concepts/infrastructure|infrastructure]], and [[concepts/public-health|public health]] [[concepts/policies|policies]] are designed to mitigate the adverse effects of climate variability on [[concepts/population-health|population health]].

Building climate resilience requires integrated approaches that address both natural and human systems. For ecological components, this involves preserving [[concepts/biodiversity|biodiversity]] and restoring ecosystems that provide natural buffers against climate impacts. For human systems, it entails strengthening healthcare infrastructure, improving [[concepts/early-intruder-detection|early warning systems]], and implementing policies that reduce [[concepts/vulnerability|vulnerability]] among at-risk populations. The ultimate goal is to ensure that both natural environments and human societies can sustain their core functions despite increasing climate uncertainty.
