# Scientific Method

The scientific method is a systematic approach to investigating natural phenomena and developing reliable knowledge about the world. Rather than a rigid set of steps, it functions as a flexible framework that emphasizes observation, hypothesis formation, experimentation, and validation. Scientists across all disciplines employ this method to minimize personal bias and ensure that conclusions are supported by empirical evidence rather than speculation or assumption.

## Core Elements

The process typically begins with observation of natural phenomena, which prompts questions about the underlying mechanisms. These questions lead to the formulation of a hypothesis, a testable prediction that explains the observed data. Researchers then design experiments or conduct further observations to gather data that can either support or refute the hypothesis. This stage requires careful control of variables to isolate the effects being studied and ensure the reliability of the results.

## Validation and Iteration

Once data is collected, it is analyzed to determine whether it aligns with the initial hypothesis. If the results contradict the prediction, the hypothesis is revised or discarded, and the cycle begins anew. If the results support the hypothesis, it may undergo further testing by independent researchers to verify reproducibility. Over time, hypotheses that withstand rigorous scrutiny and accumulate substantial evidence may evolve into scientific theories, providing robust explanations for natural laws. This iterative nature ensures that scientific knowledge remains dynamic and self-correcting.
