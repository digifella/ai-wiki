# Nanotechnology

Nanotechnology is the engineering and manipulation of matter at the nanoscale, typically involving structures and devices between 1 and 100 nanometers in size. At this scale, materials exhibit different physical and chemical properties compared to their bulk counterparts, enabling novel [[concepts/software|applications]] across semiconductors, materials science, medicine, and manufacturing.

## Semiconductor Manufacturing

One significant application of nanotechnology is in advanced semiconductor fabrication. The industry has progressively reduced transistor dimensions to improve chip performance and density, with current leading-edge processes operating at 2-3 nanometer [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]]. Manufacturing chips at these scales requires sophisticated techniques including extreme ultraviolet lithography and advanced etching processes. Companies pursuing [[concepts/vertical-integration|vertical integration]] in chip production—controlling design, fabrication, and [[concepts/supply-chains|supply chains]] internally—represent a modern approach to managing the complexity and costs of nanoscale manufacturing.

## Practical Challenges

Producing functional devices at the nanoscale presents substantial engineering challenges, including precision manufacturing tolerances, [[concepts/quality-control|quality control]], and the physical phenomena that become dominant at small scales. The transition from laboratory research to commercial production at nanometer scales requires significant capital investment in specialized facilities and equipment.
