# Cohesion

Cohesion is the fundamental property that binds particles, elements, or parts of a substance together into a unified, stable structure. At the molecular level, this phenomenon arises from attractive forces between like molecules, including van der Waals forces, [[concepts/hydrogen-bonding|hydrogen bonding]], and covalent or ionic interactions. These intermolecular forces resist separation and maintain the material's [[concepts/honesty|integrity]], ensuring that the substance remains a coherent whole rather than dispersing into its constituent parts.

The strength of these cohesive forces directly determines the physical state and macroscopic behaviour of matter. In liquids, strong cohesion manifests as surface tension, allowing the surface to resist external force and enabling phenomena such as capillary action. In solids, cohesive forces are sufficiently strong to lock particles into fixed positions, providing structural rigidity and resistance to deformation. The balance between cohesive forces within a substance and adhesive forces between different substances dictates how materials interact with their environment.

In computational contexts, particularly within [[concepts/cryptography|cryptography]] and [[concepts/open-source-philosophy|logic]], the concept is often adapted metaphorically to describe the integrity of data structures or the [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] of logical systems. High cohesion in [[concepts/software-engineering|software engineering]] or cryptographic protocols implies that components are tightly related and focused on a single, well-defined purpose, reducing complexity and enhancing [[concepts/security|security]]. Conversely, low cohesion suggests scattered responsibilities, which can lead to vulnerabilities or logical inconsistencies. Thus, whether in physical matter or abstract systems, cohesion serves as a critical metric for stability and [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]].
