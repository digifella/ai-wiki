# Ai

[[concepts/ai-technologies|Artificial Intelligence]] (AI) refers to computer systems designed to perform tasks that typically require [[concepts/human-intelligence|human intelligence]]. These tasks include [[concepts/learning|learning]] from experience, recognizing patterns, understanding language, and making decisions. AI systems [[concepts/range|range]] from narrow [[concepts/software|applications]] focused on specific tasks to broader systems with more general [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]].

## Large Language Models and Knowledge Graphs

[[concepts/large-language-model-llm|Large Language Models]] (LLMs) are a significant class of AI systems trained on vast amounts of [[concepts/text|text]] data. Recent developments have focused on integrating LLMs with [[concepts/knowledge-graph|knowledge graph]] technologies to enhance [[concepts/knowledge-bases|information retrieval]] and [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]]. The [[concepts/etl-framework|Cocoindex framework]] represents one approach to building real-time [[concepts/knowledge-graphs|knowledge graphs]] from document collections, using LLMs to extract and [[concepts/structure|structure]] information while leveraging [[concepts/graph-databases|graph databases]] like [[entities/neo4j|Neo4j]] to store and query the resulting knowledge structures.

## Transparency and Accountability

As AI systems become more prevalent in [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] contexts, transparency in [[concepts/ai-development|AI development]] and [[concepts/deployment|deployment]] has become increasingly important. This includes clear documentation of how [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] are trained, what data they use, and how their outputs should be interpreted. Questions of transparency are particularly relevant for systems that aggregate or generate information from multiple sources, where the provenance and [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] of outputs affect their usefulness and trustworthiness.
