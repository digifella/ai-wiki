# Perkins Brailler

The **[[concepts/perkins-brailler|Perkins Brailler]]** is a mechanical braille writer designed by [[entities/paul|Paul]] H. Franklin at the Perkins School for the Blind in 1951. It replaced earlier, heavier models with a compact, durable design that became the standard for tactile [[concepts/writing|writing]] among the [[entities/visually-impaired|visually impaired]].

## Design & Mechanics
- **Mechanism**: Uses a system of levers and linkages to punch six pins into paper, creating braille characters.
- **Keys**: Six main keys correspond to the six dots of a braille cell; additional keys for space, shift, and number sign.
- **Advantages**: Lightweight compared to predecessors, robust construction, and standardized production.

## Historical Context
- Predecessed by the American Braille Writer and other bulky devices.
- Revolutionized [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]] by making braille writing portable and affordable.
