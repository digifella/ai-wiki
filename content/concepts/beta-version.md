# Beta Version

A beta version is a pre-release [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] of [[concepts/software|software]] made available to a limited audience for [[concepts/testing|testing]] purposes before official public launch. Beta versions typically contain most or all intended features of the final product but may still include bugs, performance issues, or incomplete functionality. The beta [[concepts/phase|phase]] represents an intermediate stage in [[concepts/coding|software development]], positioned after alpha testing (internal testing) and before the stable release that becomes available to the general public.

## Purpose and Function

Beta versions serve as a practical testing ground where developers can evaluate [[concepts/software-performance|software performance]] across diverse [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] configurations, operating systems, and [[concepts/scenarios|use cases]]. Users who participate in beta testing provide [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] that developers use to identify and resolve problems before general release. This crowdsourced approach to [[concepts/quality-assurance|quality assurance]] allows teams to catch issues that internal testing may have missed and to prioritize fixes based on real-world usage patterns.

## Limitations and Expectations

[[concepts/beta-software|Beta software]] often comes with explicit limitations and disclaimers. Features may be incomplete, documentation incomplete, or performance suboptimal. Users should expect that beta versions may crash, lose data, or behave unpredictably. Organizations typically restrict beta access to users who can tolerate instability and provide constructive feedback, rather than those requiring production-ready [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]]. The decision to use beta software involves accepting these risks in exchange for early access to new functionality and the opportunity to [[concepts/power|influence]] the final product's development.
