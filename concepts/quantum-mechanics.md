# Quantum Mechanics

Quantum mechanics is the fundamental [[concepts/theory|theory]] describing the behavior of matter and energy at atomic and subatomic [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]]. Developed primarily in the early twentieth century through the work of physicists including Max [[entities/planck|Planck]], Werner Heisenberg, and Erwin Schrödinger, it replaces classical Newtonian mechanics in regimes where objects are extremely small or energies are very high. The theory successfully explains phenomena such as atomic structure, chemical [[concepts/link-aggregation|bonding]], and the behavior of electrons in solids, which classical [[concepts/physics|physics]] cannot account for.

## Key Principles

Central to quantum mechanics is the principle that particles do not have definite properties until measured, described by the wave function and its collapse upon observation. Heisenberg's uncertainty principle establishes [[concepts/fundamental-limits|fundamental limits]] on how precisely certain pairs of [[concepts/material-properties|physical properties]] can be simultaneously known. The theory operates probabilistically rather than deterministically, predicting the likelihood of measurement outcomes rather than their certainty.
