# Note Taking Software Migration

[[concepts/note-taking-software|Note taking software]] migration refers to the process of transferring [[concepts/notes|notes]], documents, and organizational structures from one platform to another. This typically occurs when users become dissatisfied with their current provider due to [[concepts/pricing|pricing]] changes, feature limitations, reduced functionality, or shifts in service focus. The migration process involves selecting a suitable alternative, exporting existing data, reformatting content as needed, and establishing new workflows on the destination platform.

## Triggers for Migration

Common reasons for migration include significant pricing increases, deprecation of features users rely on, changes to [[concepts/data-ownership|data ownership]] [[concepts/policies|policies]], or concerns about platform stability and longevity. The decision to migrate often represents a cost-benefit calculation where users weigh [[concepts/vendor-lock-in|switching costs]] against ongoing dissatisfaction with their current service. Some migrations occur gradually as users test alternative platforms, while others happen quickly when service changes force immediate action.

## Technical and Organizational Challenges

The migration process presents several practical challenges. Most platforms offer export functionality, but exported data may require reformatting to work properly in a new system. Users must decide how to handle organizational structures such as [[concepts/notebook-tools|notebooks]], tags, and hierarchies, which may not map directly between platforms. Establishing new workflows and retraining oneself to use different interfaces takes time, and users may [[concepts/experience|experience]] temporary [[concepts/productivity|productivity]] losses during the transition period.

## Selection of Alternative Platforms

When evaluating alternatives, users typically consider factors including pricing models, available features, data portability options, platform stability, and integration capabilities with other tools they use. Popular alternatives to departing platforms often include self-hosted solutions, privacy-focused services, and established competitors. The choice of destination platform often depends on whether the user prioritizes [[concepts/leftover-utilization|cost savings]], specific features, data [[concepts/privacy|privacy]], or ecosystem integration.
