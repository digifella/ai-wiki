# Low Energy Nuclear Reactions

**Low Energy Nuclear Reactions** ([[concepts/lenr|LENR]]), often synonymous with **[[concepts/cold-fusion]]**, refers to a hypothesized type of nuclear reaction that occurs at or near room temperature, contrasting with [[concepts/high-energy-physics]] and conventional [[concepts/nuclear-fusion]] which requires extreme temperatures and pressures. The field remains highly controversial within mainstream [[concepts/physics|physics]] due to historical reproducibility issues and a lack of a widely accepted [[concepts/theory|theoretical framework]].

## Core Concepts
- **Definition**: Nuclear reactions producing excess heat without significant neutron or [[entities/gamma|gamma]] radiation, claimed to occur in electrochemical cells or metal-hydrogen systems.
- **Mechanism**: Hypotheses involve lattice confinement, phonon-assisted tunneling, or unknown physics beyond the [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|Standard Model]], though none are universally accepted.
- **Distinction**: Differentiated from [[concepts/fusion-power|Thermonuclear Fusion]] by operating conditions (ambient vs. plasma) and claimed byproduct profiles (heat/light elements vs. high-energy radiation).

## Critical Assessment and Investment Landscape (2026)
Recent scrutiny has intensified regarding the validity of breakthrough claims and the allocation of capital in the sector. Key insights from contemporary analysis include:

- **Skepticism of Breakthroughs**: Analysis by physicists such as [[entities/sabine-hossenfelder]] highlights the complexity and often misleading nature of reported "breakthroughs" in LENR [[lab-notes/2026-05-25-Critical-Assessment-of-Cold-Fusion-LENR-Investments-and|Critical Assessment of Cold Fusion (LENR) Investments and Breakthrough Claims]].
- **Funding Surge**: Despite historical stigma, there is a noted increase in private and speculative funding for LENR research, driven by the promise of unlimited [[concepts/clean-power|clean energy]] rather than robust [[concepts/empirical-evidence|empirical evidence]].
- **Replication Issues**: Core criticism remains the inability to independently replicate excess heat results under controlled conditions, a primary criterion for scientific validity.

## Historical Context
- **1989 Pons-Fleischmann [[concepts/scientific-experiment|Experiment]]**: The announcement of cold fusion by [[entities/martin-keen|Martin]] Fleischmann and Stanley Pons sparked initial global interest but collapsed due to failed replications by major institutions.
- **Stigma and Revival**: The field was largely marginalized in academia but persisted in fringe research and private industry, leading to the current [[concepts/terminology|terminology]] shift from "Cold Fusion" to "LENR" to distance from past controversies.
