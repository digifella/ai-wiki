# System 2 thinking

**System 2 thinking** refers to the slow, effortful, logical, and rule-governed mode of cognition. It is characterized by deliberate [[concepts/attention-mechanism|attention]], complex [[concepts/computation|computation]], and self-control, contrasting sharply with the fast, automatic, and intuitive System 1 thinking.

In the context of [[concepts/artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] and software engineering, the distinction between System 1 (fast, pattern-matching) and System 2 (slow, [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]]) is critical for designing effective automation pipelines.

## Core Characteristics
- **Deliberate and Effortful:** Requires significant cognitive load and attention.
- **Logical and Rule-Based:** Follows explicit algorithms and step-by-step deduction.
- **Slow:** Computationally expensive and time-consuming compared to intuitive responses.
- **Error-Prone under Load:** Performance degrades significantly when cognitive resources are depleted.

## Application in AI and Automation
Recent developments in AI architecture emphasize the need to balance fast, low-cost [[concepts/ai-inference|inference]] with slow, high-accuracy reasoning.

- **The Reasoning vs. Automation Paradigm:** While frontier labs have historically focused on scaling "reasoning" capabilities, practical [[concepts/software-automation|software automation]] often requires a different approach.
- **Jev and [[entities/typesafe-ai|TypeSafe AI]]:** The concept of **Jev** introduces a "[[concepts/system-1-classification|System 1 Classification]]" model specifically designed for software automation. This approach contrasts with the current industry trend of prioritizing heavy reasoning models for all tasks.
    - Jev focuses on efficient classification and action execution rather than deep deliberative reasoning.
    - This aligns with the need for high-throughput, low-latency AI agents in software development workflows.
