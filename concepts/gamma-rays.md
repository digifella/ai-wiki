# Gamma Rays

[[entities/gamma|Gamma]] [[concepts/rays|rays]] represent the highest-energy form of [[concepts/light|electromagnetic radiation]], characterized by wavelengths shorter than one trillionth of a meter. They are generated through various [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]], including radioactive decay, nuclear reactions, and highly energetic cosmic phenomena. Due to their extreme energy and penetrating power, gamma rays can traverse most materials, which makes them valuable tools for scientific observation but also presents significant radiation hazards to biological tissue.

In [[concepts/astrophysics|astrophysics]], gamma rays observed from space originate from diverse high-energy sources such as [[concepts/supernovae|supernovae]], [[concepts/neutron-stars|neutron stars]], [[concepts/black-holes|black holes]], and [[concepts/active-galactic-nuclei|active galactic nuclei]]. These emissions provide critical insights into violent cosmic processes and the behavior of matter under extreme conditions. The study of these signals allows researchers to map the distribution of high-energy events across the universe and understand the dynamics of [[concepts/stellar-evolution|stellar evolution]] and compact object interactions.

Recent research has focused on analyzing gamma ray emissions from the [[concepts/milky-way|Milky Way]] to investigate the nature of [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]]. Specifically, scientists are examining these signals for potential evidence of [[concepts/wimp|Weakly Interacting Massive Particle]] (WIMP) annihilation. By identifying specific spectral features or excesses in the gamma ray background, researchers aim to distinguish between standard astrophysical processes and potential signatures of dark matter interactions, contributing to the ongoing effort to identify the [[concepts/writing|composition]] of the universe's missing mass.
