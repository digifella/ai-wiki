**[[concepts/digital-health|Digital health]] literacy** is the ability to seek, find, understand, and appraise health information from electronic sources, and apply this knowledge to addressing or solving a health problem. It encompasses the intersection of health-literacy, media-literacy, and information-literacy within digital environments.

## Core Competencies
- **Navigation**: Locating reliable health resources online.
- **Evaluation**: Appraising credibility and relevance of digital health content.
- **Application**: Using digital tools (eHR, [[concepts/apps|apps]], [[concepts/telehealth|telehealth]]) to manage personal health.
- **Communication**: Engaging with [[concepts/health|healthcare]] providers via digital channels.

## Recent Developments & Regional Context (2026)

Recent initiatives highlight the evolution of digital literacy from patient-facing [[concepts/skills|skills]] to broader workforce capabilities.

- **NHS Person-Centred Framework**: The document [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Improving-digital-literacy---NHS|Improving digital literacy - NHS]] outlines strategies developed by [[concepts/health-promotion|Health Education]] [[entities/united-kingdom|England]] in collaboration with the Royal College of Nursing. Key focuses include:
    - Enhancing digital capabilities for nursing staff and [[concepts/health-care|healthcare]] [[entities/employees|workers]] in England.
    - Aligning digital health education with [[concepts/person-centred-care|person-centred care]] models.
    - Addressing workforce [[concepts/digital-readiness|digital readiness]] to support effective [[concepts/telehealth|telehealth]] and remote patient engagement.

- **Regional Policy Implications**: While earlier frameworks focused heavily on patient autonomy in Australia and New Zealand, recent UK developments (2020-2026) emphasize institutional support structures and professional digital competency as prerequisites for equitable digital health access.
