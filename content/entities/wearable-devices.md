# Wearable Devices

Wearable devices are electronic instruments designed to be worn directly on the body, typically incorporating [[concepts/embedded-sensors|embedded sensors]] that collect physiological or [[concepts/exercise|movement]] data. These devices have become increasingly sophisticated beyond consumer fitness trackers, finding significant [[concepts/software|applications]] in [[concepts/allied-health|allied health]] settings where [[concepts/continuous-monitoring|continuous monitoring]] and objective data collection are clinically valuable.

In allied health contexts, wearable devices serve diagnostic and therapeutic purposes. They can monitor vital signs, movement patterns, gait analysis, and activity levels, providing practitioners with objective measures that inform assessment, treatment planning, and progress tracking. Common applications include accelerometers and gyroscopes for movement analysis, heart rate monitors, and pressure sensors used by physiotherapists, occupational therapists, and other [[concepts/health|health]] professionals.

The primary advantage of [[concepts/internet-of-things|wearables]] in clinical practice is their ability to capture real-world data outside controlled clinical environments. This longitudinal data collection enables practitioners to understand how clients function in daily life rather than relying solely on in-clinic observations. The continuous [[entities/nature|nature]] of monitoring also supports objective documentation of functional changes over time, which is essential for evidence-based practice and outcomes measurement in allied health.
