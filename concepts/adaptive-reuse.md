# Adaptive Reuse

Adaptive reuse is the practice of converting existing buildings or structures to serve new purposes while retaining their original architectural and historical character. Rather than demolishing outdated or underutilized structures, this approach preserves built [[concepts/cultural-heritage|cultural heritage]] and directs resources toward maintaining culturally significant spaces. It allows cities to retain their architectural identity while meeting contemporary functional needs, serving as a key strategy in urban conservation.

The concept emphasizes the [[concepts/resilience|resilience]] of the built environment by extending the lifecycle of historic structures. By repurposing these buildings, architects and planners can address modern requirements for space and utility without erasing the historical [[concepts/storytelling|narrative]] embedded in the physical fabric. This method reduces the environmental impact associated with new construction and demolition, aligning sustainability goals with [[concepts/preservation|heritage preservation]].

A notable example of this practice is the preservation and resilience of the [[concepts/historic-banking-chamber|historic Banking Chamber]] at [[concepts/melbourne-cbd-architecture|333 Collins Street]] in [[concepts/melbourne-region|Melbourne]]. The building demonstrates how significant interior spaces can be maintained and integrated into modern commercial use, ensuring that the architectural [[concepts/honesty|integrity]] of the site remains intact. Such projects illustrate the balance between honoring [[concepts/historical-significance|historical significance]] and adapting to the evolving demands of urban [[concepts/infrastructure|infrastructure]].
