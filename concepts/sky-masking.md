# Sky Masking

Sky masking is a selective editing technique in Adobe Lightroom that isolates the sky portion of an image to apply targeted adjustments without affecting other elements. By creating a mask around the sky, photographers can modify exposure, contrast, saturation, and color independently from the foreground and middle ground. This approach is particularly valuable in landscape photography, where skies often require different treatment than the land below to achieve balanced, visually compelling results.

## Challenges with Foreground Elements

A common difficulty in sky masking occurs when tree branches, vegetation, or other foreground elements extend into the sky area. These intricate edges create irregularities that standard masking tools struggle to follow precisely, potentially resulting in halos around the branches or unwanted adjustments bleeding into the foreground. The fine details of natural elements make automatic selection methods prone to error.

## Practical Solutions

Several approaches can improve sky masks in the presence of foreground obstacles. Manual refinement using Lightroom's adjustment brush allows editors to paint masks with greater control around complex edges. The Dehaze slider can help separate the sky from branches through tonal differences. Additionally, using Lightroom's range mask based on luminance or color helps distinguish sky tones from darker foreground elements. Some photographers also refine masks in external editing software for maximum precision before returning to Lightroom for final adjustments.
