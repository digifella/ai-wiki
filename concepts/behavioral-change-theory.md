# Behavioral Change Theory

[[concepts/behavioral-adaptation|Behavioral Change]] [[concepts/theory|Theory]] is a scientific framework designed to understand how individuals modify their behaviors and to design interventions that support such modifications. The theory integrates principles from [[concepts/psychology|psychology]], [[concepts/neuroscience|neuroscience]], and social [[concepts/science|science]] to explain the factors influencing behavioral decisions and the [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] through which change occurs. Rather than attributing behavior solely to individual willpower or treating it as fixed, the theory recognizes that behavior emerges from complex interactions between personal factors, environmental conditions, and social contexts.

In the context of [[concepts/digital-health|digital health]] and [[concepts/mental-health|mental health]], this framework provides the theoretical basis for structured interventions. A notable application is the [[concepts/jungian-grief-model|SES One Intervention]], developed at the University of Copenhagen. This intervention utilizes the theory to address specific health behaviors by leveraging digital tools to facilitate sustained change, demonstrating how theoretical models can be operationalized in practical, technology-mediated settings.

The approach emphasizes that effective change requires addressing multiple levels of influence simultaneously. By mapping the interplay between internal [[concepts/human-cognition|cognitive processes]] and external environmental cues, practitioners can create more robust support systems. This multidisciplinary perspective allows for the development of interventions that are not only [[entities/theoretically-media|theoretically]] sound but also adaptable to the diverse needs of individuals in real-[[entities/earth|world]] [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]].
