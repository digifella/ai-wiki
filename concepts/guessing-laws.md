# Guessing Laws

Guessing Laws refers to the initial [[concepts/phase|phase]] of [[concepts/feynman|Richard Feynman]]’s three-step scientific method, wherein a [[entities/tomasz-janowski|researcher]] formulates a hypothesis or educated conjecture regarding the operation of a natural phenomenon. This step prioritizes the active proposal of a tentative explanation over passive data collection, requiring the scientist to synthesize existing [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|theoretical frameworks]], prior observations, and established knowledge into a coherent [[concepts/user-attention-prediction|prediction]].

The concept emphasizes that [[concepts/scientific-method|scientific inquiry]] does not begin with a blank slate but with an informed guess grounded in current understanding. This hypothesis serves as the necessary foundation for the subsequent steps of the method: rigorous [[concepts/computation|computation]] and comparison with empirical observations. By establishing a specific prediction first, the researcher can design targeted experiments to test the validity of the proposed law, thereby driving the [[concepts/iterative-refinement|iterative process]] of [[concepts/scientific-discovery|scientific discovery]].
