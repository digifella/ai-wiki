# Number Theory

Number theory is a branch of pure [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] that studies the properties, [[concepts/relationships|relationships]], and patterns of integers. Historically one of the oldest mathematical disciplines, it examines fundamental questions about whole numbers, divisibility, [[concepts/prime-numbers|prime numbers]], and integer solutions to equations. Number theory combines abstract [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] with concrete computational investigation, making it both [[entities/theoretically-media|theoretically]] profound and practically applicable.

## Historical Development and Core Areas

The field traces its roots to ancient civilizations, with systematic study formalized by Greek mathematicians like Euclid. Modern number theory encompasses several major branches: elementary number theory focuses on divisibility and [[concepts/prime-lens|prime]] properties; analytic number theory applies calculus and complex analysis to integer problems; [[concepts/algebraic_number|algebraic number]] [[concepts/theory|theory]] studies [[concepts/number-systems|number systems]] beyond ordinary integers; and additive number theory examines sums and partitions of integers. The [[concepts/riemann-hypothesis|Riemann Hypothesis]], concerning the [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] of prime numbers, remains one of mathematics' most significant [[concepts/unsolved-problems|unsolved problems]].

## Applications and Connections

While traditionally considered pure mathematics, number theory has become increasingly important in applied contexts, particularly in [[concepts/cryptography|cryptography]] and information [[concepts/security|security]]. Prime numbers form the mathematical foundation of many encryption systems protecting digital communications. Number theory also intersects with other mathematical domains, including abstract algebra, topology, and dynamical systems, demonstrating the interconnected [[entities/nature|nature]] of modern mathematics.
