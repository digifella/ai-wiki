# Optics

Optics is the branch of physics that studies light and its interactions with matter. It examines how light propagates through space and various media, and how it behaves when encountering surfaces and materials. The field encompasses the entire electromagnetic spectrum from ultraviolet through visible to infrared radiation, though it historically focused on visible light. Optics bridges fundamental physics theory with practical applications in everyday technology, from eyeglasses and cameras to fiber optics and laser systems.

## Core Phenomena

The fundamental phenomena studied in optics include reflection, refraction, diffraction, and polarization. Reflection occurs when light bounces off a surface, while refraction describes the bending of light as it passes between media of different optical densities. Diffraction refers to the bending of light around obstacles or through narrow openings, and polarization describes the orientation of light's electric field oscillations. These phenomena arise from the wave nature of light and its interaction with matter at various scales.

## Branches and Applications

Optics divides into geometric (or ray) optics and wave optics, depending on the scale of phenomena being studied. Geometric optics treats light as rays traveling in straight lines and is sufficient for explaining lenses, mirrors, and simple optical systems. Wave optics accounts for light's wave properties and is necessary to explain interference, diffraction, and other phenomena that depend on light's wavelength. Modern optics also encompasses quantum aspects of light through quantum electrodynamics, describing photons and their interaction with matter.

Practical applications of optics are extensive and fundamental to modern technology. These include corrective lenses for vision, microscopes and telescopes for magnification, optical fibers for telecommunications, lasers for manufacturing and medicine, and photonic devices for computing and sensors. Understanding optical principles remains essential for developing new technologies in energy, information processing, and medical imaging.
