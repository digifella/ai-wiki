# Aristotelian Philosophy

**Aristotelian [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]]** encompasses the diverse works and [[concepts/ideas|ideas]] of Aristotle, forming a comprehensive system that influenced [[concepts/western-thought|Western thought]] for centuries. It stands in [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] to and builds upon Platonism, emphasizing empirical observation and logical analysis over pure abstract idealism.

## Core Pillars

### Logic and Epistemology
- **Organon**: The foundation of Aristotelian [[concepts/open-source-philosophy|logic]], including syllogistic [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]].
- **Empiricism**: Knowledge derives from sensory [[concepts/experience|experience]]; [[concepts/the-mind|the mind]] is a *tabula rasa* at birth.
- **Four [[concepts/causes|Causes]]**: Explanation of existence via Material, Formal, Efficient, and Final causes.

### Metaphysics
- **Substance and Form**: Reality consists of individual substances (hylomorphism) rather than abstract Forms.
- **Actuality and Potentiality**: Change is the [[concepts/exercise|movement]] from potential to actual state.
- **Unmoved Mover**: The ultimate cause of all motion, pure actuality and thought.

### Ethics and Politics
- **[[concepts/positive-psychology|Eudaimonia]]**: The highest human good, often translated as flourishing or [[concepts/happiness|happiness]], achieved through virtue.
- **Golden Mean**: Virtue is the intermediate state between two extremes (excess and deficiency).
- **Civic Engagement**: Humans are *zoon politikon* (political animals); [[concepts/ethics|ethics]] cannot be separated from the Polity.

### Physics and Natural Science
- **Teleology**: Nature acts for a purpose (*telos*).
- **[[concepts/cosmology|Cosmology]]**: A geocentric universe with distinct terrestrial and celestial spheres.

## Historical Context & Legacy
- Systematized knowledge into distinct disciplines (biology, [[concepts/physics|physics]], ethics, etc.).
- Heavily influenced Scholasticism and medieval Christian theology (e.g., Thomas Aquinas).
- Subject to critique by Modern Philosophy (e.g., Francis Bacon, Isaac Newton) regarding scientific methodology.
