## Searching For Meaning In Randomness
**Clip title:** Searching For Meaning In Randomness
**Author / channel:** The Rest Is Science
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tiXIOpq_tQ0

### Summary
In this video, podcast hosts Hannah Fry and Michael Steeples discuss randomness, entropy, and how meaning emerges from chaos. They explore how humans are inherently meaning-making creatures who seek patterns and order in a chaotic universe. They also discuss how science uses randomness to understand the world and how we can find meaning in the universe despite its inherent randomness.

The hosts delve into the concept of randomness and entropy, noting that while things may appear random at a microscopic level, they can become predictable when viewed from a larger perspective. They use examples like the Big Bang and the distribution of words in language to illustrate this concept. They also discuss how humans are driven to find meaning and patterns in data and how this drive can lead to insights or biases.

Overall, the video offers a fascinating exploration of the relationship between randomness, order, and meaning. It highlights the complex ways in which science and human psychology interact to make sense of the universe, suggesting that meaning is not something inherent in the universe, but rather something we create by imposing order on chaos.

### Video Description & Links
#### Description
What do we mean when we call an event random? 

Most people view randomness as a fundamental property of the universe, but is it just a label for our own lack of knowledge? Whether it is a weighted coin toss, a scratch card, or the digits of Pi, unpredictability usually emerges from rules and patterns that sit just beyond our perception. 

Professor Hannah Fry and Michael Stevens dismantle the logic of chance, exploring how chaos is governed by strict mathematical laws and why a coin can be '50 / 50' until the moment you apply the laws of physics to its flight.

How does probability measure what we cannot see rather than what will happen. Why do patterns inevitably emerge when we zoom out, how minute uncertainties allowed for the formation of galaxies, and why is the human mind is so determined to find meaning in a world built on statistical mechanics.
