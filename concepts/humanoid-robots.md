# Humanoid Robots

Humanoid robots are mobile [[concepts/robotics|robotic systems]] engineered with a body structure that mimics human anatomy, typically comprising a torso, head, arms, and legs. This specific form factor allows them to operate effectively in environments originally designed for human [[entities/employees|workers]], such as factories, construction sites, and homes. By retaining human-like proportions, these robots can utilize existing tools and navigate standard spaces without the need for specialized [[concepts/infrastructure|infrastructure]] [[concepts/adaptations|adaptations]], enabling direct integration into current workflows.

The primary advantage of the humanoid design lies in its ability to perform tasks requiring human-like dexterity and mobility. This capability makes them suitable for a wide range of applications, including manufacturing, [[concepts/health|healthcare]], and domestic assistance. Because they can interact with human-centric objects and environments, they offer a versatile [[concepts/solution|solution]] for automating complex physical tasks that are difficult to address with specialized, single-purpose machines.

Recent advancements in [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] have significantly enhanced the control and autonomy of these systems. For instance, [[concepts/nvidia-sonic|NVIDIA Sonic]] is an [[concepts/ai-system|AI system]] developed to facilitate the teleoperated control of humanoid robots, improving the [[concepts/accuracy|precision]] and responsiveness of remote operations. Such technologies aim to bridge the gap between remote human input and real-time robotic execution, addressing challenges related to latency and complex motor control in dynamic environments.
