# Assistive Devices

Assistive devices are tools, instruments, or systems designed to maintain, improve, or facilitate the functional independence and [[concepts/quality-of-life|quality of life]] for individuals with disabilities. These technologies span physical, sensory, and cognitive domains, enabling users to perform tasks that might otherwise be difficult or impossible.

## Key Categories

### Visual Impairment Tools
*   **[[concepts/braille|Braille]] [[concepts/writing|Writing]] Instruments**: Mechanical devices allowing tactile writing of Braille characters.
    *   See [[concepts/perkins-brailler|Perkins Brailler]]: Historical Overview, Mechanical Design, and Braille's Evolution for detailed analysis.
    *   The **[[entities/perkins-brailler|Perkins Brailler]]** stands as a pivotal adaptive writing technology, combining historical significance with robust mechanical design to enable efficient Braille production.
*   **Screen Readers**: Software that converts digital text to synthesized speech or Braille output.
*   **Magnification Aids**: Optical or digital tools (e.g., CCTV, screen zoom) to enlarge visual content.

### Mobility Aids
*   **Wheelchairs**: Manual and power-assisted chairs for locomotion.
*   **Prosthetics**: Artificial limbs replacing missing body parts.
*   **Walkers and Canes**: Devices providing stability and balance support.

### Hearing Impairment Tools
*   **Hearing Aids**: Devices amplifying sound waves.
*   **Cochlear Implants**: Electronic devices directly stimulating the auditory nerve.

## Design Principles
*   **[[concepts/inclusive-design|Universal Design]]**: Creating products usable by all people to the greatest extent possible.
*   **User-Centricity**: [[concepts/customization|Customization]] for individual physical and cognitive needs.
*   **Durability**: Robust construction to withstand frequent use.
