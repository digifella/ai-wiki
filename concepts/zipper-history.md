# Zipper History

## Overview
The zipper is a ubiquitous fastener used in numerous applications including garments, luggage, and pouches. Its invention marked a significant advancement over earlier fastening methods such as [[concepts/buttons|buttons]], [[concepts/hooks|hooks]], and eyelets.

### Key Concepts
- **Invention Timeline**: Early 20th century to present.
- **Mechanism**: Consists of two flexible strips, each containing [[concepts/interlocking-teeth|interlocking teeth]] that can be pulled together or slid apart by means of a slider mechanism.
- **Variants**: Coil [[concepts/zippers|zippers]], [[concepts/metal|metal]] zippers, invisible zippers.

### Historical Milestones
- **Early 1900s**: Elias Howe invents the "Automatic Continuous Clothing Closure," but it fails commercially.
- **1913**: Gideon Sundback introduces his design which becomes more successful and closely resembles modern zippers.
