# Electronic Engine Control

**[[concepts/engine-management-systems|Electronic Engine Control]]** refers to the use of ECU and sensors to manage [[concepts/engine-thrust|engine performance]], emissions, and efficiency. Contrary to the popular "too computerized" myth, modern engines have not been fully mechanical for decades, and this transition is fundamentally beneficial.

## Key Insights & History
*   **Dispelling the "Too Computerized" Myth:** The perception that modern cars are overly reliant on fragile electronics is a misconception. Electronic control has been a standard, fundamental feature for decades [[lab-notes/2026-09-29-Electronic-Engine-Control-Benefits-History-and-Dispellin|Electronic Engine Control: Benefits, History, and Dispelling "Too Computerized" Myth]].
*   **[[concepts/historical-context|Historical Context]]:** Engines transitioned from purely [[concepts/hardware|mechanical systems]] to electronic control well before the current era, making the "purely mechanical" past a relatively short chapter in automotive history.
*   **Benefits:** Electronic control enables precise management of [[concepts/fuel-injection|fuel injection]], ignition timing, and emissions, leading to improved efficiency and [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] compared to older mechanical carburetor systems.
