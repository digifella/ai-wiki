# Observable Universe

The observable universe is a spherical volume centered on the observer, comprising all matter that can be observed from Earth [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] present time, due to the finite [[concepts/speed|speed]] of [[concepts/light|light]] and the age of the universe. The current size of this sphere is approximately 93 billion light-years in diameter.

## Notable Facts
- [[concepts/only-39-digits-of-pi-are-necessary-to-measure-the-circumference-of-the|Only 39 digits of Pi are necessary to measure the circumference of the observable universe]] with precision far beyond any measurable [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]]. This fact underscores both the mathematical and practical limits of Pi's application in cosmological measurements.
- The number of [[concepts/decimal-places|decimal places]] required for Pi increases dramatically when calculating extremely small [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]], such as atomic or subatomic dimensions, where more precision becomes crucial.
- The composition of the universe extends beyond what is directly observable, involving components like [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] and dark energy which constitute the vast majority of the total energy-mass budget.
