# Pi

Pi (π) is a mathematical constant representing the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter. Its value is approximately 3.14159, though it cannot be expressed exactly as a finite decimal or fraction. Pi is a [[concepts/transcendental-number|transcendental number]], meaning it is not the root of any polynomial equation with rational coefficients. As a result, its decimal expansion continues infinitely without repeating.

## Mathematical and Physical Applications

Pi appears throughout mathematics and physics in contexts far beyond basic circle geometry. It emerges in trigonometry, calculus, probability theory, and complex analysis. In physics, pi appears in formulas describing wave behaviour, quantum mechanics, and electromagnetic phenomena. The constant has been computed to trillions of decimal places through various algorithms, though only the first few dozen digits are needed for any practical calculation.

## Cultural and Fictional References

Beyond its mathematical significance, pi has occupied a place in popular culture and speculative fiction. Most notably, Carl Sagan's 1985 novel *Contact* uses pi as a plot device, suggesting that messages from an advanced civilization might be encoded in pi's digits when expressed in alternative number bases, such as base-11. This concept plays on the notion that pi's seemingly random digit sequence might contain hidden structure or meaning, though this remains purely fictional speculation.
