# Perfect Scrambled Eggs

Perfect scrambled eggs represent a cooking technique aimed [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] achieving a specific, desirable [[concepts/texture|texture]]—typically creamy and custard-like with small, delicate curds rather than the rubbery or overly dry result of overcooking. The concept centers on balancing heat, timing, and technique to control protein coagulation in eggs, which begins around 63°C (145°F) for [[concepts/whites|whites]] and 65°C (149°F) for yolks.

## Key Variables

The primary factors influencing the outcome are heat level, [[concepts/cooking-time|cooking duration]], and constant agitation. Low to [[entities/medium|medium]] heat allows gradual, even cooking that produces smaller curds and a silkier final texture. Continuous [[concepts/stirring-technique|stirring]] encourages uniform heat [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] and prevents large clumps from forming. Some approaches involve removing eggs from heat slightly before they appear fully set, relying on carryover cooking to achieve the final [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]].

## Method Variations

Different culinary traditions and individual preferences create variations in the approach. The French method typically uses low heat, butter, and patience to create very soft, almost custard-like results. Other approaches use slightly higher heat with more frequent stirring for a faster outcome. The addition of cream, milk, or other ingredients can [[concepts/power|influence]] both texture and richness, though some practitioners argue this obscures the pure [[concepts/egg-flavor|egg flavor]].
