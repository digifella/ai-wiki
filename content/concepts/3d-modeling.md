# 3D Modeling

3D modeling is the digital process of creating three-dimensional representations of objects, environments, or characters using specialized software. A 3D model consists of vertices, edges, and polygons arranged in virtual space to define shape, form, and surface properties. These models serve as the foundation for visualization, animation, simulation, and manufacturing across numerous industries.

## Techniques and Approaches

3D modelers use various methodologies depending on their goals and subject matter. Polygonal modeling, the most common approach, builds objects from interconnected geometric shapes. Sculpting software allows artists to work intuitively, pushing and pulling digital clay-like materials to create organic forms. Parametric modeling uses mathematical definitions and constraints to generate precise geometry, particularly useful in engineering and architectural applications. Point cloud modeling converts real-world scan data into 3D representations.

## Applications

3D models are fundamental to entertainment production including film, television, and video games. Architects and engineers use modeling for visualization, planning, and design iteration before physical construction. Product designers create digital prototypes for testing and presentation. Medical imaging, scientific research, and archaeological documentation increasingly rely on 3D modeling to capture and analyze complex structures. Manufacturing and 3D printing require precise digital models as production blueprints.

## Tools and Software

Industry-standard software includes Blender (open-source), Maya, Cinema 4D, and 3ds Max for general modeling and animation; ZBrush for digital sculpting; and specialized tools like CAD software for technical modeling. These platforms provide varying levels of complexity, from beginner-friendly interfaces to advanced professional environments with rendering, simulation, and animation capabilities.
