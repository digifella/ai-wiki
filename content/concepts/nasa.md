# NASA

The National Aeronautics and Space Administration ([[entities/nasa|NASA]]) is the [[entities/united-states|United States]] federal agency responsible for the civilian space program and aeronautics research. Established in 1958 during the Cold War space race, NASA consolidates American efforts in [[concepts/space-exploration|space exploration]], [[concepts/scientific-discovery|scientific discovery]], and aviation technology development. The agency reports to the executive branch and operates under Congressional oversight, with a mandate to conduct peaceful space activities for scientific and technological advancement.

## Mission and Operations

NASA's primary responsibilities include operating crewed and uncrewed space missions, maintaining the International Space Station, developing launch vehicles and spacecraft, and conducting Earth and [[concepts/planetary-science|planetary science]] research. The agency manages multiple research centers and facilities across the United States, employing thousands of civil servants, contractors, and researchers. Its [[concepts/software|programs]] span [[concepts/human-spaceflight|human spaceflight]], robotic exploration, Earth [[concepts/science|science]], [[concepts/astrophysics|astrophysics]], and aeronautics [[concepts/innovation|innovation]].

## Relationship to Commercial Space

NASA increasingly collaborates with private aerospace companies for space transportation and cargo delivery services. These partnerships, formalized through commercial cargo and crew programs, have reduced NASA's dependence on operating its own launch infrastructure while advancing the broader American space industry.
