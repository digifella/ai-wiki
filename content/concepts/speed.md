# Speed

Speed in the context of [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] refers to the [[concepts/inference|inference]] latency and response time of language models during execution. This metric is critical for real-world [[concepts/software|applications]] where users expect rapid [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] and where [[concepts/agents|agents]] must make timely decisions. Speed encompasses both the time required to process input and generate output, as well as the [[concepts/computational-efficiency|computational efficiency]] needed to achieve fast [[concepts/responses|responses]] at scale.

## Gemini 3 Flash

[[concepts/google-search|Google]]'s Gemini 3 Flash represents an approach to optimizing speed alongside cost and efficiency. Released in 2026, the model was designed as a lighter-weight alternative to larger variants, enabling faster inference while maintaining functional capability for a range of tasks. This design [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]] reflects a broader industry trend toward creating [[concepts/custom-models|specialized models]] that prioritize performance characteristics beyond raw benchmark scores.

## Practical Implications

For [[concepts/ai-productivity-agents|AI agent systems]], speed directly impacts [[concepts/user-experience-design|user experience]] and operational feasibility. Faster models enable agents to respond to time-sensitive queries, execute multiple [[concepts/reasoning-steps|reasoning steps]] within acceptable latency [[entities/windows|windows]], and operate within computational constraints of edge devices or real-time systems. The trade-off between speed and model capability remains a key consideration when selecting or designing agents for specific applications.
