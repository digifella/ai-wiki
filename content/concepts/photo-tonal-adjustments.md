# Photo Tonal Adjustments

Photo [[concepts/tonal-adjustments|tonal adjustments]] refer to modifications made to the brightness, [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], and color balance of digital photographs. These adjustments are fundamental to the post-processing [[concepts/workflow|workflow]] in photography and digital [[concepts/image-editing|image editing]]. By altering the tonal [[concepts/range|range]] of an image—from [[concepts/shadows|shadows]] and midtones to [[concepts/highlights|highlights]]—photographers can enhance visual impact, correct [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] problems, or achieve a specific artistic [[concepts/computer-vision|vision]].

## Camera Raw Filter in Photoshop

The [[concepts/camera-raw-filter|Camera Raw Filter]] in [[concepts/photoshop|Photoshop]] is a dedicated tool for making non-destructive tonal and color adjustments to photographs. It provides access to controls including exposure, highlights, shadows, whites, [[concepts/blacks|blacks]], clarity, and vibrance, allowing precise manipulation of how an image renders. The filter works on both raw image [[concepts/files|files]] and other formats, and adjustments can be modified or undone [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] any point without degrading image quality.

## Color Grading Applications

Color grading extends basic tonal adjustments to establish mood and aesthetic coherence across [[concepts/images|images]]. Through the Camera Raw Filter, photographers and editors can apply [[concepts/color-casts|color casts]], adjust individual color channels, and use split toning to add different colors to highlights and shadows. This process is essential in professional photography workflows, from [[concepts/portrait-retouching|portrait retouching]] to cinematic color treatment in editorial and commercial work.
