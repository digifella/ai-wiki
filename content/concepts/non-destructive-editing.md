# Non Destructive Editing

Non-destructive editing is a [[concepts/digital-imaging-workflow|digital imaging workflow]] that preserves the original pixel data of an image file while allowing unlimited [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] and modifications. Rather than permanently altering pixels, this approach applies changes as adjustable [[concepts/metadata|metadata]], separate layers, or [[concepts/instruction-sets|instruction sets]] that can be modified or removed at any time. This fundamental principle enables photographers and designers to experiment freely without risk of losing image quality or the ability to revisit earlier creative decisions.

## Core Mechanism

The workflow typically uses one of two technical approaches. Some [[concepts/software|applications]], like Lightroom, store all edits as metadata [[concepts/instructions|instructions]] separate from the source file, leaving the original image completely untouched. Others, like [[concepts/photoshop|Photoshop]], employ layered documents where adjustments exist on separate layers above the original image data. In both cases, the original pixel information remains intact and unmodified throughout the editing process.

## Practical Advantages

Non-destructive editing enables photographers to make significant changes—adjusting [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|color grading]], cropping, or applying filters—while maintaining the ability to revert to previous states or make corrections without image degradation. This approach is particularly valuable in professional workflows where client [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] may require substantial revisions, or when exploring multiple creative directions from a single source file. The separation of edits from source data also facilitates easier file management and archiving.
