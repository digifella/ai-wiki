# Genetic Disorders

Genetic disorders are medical conditions caused by abnormalities in an organism's DNA. These conditions arise from mutations in single genes, variations across multiple genes, or chromosomal abnormalities. Some genetic disorders follow predictable inheritance patterns and are passed from parents to offspring—such as autosomal dominant or recessive conditions—while others result from spontaneous mutations occurring during reproduction or early development. The severity and clinical [[concepts/presence|manifestation]] of genetic disorders vary widely, ranging from minor conditions with minimal [[concepts/health|health]] impact to severe, life-limiting diseases.

The classification of these disorders generally distinguishes between monogenic disorders, which result from a mutation in a single gene, and complex disorders influenced by multiple genetic and environmental factors. Chromosomal disorders involve changes in the number or structure of chromosomes, such as deletions, duplications, or translocations. Understanding the specific genetic basis of a disorder is crucial for accurate diagnosis, genetic [[concepts/offering-counseling-services|counseling]], and the development of targeted therapeutic interventions.

Recent advancements in [[concepts/gene-editing|gene-editing]] technologies have opened new avenues for addressing the [[concepts/causes|root causes]] of genetic disorders. CRISPR-Cas9 and successor approaches, developed and refined by researchers such as [[entities/feng-zhang|Feng Zhang]], allow for [[concepts/granular-control|precise modifications]] to the genome. These tools enable scientists to correct pathogenic mutations in vitro and are being actively investigated for potential clinical applications in treating conditions that were previously considered incurable.
