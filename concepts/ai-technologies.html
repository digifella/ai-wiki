AI Technologies

Overview

AI technologies encompass a broad range of tools and systems designed to perform tasks that typically require human intelligence, such as learning, reasoning, problem-solving, perception, and language understanding.

Key Areas

  • Machine Learning: Algorithms that improve through experience and data.
  • Natural Language Processing (NLP): Enables machines to understand and generate human language.
  • Computer Vision: Allows machines to interpret and make decisions based on visual input.
  • Robotics: Integration of AI with physical systems to automate tasks.

Applications

  • Healthcare: Diagnostic tools, treatment recommendations, and administrative efficiency.
  • Finance: Fraud detection, algorithmic trading, and customer service.
  • Transportation: Autonomous vehicles and route optimization.

Governance and Ethics

  • AI Ethics: Principles guiding the development and use of AI to ensure fairness, transparency, and accountability.
  • AI Oversight: Mechanisms to monitor and regulate AI systems to mitigate risks and ensure compliance with ethical standards.

Challenges

  • Bias and Fairness: Ensuring AI systems do not perpetuate or amplify existing biases.
  • Privacy Concerns: Balancing data utilization with individual privacy rights.
  • Regulatory Frameworks: Developing policies that keep pace with technological advancements.

Notes

  • New AI technologies are being rapidly adopted by healthcare professionals, yet existing organisational governance often lacks the processes necessary to oversee their safe and responsible use.
  • Previous AI governance frameworks have largely focused on high-level AI ethics principles, leaving healthcare organisations struggling to translate these principles into practice, assess risk, and embed AI oversight into existing processes.
  • This study aimed to develop and validate a practice-oriented AI governance framework.
