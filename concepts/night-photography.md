# Night Photography

Night photography encompasses the techniques and equipment used to capture images in low-[[concepts/light|light]] conditions, typically after sunset or in dimly lit environments. This practice requires understanding how cameras respond to reduced available light and involves deliberate adjustment of [[concepts/camera-settings|exposure settings]] to produce clear, usable images. The fundamental challenge lies in gathering sufficient light to create a properly exposed photograph while managing the technical constraints that low-light shooting presents.

## Technical Fundamentals

Successful night photography depends on manipulating the exposure triangle—aperture, shutter [[concepts/speed|speed]], and [[concepts/iso-settings|ISO sensitivity]]. A wider aperture allows more light through the lens, while longer shutter speeds extend the time light reaches the sensor. Higher ISO values increase sensor sensitivity but introduce [[concepts/digital-image-noise|digital noise]]. Photographers must balance these variables depending on whether they are photographing [[concepts/stationary-subjects|static subjects]] like landscapes or [[concepts/moving-subjects|moving subjects]] like people or traffic.

## Equipment and Tools

Dedicated night photography often employs a tripod to stabilize the camera during long exposures, eliminating motion blur caused by hand-holding. Fast lenses with wide apertures are valued for their light-gathering ability. Modern smartphones and dedicated camera [[concepts/apps|apps]], such as specialized computational photography tools, have made night photography more accessible by automatically optimizing settings for low-light capture.

## Common Applications

Night photography is used across multiple genres, including astrophotography for capturing [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]] and celestial events, urban photography of illuminated cityscapes, and [[concepts/long-exposure-techniques|long-exposure photography]] that captures light trails from vehicles or [[concepts/water-flow|water movement]]. Light painting and intentional camera movement are creative techniques employed by photographers working in darkness.
