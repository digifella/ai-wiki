# Clickable Prototyping

Clickable prototyping is an interactive [[concepts/design-thinking|design methodology]] that allows creators to construct functional, navigable models of digital products without requiring extensive [[concepts/coding|coding]]. These prototypes enable users to simulate interactions, test user flows, and validate design decisions prior to full-scale development. By bridging the gap between static visual mockups and fully functional applications, this approach facilitates efficient [[concepts/design-iteration|design iteration]], comprehensive user testing, and clearer communication with stakeholders.

The primary purpose of clickable prototypes is to provide a tangible representation of the [[concepts/user-experience-design|user experience]] during the early stages of development. They allow designers to identify usability issues and refine navigation structures before committing resources to [[entities/national-academies|engineering]]. This process supports rapid [[concepts/systems|feedback loops]], ensuring that design choices align with user needs and business objectives. Consequently, teams can reduce development risks and costs by addressing potential problems in the prototype [[concepts/phase|phase]] rather than during or after coding.

Tools such as [[concepts/gemini-30-pro|Google Stitch 2.0]] exemplify the evolution of this practice by offering accessible platforms for building these interactive models. Tutorials and walkthroughs, such as those provided by the [[entities/youtube|YouTube]] channel Teacher's Tech, demonstrate how designers can leverage such software to create realistic simulations. These resources highlight the practical application of clickable prototyping in modern UX workflows, emphasizing its role in streamlining the transition from concept to implementation.
