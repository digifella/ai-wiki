# Black And White

Concert photography presents distinctive color correction challenges due to the extreme and rapidly changing lighting conditions found in live music venues. Stage lighting systems commonly produce intense color casts in blues, reds, greens, and magentas that can overwhelm a camera's automatic white balance, resulting in images with severe color shifts. These problems are compounded by the dynamic nature of concert lighting, which may shift dramatically between shots or even within a single performance sequence.

To address these issues, photographers often utilize Adobe Lightroom Classic to manually correct white balance and neutralize unwanted tints. The process typically involves using the eyedropper tool on neutral gray areas or adjusting the temperature and tint sliders to counteract the dominant stage hues. This manual intervention is necessary because automatic algorithms frequently fail to interpret complex, multi-colored stage environments accurately.

Converting the final image to black and white is a common strategy to eliminate color correction difficulties entirely. By removing chromatic information, the photographer can focus on contrast, texture, and composition without worrying about color accuracy. This approach is particularly effective for high-contrast concert shots where the interplay of light and shadow is more critical than the specific hues of the lighting rig.

Effective execution requires balancing the loss of color information with the preservation of tonal range. Photographers must ensure that the conversion does not result in muddy midtones or crushed blacks, which can obscure details in the subject. Careful adjustment of individual color channel mixers during the conversion process allows for selective lightening or darkening of specific tones, ensuring the final monochrome image retains depth and clarity despite the original lighting chaos.
