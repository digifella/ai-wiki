# Plan Driven Development

Plan-driven development, commonly known as waterfall methodology, is a [[concepts/software-engineering|software engineering]] approach that emphasizes comprehensive upfront planning and sequential execution of distinct phases. The methodology structures development into clearly defined stages—typically requirements gathering, design, implementation, testing, and deployment—where each phase must be substantially completed before the next begins. This linear progression prioritizes predictability and control by establishing detailed specifications and documentation early in the development process.

## Key Characteristics

Plan-driven development relies on thorough requirements analysis and architectural design before coding begins. Teams create extensive documentation that serves as the blueprint for implementation, reducing ambiguity and providing clear acceptance criteria. Changes to requirements are discouraged once development is underway, as they can significantly impact schedules and budgets. This approach works best for projects with well-understood requirements, fixed scope, and stable environments where late-stage modifications are costly.

## Applications and Constraints

Plan-driven development has proven effective for large-scale projects with regulatory requirements, such as aerospace, defense, and financial systems, where comprehensive documentation and traceability are mandatory. However, the approach can struggle with projects requiring flexibility or rapid iteration, particularly when requirements are uncertain or customer needs evolve. The methodology's emphasis on upfront planning and sequential phases can result in longer time-to-delivery compared to more iterative approaches, though it may reduce rework and unexpected integration issues.
