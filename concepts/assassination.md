# Assassination

Targeted killing of a high-value individual (political, military, or cultural leader) executed for ideological, strategic, or state objectives rather than personal gain. Distinct from Murder, judicial Execution, or battlefield engagement due to premeditated targeting, operational secrecy, and systemic impact. Part of the broader spectrum of [[concepts/covert-operations|covert operations]] which includes non-lethal intelligence activities like [[entities/number-stations|Number Stations]]: Shortwave Espionage, Unbreakable Ciphers, Covert Operations.

## Core Characteristics
- **Motive:** Regime destabilization, [[concepts/leadership|leadership]] decapitation, ideological signaling, or tactical advantage in asymmetric [[concepts/conflict]].
- **Targets:** Heads of state, commanders, diplomats, revolutionary figures, or symbolic authorities.
- **Methods:** Assassination Tools & Methods, poison, firearms, explosives, proxy contracts, or modern cyber-physical strikes.
- **Aftermath:** Frequently triggers Political Violence, [[concepts/ecological-succession|succession]] crises, or escalation via Retaliation Cycles.

## Historical & Cultural Context
- Ancient precedents: Greek Thirty Tyrants, Roman Ides of March.
- Modern Era: Cold War proxy killings, contemporary drone strikes.

## Related Covert Operations: Signal Intelligence
Beyond kinetic actions, covert operations utilize [[concepts/espionage|espionage]] networks to maintain agent communication through steganographic means.
- **Number Stations:** [[concepts/shortwave-radio|Shortwave radio]] broadcasts containing encoded messages (numbers, letters, or words) transmitted by intelligence agencies to operatives in hostile territory. See detailed analysis in [[lab-notes/2026-06-17-Number-Stations-Shortwave-Espionage-Unbreakable-Ciphers|Number Stations: Shortwave Espionage, Unbreakable Ciphers, Covert Operations]].
- **Operational [[concepts/security|Security]]:** These stations serve as a deniable link, minimizing digital footprints compared to satellite or internet-based comms.
