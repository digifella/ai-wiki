# Color Wheels

## Definition
A Color Wheel is a [[concepts/visual-representation|visual representation]] of colors arranged by their chromatic relationship. It serves as a foundational tool in [[concepts/color-theory]], illustrating primary, secondary, and tertiary colors, and facilitating the understanding of color [[concepts/musical-harmony|harmony]], [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], and emotional impact in visual media.

## Theoretical Foundations
- **Chromatic [[concepts/relationships|Relationships]]**: Defines Complementary Colors, Analogous Colors, and Triadic Colors.
- **Color [[concepts/models|Models]]**: Distinctions between additive (RGB) and subtractive (CMYK) models.
- **[[concepts/psychology|Psychology]]**: How [[concepts/hue|hue]] saturation and temperature [[concepts/power|influence]] viewer perception and emotional response.

## Practical Application in Post-Processing
Modern digital workflows utilize color wheels for non-linear [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]], particularly in professional editing [[concepts/software|software]].

- **[[entities/adobe-lightroom|Adobe Lightroom]] [[concepts/adoption|Implementation]]**:
  - [[lab-notes/2026-05-23-Lightroom-Color-Grading-Foundations-Tools-and-Cinematic|Lightroom Color Grading: Foundations, Tools, and Cinematic Techniques]] details the specific application of color wheels in the [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|Color Grading]] panel.
  - **Foundations**: Emphasizes that color grading is distinct from basic correction; it dictates the emotional narrative and aesthetic [[concepts/tone|tone]].
  - **Tools**: Utilizes the color wheel interface to target specific luminance ranges ([[concepts/shadows|shadows]], midtones, [[concepts/highlights|highlights]]) for hue/saturation shifts rather than global application.
  - **[[concepts/cinematic-techniques|Cinematic Techniques]]**:
    - Creating split-toning effects by placing complementary or analogous hues on opposite ends of the shadow/highlight spectrum.
    - Achieving "natural" results by avoiding oversaturation and maintaining luminance contrast while shifting hues.
    - Using the "Balance" slider to neutralize [[concepts/color-casts|color casts]] before applying artistic grading.
