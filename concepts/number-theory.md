# Number Theory

Number [[concepts/theory|theory]] is a branch of pure [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] that studies the properties, [[concepts/relationships|relationships]], and patterns of integers. As one of the oldest mathematical disciplines, it addresses fundamental questions about whole numbers, divisibility, [[concepts/prime-numbers|prime numbers]], and integer solutions to equations. The field combines abstract [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] with concrete computational investigation, allowing mathematicians to explore both theoretical concepts and practical applications.

## Core Areas of Study

Classical number theory examines divisibility properties, the [[concepts/distribution-of-prime-numbers|distribution of prime numbers]], and solutions to Diophantine equations—problems asking for integer solutions to polynomial equations. [[concepts/prime-lens|Prime]] numbers occupy a central place in the discipline, serving as the building blocks of all integers through the fundamental theorem of arithmetic. Number theorists also investigate modular arithmetic, which studies remainders and congruences, along with properties of special sequences.

### Recent Developments: Bounded Prime Gaps

Significant progress has been made regarding the distribution of [[concepts/prime-number|prime]] numbers, specifically concerning the [[concepts/twin-prime-conjecture|Twin Prime Conjecture]]. Key milestones include:

*   **Yitang [[entities/peng-zhang|Zhang]]'s Breakthrough**: In a landmark result discussed in [[lab-notes/2026-06-15-Yitang-Zhangs-Proof-Bounded-Prime-Gaps-and-the-Twin-Prim|Yitang Zhang's Proof: Bounded Prime Gaps and the Twin Prime Conjecture]], Zhang proved that there are infinitely many pairs of primes that differ by less than 70 million.
*   **Impact on the Field**: This [[concepts/proof|proof]] established that [[concepts/prime-gaps|prime gaps]] are bounded, fundamentally shifting the landscape of analytic number theory and bringing mathematicians closer to resolving whether the gap can be reduced to 2 (the Twin Prime Conjecture).
