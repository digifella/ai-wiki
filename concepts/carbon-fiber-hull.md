# Carbon Fiber Hull

**Carbon Fiber Hull** refers to the structural application of Carbon Fiber Reinforced Polymer (CFRP) in pressure vessels, specifically for deep-sea submersibles. Unlike traditional Titanium or Steel hulls, CFRP offers high strength-to-weight ratios but presents unique challenges regarding fatigue, impact resistance, and manufacturing [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] under extreme [[concepts/hydrostatic-pressure|hydrostatic pressure]].

## Key Characteristics
- **Anisotropy**: Strength varies by direction; requires precise fiber orientation for omnidirectional pressure resistance.
- **Fatigue Resistance**: Generally superior to [[concepts/metal|metals]], but susceptible to delamination and micro-cracking if manufacturing defects exist.
- **Failure Mode**: Brittle failure (catastrophic implosion) rather than plastic deformation, offering no warning signs before collapse.

## Case Study: OceanGate Titan
The [[concepts/oceangate-titan|OceanGate Titan]] submersible utilized a custom CFRP hull. Its catastrophic failure in 2023 prompted extensive [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] reviews.

- **Investigation Findings**: Recent analyses indicate the failure was not inherent to the carbon fiber [[concepts/material-properties|material properties]], but resulted from **inadequate engineering processes** and manufacturing flaws.
- **Specific Failures**: Issues included improper resin infusion, lack of standardized testing protocols, and deviations from established aerospace-grade manufacturing standards.
- **Source Integration**: Detailed analysis of these engineering oversights is documented in [[lab-notes/2026-07-09-OceanGate-Titan-Implosion-Inadequate-Engineering-and-Man|OceanGate Titan Implosion: Inadequate Engineering and Manufacturing Failures]].

## Engineering Considerations
- **[[concepts/quality-control|Quality Control]]**: CFRP hulls require rigorous non-destructive testing (NDT) to detect voids or delamination.
- **Design Margins**: Must account for the brittle nature of composites; safety factors often need to be higher than for ductile metals.
- **Regulatory Gaps**: The [[entities/titan|Titan]] incident highlighted a lack of regulatory oversight for experimental submersible materials.
