## Meta Muse Spark: Features, Performance, and Strategic Shift to Proprietary AI
**Clip title:** Meta's NEW [[entities/llama|Llama]] Replacement - Muse Spark
**Author / channel:** [[entities/sam-witteveen|Sam Witteveen]]
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vkybiVRSm0

### Summary
The video provides a [[concepts/critical-thinking|critical analysis]] of [[entities/meta-ai|Meta AI]]'s latest model, Muse Spark, which was widely rumored as "Avocado" prior to its release. The presenter frames the release as somewhat confusing, particularly in [[concepts/light|light]] of Meta's previous [[entities/llama-4|Llama 4]] model, which was perceived as a significant disappointment and was quickly taken offline. This context sets the stage for examining Muse Spark not just on its technical merits, but also within the broader strategic shifts at Meta, including a substantial investment in its "Superintelligence [[entities/labs|Labs]]" and a talent poaching spree led by Alexandr Wang.

Muse Spark is introduced as a proprietary, [[concepts/multimodal-ai|multimodal AI]] with capabilities spanning [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]], [[concepts/computer-vision|vision]], and agentic tasks. The model boasts "Instant" and "Thinking" modes, with the latter offering a visualization of its [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] process. It integrates deeply with Meta's ecosystem, allowing users to connect Google Calendar, [[entities/gmail|Gmail]], and Outlook, and offering tools for web browsing, content search across Instagram/Facebook, shopping [[concepts/catalog|catalog]] searches, image/web artifact generation, [[concepts/python|Python]] [[concepts/code-execution|code execution]], file management, [visual grounding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_Grounding), and [[entities/agent|agent]] spawning. Benchmarks shared by Meta and third-party analysts like [[entities/artificial-analysis|Artificial Analysis]] indicate that Muse Spark is a competitive model, often ranking within the top five, demonstrating [[concepts/token-optimization|token efficiency]], strong [[concepts/vision-capabilities|vision capabilities]], and solid performance in instruction-following evaluations.

However, the video highlights significant criticisms and strategic implications. A major point of contention is that Muse Spark is *not* an [[concepts/open-source|open-source]] model, a departure from Meta's [[entities/llama|Llama]] series, and currently lacks a public API. This move is seen as Meta prioritizing internal platform [[concepts/integration|integration]] over contributing to the open AI community. Furthermore, transparency regarding the model's size, [[concepts/training-data|training data]], and pre-training [[concepts/tokens|tokens]] is absent, a trend the presenter notes is becoming increasingly common. Despite Meta's acquisition of [[concepts/manus|Manus]], a multi-user agent system, Muse Spark's agentic performance "does not stand out" in third-party benchmarks. The overarching goal, framed as "[Personal Superintelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_Superintelligence)," appears to be the [[concepts/deployment|deployment]] of [[concepts/ai-agents|AI agents]] deeply integrated into Meta's applications like WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram, and Threads to empower individual users with everyday tasks.

The video concludes that while Muse Spark is technically a solid model and a significant improvement for Meta, its impact is dampened by its proprietary nature and the rapid advancements in the broader [[concepts/ai-landscape|AI landscape]], especially from established players and emerging [[concepts/open-source|open-source]] alternatives. Meta's focus seems to be on developing highly efficient, scalable AI for its vast user base, capable of performing well at a lower computational [[concepts/cost|cost]] than previous models. This [[concepts/strategic-pivot|strategic pivot]], driven by massive investments in talent and infrastructure following the [[entities/llama-4|Llama 4]] setback, indicates Meta's determination to integrate AI [[concepts/agents|agents]] into its core services, irrespective of whether the models achieve open-source [[concepts/leadership|leadership]] or groundbreaking scientific firsts.
