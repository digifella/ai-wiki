# Threat Intelligence

Threat intelligence encompasses the collection, analysis, and application of information about current and potential [[concepts/security|security]] threats to an [[concepts/organization|organization]]'s systems and data. It serves as a foundational component of security infrastructure, enabling organizations to understand adversarial [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]], intentions, and methods. Threat intelligence sources [[concepts/range|range]] from internal security logs and network data to external feeds from government agencies, commercial vendors, academic institutions, and security research communities.

## Integration with RAG Systems

Threat intelligence has become increasingly relevant to [[concepts/answer-generation|retrieval-augmented generation]] (RAG) systems, where accurate, timely threat data must be rapidly synthesized and presented to decision-makers. Building effective [[concepts/contextualized-language-understanding|RAG systems]] for security contexts requires integrating diverse threat intelligence sources—including [[concepts/indicators|indicators]] of compromise, [[concepts/vulnerability|vulnerability]] disclosures, attack patterns, and threat actor profiles—into searchable [[concepts/knowledge-bases|knowledge bases]] that can provide contextual [[concepts/responses|responses]] to security queries.

## Data Quality and Sourcing

The [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] of threat intelligence depends heavily on source quality and [[concepts/verification|verification]]. Organizations typically combine multiple intelligence sources to validate findings and reduce false positives. Factors such as timeliness, [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]], and relevance determine whether threat intelligence can be effectively operationalized within security workflows and [[concepts/automations|automated systems]].
