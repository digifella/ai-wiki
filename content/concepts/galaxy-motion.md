# Galaxy Motion

Galaxy Motion refers to the study of kinematic patterns and dynamics exhibited by galaxies, particularly the [[concepts/milky-way|Milky Way]]. Understanding galactic motion requires accounting for the gravitational influence of all matter present, including the invisible [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] that comprises approximately 85% of the universe's matter content. Observations of galactic rotation curves, stellar velocities, and large-scale [[concepts/structure|structure]] movements provide crucial data for reconstructing the mass [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] and motion within galaxies.

## Dark Matter Constraints

Recent observations of gamma-ray emissions from the Milky Way have provided new constraints on dark matter distribution models. These [[concepts/gamma-rays|gamma rays]] are interpreted as products of WIMP (Weakly Interacting Massive Particle) annihilation events occurring in regions of high dark matter density. The spatial distribution and intensity of these signals can inform models of how dark matter is distributed throughout the galactic halo, which in turn affects calculations of orbital dynamics and motion patterns for visible galactic components.

The integration of these indirect dark matter detection signatures with traditional kinematic measurements—such as proper motions of stars and globular clusters—allows for more comprehensive models of galactic structure and evolution. This multi-faceted approach helps clarify the relationship between visible matter trajectories and the underlying dark matter framework that governs galactic gravitational dynamics.
