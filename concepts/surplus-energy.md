# Surplus Energy

**Surplus Energy** refers to the excess electrical or thermal energy generated by Renewable Energy sources (e.g., Solar Power, Wind Power) that exceeds immediate demand. Effective management of surplus energy is critical for grid stability and maximizing the return on investment for renewable infrastructure.

## Key Challenges
- **Intermittency:** Mismatch between generation peaks and consumption patterns.
- **Curtailement:** Wasted energy when grid capacity is exceeded.
- **[[entities/storage|Storage]] Efficiency:** Traditional Battery systems face limitations in long-duration storage due to degradation and cost.

## Storage Solutions
- **Short-term:** Lithium-ion Batteries, Pumped Hydro Storage.
- **Long-term/Seasonal:**
	- Hydrogen Production via electrolysis.
	- Thermal Energy Storage systems.
	- [[lab-notes/2026-07-16-Seasons-Thermochemical-Heat-Storage-Long-term-Renewable|Season's Thermochemical Heat Storage: Long-term Renewable Energy Solution]]

## Recent Developments
- **Thermochemical Storage:** Emerging technologies focus on [[concepts/storing|storing]] energy in chemical bonds for months with minimal loss.
	- **[[entities/season|Season]] Technology:** A Swiss-developed [[concepts/solution|solution]] utilizing thermochemical reactions to store surplus renewable energy for long durations, addressing the seasonal mismatch in [[concepts/energy-source|energy supply]].
	- **Efficiency:** Claims of minimal loss over months, potentially outperforming traditional [[concepts/heat-pumps|heat pumps]] in specific long-term contexts.
