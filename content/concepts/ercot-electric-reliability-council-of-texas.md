# Ercot Electric Reliability Council Of Texas

[[entities/ercot|ERCOT]] ([[concepts/electric-reliability|Electric Reliability]] Council of [[entities/texas|Texas]]) is the independent system operator (ISO) responsible for managing electricity transmission and ensuring reliable [[concepts/power|power]] [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] across most of Texas. The [[concepts/organization|organization]] oversees the operation of the interconnected transmission grid serving approximately 26 million customers in central, eastern, and northern portions of the state. ERCOT does not own generation, transmission, or distribution assets itself, but rather coordinates real-time operations among generators, transmission operators, and retail electric providers to maintain system stability and balance supply with demand.

## Operational Responsibilities

ERCOT's primary functions include monitoring power generation and demand in real time, managing wholesale electricity markets, and issuing directives to maintain grid frequency and voltage stability. The organization operates a competitive energy market where generators bid to supply electricity, allowing for price discovery while maintaining operational [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]]. ERCOT also oversees reserve margins—ensuring adequate generation capacity exists to handle peak demand and unexpected outages—and manages [[concepts/emergency-protocols|emergency protocols]] during periods of system [[concepts/stress|stress]].

## Historical Context and Challenges

ERCOT gained significant public [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] following the 2021 Texas winter [[concepts/power-outages|power outages]], during which the grid experienced widespread failures resulting in blackouts across the state. The outages, triggered by extreme [[concepts/winter-weather|winter weather]] and reduced natural gas supply, exposed vulnerabilities in generation capacity, [[concepts/weatherization|weatherization]] of power plants, and coordination between energy suppliers. Subsequent analysis and regulatory scrutiny prompted discussions about winterization [[concepts/open-standards|standards]], reserve requirements, and the grid's capacity to handle [[concepts/extreme-weather-events|extreme weather events]] under climate variability.
