# Atmospheric Interaction

Atmospheric interaction refers to the processes by which various particles and radiation from outer space interact with [[concepts/earths-atmosphere|Earth's atmosphere]] upon entering it. This includes the impact of high-energy [[concepts/cosmic-rays|cosmic rays]] such as [[concepts/protons|protons]] on atmospheric components, leading to [[concepts/ionization|ionization]] effects that can cause damage to satellites and other technological infrastructure.

- **[[concepts/lhaaso|LHAASO]]'s Confirmation: The Universe's Most Powerful [[concepts/high-energy-particles|Cosmic Ray Sources]]** introduces the significance of cosmic ray interaction with [[entities/earth|Earth]]'s atmosphere:
  - [[concepts/cosmic-rays|Cosmic rays]] are high-energy particles predominantly composed of protons.
  - Upon entering the atmosphere, these particles trigger a [[concepts/domino-effect|chain reaction]] that leads to [[concepts/ionization|ionization]] and can damage satellites. WikiLink
