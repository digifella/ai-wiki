# Papua New Guinea

Papua New Guinea is a sovereign nation occupying the eastern half of the island of New Guinea in Oceania, sharing the landmass with Indonesia to the west. Situated in the southwestern Pacific Ocean, the country features rugged mountainous terrain, extensive tropical rainforests, and high geographical diversity. It gained independence from Australia in 1975 and remains a member of the Commonwealth of Nations. The nation is renowned for its profound linguistic and cultural diversity, with hundreds of distinct languages spoken across its population.

## Kuru and Medical Significance

The Fore people, an indigenous group residing in the Eastern Highlands of Papua New Guinea, are central to the history of prion disease research due to the prevalence of Kuru. This fatal neurodegenerative disorder was historically transmitted through endocannibalistic funeral rites involving the consumption of deceased relatives. The study of Kuru among the Fore provided critical evidence for the Protein World Hypothesis, demonstrating that infectious agents could consist solely of misfolded proteins without nucleic acids. This discovery fundamentally advanced the understanding of transmissible spongiform encephalopathies and established the scientific basis for identifying other prion-related conditions.
