# Mask Creation

Mask creation is a fundamental technique in digital photo editing that involves isolating specific areas of an image for selective adjustments. In Lightroom and Camera Raw, masks allow photographers to apply edits to particular regions while leaving the rest of the image unaffected, enabling precise control over exposure, color, clarity, and other parameters. This process transforms global adjustments into targeted interventions, ensuring that enhancements do not degrade areas of the photograph that are already satisfactory.

The workflow typically begins with the selection of a subject or area using automated tools such as the Subject, Sky, or Background masks. These AI-driven features generate initial selections that can be refined manually. Users can adjust the mask's feathering to soften edges and modify the density to control the intensity of the applied adjustments.

## Edge Refinement

Fixing mask edges is critical for achieving natural-looking results, particularly around complex boundaries like hair or foliage. Colin Smith demonstrates techniques for addressing common issues where masks bleed into adjacent areas or fail to capture fine details. This involves using the brush tool to add or subtract from the mask selection, ensuring that the transition between edited and unedited regions is seamless.

## Practical Application

Effective mask creation requires balancing automation with manual correction. While initial selections provide a strong foundation, manual adjustments are often necessary to handle intricate details. By focusing on edge quality and precision, editors can apply localized changes that enhance the overall image without introducing visible artifacts or unnatural transitions.
