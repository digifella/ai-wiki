# Traumatic Births

Traumatic birth refers to childbirth experiences that result in significant psychological distress for those present, including the birthing person and their support partners. Research indicates that approximately 30% of births may be experienced as traumatic by attendees. These experiences can stem from various factors including medical emergencies, unexpected complications, loss of control during labour, inadequate pain management, or feeling unsupported during the birthing process. The psychological impact of traumatic birth can persist long after delivery, affecting mental health, bonding with the infant, and relationship dynamics.

## Support Partners and Father Involvement

The presence and active involvement of support partners, particularly fathers, significantly influences the birth experience for all involved. When fathers and other support partners receive adequate preparation and support during labour and delivery, they are better positioned to provide emotional reassurance to the birthing person and advocate for their needs. Enhanced service delivery that includes training for support partners, clear communication from medical staff, and recognition of their role can reduce feelings of helplessness and improve outcomes for the entire family unit. Research suggests that well-supported fathers report more positive birth experiences and show improved engagement in early parenting and family relationships.

## Impact and Recovery

The aftermath of a traumatic birth may include post-traumatic stress symptoms, postnatal depression, or anxiety in any family member present. Recovery often requires professional support including counselling, trauma-informed care, and peer support. Healthcare services that acknowledge the possibility of traumatic birth experiences and provide accessible resources for processing and healing contribute to better long-term wellbeing for birthing persons, partners, and families.
