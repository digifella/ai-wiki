# AI Hardware Evolution

The progression of computational hardware has been fundamental to advances in [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]]. From early CPU-based systems to modern specialized [[concepts/central-processing-units|processors]], the hardware landscape has continuously adapted to meet the computational demands of increasingly complex [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]]. This evolution encompasses improvements in [[concepts/compute-capacity|processing power]], [[concepts/memory|memory]] capacity, [[concepts/energy-efficiency|energy efficiency]], and the development of architectures specifically optimized for [[concepts/machine-learning|machine learning]] workloads.

## Specialized Processors

The [[concepts/emergent-behavior|emergence]] of GPUs ([[concepts/webgpu|Graphics]] Processing Units) in the 2010s marked a significant shift in AI training capabilities, as their parallel architecture proved well-suited for the matrix operations required by [[concepts/neural-networks|neural networks]]. This was followed by the development of application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs) like [[concepts/google-search|Google]]'s TPU (Tensor Processing Unit), designed explicitly for AI [[concepts/inference|inference]] and training tasks. These specialized processors offer performance advantages and efficiency gains compared to general-purpose CPUs for machine [[concepts/learning|learning]] operations.

## Scaling Infrastructure

Hardware evolution has enabled the [[concepts/scale-effect|scale effect]] in AI, where larger models trained on more data demonstrate improved performance. Modern [[concepts/ai-development|AI development]] relies on [[concepts/large-scale-computing|distributed computing]] systems that connect multiple processors across clusters and [[concepts/techno-economics|data centers]]. The ability to scale horizontally—adding more hardware [[concepts/nodes|nodes]]—has become increasingly important as models have grown from millions to billions to trillions of parameters. This infrastructure [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]] requires advances in interconnection technologies, [[concepts/cooling-systems|cooling systems]], and power delivery alongside [[concepts/cpu|processor]] improvements.

## Energy and Practical Considerations

As AI systems have scaled, [[concepts/energy-consumption|energy consumption]] has become a critical concern. Recent hardware evolution has focused not only on raw computational throughput but also on power efficiency metrics like performance-per-watt. Advances in semiconductor manufacturing processes, memory hierarchies, and low-[[concepts/accuracy|precision]] computing have attempted to address the escalating energy demands of training [[concepts/frontier-models|state-of-the-art models]].
