# Millennium Prize Problems

The **Millennium Prize Problems** are seven fundamental [[concepts/unsolved-problems|unsolved problems]] in [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] identified by the Clay Mathematics Institute (CMI) in 2000. Each problem carries a $1 million prize for its solution. As of 2026, six remain unsolved; the [[concepts/a-video-titled-p-vs-np|P vs NP Problem]] is the only one with a widely accepted [[concepts/solution|resolution]] pending final [[concepts/verification|verification]], while recent developments suggest potential breakthroughs in others.

## The Seven Problems

1.  **Poincaré Conjecture**: Solved by Grigori Perelman (2003).
2.  **Hodge Conjecture**: Unsolved. Relates algebraic geometry to topology.
3.  **[[concepts/riemann-hypothesis|Riemann Hypothesis]]**: Unsolved. Concerns the distribution of [[concepts/prime-numbers|prime numbers]] via the zeros of the [[concepts/riemann-zeta-function|Riemann zeta function]].
4.  **[[concepts/business-logistics|P vs NP Problem]]**: Unsolved. Determines if every problem whose solution can be quickly verified can also be quickly solved.
5.  **Navier-Stokes [[concepts/existence-and-smoothness|Existence and Smoothness]]**: Unsolved. Addresses the behavior of fluid flows.
6.  **Yang-Mills Existence and Mass Gap**: Unsolved. Relates to [[concepts/quantum-field-theory|quantum field theory]].
7.  **Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture**: Unsolved. Relates to the number of rational points on elliptic curves.
