## Community Health Nursing: Core Terminology and Nursing Roles
**Clip title:** Terminology and Roles: [[concepts/population-health|Community Health]] Fundamentals of Nursing | @LevelUpRN
**Author / channel:** Level Up RN
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WcA8bDDuVtE

### Summary
This video, presented by [[entities/ellis-parker|Ellis Parker]] of [[entities/level-up-rn|Level Up RN]], provides an insightful overview of [[concepts/community-health-nursing|community health nursing]], focusing on essential terminology and the diverse roles nurses play in community settings. The content is designed to help nursing students and professionals understand the foundational concepts of community health and population-focused care.

The video begins by defining core terminology crucial to understanding community health. A "community" is described as a group of people connected through social, personal, or geographical links, such as residents of a specific neighborhood. An "[aggregate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/aggregate)" is introduced as a subgroup within a larger community that shares common concerns or characteristics, like an LGBTQ+ group within a neighborhood. A key distinction is made between "[community-oriented nursing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/community-oriented_nursing)," which emphasizes health promotion and disease prevention for a community or population (e.g., [[concepts/public-health|public health]] nursing, screenings, immunizations, outreach, education), and "[community-based nursing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/community-based_nursing)," which focuses on providing direct illness care to individuals and families within the community (e.g., home health or hospice nursing in a home setting).

Following the definitions, the video highlights several specific nursing roles within community health. Home health nurses provide care directly in patients' residences, encompassing assessments, treatments like wound care and medication administration, and extensive education for patients, families, and caregivers, requiring strong communication skills. Hospice nurses offer [palliative care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/palliative_care) and emotional support to patients and their families during the end-of-life process. Occupational health nurses work to prevent workplace injuries and accidents through screenings, surveillance, and educational programs. Faith-based or parish nurses integrate health education and promotion within religious communities, providing holistic care that often includes spiritual health. Lastly, school nurses deliver [[concepts/preventive-care|preventative care]], such as [[concepts/computer-vision|vision]] and hearing screenings, acute and emergency care, and coordinate care for students with chronic illnesses.

In conclusion, the video effectively outlines the broad scope of [[concepts/fundamentals-of-nursing|community health nursing]], demonstrating how nurses contribute to the well-being of populations in various settings beyond traditional hospital environments. It emphasizes the importance of both preventive care and direct illness management tailored to specific community needs, along with the distinct approaches of community-oriented and community-based nursing. Understanding these concepts and roles is vital for nurses aiming to [[entities/make|make]] a significant impact on public health and individual patient care within communities.
