# Hybrid aircraft design

Hybrid aircraft design is an engineering approach that integrates the functional advantages of Rotorcraft (vertical lift) and Fixed-wing aircraft (high-[[concepts/speed|speed]] cruise) to achieve [[concepts/vertical-take-off-and-landing|Vertical Take-Off and Landing (VTOL)]] capability within a high-efficiency airframe.

### Core Engineering Principles
- **[[concepts/nacelle-tilting-mechanism|Tiltrotor Mechanism]]**: Uses rotating engine nacelles to transition the [[concepts/engine-thrust|thrust]] vector between Hover and Forward flight.
- **Conversion flight**: The complex aerodynamic transition phase where lift-bearing responsibility shifts from rotors to wings.
- **[[concepts/dual-flight-modes]]**: The management of disparate aerodynamic requirements for vertical and horizontal flight profiles.

### Case Study: V-22 Osprey Tiltrotor
- Demonstrates "insane engineering" required to manage highly complex [[concepts/dual-flight-modes]].
- Utilized as a critical tool for high-stakes operations, such as night rescue missions in the Libyan desert.
