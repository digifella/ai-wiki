# Egg Flavor

Egg flavor in cooked eggs is influenced by technique, ingredient selection, and [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] methods. The natural taste of eggs can be enhanced or diminished depending on how heat is applied, what liquids are introduced, and when seasoning is added. Understanding these variables allows cooks to maximize the delicate flavor of eggs while achieving desired [[concepts/texture|texture]].

## Scrambled Eggs

Scrambled eggs benefit from careful [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to [[concepts/salt-timing|salt timing]], liquid additions, and cookware choice. Salt added before cooking can denature proteins and draw out moisture, while salt added during cooking integrates more evenly into the eggs. The addition of small amounts of liquid—such as cream, milk, or water—can improve texture and flavor perception. Low to [[entities/medium|medium]] heat with a heavy-bottomed pan or non-stick surface prevents overcooking and allows for gentle, even cooking that preserves tender curds and subtle egg flavor.

## Other Egg Preparations

Poached eggs and other [[concepts/cooking-methods|cooking methods]] also depend on technique to preserve flavor. Water temperature, the use of vinegar or salt in poaching liquid, and handling methods all affect the final taste and texture. Each preparation method presents different opportunities to either highlight or mask the underlying egg flavor.
