# Drift Rate

**Drift rate** refers to the velocity at which tectonic plates move across the [[entities/earth|Earth]]'s surface, typically measured in centimeters per year. It is a fundamental parameter in understanding the dynamics of [[concepts/tectonic-plate-shifts|Plate Tectonics]], mantle convection, and the [[concepts/ancient-earth|geological history of Earth]].

## Key Metrics
- **Modern Rates**: Current plate velocities range from <1 cm/yr (e.g., Antarctic Plate) to >15 cm/yr (e.g., Pacific Plate).
- **[[concepts/historical-context|Historical Context]]**: Drift rates are not constant; they vary over geological time [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] due changes in mantle viscosity, plate geometry, and driving forces.
- **Measurement**: Determined via paleomagnetism, hotspot tracks, and modern GPS data.

## Recent Findings
- **Deep Time Tectonics**: New research suggests plate tectonics may have initiated much earlier than previously confirmed, potentially influencing early atmospheric and magnetic field evolution.
  - See: [[lab-notes/2026-05-27-Earliest-evidence-of-plate-tectonics|Earliest evidence of plate tectonics]] for details on 3.48 Ga [[concepts/magnetic-traces|magnetic traces]].
- **Implications**: Early onset of [[concepts/crust-movement|tectonic motion]] supports models linking [[concepts/continental-drift]] to the stabilization of the [[concepts/earths-magnetic-field|Geodynamo]] and the development of life-supporting conditions.
