# Color Masking

Color masking is a selective editing technique that allows photographers and digital artists to apply adjustments to specific areas of an image based on color information. Rather than modifying an entire photograph uniformly, color masking isolates regions according to color ranges, tonal values, or spatial boundaries. This approach provides precise control over which parts of an image receive particular edits, making it especially useful for targeted adjustments to specific elements like skies, foliage, or skin tones without affecting the rest of the composition.

## AI-Powered Implementation in Lightroom Classic

Modern versions of Lightroom Classic incorporate AI-powered masking tools that automate the creation of color masks. These tools analyze the image to detect and isolate areas matching specified color ranges, reducing the manual effort required for traditional masking techniques. Users can define masks based on specific colors or luminosity ranges, then apply exposure, saturation, temperature, and other adjustments exclusively to those masked regions. The AI assistance streamlines workflows by automatically detecting boundaries between color zones.

## Practical Applications

Color masking is commonly used for enhancing specific image elements. Photographers frequently apply it to selectively brighten or adjust the saturation of skies without altering the landscape below, warm up skin tones independently from background colors, or enhance foliage while preserving other areas. The technique is valuable in workflows where uniform adjustments would compromise the overall image balance or where different parts of a composition require contradictory edits.
