# Visual Representation

Visual representation functions as a critical interface between internal subjective experience and external communication. This process is particularly significant for conditions that are often misunderstood or invisible to observers, such as dyslexia. By converting abstract cognitive phenomena into tangible forms, visual representation renders private experiences public and intangible concepts concrete. This transformation facilitates both personal insight and shared understanding among those who do not share the specific perceptual challenges.

Artist Kim Percy has applied this concept to visualize the lived experience of dyslexia through her artistic practice. Her work aims to bridge the gap between the specific perceptual difficulties associated with the condition and the broader public's comprehension of them. Percy’s approach demonstrates how art can serve as a medium for translating complex, often isolated, cognitive realities into accessible visual narratives.

The utility of this method lies in its ability to make the invisible visible. For conditions like dyslexia, where the struggle is primarily cognitive rather than physical, traditional descriptions may fail to convey the full scope of the experience. Visual representation allows for a direct engagement with these challenges, offering a form of empathy and clarity that text alone may not achieve. This approach highlights the role of creative pursuits in documenting and communicating neurodivergent experiences.
