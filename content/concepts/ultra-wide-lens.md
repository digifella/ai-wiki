# Ultra Wide Lens

An ultra wide lens is a camera lens with an exceptionally broad field of view, typically 100 degrees or greater. These lenses are characterized by their short focal lengths, generally ranging from 14mm to 24mm on full-frame cameras, or proportionally shorter on crop-sensor formats. The expansive field of view allows photographers to capture significantly more of a scene than standard lenses, making them particularly useful for landscape, architectural, and environmental photography.

## Panoramic Photography and Stitching

Ultra wide lenses are commonly employed in [[concepts/long-lens-photography|panoramic photography]], where multiple [[concepts/images|images]] are captured and digitally stitched together to create an extended horizontal or vertical composition. While a single ultra wide shot provides immediate breadth, the stitching technique allows photographers to extend the field of view even further by combining overlapping frames. This approach provides flexibility in composition and can result in higher resolution final images than a single ultra wide [[concepts/exposure|exposure]].

## Optical Considerations

Ultra wide lenses typically exhibit pronounced distortion characteristics, including barrel distortion and perspective exaggeration. These optical properties are inherent to the wide field of view and can be managed through in-camera or post-processing correction. Achieving [[concepts/sharpness|sharp focus]] across the entire frame with ultra wide lenses often requires careful aperture selection and [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to depth of field, as the broad perspective can [[entities/make|make]] focus inconsistencies more apparent to viewers.
