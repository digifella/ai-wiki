## Photoshop Depth Map: Mastering Precise 3D Photo Adjustments
**Clip title:** Photoshop's Hidden Depth Mask: Make Photos Look 3D!
**Author / channel:** PiXimperfect
**URL:** https://youtu.be/ljf8IhxqS20

### Summary
This video [[concepts/tutorial|tutorial]], presented by [[entities/unmesh-dinda|Unmesh Dinda]] from [[entities/piximperfect|PixImperfect]], delves into the often-overlooked power of [[concepts/photoshop|Photoshop]]'s [[concepts/depth-map|depth map]] feature for creating advanced, realistic photo [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]]. While the new [[concepts/lens-blur|Lens Blur]] tool in [[concepts/camera-raw|Camera Raw]] and [[concepts/lightroom|Lightroom]] automatically utilizes depth information, Dinda demonstrates how to extract this depth map as a separate channel within Photoshop. This allows for an incredibly precise and non-destructive [[concepts/workflow|workflow]], enabling targeted [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] to different depths of an image, going far beyond a simple blur. The core concept revolves around understanding that the depth map represents distance from the camera in grayscale, with darker areas being closer and brighter areas being further away.

The [[concepts/tutorial|tutorial]] meticulously guides viewers through several key applications of the depth map. First, it shows how to generate the depth map using [[concepts/neural-filters|Neural Filters]] in Photoshop, advising users to save it as an [Alpha Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_compositing) rather than deleting the generated layer. This saved depth map is then used to create precise masks for Curves [[concepts/adjustment-layers|adjustment layers]], allowing for "[[concepts/contrast|contrast]] separation." By inverting masks and adjusting Levels, Dinda illustrates how to selectively boost [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] in foreground elements while subtly fading distant elements, mimicking natural [atmospheric perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_perspective) seen in real-world landscapes. This [[concepts/granular-control|granular control]] ensures that contrast and brightness adjustments enhance the perceived depth without affecting unintended areas.

Building upon contrast separation, the video demonstrates using [[concepts/depth-maps|depth maps]] for [[concepts/targeted-color-adjustments|selective color grading]]. Applying a warm color lookup table (LUT) to the foreground (closer areas) and a cooler, desaturated LUT to the background (farther areas) helps further enhance the sense of depth and atmosphere. Crucially, Dinda introduces the "[[concepts/blend-if|Blend If]]" feature in [[concepts/layer-styles|Layer Styles]], allowing for even finer control by preventing adjustments from crushing [[concepts/shadows|shadows]] or highlights based on the underlying image's [luminosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminosity). This technique is also employed when adding subtle [[concepts/light|light]] flares to ensure they interact realistically with the scene's depth.

Finally, the tutorial covers advanced touches like blurring the background to create separation, enhancing the sky's detail using Photoshop's "Select Sky" feature and [[concepts/camera-raw|Camera Raw]]'s lens blur for a dreamy effect, and adding a [[concepts/film-grain|film grain]] overlay to tie all the elements together. The overarching takeaway is that by understanding and actively utilizing the depth map, photographers gain unparalleled control over their [[concepts/images|images]], transforming flat photos into dynamic, multi-dimensional scenes. This comprehensive approach empowers users to achieve more realistic and artistic results, reminding them not to get "lost in the awesome Lens Blur" but to explore the underlying depth map for limitless creative possibilities.
