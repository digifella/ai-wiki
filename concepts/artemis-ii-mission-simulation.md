# Artemis II Mission Simulation

The [[concepts/artemis-ii-mission|Artemis II Mission]] [[concepts/simulation|Simulation]] is an operational [[concepts/exercise|exercise]] conducted by [[concepts/nasa|NASA]] to evaluate the readiness and performance of the crewed Artemis II mission. This comprehensive simulation tests integrated [[concepts/spacecraft-systems|spacecraft systems]], crew procedures, and vehicle operations across the complete mission timeline, from pre-launch [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] through [[entities/earth|Earth]] return. The exercise serves as a critical validation tool to identify potential issues and refine operational protocols before actual flight.

## Crew Operations and Training

The simulation focuses on [[concepts/human-performance|crew performance]] across nominal and contingency [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]]. Astronauts practice procedures for launch, orbital operations, lunar transit, and recovery phases while interacting with Mission Control. The exercise evaluates crew communication, [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]], and response to system anomalies, ensuring teams are prepared for the actual mission environment.

## Orion and Vehicle Systems

The simulation comprehensively tests the [[concepts/orion-systems|Orion spacecraft]]'s systems, including life support, power, [[concepts/recommendations|guidance]], navigation, and thermal management. Testing covers hardware integration between Orion and the Space Launch System (SLS) rocket, ensuring all vehicle components function correctly during integrated operations.

## Mission Validation and Updates

Results from the simulation inform refinements to flight procedures, timelines, and contingency plans. [[concepts/starship|Starship]] developments and any [[concepts/software-updates|updates]] to launch infrastructure or mission architecture are evaluated as part of the broader [[concepts/artemis-program|Artemis program]] assessment, helping NASA optimize operations for the actual crewed lunar mission.
