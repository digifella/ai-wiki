# Expert Systems

Expert systems were a class of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] [[concepts/software|programs]] developed primarily during the 1980s that sought to replicate human [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] within specialized domains. These systems operated by [[concepts/encoding|encoding]] [[concepts/domain-specific-knowledge|domain-specific knowledge]], provided by human experts, into formal rule sets and logical [[concepts/inference-engines|inference engines]]. By applying these explicit rules to specific inputs, the software could diagnose problems, explore mineral deposits, or troubleshoot equipment, effectively automating the [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] processes traditionally associated with human specialists in fields such as medicine and [[entities/national-academies|engineering]].

The development of expert systems was driven by the symbolic AI paradigm, which assumed that human [[concepts/expertise|expertise]] could be systematically captured and represented through explicit rules. This approach marked a significant research focus in the early era of AI, distinguishing itself from earlier connectionist models by relying on transparent, hand-crafted [[concepts/open-source-philosophy|logic]] rather than statistical [[concepts/learning|learning]]. The architecture typically consisted of a [[concepts/knowledge-base|knowledge base]] containing [[concepts/factual-knowledge|facts]] and rules, and an [[concepts/engine|inference engine]] that processed these rules to derive conclusions.

Critiques of the expert system model, notably by philosophers and cognitive scientists such as Hubert [[entities/dreyfus|Dreyfus]], highlighted fundamental limitations in capturing the tacit, intuitive aspects of human expertise. Subsequent analyses by researchers like [[entities/gobel|Gobel]] and [[entities/chassy|Chassy]] further explored the constraints of [[concepts/training-process|model training]] and knowledge representation in this context. These perspectives underscored the difficulty of formalizing the nuanced, context-dependent judgment that characterizes true [[concepts/unconscious-competence|expert performance]], contributing to the eventual shift toward [[concepts/machine-learning|machine learning]] approaches that do not rely solely on explicit rule encoding.
