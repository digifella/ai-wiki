# Eastern Acceptance

Eastern acceptance is a philosophical stance that emphasizes [[concepts/musical-harmony|harmony]] with [[entities/nature|nature]] and the universe, embracing the impermanence and interconnectedness of all things. This concept contrasts sharply with [[concepts/western-resistance|Western resistance]] to concepts like [[concepts/zero|zero]], which symbolizes absence or nothingness but also embodies profound mathematical and existential ideas.

## Key Aspects
- **Harmony**: Embracing natural cycles and rhythms without [[concepts/conflict|conflict]].
- **Interconnectedness**: Recognizing the inherent [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] between all elements of existence.
- **Impermanence**: Accepting change as an inevitable part of life, rather than resisting it.

### Western Philosophical Resistance
The Western world's historical reluctance to accept zero highlights a broader resistance to concepts that embody absence or non-being. This philosophical stance has roots in the Greek and Roman traditions, which valued [[concepts/satiety|fullness]] and presence over emptiness and void (see Western-Thought for more).

### Eastern Acceptance of Zero
In [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], Eastern cultures were quicker to adopt the concept of zero, seeing it as a reflection of the Buddhist principle that all phenomena are ultimately empty or devoid of inherent existence ([[concepts/buddhism]]).
