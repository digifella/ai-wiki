# Virgo

Virgo is a gravitational wave detector located in Cascina, Italy, operated by the European Gravitational-Wave Observatory (EGO). As one of the primary instruments in the global network of gravitational wave observatories alongside LIGO's facilities in the United States, Virgo uses laser interferometry to detect ripples in spacetime caused by violent cosmic events. The detector became operational in 2007 and has been integral to confirming and localizing gravitational wave sources since the first detection in 2015.

## Technical Design

Virgo employs a Michelson interferometer with 3-kilometer-long arms arranged in an L-shape. The facility utilizes high-power lasers and ultra-high vacuum systems to minimize noise and maximize sensitivity to minute distortions in spacetime. Its design allows for the detection of changes in arm length smaller than one ten-thousandth the width of a proton, enabling the observation of phenomena such as black hole mergers and neutron star collisions.

## Scientific Contributions

The collaboration between Virgo and LIGO has significantly improved the sky localization of gravitational wave events, allowing electromagnetic telescopes to identify the origins of these cosmic disturbances. Recent analyses involving data from the Virgo detector have contributed to evidence regarding the detection of sub-solar mass primordial black holes via gravitational waves, offering potential insights into the early universe and the nature of dark matter.
