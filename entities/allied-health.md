# Allied Health

Allied health encompasses healthcare services provided by professionals other than physicians and nurses. These practitioners include physiotherapists, occupational therapists, dietitians, speech-language pathologists, radiographers, and other specialized healthcare workers. Allied health professionals work collaboratively with medical doctors and nursing staff to deliver comprehensive patient care across clinical settings, rehabilitation facilities, and community-based programs.

## Scope and Practice

Allied health professionals form a diverse workforce addressing specific aspects of patient health and wellbeing. Their roles span diagnostic, therapeutic, and preventive services. Physiotherapists focus on movement and physical rehabilitation, occupational therapists support functional independence in daily activities, dietitians manage nutritional care, and speech-language pathologists address communication and swallowing disorders. Other allied health roles include radiography, pathology, audiology, and podiatry. These professionals typically undergo formal education and credentialing specific to their discipline.

## Technology and Monitoring

Modern allied health practice increasingly incorporates wearable devices and embedded sensors for continuous monitoring and data collection. These technologies enable remote patient monitoring, objective measurement of rehabilitation progress, and collection of real-world activity data outside clinical settings. Such innovations support evidence-based practice and facilitate earlier intervention when clinical parameters change, particularly in chronic disease management and post-rehabilitation care.
