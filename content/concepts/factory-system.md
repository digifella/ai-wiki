# Factory System

Centralized organizational model for [[concepts/mass-production|mass production]] consolidating machinery, raw materials, and wage labor within a single facility, superseding the Domestic System and artisanal workshops during the [[concepts/industrial-revolution]].

## Core Characteristics
- **Centralization:** Aggregation of production processes, tools, and workers under one roof to streamline oversight, [[concepts/quality-control|quality control]], and logistics.
- **Mechanization:** Heavy reliance on capital-intensive machinery (e.g., steam engines, [[concepts/power|power]] looms) to amplify [[concepts/output|output]], reduce unit costs, and standardize goods.
- **Division of Labor:** Fragmentation of [[concepts/complex-tasks|complex tasks]] into repetitive, specialized operations to maximize efficiency and reduce individual [[concepts/skill|skill]] requirements.
- **Temporal Discipline:** Imposition of rigid schedules, precise timekeeping, and hierarchical supervision, contrasting with the autonomy of piece-rate domestic work.
- **Wage Dependency:** Transition to a monetized labor market where workers sell time rather than sell finished products, structurally forming the industrial Proletariat.

## Socio-Economic Implications
- **[[concepts/urbanization|Urbanization]]:** Rapid migration from rural areas to industrial centers, creating dense working-class districts, altering demographic structures, and straining infrastructure.
- **Capital Concentration:** Shift of economic power to factory owners and investors, widening disparities between capital accumulation and labor compensation.
- **Skill Devaluation:** Deskilling of artisans as machinery assumes functions previously requiring years of apprenticeship, undermining traditional guild protections.
- **Enclosure Synergy:** Often accelerated by the Enclosure [[concepts/exercise|Movement]], which displaced agrarian populations, creating a surplus labor force available for factory employment.

## Resistance and Adaptation
- **[[concepts/luddism|Luddite Movement]]:** Organized sabotage and protest in early 19th century Britain, frequently mischaracterized as anti-technology; grievances centered on wage suppression, breach of customary [[concepts/open-standards|standards]], and management exploitation facilitated by mechanization rather than opposition to machines per se.
- **Labor [[concepts/consolidation|Consolidation]]:** Harsh conditions, deskilling, and collective discipline eventually spurred the formation of trade unions, strikes, and demands for regulatory reforms (e.g., Factory Acts).
