### Summary
On September 30, 2026, FlyDubai Flight 1073, a [[concepts/boeing-737-max-8|Boeing 737 Max 8]] carrying 174 passengers and 6 crew (including two off-duty pilots), embarked on a routine passenger service from Dubai to Tel Aviv. Approximately two hours into the flight, an unimaginable [[concepts/crisis|crisis]] unfolded in the cockpit. The First Officer, [[entities/hamam-al-hammami|Hamam Al-Hammami]], an Omani national with a history of extremist material possession and a rejected pilot application from another airline, attacked Captain [[entities/smit-machchhar|Smit Machchhar]] with a cockpit crash axe. This shocking assault, which Captain Machchhar bravely fought off while severely injured, [[concepts/engine-thrust|thrust]] the flight into an emergency that would become known as "the miracle over the desert."

The incident escalated rapidly when Captain Machchhar, despite his injuries, managed to open the reinforced cockpit door. This pivotal action allowed passengers and cabin crew, who had heard the commotion, to intervene. While passenger Yaniv Hayun and others were subduing Al-Hammami, the First Officer managed to put the aircraft into an uncontrolled, steep dive, disconnecting the [[concepts/autopilot-agent|autopilot]] in the process. Flight data indicates the plane descended at extreme rates, potentially over 30,000 feet per minute, and sustained significant damage, including the destruction of most of its rudder. Furthermore, investigators believe Al-Hammami intentionally cut off both engines as his original plan to crash the aircraft at Ben Gurion Airport was thwarted.

Amidst this chaos, the two deadheading FlyDubai pilots demonstrated extraordinary [[concepts/resilience|resilience]]. They entered a blood-soaked cockpit, faced with a damaged aircraft, no [[concepts/autopilot-agent|autopilot]], no functioning rudder, and potentially dead engines. Their immediate task was to restart the engines, restore electrical and hydraulic power using the Auxiliary Power Unit (APU) and "windmilling" techniques, and regain control of the severely compromised aircraft. Displaying immense [[concepts/skill|skill]], they were able to stabilize the 737, dial in the international emergency codes for emergency (7700) and hijacking (7500), and successfully divert to Tabuk Airport in [[entities/saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], where they executed a safe landing.

The survival of all 180 individuals on board Flight 1073 is an incredible testament to human [[concepts/courage|courage]] and professionalism. The incident highlights critical failures in airline pilot vetting processes, as Al-Hammami's troubled past and pre-meditated intent to hijack and crash the plane were not identified before his employment. However, the overwhelming takeaway is the extraordinary heroism and [[concepts/resilience|resilience]] shown by Captain Machchhar, who fought off a brutal attack and opened the door, the quick-[[concepts/human-cognition|thinking]] passengers who subdued the attacker, and the two additional pilots who managed to land a severely damaged aircraft under unprecedented circumstances, averting a catastrophic outcome. This event serves as a powerful reminder of the [[concepts/value|importance]] of robust [[concepts/risk-mitigation|security measures]] and the remarkable capabilities of trained aviation professionals in the face of extreme adversity.
