# Needs Based Accounts

Needs-based accounts are philosophical frameworks for understanding wellbeing that ground human flourishing in the satisfaction of fundamental needs rather than subjective preferences or external measures. This approach diverges from purely subjective theories of wellbeing, which define it as the satisfaction of whatever an individual desires, by proposing that certain needs, when met, objectively contribute to genuine wellbeing regardless of whether a person recognizes or values them.

The core distinction between needs-based and preference-based theories lies in the source of value. While preference-based accounts rely on the individual's conscious desires, needs-based accounts argue that there are objective conditions necessary for a good life. These fundamental needs are considered universal prerequisites for human flourishing, suggesting that wellbeing can be assessed independently of an individual's current psychological state or cultural context.

This framework emphasizes that the satisfaction of these needs contributes to wellbeing in a non-contingent way. Even if an individual does not subjectively desire the fulfillment of a specific need, its satisfaction still enhances their overall state of flourishing. This perspective allows for a more robust evaluation of wellbeing that accounts for cases where individuals may be unaware of their own deficits or may have adapted their desires to poor circumstances.
