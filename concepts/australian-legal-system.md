# Australian legal system

The Australian legal system is a Common law system characterized by a [[concepts/hierarchy|hierarchy]] of courts, the rule of law, and the separation of powers. It operates under the Constitution of Australia and includes both federal and state/territory jurisdictions.

## Key Components

- **Court Hierarchy**: Cases typically move from lower courts (e.g., Local Court, Magistrates' Court) to intermediate courts (e.g., Territory) and finally to the High Court of Australia as the court of last resort.
- **Adversarial System**: Proceedings are contested between opposing parties (prosecution vs. defense or plaintiff vs. defendant), with an impartial judge or jury determining the outcome.
- **[[concepts/legal-appeal|Appeal Process]]**: Allows for review of decisions by higher courts on questions of law or, in limited circumstances, fact. Grounds for appeal include miscarriage of justice, procedural unfairness, or error of law.
