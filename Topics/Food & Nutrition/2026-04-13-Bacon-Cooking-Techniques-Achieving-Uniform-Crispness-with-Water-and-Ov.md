## Bacon Cooking Techniques: Achieving Uniform Crispness with Water and Oven Methods
**Clip title:** The Best Way To Cook Bacon? | Techniquely with Lan Lam
**Author / channel:** [[entities/america|America]]'s Test Kitchen
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tDBSQKEKrW4

### Summary
This video from [[entities/americas-test-kitchen|America's Test Kitchen]], hosted by [[entities/lan-lam|Lan Lam]], delves into the [[concepts/art|art]] and science of cooking bacon to perfection, addressing the common frustrations of inconsistent results. While even "mediocre" bacon is tasty, the aim is to achieve uniformly crispy, flavorful strips, regardless of cooking method. Lam highlights how subtle [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] to technique can elevate basic bacon [[concepts/preparation|preparation]].

Lam first demonstrates the conventional [[concepts/skillet-frying|skillet-frying]] method, placing cold bacon into a cold pan over [[entities/medium|medium]] heat. This approach, while familiar, often yields inconsistent results: strips buckle and curl as the meat and fat contract at different rates, leading to uneven browning, some dry/brittle meat, and some flabby, under-rendered fat. Success hinges on constant [[concepts/attention|attention]], flipping, and relying on sensory cues like the sizzling sound (water evaporating) and bubble size (larger for chewy, foamy for crispy) to gauge doneness. However, this method is slow and requires constant vigilance.

To combat these inconsistencies in pan-frying, the video introduces a game-changing technique: adding 1/4 cup of water to the cold skillet with the bacon before heating. The water gently and evenly heats the bacon, preventing early browning and buckling, allowing the fat to render slowly and thoroughly. Once the water evaporates, the bacon begins to fry in its own rendered fat, resulting in a more uniform, perfectly textured outcome with less hands-on tending. For larger quantities, the oven-fried method is recommended. Spreading bacon on a sheet pan (touching but not overlapping) and baking at 400°F offers a hands-off approach that yields evenly cooked, flat strips, typically taking 12-15 minutes for a pound.

The video also briefly touches on different bacon varieties, explaining that "center-cut" bacon has less fat but is often more expensive due to trimming waste. "Uncured" or "nitrate-free" labels can be misleading, as many use natural ingredients like celery powder or juice, which are rich in nitrates and act as curing agents. Finally, [thick-cut bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thick-cut_bacon), while more substantial, contains more water and thus requires longer cooking times to achieve crispiness. The ultimate takeaway is that understanding these techniques and bacon characteristics empowers cooks to consistently produce their preferred bacon – whether chewy or crispy, thick or thin, flat or ruffled – with confidence and better results.
