# Galaxy Formation And Evolution

[[concepts/galaxy-formation|Galaxy formation]] represents one of the central problems in modern [[concepts/cosmology|cosmology]] and [[concepts/astrophysics|astrophysics]]. Galaxies are thought to form through the [[concepts/gravitational-collapse|gravitational collapse]] of primordial density fluctuations in the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]], beginning roughly 100-200 million years after the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]]. These initial perturbations, seeded by quantum fluctuations and amplified through cosmic expansion, allowed gas and [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] to accumulate into the first structures. The earliest galaxies were smaller and more chaotic than present-day systems, with vigorous star formation and frequent mergers shaping their development.

## Formation Mechanisms

The [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|standard model]] of galaxy formation relies on hierarchical assembly, wherein smaller structures merge to form progressively larger galaxies over billions of years. Primordial gas cools and concentrates within dark matter potential wells, eventually becoming dense enough to ignite star formation. Early galaxies likely contained massive, short-lived stars (Population III) composed purely of hydrogen and helium. Subsequent generations of stars (Population II and I) incorporated heavy elements produced by [[concepts/solar-nucleosynthesis|stellar nucleosynthesis]], enriching the interstellar [[entities/medium|medium]] and enabling the formation of planetary systems.

## Evolutionary Processes

Galaxies evolve through multiple interconnected processes operating across vast timescales. [[concepts/stellar-evolution|Stellar evolution]] within galaxies generates [[concepts/supernovae|supernovae]] and [[concepts/active-galactic-nuclei|active galactic nuclei]] that inject energy into surrounding gas, regulating star formation rates. Gravitational interactions and mergers between galaxies redistribute mass and angular momentum, transforming galaxy morphologies from disk-dominated systems to elliptical forms. The accretion of gas from the intergalactic medium and the ejection of material through galactic winds also modulate galaxy growth. Over [[concepts/cosmic-time|cosmic time]], star formation rates have declined, and the universe has transitioned from a star-forming epoch in the early universe to a quiescent present day dominated by older stellar populations.
