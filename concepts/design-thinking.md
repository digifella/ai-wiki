# Design Thinking

Design [[concepts/human-cognition|thinking]] is a structured [[concepts/problem-solving-skills|problem-solving]] methodology that prioritizes understanding user needs and context before developing solutions. Rather than beginning with predetermined answers, the approach emphasizes empathetic exploration of constraints and requirements, followed by [[concepts/iterative-learning|iterative refinement]]. This human-centered orientation distinguishes design thinking from purely technical or intuitive problem-solving methods, making it broadly applicable across disciplines including [[concepts/product-design|product development]], organizational strategy, and service design.

## Core Process

The methodology typically follows a non-linear sequence of phases, beginning with empathizing with the end-user to define the problem accurately. This is followed by ideation, where a wide range of [[concepts/potential-solutions|potential solutions]] are generated without immediate judgment. The process then moves to prototyping, creating simplified versions of the [[concepts/solution|solution]] to test core hypotheses, and finally to testing, where [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] is gathered to refine the design. This cycle of [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] allows for continuous improvement based on [[concepts/real-world-data|real-world data]] rather than theoretical assumptions.

## Application in Mathematics and Cryptography

In the domain of [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]], [[concepts/open-source-philosophy|logic]], and [[concepts/cryptography|cryptography]], design thinking is applied to the creation of backgrounds and system architectures. As discussed by [[entities/simon-dentremont|Simon D'Entremont]], this approach involves analyzing the structural constraints and logical requirements of cryptographic systems before implementing specific [[concepts/algorithms|algorithms]]. By focusing on the user's interaction with the underlying mathematical framework, designers can create more intuitive and [[concepts/secure|secure]] environments. This method ensures that the complexity of cryptographic logic does not obscure usability, bridging the gap between abstract mathematical principles and practical application.
