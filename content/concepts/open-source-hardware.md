# Open Source Hardware

Open source hardware refers to physical devices, machines, and equipment whose design specifications, schematics, and documentation are publicly available under licenses that permit modification, [[concepts/distribution|distribution]], and study. Similar to open source [[concepts/software|software]], this approach emphasizes transparency and collaborative development, allowing engineers and makers to inspect, improve, and adapt [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] designs. Examples range from single-board computers and microcontrollers to scientific instruments, 3D printers, and [[concepts/robotics|robotics]] platforms.

## Design and Accessibility

The open source hardware model democratizes access to technology by removing barriers to understanding how devices work. Detailed documentation, circuit [[concepts/diagrams|diagrams]], and bill of materials (BOM) enable individuals and organizations to build, modify, or repair equipment without proprietary restrictions. This approach has proven particularly valuable in educational contexts, rapid prototyping, and resource-constrained environments where commercial alternatives may be prohibitively expensive or inflexible.

## Applications in Physics and Science

In scientific research, open source hardware enables reproducibility and [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] of experimental apparatus. Researchers can share instrument designs, modify them for specific applications, and build upon previous work without [[concepts/licensing|licensing]] constraints. Notable examples include spectrometers, particle detectors, and data acquisition systems developed collaboratively by the research community. The emerging [[entities/nano-banana|Nano banana]] project exemplifies contemporary efforts to create accessible, modifiable hardware tools for scientific and educational purposes.
