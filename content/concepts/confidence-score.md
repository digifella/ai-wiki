# Confidence Score

A confidence score is a numerical metric that quantifies the certainty or [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] of outputs generated by [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|Large Language Models]]. These scores help identify when an LLM may be producing hallucinations—plausible but factually incorrect information—by providing a measure of how confident the model is in its response. By surfacing this uncertainty, confidence scores enable both developers and end-users to assess the trustworthiness of model outputs and determine when additional [[concepts/verification|verification]] is needed.

## Implementation in RAG Systems

Confidence scores are particularly valuable in [[concepts/answer-generation|Retrieval-Augmented Generation]] (RAG) systems, where [[concepts/external-knowledge|external knowledge]] [[concepts/number-systems|bases]] provide grounding for model [[concepts/responses|responses]]. When a model retrieves relevant documents and generates an answer, the confidence score can reflect how well the generated [[concepts/text|text]] aligns with the retrieved sources. A low confidence score may indicate that the model is extrapolating beyond the provided context, signaling potential [[concepts/data-hallucination|hallucination]] risk.

## Integration with Prompt Engineering

Through careful [[concepts/prompt-based-modeling|prompt engineering]], developers can encourage [[concepts/models|models]] to [[concepts/output|output]] explicit confidence scores alongside their responses. Techniques such as asking the model to evaluate its own certainty, requesting multi-step [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] that exposes areas of uncertainty, or using [[concepts/structured-output|structured output]] formats can all contribute to more reliable confidence metrics. This allows for systematic filtering of low-confidence outputs before they reach end-users.
