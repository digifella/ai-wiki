# Satiety

Satiety is the physiological and psychological state of feeling full and satisfied after consuming food. It represents the opposite of hunger and plays a crucial role in regulating eating behavior and food intake. The sensation of satiety signals to the brain that the body has received adequate [[concepts/nutrition|nutrition]], typically leading to the cessation of eating. This mechanism is essential for maintaining energy balance and preventing overconsumption.

## Biological Mechanisms

Satiety is regulated through multiple interconnected systems in the body. The digestive process triggers the release of hormones such as cholecystokinin (CCK), peptide YY, and glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1), which signal fullness to the brain. Additionally, the stomach's physical distension from food volume contributes to satiety signals. Blood glucose levels, nutrient absorption, and the presence of certain macronutrients—particularly protein and fiber—also influence satiety [[concepts/responses|responses]].

## Practical Significance

Understanding satiety is relevant to nutrition planning and [[concepts/weight-loss|weight management]], as foods that promote sustained satiety can help reduce overall calorie intake and support weight loss goals. Different foods have varying satiety effects; for instance, protein-rich and fiber-containing foods typically produce greater satiety than refined carbohydrates. The concept of satiety index has been developed to measure and compare the satiating power of different foods, assisting individuals in making informed dietary choices.
