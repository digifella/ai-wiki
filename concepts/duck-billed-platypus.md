# Duck Billed Platypus

The duck-billed platypus is a [[concepts/egg-laying-mammal|monotreme]], a rare group of egg-laying [[entities/mammals|mammals]], native to eastern Australia and Tasmania. It is distinguished by its distinctive leathery bill resembling that of a duck, dense waterproof fur, and venomous spurs located on the hind legs of males. As a semi-aquatic species, it spends much of its time in freshwater rivers and streams, where it hunts for invertebrates.

The platypus is primarily nocturnal and relies on specialized sensory organs embedded in its bill to locate prey. These electroreception sensors detect the faint electrical fields generated by muscle contractions in hidden prey, allowing the animal to hunt effectively in murky water with its eyes, ears, and nose closed. This unique adaptation highlights the evolutionary divergence of monotremes from other mammalian lineages.
