# Knowing Versus Understanding

[[concepts/feynman|Richard Feynman]] distinguished between knowing the name of something and understanding what it actually is. In his view, merely being able to recite facts, definitions, or the names of phenomena does not constitute genuine understanding. For example, knowing that a bird's tail helps it balance during flight is different from understanding the aerodynamic principles, evolutionary pressures, and biomechanical mechanisms that explain why this is so. Feynman emphasized that true understanding involves being able to explain something in multiple ways, make predictions with it, and connect it to other knowledge.

## The Problem with Equivalent Theories

A particular challenge to understanding arises when multiple theories make identical predictions but rest on fundamentally different assumptions about reality. Scientists may "know" that two competing theories produce the same results without understanding which one—if either—actually describes how [[entities/nature|nature]] works. This equivalence can create a false sense of understanding, since the ability to make accurate predictions masks deeper questions about mechanism and causation. Feynman argued that progress requires moving beyond equivalence to ask which framework more truthfully represents physical reality.

## Understanding and Scientific Progress

Feynman contended that genuine understanding, rather than mere [[concepts/factual-knowledge|factual knowledge]], drives [[concepts/scientific-advancement|scientific advancement]]. A researcher who truly understands the principles underlying a phenomenon is better positioned to identify gaps, propose novel [[concepts/explanations|explanations]], and discover new [[concepts/software|applications]]. Understanding allows scientists to recognize when existing knowledge is incomplete and to develop more powerful theories. This perspective suggests that education and research should prioritize deep comprehension over the accumulation of facts or the ability to apply formulas mechanically.
