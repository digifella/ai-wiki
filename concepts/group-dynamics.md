# Group Dynamics

**[[concepts/team-dynamics|Group dynamics]]** refers to the scientific study of groups, typically human groups, and the patterns within and among them. It encompasses the forces that influence the behavior of group members, the interaction processes, and the structural properties of the group.

## Core Components

- **Interdependence**: Group members share a common fate or goal, influencing one another's outcomes.
- **Norms**: Shared expectations and rules that guide member behavior.
- **Roles**: Specific patterns of behavior expected from an individual in a group setting.
- **[[concepts/cohesion|Cohesion]]**: The extent to which members are attracted to the group and motivated to stay in it.
- **Power and Status**: Hierarchical structures emerging within the group that influence [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] and resource allocation.

## Key Processes

- **Communication**: The [[concepts/flow|flow]] of information, which can be formal or informal, impacting group efficiency.
- **[[concepts/ses-family|Conflict Resolution]]**: [[concepts/causes|Mechanisms]] for managing disagreements to maintain group functionality.
- **Decision Making**: Processes such as consensus, majority [[concepts/vote|vote]], or authoritarian decree.
- **Social Influence**: How individuals are affected by peer pressure, conformity, and obedience.

## Research Methods and Measurement

Understanding group dynamics often requires qualitative and quantitative methods to observe interactions and measure outcomes.

- [[entities/focus-group|Focus Group]] Interviewing is a prominent qualitative technique used to explore attitudes, beliefs, and perceptions within a group setting. This method leverages group interaction to elicit deeper insights than individual interviews might provide.
- [[Lab-Notes/2026-05-26-Focus-Group-Interviewing|Focus Group Interviewing]] serves as a practical application of studying how group context influences individual expression and collective meaning-making.
- Observational Studies and Social Network Analysis are also critical for mapping [[concepts/behavioral-types|interaction patterns]] and influence flows.
