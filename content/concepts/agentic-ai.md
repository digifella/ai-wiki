# Agentic AI

[[concepts/action-oriented-ai|Agentic AI]] refers to [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems designed to operate autonomously within defined domains, performing specific tasks with minimal human intervention. Unlike traditional [[concepts/ai-powered-applications|AI applications]] that require explicit step-by-step [[concepts/instructions|instructions]] for each action, [[concepts/agentic-frameworks|agentic systems]] can take independent decisions, plan sequences of actions, and work toward predefined objectives based on [[concepts/contextual-understanding|contextual understanding]]. These systems typically combine [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|large language models]] with planning mechanisms, [[concepts/memory|memory]] systems, and integrations with [[concepts/external-tools|external tools]] and APIs to execute [[concepts/complex-workflows|complex workflows]].

## Characteristics and Operation

[[concepts/ai-agentic-applications|Agentic AI systems]] function by perceiving their environment, [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] about appropriate actions, and executing tasks without requiring approval at each step. They operate within defined boundaries and objectives, using [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] loops to assess progress and adjust their approach as needed. Common [[concepts/software|applications]] include [[concepts/coding|software development]] assistance, where systems like [[entities/kombai|Kombai]] integrate with integrated [[concepts/developer-platforms|development environments]] (IDEs) to generate code and implement features; customer service [[concepts/automation|automation]]; research and analysis; and process automation across enterprise systems.

## Distinction from Standard AI

The key distinction between agentic and standard AI lies in autonomy and decision-making capability. Standard AI systems typically respond to direct queries or follow predetermined workflows, while agentic systems take initiative within their scope, break down complex problems into manageable steps, and persist toward goals across multiple interactions. This requires robust error-handling, the ability to recover from failed actions, and sometimes the capacity to seek clarification or request human intervention when encountering situations outside their operational [[concepts/parameters|parameters]].
