# Decluttering

## Overview

Decluttering is the process of removing unnecessary items from a space to create a more organized and functional environment. While many people focus on one-time decluttering projects, maintaining a clutter-free space requires ongoing [[concepts/habits|habits]] and systems to prevent items from accumulating again.

## The One-Minute Rule Approach

[[entities/gretchen-rubin|Gretchen Rubin]]'s [[concepts/daily-routines|One-Minute Rule]] offers a practical strategy for preventing clutter recurrence. The principle is straightforward: if a task takes less than one minute to complete, do it immediately rather than setting it aside for later. Applied to decluttering, this means addressing items as they arrive or accumulate—hanging up a coat, filing a document, or putting away a purchase—before they contribute to clutter buildup.

## Practical Application

This approach works by reducing the [[concepts/friction|friction]] between acquiring something and finding its proper place. By establishing the habit of immediate action on small tasks, people can prevent the gradual accumulation that typically leads to cluttered spaces. The rule acknowledges that clutter often results not from major [[concepts/acquisitions|acquisitions]] but from small items left in temporary locations that become permanent.
