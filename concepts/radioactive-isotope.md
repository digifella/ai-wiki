# Radioactive Isotope

A radioactive isotope (radioisotope) is an isotope of an element that possesses excess nuclear energy, [[concepts/fat-rendering|rendering]] it unstable. To achieve stability, it undergoes **radioactive decay**, emitting radiation in the form of particles (e.g., [[concepts/fine-structure-constant|alpha]], beta) or electromagnetic waves (e.g., [[concepts/gamma-rays|gamma rays]]). This process transforms the nuclide into a different isotope or element, often releasing significant energy.

## Key Characteristics
- **Instability**: Arises from an imbalance between [[concepts/protons|protons]] and neutrons in the nucleus.
- **Half-life ($t_{1/2}$)**: The time required for half of the [[concepts/radioactive-atoms|radioactive atoms]] in a sample to decay.
- **Applications**: Used in medicine (imaging, therapy), industry (tracing, sterilization), archaeology (radiometric dating), and energy production.

## Notable Radioisotopes & Observations

### Iron-60 ($^{60}\text{Fe}$)
$^{60}\text{Fe}$ is a rare, long-lived radioisotope produced in [[concepts/supernovae|supernova explosions]]. Its detection in terrestrial archives provides critical evidence regarding the solar system's interaction with the **interstellar [[entities/medium|medium]]**.

- **[[concepts/antarctica-ice-core|Antarctic Ice Core]] Analysis**: Recent analysis of Antarctic ice cores has detected anomalies in $^{60}\text{Fe}$ concentrations.
- **Interstellar Context**: These findings suggest the solar system is currently entering the **Local Interstellar Cloud**, a dense region of gas and dust. The influx of cosmic dust carrying supernova-derived $^{60}\text{Fe}$ serves as a tracer for this transition.
- **Source Reference**: [[lab-notes/2026-06-02-Antarctic-Ice-Fe-60-Solar-Systems-Entry-into-Local-Inter|Antarctic Ice Fe-60: Solar System's Entry into Local Interstellar Cloud]]

### Other Common Radioisotopes
- **Carbon-14 ($^{14}\text{C}$)**: Used for dating organic materials up to ~50,000 years old.
- **Uranium-235 ($^{235}\text{U}$)** & **Uranium-238 ($^{238}\text{U}$)**: Primary fuels for [[concepts/nuclear-reactors|nuclear reactors]] and weapons; parent isotopes for uranium-lead dating.
- **Potassium-40 ($^{40}\text{K}$)**: A naturally occurring radioisotope found in potassium; contributes to background radiation and used in potassium-argon dating.
- **Cobalt-60 ($^{60}\text{Co}$)**: Widely used in medical [[concepts/radiation-therapy|radiotherapy]] and industrial radiography.
