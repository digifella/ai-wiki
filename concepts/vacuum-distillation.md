# Vacuum Distillation

**Vacuum distillation** is a separation process that reduces the pressure above a liquid mixture, allowing components to boil at temperatures significantly lower than their standard atmospheric boiling points. This technique is critical for separating heat-sensitive compounds, preventing thermal decomposition, and enabling the distillation of high-boiling-point substances.

## Principles and Mechanisms
- **Lowered Boiling Points:** By reducing ambient pressure, the vapor pressure of the liquid equals the external pressure at a lower temperature.
- **Thermal [[concepts/secure|Protection]]:** Prevents degradation of thermally labile compounds (e.g., essential oils, pharmaceuticals, food extracts).
- **[[concepts/energy-efficiency|Energy Efficiency]]:** Can reduce [[concepts/energy-consumption|energy consumption]] compared to atmospheric distillation for high-boiling fractions.

## Applications

### Food and Beverage Industry
- **[[concepts/caffeine-removal|Decaffeination]]:** Used in the Swiss [[concepts/water-process|Water Process]] and other solvent-based methods to remove caffeine from coffee beans and tea leaves without destroying flavor compounds.
	- See: [[lab-notes/2026-06-24-Decaffeination-and-Non-Alcoholic-Beverages-Methods-Histo|Decaffeination and Non-Alcoholic Beverages: Methods, History, and Chemistry]]
- **[[concepts/non-alcoholic-beverages|Non-Alcoholic Beverages]]:** Employed to remove ethanol from wine and beer while retaining aroma and taste profiles.
	- Low-temperature vacuum distillation allows alcohol to evaporate at temperatures that preserve volatile organic compounds responsible for flavor.
- **Essential Oil Extraction:** Separation of delicate aromatic compounds from plant materials.

### Chemical and Pharmaceutical Industry
- **Purification:** [[concepts/disconnection|Isolation]] of heat-sensitive pharmaceuticals and vitamins.
- **High-Boiling Solvents:** Recovery and purification of solvents with high atmospheric boiling points.
