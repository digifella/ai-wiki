# Natural Ground Detection

Natural Ground Detection is a post-processing technique utilized within Adobe Lightroom and Adobe Camera Raw to identify and isolate terrestrial surfaces in landscape photography. By leveraging AI-powered masking tools, the software automatically distinguishes ground elements—such as soil, dirt, grass, and rock—from other image components like the sky, water, and vegetation. This selective isolation allows photographers to apply targeted adjustments to the terrain without affecting the rest of the composition.

The process relies on machine learning algorithms that analyze texture, color, and edge data to create precise selection masks. Users typically initiate this function through the Masking panel, where the "Ground" option generates a mask based on the detected horizon line and surface characteristics. This automation reduces the manual effort required for complex selections, particularly in images with intricate foreground details or uneven terrain.

Once the ground mask is generated, it can be refined using brush or gradient tools to correct any inaccuracies in the AI's detection. Photographers commonly use this isolation to enhance local contrast, adjust exposure, or modify color grading specifically for the earth elements. This ensures that adjustments to the ground do not inadvertently alter the sky or other non-terrestrial parts of the frame, maintaining a balanced and natural appearance in the final image.
