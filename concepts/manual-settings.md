# Manual Settings

Manual settings refer to the direct controls on cameras and scanning hardware that allow users to override automated functions. By disabling automatic exposure, focus, and color correction algorithms, operators gain precise command over the technical parameters of image capture or digitization. This approach is critical when dealing with source materials that exhibit high contrast, unusual color casts, or varying densities, as automatic systems often struggle to interpret these conditions consistently.

In the context of home film scanning, manual adjustments enable the preservation of original tonal ranges and color fidelity that software algorithms might otherwise flatten or distort. This method is particularly valuable for archival purposes, where the goal is to create a neutral, unaltered digital negative that retains the maximum amount of data from the physical film stock.

## Exposure and Density Control

Film scanners typically offer manual controls for exposure time and gain, which correspond to the brightness and sensitivity of the sensor. Operators must adjust these settings based on the specific density of the film negative, ensuring that highlights are not clipped and shadows retain detail. This process requires a understanding of the film's characteristic curve and the dynamic range of the scanning device.

## Color Calibration

Manual color settings allow users to define white balance and color profiles independently of the scanner’s internal database. This is essential for correcting color casts introduced by film aging or improper storage conditions. By manually adjusting the red, green, and blue channels, users can achieve a more accurate representation of the original scene, avoiding the inconsistent results often produced by automated color correction engines.
