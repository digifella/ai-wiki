# Uptime

Uptime refers to the period during which a system, service, or application remains operational and accessible without interruption. It is typically expressed as a percentage of total time over a defined period—such as a month or year—and serves as a standard measure of system reliability. For instance, 99.9% uptime means a system can tolerate approximately 43 minutes of downtime per month. The complementary metric, downtime, represents the periods when a system is unavailable or non-functional.

## Measurement and Standards

Uptime is commonly measured through continuous monitoring of service availability, often utilizing automated tools that ping endpoints at regular intervals. These tools track response times and error rates to determine if a service is functioning within expected parameters. Standards for acceptable uptime vary by industry and service level agreements (SLAs), with critical infrastructure often requiring higher availability thresholds than less essential applications.

## Impact on Operations

High uptime is crucial for maintaining user trust and ensuring business continuity, particularly for services that rely on constant accessibility. Conversely, frequent downtime can lead to significant financial losses, reputational damage, and operational disruptions. Organizations often implement redundancy, failover mechanisms, and robust maintenance schedules to minimize unplanned outages and maximize overall system reliability.
