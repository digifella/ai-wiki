# Lunar Flyby

A lunar flyby is a [[concepts/human-spaceflight|crewed spaceflight]] trajectory that brings a spacecraft and its crew near the Moon without entering lunar orbit or landing on the surface. This mission profile allows astronauts to travel beyond Earth orbit, conduct scientific observations, and test [[concepts/spacecraft-systems|spacecraft systems]] in the deep [[concepts/vacuum-of-space|space environment]] while returning to Earth on a predetermined path.

## Artemis II

[[entities/artemis-ii|Artemis II]] represents [[concepts/nasa|NASA]]'s second crewed mission in the [[concepts/artemis-program|Artemis program]], planned as a lunar flyby rather than a landing mission. The flight [[entities/will|will]] carry a crew aboard the [[entities/orion|Orion spacecraft]], which will travel to the Moon, [[concepts/loop|loop]] around it, and return to Earth. This mission serves as a critical test of Orion's life support systems, thermal protection, and [[concepts/crew-operations|crew operations]] procedures in the lunar environment before subsequent missions attempt a crewed landing.

## Spacecraft and Systems

The Orion spacecraft provides the life support, propulsion, and reentry systems necessary for a lunar flyby mission. During the flight, the crew will evaluate Orion's performance in deep space, including navigation, communications, and the behavior of onboard systems under the conditions encountered beyond Earth's protective magnetic field. [[concepts/testing|Testing]] these systems during an uncrewed or minimally crewed flight helps validate procedures and identify any operational challenges before more complex lunar missions.
