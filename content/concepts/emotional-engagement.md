# Emotional Engagement

Emotional engagement in [[concepts/health|healthcare]] refers to the psychological and affective [[concepts/connection|connection]] patients develop with their own health conditions, treatment plans, and the [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] formed with care providers. This concept recognizes that health outcomes are not determined by clinical factors alone, but also by the patient's internal emotional investment in their care journey. Strong emotional engagement has been associated with improved adherence to treatment, better self-management of chronic conditions, and higher satisfaction with healthcare experiences.

## Dimensions of Patient Engagement

Emotional engagement operates across several interconnected dimensions. It includes the patient's willingness to actively participate in health decisions, their emotional response to diagnosis and prognosis, and the quality of trust established with healthcare providers. The strength of these emotional connections influences how patients process health information, make behavioral changes, and cope with ongoing treatment or lifestyle modifications.

## Clinical Significance

Research indicates that patients with higher emotional engagement demonstrate better health literacy comprehension, greater [[concepts/motivation|motivation]] for [[concepts/preventive-care|preventive care]], and more effective communication with their care teams. Conversely, low emotional engagement may manifest as treatment avoidance, medication non-adherence, or [[concepts/disengagement|disengagement]] from necessary health interventions. Healthcare providers can foster emotional engagement through empathetic communication, shared decision-making practices, and [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to the psychological dimensions of care alongside clinical considerations.
