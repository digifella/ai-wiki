# Spaceflight Operations

Spaceflight operations comprise the integrated systems, procedures, and management protocols required to conduct crewed [[concepts/space-exploration|space missions]] safely and effectively. These operations span multiple mission phases, from pre-launch [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] through in-flight management to post-mission recovery. The discipline involves [[concepts/coordination|coordination]] between ground control centers, [[concepts/spacecraft-systems|spacecraft systems]], and crew members to maintain mission objectives while responding to real-time conditions and unforeseen challenges.

## Mission Phases and Ground Control

Spaceflight operations are organized around distinct mission phases, each with specific procedural requirements and decision points. Ground control teams monitor vehicle systems, trajectory, and crew status continuously during flight, issuing [[concepts/commands|commands]] and [[concepts/recommendations|guidance]] to the spacecraft. This coordination requires robust communication infrastructure, redundant systems for critical functions, and established protocols for [[concepts/anomaly|anomaly]] response. Real-time [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] by mission controllers depends on accurate telemetry data and pre-planned contingency procedures.

## Crew Operations and System Management

Onboard operations require crews to execute complex procedures, monitor spacecraft systems, and manage consumables such as power, water, and oxygen. Astronauts undergo extensive training to operate systems in nominal and off-nominal conditions, coordinate with ground control, and execute time-critical activities. The [[entities/orion|Orion spacecraft]] and similar vehicles incorporate automation for routine functions while requiring crew intervention for [[concepts/complex-tasks|complex tasks]] and emergency [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]].

## Mission-Critical Challenges

Spaceflight operations must address inherent challenges including communication delays, system redundancy requirements, and the unforgiving environment of space. Operators must account for vehicle performance under various conditions, crew factors including fatigue and [[concepts/stress|stress]], and the limited ability to provide in-person support. Successful operations depend on thorough pre-mission planning, comprehensive crew training, and well-established communication protocols between all parties involved in the mission.
