# Landscape Photography Editing

Landscape photography editing focuses on enhancing natural scenes while preserving their authentic appearance through a structured workflow of technical adjustments. The process typically begins with global corrections to establish a solid tonal foundation, including fine-tuning exposure, white balance, and contrast. These initial steps ensure that the image’s overall brightness and color temperature accurately reflect the lighting conditions of the original scene before more specific manipulations are applied.

Following global adjustments, the editor addresses local variations in light and detail using masking tools. This stage involves selectively adjusting clarity, dehazing, and saturation to recover shadow details or manage bright skies without introducing artifacts. Modern workflows increasingly utilize automated AI masking presets in Lightroom Classic to identify subjects such as skies, foregrounds, and vegetation, significantly reducing the time required for precise selection and allowing for more nuanced tonal grading.

The final phase involves refining the image’s overall mood and sharpness. This includes applying lens corrections to fix distortion and chromatic aberration, followed by targeted sharpening and noise reduction. The goal is to produce a cohesive image that balances technical accuracy with artistic interpretation, ensuring that the final output retains the natural texture and atmosphere of the captured landscape.
