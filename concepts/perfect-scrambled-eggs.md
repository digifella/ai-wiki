# Perfect Scrambled Eggs

Perfect scrambled eggs are defined by a creamy, custard-like texture composed of small, delicate curds. This culinary standard contrasts sharply with common failures such as rubbery, dry, or watery results, which typically stem from improper heat management or excessive cooking times. The technique prioritizes the preservation of moisture and the refinement of mouthfeel through precise control over the cooking process.

The success of this method relies heavily on managing protein coagulation, a chemical process that begins at relatively low temperatures. Egg whites start to set at approximately 60°C (140°F), while yolks coagulate at slightly higher temperatures. This difference creates a specific window of control, allowing the cook to remove the eggs from heat before the proteins tighten excessively and expel moisture.

Achieving the ideal consistency requires maintaining low, steady heat and constant agitation. By stirring the eggs continuously as they warm, the cook ensures that curds form uniformly and remain small. The eggs are typically removed from the heat source while they still appear slightly underdone, as residual heat will continue to cook them to the final desired state without overcooking.
