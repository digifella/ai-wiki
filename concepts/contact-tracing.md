# Contact Tracing

**[[entities/contact|Contact]] tracing** is a [[concepts/population-health|public health]] strategy to identify and monitor individuals who have been in contact with someone infected with a contagious disease. It aims to break chains of transmission by isolating cases and quarantining contacts.

## Methods & Evolution

*   **Manual Tracing:** Traditional method relying on trained [[entities/employees|personnel]] interviewing cases to identify contacts. Labor-intensive but effective for targeted outbreaks.
*   **Digital Tracing:** Utilization of technology to supplement or replace manual efforts, particularly during large-scale pandemics like [[concepts/covid-19]].
    *   Involves use of mobile [[concepts/apps|apps]], Bluetooth proximity logging, and GPS data.
    *   Raises significant legal and ethical concerns regarding [[concepts/ai-security]], [[concepts/gdpr|GDPR]], and surveillance states.
    *   See: [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Bradford---COVID-19-contact-tracing-apps|Bradford - CO

## Framework Context & Systems Integration

*   Digital disease control efforts are increasingly viewed through the lens of broader digital health transformations, as outlined in frameworks like the HIMSS - WP-Digital-Health-A-Framework-For-Healthcare-Transformation.
*   **Systemic Integration:** Effective control requires integrating surveillance data with predictive analytics to move from reactive tracing to proactive risk mitigation.
*   **Person-Enabled Health]]:** Modern frameworks emphasize shifting from purely provider-centric surveillance to models where patients actively manage and share data, requiring robust [[concepts/privacy|privacy]] safeguards.
*   **Indicator Metrics:** [[concepts/success|Success]] is measured not just by case reduction but by system-wide [[concepts/indicators|indicators]] including data interoperability and patient engagement levels.
