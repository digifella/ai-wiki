# Batch Testing

Batch [[concepts/testing|testing]] is a [[concepts/quality-control|quality control]] methodology in pharmaceutical and chemical manufacturing in which multiple samples from production batches are analyzed simultaneously to identify variations in substance properties, composition, or behavior. This approach enables rapid detection of anomalies across a series of manufactured units rather than testing individual items in isolation.

## Application to the Ritonavir Crisis

Batch testing played a critical role in investigating the Ritonavir [[concepts/crystalline-structure|polymorph crisis]] of the mid-1990s. The HIV protease inhibitor Ritonavir, approved and distributed by [[entities/abbott-laboratories|Abbott Laboratories]], began failing bioavailability [[concepts/open-standards|standards]] in clinical use despite meeting all specifications during initial testing. Systematic batch testing of archived samples revealed that the drug had spontaneously converted from one crystalline polymorph to another—a more stable but poorly absorbable form—during [[entities/storage|storage]] and [[concepts/distribution|distribution]]. This discovery demonstrated that batch testing could expose manufacturing and stability issues that single-sample testing might miss, and highlighted the importance of monitoring polymorphic transitions in pharmaceutical development.

## Significance

The Ritonavir case established batch testing as an essential practice for detecting polymorphic changes and other time-dependent variations in drug substances. The methodology underscored that pharmaceutical [[concepts/quality-assurance|quality assurance]] requires not only meeting initial specifications but also tracking batch behavior across storage conditions and time periods.
