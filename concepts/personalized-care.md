# Personalized Care

[[concepts/personalised-care|Personalized care]] in [[concepts/allied-health|allied health]] refers to [[concepts/health|health]] interventions tailored to the individual characteristics, preferences, and measured health data of specific patients. Rather than applying standardized treatment protocols uniformly, personalized care uses information gathered about each patient to inform [[concepts/clinical-reasoning|clinical decision-making]] and adjust care plans accordingly.

## Wearable Monitoring Technology

[[concepts/wearable-devices|Wearable devices]] equipped with [[concepts/embedded-sensors|embedded sensors]] play a central role in gathering the continuous health data necessary for [[concepts/personalization|personalization]]. These devices track metrics such as heart rate, [[concepts/exercise|physical activity]], [[concepts/sleep|sleep]] patterns, and other physiological markers. The data collected provides [[entities/allied-health|allied health]] practitioners with real-time information about patient behavior and health status between clinical visits, enabling more informed and timely [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] to treatment plans.

## Clinical Application

In allied health practice—which includes professions such as physiotherapy, occupational therapy, and rehabilitation services—personalized care allows practitioners to modify therapeutic approaches based on [[concepts/purpose|objective]] measurement and individual response. For example, a physiotherapist might adjust exercise intensity or type based on activity data from [[concepts/internet-of-things|wearables]], or monitor patient [[concepts/compliance|compliance]] and progress in real time rather than relying solely on self-report or periodic assessments.
