# Automated Redlining

Automated redlining refers to the use of [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems to review documents and suggest edits, particularly in legal and professional contexts. Rather than relying solely on manual human review, these systems automatically identify potential issues such as inconsistencies, formatting errors, policy violations, and areas requiring revision. The AI flags these findings for human reviewers to evaluate, rather than making changes independently.

## Workflow and Implementation

Automated redlining tools integrate into existing document workflows, often as [[concepts/plugins|plugins]] or extensions within word [[concepts/central-processing-units|processors]] like Microsoft Word. Claude for Word exemplifies this approach, functioning as an AI co-pilot that operates within the familiar Microsoft Word environment. Users can request document review, and the system analyzes text against specified criteria, generating suggested edits and [[concepts/explanations|explanations]] for proposed changes.

## Benefits and Limitations

The primary advantage of automated redlining is efficiency—it reduces the time spent on initial document review passes by handling routine identification tasks. This allows human reviewers to focus on substantive decisions, strategic revisions, and matters requiring judgment or domain expertise. However, these systems function as assistive tools rather than replacements for human review, as legal and professional documents often require [[concepts/contextual-understanding|contextual understanding]] and [[concepts/accountability|accountability]] that currently remains within human purview.
