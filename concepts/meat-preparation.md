Meat [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] encompasses the techniques and methods used to cook various protein sources, including poultry, pork, beef, and fish. These methods transform raw meat into safe, palatable dishes through the application of heat and complementary cooking processes. The choice of preparation method depends on several factors, including the cut of meat, desired [[concepts/texture-slider|texture]], flavor profile, [[concepts/cooking-time|cooking time]] available, and equipment at hand.

## Primary Cooking Methods

[[concepts/bbq|Grilling]] involves cooking meat directly over heat, typically on a grate above charcoal, gas, or wood flames. This method creates a browned exterior through the [[concepts/maillard-reaction|Maillard reaction]] while sealing in juices. Oven-cooking provides controlled, indirect heat suitable for larger cuts and tougher pieces that benefit from longer cooking times. [[concepts/seafood-poaching|Poaching]] submerges meat in simmering liquid, a gentler method that preserves moisture and is commonly used for chicken and fish. Other techniques include [[concepts/pan-searing|pan-searing]], roasting, and braising, each suited to different cuts and desired outcomes.

## Preparation Techniques

Beyond [[concepts/cooking-methods|cooking methods]], several preparatory techniques enhance flavor and texture. [[concepts/marinating|Marinating]] involves soaking meat in a seasoned liquid before cooking, which can tenderize tougher cuts and infuse flavor. Seasoning with salt, spices, and herbs is applied either before cooking or incorporated into marinades. Proper handling, including appropriate thawing procedures and maintaining food [[concepts/product-safety|safety standards]], is essential to prevent foodborne illness.

The internal temperature reached during cooking is critical for both safety and desired doneness. Different meats have different safe minimum temperatures; for example, poultry requires higher temperatures than beef to eliminate pathogens. Understanding these variables allows cooks to prepare meat that is both safe to eat and suited to individual preferences.
