*   **Batteries and Energy Storage: ** DC is the native power source for rechargeable batteries.
*   **Constant Value: ** Voltage and current are stable over time (in ideal systems).
*   **Electronics: ** Most modern electronic circuits operate on DC power.
*   **Polarity: ** DC has a defined positive and negative terminal.
*   **Renewable Energy Systems: ** Solar panels and battery storage systems inherently produce DC power.
*   **Specialized Transmission: ** DC transmission is utilized in high-voltage applications where efficiency is prioritized.
*   **Unidirectional Flow: ** The charge moves in a single direction.

## HVDC Transmission

[[concepts/high-voltage-direct-current-hvdc|High-Voltage Direct Current (HVDC)]] transmission is a method used to transmit large amounts of electrical power over long distances with lower [[concepts/energy-dissipation|energy loss]] compared to AC transmission.

*   **Principle:** HVDC uses electricity flowing in a direct current format to minimize reactive power issues during long-distance transmission.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Reduced energy loss over long distances.
    *   Allows for asynchronous interconnection of [[concepts/grids|grids]].
*   **Relevance:** Developments in HVDC technology are seeing a renewed focus in specialized areas, such as the advancements demonstrated by Sweden's HVDC [[concepts/innovation|Innovation]]: Resurgence of Direct Current Transmission.

## Historical Context

The evolution of power transmission has involved a rivalry between DC and AC systems. AC systems became dominant due to their ability to easily step voltage up and down (using [[concepts/transformers|transformers]]), which made long-distance power distribution more practical. However, DC remains crucial in the realm of energy [[entities/storage|storage]] and specialized, high-efficiency transmission methods.
