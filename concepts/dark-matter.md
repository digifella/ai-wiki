# Dark Matter

**Dark matter** is a hypothetical form of matter that is thought to account for approximately 85% of the matter in the universe and about 27% of its total mass–energy [[concepts/density|density]]. Its presence is inferred from [[concepts/gravitational-effects|gravitational effects]] on visible matter, radiation, and the large-scale structure of the universe.

## Key Properties
- **Non-luminous:** Does not emit, absorb, or reflect [[concepts/light|light]], making it invisible to electromagnetic observations.
- **Gravitational Influence:** Interacts with normal matter primarily through [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]].
- **[[concepts/writing|Composition]]:** Likely consists of [[concepts/wimp-particles|weakly interacting massive particles]] ([[concepts/wimp|WIMPs]]) or axions, though the exact nature remains unknown.

## Detection Methods
- **Gravitational Lensing:** Observing how dark matter bends light from [[concepts/distant-galaxies|distant galaxies]].
- **Galaxy Rotation Curves:** Analyzing the [[concepts/speed|speed]] of [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]] in galaxies to infer unseen mass.
- **[[concepts/cosmic-background-radiation|Cosmic Microwave Background]]:** Analyzing anisotropies in the CMB to constrain dark matter density.
- **Space-Based Observatories:** Utilizing advanced telescopes like the [[concepts/falcon-heavy|Roman Space Telescope]]: Initial Successes and Projected 22-Year Lifespan to map dark matter distribution via weak lensing surveys.

## Recent Developments
- The **[[concepts/nancy-grace-roman-space-telescope|Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope]]** has demonstrated initial successes in simulating launch parameters and optical performance, projecting a 22-year lifespan.
- These capabilities are critical for next-generation gravitational-lensing studies, allowing for high-[[concepts/accuracy|precision]] mapping of dark matter [[concepts/halos|halos]] and their influence on large-scale structure.
