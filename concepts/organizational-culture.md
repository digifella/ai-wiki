# Organizational Culture

Organizational culture encompasses the [[concepts/cultural-ethos|shared values]], beliefs, norms, and behavioral patterns that characterize how an organization functions. Developed through repeated interactions and collective experiences over time, culture operates as an informal system of guidelines that influences how members communicate, make decisions, and perform their work. Unlike formal [[concepts/policies|policies]] and procedures, organizational culture is often implicit and deeply internalized by organizational members, shaping both visible behaviors and underlying assumptions about how things should be done.

## Formation and Transmission

Organizational culture typically emerges from the founding principles established by an organization's leaders, the experiences the organization has accumulated, and the individuals who comprise it. New members learn cultural norms through observation, mentorship, and [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] from established members. Stories, rituals, symbols, and ceremonies serve as [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] through which cultural values are transmitted and reinforced across generations of organizational members.

## Impact and Function

Culture influences organizational outcomes by affecting [[concepts/motivation|motivation]], collaboration, and [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] processes. A strong organizational culture can enhance member commitment and performance, while also establishing shared identity. Conversely, cultural disconnects between formal organizational goals and actual practices can lead to inefficiency or internal [[concepts/conflict|conflict]]. Culture also plays a role in how organizations adapt to external changes and how they are perceived by external stakeholders.

## Cultural Variation

Different organizations develop distinct cultures based on their industry, history, [[concepts/leadership|leadership]], and environmental context. Subcultures often emerge within larger organizations, particularly across departments or geographic locations, creating variation in values and practices within a single organizational entity.
