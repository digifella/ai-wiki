# Telephoto Lens

A telephoto lens is a camera lens with a long [[concepts/focal-length|focal length]], typically 70mm or greater, designed to magnify distant subjects and bring them closer in the frame. These lenses allow photographers to capture detail from subjects that are far away without physically moving closer, making them essential tools for wildlife, sports, and [[concepts/portrait-photography|portrait photography]]. The magnification effect is proportional to the focal length—a 200mm lens provides approximately twice the magnification of a 100mm lens.

## Optical Design and Function

Telephoto lenses use a specialized internal optical design that makes the physical lens length shorter than the actual focal length. This is achieved through a telephoto group—a configuration of lens elements that extends the focal length while keeping the lens body compact. The result is a lens that can be held and used handheld, unlike simple long-focal-length designs that would be impractically large and heavy.

## Trade-offs and Practical Considerations

While telephoto lenses excel at magnification, they come with specific trade-offs. They typically have narrower maximum apertures (such as f/5.6 or f/8) compared to wider lenses, which reduces light gathering ability and requires faster shutter speeds to avoid motion blur. Their narrow field of view demands precise composition and framing. Telephoto lenses are also more prone to camera shake due to magnification of any hand [[concepts/exercise|movement]], making image stabilization technology or tripod use increasingly important at longer focal lengths.
