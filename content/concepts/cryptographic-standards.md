# Cryptographic Standards

Cryptographic [[concepts/open-standards|standards]] are established protocols and algorithms used to [[concepts/secure|secure]] digital communications and protect sensitive data through encryption. These standards have evolved over decades to maintain [[concepts/security|security]] against computational threats, with widely adopted examples including RSA, elliptic curve [[concepts/cryptography|cryptography]] (ECC), and various symmetric encryption schemes. They form the foundation of modern [[concepts/cybersecurity|cybersecurity]] infrastructure, protecting everything from financial transactions to state communications.

## Quantum Computing Threat

The emergence of [[concepts/quantum-computing|quantum computing]] presents a significant challenge to current cryptographic standards. Quantum computers, which leverage quantum mechanical properties to perform computations fundamentally differently from classical computers, are [[entities/theoretically-media|theoretically]] capable of breaking many widely-used encryption schemes in practical timeframes. Researchers anticipate this threshold, termed "[[concepts/q-day|Q-Day]]," could occur around 2029, though estimates remain uncertain and depend on advances in quantum [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] development.

## Post-Quantum Cryptography

In response to this threat, cryptographic standards bodies including NIST have initiated efforts to develop and standardize post-quantum [[concepts/cryptographic-algorithms|cryptographic algorithms]]—[[concepts/encryption-methods|encryption methods]] resistant to attacks by both classical and quantum computers. These new standards are expected to eventually replace vulnerable legacy algorithms, requiring substantial updates to existing [[concepts/digital-infrastructure|digital infrastructure]] and security systems worldwide. The transition represents one of the largest cryptographic standardization efforts undertaken since modern encryption became ubiquitous.
