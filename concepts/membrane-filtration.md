# Membrane Filtration

**Membrane filtration** is a physical separation process that uses semi-permeable membranes to separate particles, macromolecules, or other substances from a [[concepts/solution|solution]] based on size exclusion, charge, or affinity. It is widely used in water treatment, pharmaceuticals, and food processing.

## Core Principles
- **Semi-permeable Membranes**: Barriers that allow certain molecules or ions to pass through by diffusion or via active transport while blocking others.
- **Driving Forces**: Pressure (pressure-driven), concentration gradients (osmosis/dialysis), or electrical potential (electrodialysis).
- **Separation [[concepts/causes|Mechanisms]]**:
  - **Size Exclusion**: Primary mechanism for microfiltration and ultrafiltration.
  - **Solution-Diffusion**: Dominant in [[concepts/desalination-technology|reverse osmosis]] and nanofiltration.
  - **Charge Repulsion**: Significant in nanofiltration and electrodialysis.

## Classification by Pore Size
1. **Microfiltration (MF)**: 0.1–10 μm. Removes bacteria, suspended solids, and large colloids.
2. **Ultrafiltration (UF)**: 0.01–0.1 μm. Separates macromolecules (proteins, polysaccharides) and viruses.
3. **Nanofiltration (NF)**: 1–10 nm. Removes divalent ions, organic molecules, and some monovalent ions.
4. **Reverse Osmosis (RO)**: <1 nm. Removes nearly all dissolved salts, ions, and small organic molecules.

## Applications
- **Water Treatment**: [[concepts/desalination|Desalination]], wastewater recycling, and potable water purification.
- **Food and Beverage**: Concentration of juices, dairy processing (cheese whey), and clarification of wines/beers.
- **Pharmaceuticals**: Sterilization, protein purification, and virus removal.
- **Industrial**: [[concepts/metal|Metal]] recovery, chemical recycling, and effluent treatment.

## Integration with Beverage Processing
Membrane technologies are increasingly relevant in the production of specialized beverages, including decaffeinated and non-alcoholic products. Recent analyses highlight the intersection of traditional chemical extraction methods with modern separation techniques.

- **[[concepts/caffeine-removal|Decaffeination]] Context**: While traditional decaffeination often relies on [[concepts/solvent-extraction|solvent extraction]] (e.g., methylene chloride, ethyl acetate) or supercritical CO₂, membrane-based approaches are being explored for selective removal of caffeine without compromising flavor compounds. See [[lab-notes/2026-06-24-Decaffeination-and-Non-Alcoholic-Beverages-Methods-Histo|Decaffeination and Non-Alcoholic Beverages: Methods, History, and Chemistry]] for a detailed overview of historical and chemical methods in decaffeination.
- **[[concepts/non-alcoholic-beverages|Non-Alcoholic Beverages]]**: Membrane distillation and [[concepts/vacuum-distillation|vacuum distillation]] are key technologies for removing ethanol from wine and beer while retaining volatile aroma compounds, a process distinct from simple filtration but complementary in beverage [[entities/national-academies|engineering]].

## Advantages and Limitations
- **Advantages**:
  - No [[concepts/state-of-matter-phase-transition|phase change]] required (lower energy than distillation).
  - Operates at ambient temperatures (preserves heat-sensitive compounds).
  - Scalable and modular.
- **Limitations**:
  - **Fouling**: Accumulation of contaminants on membrane surfaces reduces flux and requires cleaning.
  - **Membrane Degradation**: Chemical or biological breakdown over time.
  - **High Pressure Requirements**: Especially for RO, leading to significant [[concepts/energy-consumption|energy consumption]].
