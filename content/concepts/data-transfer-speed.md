# Data Transfer Speed

Data transfer [[concepts/speed|speed]] refers to the rate [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] which information moves through a cable or [[concepts/connection|connection]], typically measured in gigabits per second (Gbps). In the context of [[concepts/usb-20|USB-C cables]], this specification represents one of several technical [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] that users must evaluate when selecting a cable, though it is frequently confused with other features such as [[concepts/power|power]] delivery capacity or charging speed.

## USB-C Specification Complexity

[[entities/usb-c-port|USB-C]] cables support multiple data transfer [[concepts/open-standards|standards]], including USB 2.0 (480 Mbps), [[concepts/usb-30|USB 3.0]] (5 Gbps), USB 3.1 (10 Gbps), and USB 3.2 (20 Gbps). The actual transfer speed achieved depends on both the cable's rating and the devices it connects, as both must support the same standard for that speed to be realized. Many commercially available USB-C cables do not clearly label their data transfer capabilities, leading to consumer confusion about whether a cable is suitable for high-speed data transfer versus basic charging.

## Practical Implications

For most everyday users transferring [[concepts/files|files]] between computers and external drives, the distinction between different transfer speeds may have minimal practical impact. However, professionals working with large video files, photographers transferring image libraries, or those using external [[entities/storage|storage]] systems may notice significant time differences between cables supporting different USB standards. Understanding a cable's actual specifications—rather than relying on marketing claims or physical appearance—is essential for matching it to intended [[concepts/scenarios|use cases]].
