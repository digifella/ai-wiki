# Mountain Detection

Mountain Detection refers to a [[concepts/tutorial|tutorial]] technique demonstrated by [[entities/colin-smith-channel|Colin Smith]] for using [[concepts/landscape-masks|Landscape Masks]] in [[entities/adobe-lightroom|Adobe Lightroom]] and [[concepts/camera-raw|Camera Raw]]. The method enables photographers to selectively edit specific areas of landscape [[concepts/images|images]], particularly mountain regions, with precision. By isolating these geographic features through [[concepts/layer-masks|masking]] tools, editors can apply targeted [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] without affecting other parts of the photograph.

## Application in Post-Processing

The technique leverages built-in [[concepts/masking|masking]] [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] within Lightroom and [[entities/camera-raw-filter|Camera Raw]] to identify and select landscape elements automatically or manually. Once a mountain or similar feature is isolated through a landscape mask, adjustments to [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], clarity, color, and other [[concepts/parameters|parameters]] can be applied exclusively to that region. This selective approach allows for more nuanced edits than global adjustments across the entire image.

## Relevance to Image Sharpness

The tutorial emphasizes achieving tack-sharp photographs through post-processing refinement. By using landscape detection and masking, photographers can apply localized sharpening, clarity, and [[concepts/texture|texture]] enhancements to mountain peaks and distant landscape features where detail is critical. This targeted sharpening approach can enhance perceived image quality without introducing artifacts in other image areas.
