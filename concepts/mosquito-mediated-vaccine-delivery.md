# Mosquito Mediated Vaccine Delivery

Mosquito-mediated vaccine delivery is an immunization strategy that utilizes mosquitoes as biological vectors to administer vaccines through their bites. Instead of relying on conventional injection or oral administration, vaccine agents are incorporated into the mosquitoes and delivered to human hosts during blood feeding. This approach leverages the mosquito's natural feeding behavior and the localized immune response triggered at the bite site to generate protective immunity.

The mechanism involves engineering mosquitoes to carry specific vaccine antigens, which are then introduced into the host's skin during the feeding process. This method aims to stimulate a robust immune response directly at the site of inoculation. By utilizing the vector's natural behavior, the strategy seeks to improve vaccine uptake and efficacy, particularly in regions where traditional healthcare infrastructure is limited.

Research has focused on applying this technique to malaria vaccines, with studies indicating the potential for achieving long-term protection. Despite the short half-life of the delivered drug or antigen components, the method has demonstrated the ability to induce sustained immunity. This makes it a promising avenue for combating vector-borne diseases in areas with high transmission rates.
