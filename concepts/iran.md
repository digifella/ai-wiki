# Iran

[[entities/iran|Iran]] is a country located in Western Asia, positioned at the crossroads of the Middle East, South Asia, and Central Asia. With a population of approximately 88 million people, it is the second-most populous country in the Middle East after Egypt. The capital and largest city is Tehran. Iran's strategic geographic location has made it historically and contemporaneously significant for trade, culture, and [[concepts/geopolitics|geopolitics]].

## Strategic Importance

Iran's most significant geopolitical asset is its control of the [[entities/strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], a critical maritime chokepoint through which approximately one-fifth of global oil exports pass. This geographic advantage has shaped Iran's regional influence and its [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] with major international powers. The country's position also provides access to important trade routes connecting the Persian Gulf, Arabian Sea, and broader Indian Ocean region.

## Climate and Natural Resources

Iran possesses substantial natural resource wealth, particularly in oil and natural gas reserves. However, the country [[concepts/faces|faces]] significant climate challenges, including [[concepts/water-scarcity|water scarcity]], desertification, and periodic droughts that have affected [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]] and urban water supplies. These environmental pressures have implications for both domestic stability and regional [[concepts/security|security]].

## International Relations

Iran's foreign policy and international standing have been shaped by decades of geopolitical tensions, including sanctions regimes, [[concepts/nuclear-diplomacy|nuclear negotiations]], and regional conflicts involving various state and [[concepts/civic-society|non-state actors]]. These dynamics continue to influence Iran's role in Middle Eastern affairs and its relationships with global powers.
