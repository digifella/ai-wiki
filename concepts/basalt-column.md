# Basalt Column

**Basalt columns** are naturally occurring, polygonal rock formations characterized by vertical, column-like structures. They are a classic example of columnar jointing, typically resulting from the cooling and contraction of basaltic lava.

## Formation Mechanism

The formation process is driven by thermodynamic principles during the solidification of [[concepts/lava-flows|lava flows]]:

*   **Cooling and Contraction:** As basaltic lava flows and cools, it undergoes significant [[concepts/thermal-contraction|thermal contraction]].
*   **[[concepts/stress|Stress]] Accumulation:** The contraction generates [[concepts/tensile-stress|tensile stress]] within the rock mass.
*   **Fracture Propagation:** To relieve this stress, the rock fractures in a pattern that minimizes surface energy, typically forming hexagonal or polygonal shapes.
*   **Vertical Growth:** Fractures propagate vertically downward from the cooling surface, creating the characteristic columnar structure.

## Key Concepts

*   Columnar Jointing: The geological term for this type of fracture pattern.
*   Thermal Contraction: The physical process of material shrinking as it cools.
*   Basalt: The fine-grained [[concepts/volcanic-rock|volcanic rock]] that forms these columns.
*   Iceland: A prominent location for observing large-scale basalt column formations.
*   Giant's Causeway: A famous example of basalt columns in [[entities/united-kingdom|Northern Ireland]].
