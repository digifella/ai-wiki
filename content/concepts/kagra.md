# Kagra

Kagra is a gravitational wave detector located in Japan, operated by the National Astronomical Observatory of Japan in collaboration with international partners. It is one of the major ground-based laser interferometers designed to detect [[concepts/gravitational-waves|gravitational waves]] produced by colliding massive objects in space, complementing the [[concepts/ligo|LIGO]] and [[concepts/virgo|Virgo]] detectors in the [[entities/united-states|United States]] and [[entities/europe|Europe]].

The detector operates by measuring minute distortions in spacetime caused by passing gravitational waves. When two massive objects such as [[concepts/black-holes|black holes]] or [[concepts/neutron-stars|neutron stars]] merge, they emit gravitational waves that propagate outward. Kagra's sensitive interferometer can detect the resulting strain in the fabric of spacetime, allowing astronomers to confirm these cosmic collisions and study their properties.

## Scientific Applications

Kagra contributes to the growing [[concepts/catalog|catalog]] of gravitational wave events and has participated in observations that may indicate the presence of [[concepts/primordial-black-hole|primordial black holes]]—theoretical black holes formed in the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]] rather than from stellar collapse. The detection of [[concepts/stellar-mass|sub-solar mass objects]] through gravitational waves would provide evidence for this class of objects and offer insights into the early universe's conditions and [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] composition.
