# Digital Technology Literacy

**Digital technology literacy** refers to the ability to find, evaluate, utilize, share, and create content using information technologies and the Internet. In professional contexts, it extends beyond basic operational [[concepts/skills|skills]] to include [[concepts/scientific-skepticism|critical evaluation]] of digital tools and their integration into workflow.

## Healthcare Context

In clinical settings, digital literacy is a subset of [[concepts/clinical-informatics|health informatics]] competency. It involves the capacity to use [[concepts/electronic-health-records|electronic health records]] (EHRs), [[concepts/telehealth|telehealth]] platforms, and decision-support systems effectively.

### Barriers and Influencing Factors

Recent integrative reviews highlight that capability to use digital technology in nursing is influenced by multiple factors beyond technical [[concepts/skill|skill]]:

*   **Individual Factors:** Age, prior [[concepts/experience|experience]], and self-efficacy significantly impact [[concepts/adoption|adoption]].
*   **[[concepts/organizational-support|Organizational Support]]:** Availability of training, technical support, and time for [[concepts/learning|learning]] are critical.
*   **Technology Usability:** Poor [[concepts/user-interface|interface design]] and [[concepts/application-integration|system interoperability]] hinder effective use.
*   **Professional Identity:** Perceptions of technology's role in [[concepts/roles-in-community-settings|patient care]] versus [[concepts/administrative-overload|administrative burden]] affect engagement.

For a detailed analysis of these issues, see: [[lab-notes/2026-05-26-Journal-of-Clinical-Nursing---2020---Brown---Issues-affe|Journal of Clinical Nursing - 2020 - Brown - Issues affecting nurses  capability to use digital technology at work  An]]

## Key Competencies

1.  **Operational Skills:** [[concepts/excellence|Proficiency]] in hardware and software navigation.
2.  **Information Literacy:** Ability to search for, assess, and manage [[concepts/digital-information|digital information]].
3.  **[[concepts/critical-thinking|Critical Thinking]]:** Evaluating the credibility and relevance of digital sources.
4.  **Ethical and Legal [[concepts/conscious-thought|Awareness]]:** Understanding data [[concepts/privacy|privacy]], [[concepts/security|security]], and ethical implications of digital communication.
