# Earthquake Base Isolation

Earthquake [[concepts/base-isolation-systems|base isolation]] is a structural [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] technique designed to decouple buildings from ground motion during [[concepts/earthquakes|seismic events]]. Instead of rigidly anchoring a structure to its foundation, this method employs specialized bearing systems installed between the foundation and the superstructure. These isolation layers allow for controlled relative [[concepts/exercise|movement]], effectively reducing the transfer of seismic energy into the building and its contents.

The primary mechanism involves increasing the natural period of vibration of the structure. By lengthening this period, the building’s resonant frequency is shifted away from the dominant frequencies of typical earthquake ground motions. This detuning prevents the amplification of forces that would otherwise occur if the building were fixed directly to the shaking ground, thereby minimizing structural damage and preserving the [[concepts/honesty|integrity]] of non-structural components.

Common isolation systems include lead-rubber bearings, which combine the flexibility of rubber with the [[concepts/energy-dissipation|energy dissipation]] properties of lead cores, and sliding bearings that allow the structure to glide over the foundation. These systems are particularly effective in regions with high seismic activity, where they provide a reliable means of protecting critical [[concepts/infrastructure|infrastructure]], hospitals, and historical buildings from the destructive effects of earthquakes.
