# Richard Feynman

Richard [[entities/david-jp-phillips|Phillips]] [[concepts/feynman|Feynman]] (May 11, 1918 – February 15, 1988) was an American theoretical physicist, known for his work in the path integral formulation of [[concepts/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]], the [[concepts/theory|theory]] of quantum electrodynamics, and the [[concepts/physics|physics]] of the superfluidity of supercooled liquid helium. Feynman is recognized as one of the most influential physicists of the second half of the 20th century.

- [[concepts/quantum-electrodynamics|quantum electrodynamics]]
- [[concepts/path-integral|path integral]]
- [[concepts/superfluidity|superfluidity]]

### Summary of Works
- Co-developed quantum electrodynamics for which he won a Nobel Prize in [[concepts/physics|Physics]] (1965).
- Authored several influential books including "The [[concepts/feynman-lectures|Feynman Lectures]] on Physics" and "Surely You're Joking, Mr. [[concepts/feynman|Feynman]]!".
- Known for his educational approach to complex topics.

### Additional Notes
- Feynman was also known for his curiosity-driven research and playful spirit.
- He contributed significantly to the understanding of [[concepts/particle-physics|particle physics]] and the [[concepts/history-of-science|history of science]].
- [[concepts/mathematics|Mathematics]] as a tool in [[concepts/understanding-the-physical-world|understanding the physical world]].
- Feynman famously expressed frustration regarding the lack of understanding of the [[concepts/fine-structure-constant|fine structure constant]] (approx. 1/137), a [[concepts/dimensionless-number|dimensionless number]] fundamental to [[concepts/electromagnetic-interaction|electromagnetism]], describing its origin as one of the great mysteries of physics as discussed in [[lab-notes/2026-06-12-Fine-Structure-Constant-1137-Derivation-Significance-and|Fine Structure Constant (1/137): Derivation, Significance, and Quantum Enigma]].
