# Trust-Mediation Matrix

The **Trust-Mediation Matrix** is a conceptual framework for analyzing how interpersonal [[concepts/trust|trust]] is established, mediated, and measured within collaborative systems. It posits that trust is not a binary state but a multidimensional variable influenced by structural factors, [[concepts/vulnerability|psychological safety]], and behavioral [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]].

## Core Dimensions

- **Structural Mediation**: [[concepts/causes|Mechanisms]] (tools, protocols, hierarchies) that facilitate or hinder trust transfer between [[concepts/nodes|nodes]].
- **Psychological Safety**: The shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking, [[concepts/acting|acting]] as the primary substrate for trust.
- **[[concepts/software-reliability|Reliability]] [[concepts/verification|Verification]]**: The [[concepts/iterative-refinement|iterative process]] of confirming competence and dependability through repeated interactions.

## Operational Implications

- Teams must audit their **trust mediation channels** (communication tools, meeting structures) to ensure they support rather than obstruct psychological safety.
- Metrics for team [[concepts/health|health]] should prioritize behavioral [[concepts/indicators|indicators]] of trust (e.g., equitable speaking time, error admission rates) over output velocity alone.
- [[concepts/leadership|Leadership]] interventions should focus on mediating conflicts quickly to prevent erosion of the trust substrate, aligning with the matrix's dynamic equilibrium model.
