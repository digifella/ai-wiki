# Human Lunar Flyby

A human [[concepts/lunar-flyby|lunar flyby]] is a [[concepts/human-spaceflight|crewed spaceflight]] mission that travels to the Moon and returns to [[entities/earth|Earth]] without landing on the lunar surface. The spacecraft follows a trajectory around the Moon, allowing astronauts to observe the lunar environment and conduct scientific observations before returning home. This mission profile serves as an intermediate step in [[concepts/lunar-exploration|lunar exploration]], enabling human spaceflight beyond Earth orbit while requiring less complex infrastructure and lower risk than a [[concepts/lunar-landing|lunar landing]].

## Mission Architecture

Lunar flyby missions typically use high-energy trajectories that take spacecraft beyond Earth orbit into cislunar space. The spacecraft approaches the Moon, uses its [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]] to alter course, and returns to Earth. This trajectory profile reduces the delta-v requirements compared to lunar orbit insertion or landing, making flyby missions more accessible for testing life support systems, navigation capabilities, and [[concepts/crew-operations|crew operations]] in the cislunar environment.

## Artemis II

[[concepts/nasa|NASA]]'s [[entities/artemis-ii|Artemis II]] represents the primary near-term example of a crewed lunar flyby. Scheduled to carry four astronauts aboard the [[concepts/orion-systems|Orion spacecraft]], Artemis II [[entities/will|will]] demonstrate the capabilities of [[entities/nasa|NASA]]'s [[concepts/deep-space-exploration|deep space exploration]] systems before subsequent Artemis missions attempt lunar landings. The mission validates the hardware and procedures necessary for sustained human exploration of the Moon and beyond.

Human lunar flybys bridge crewed spaceflight capabilities between [[concepts/low-earth-orbit-leo|low Earth orbit]] operations and lunar surface missions, providing essential validation of systems and crew procedures for extended [[concepts/space-exploration|deep space exploration]].
