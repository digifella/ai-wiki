# Targeted Color Adjustments

Targeted color adjustments allow photographers and editors to modify specific colors within an image while leaving others unchanged. In Lightroom Classic, this functionality is primarily accessed through the [[concepts/color-mixer|Color Mixer]] panel, which provides independent control over hue, saturation, and lightness (HSL) for individual [[concepts/color-ranges|color ranges]]. Rather than adjusting all reds, greens, or blues globally, editors can isolate and modify narrower color bands, enabling precise control over the final image appearance.

## Color Mixer Controls

The Color Mixer in Lightroom Classic divides the color spectrum into eight primary color ranges: red, orange, yellow, green, aqua, blue, purple, and magenta. For each range, three sliders control distinct properties: the hue slider shifts the color itself along the spectrum, the saturation slider adjusts [[concepts/color-saturation|color intensity]] from neutral to vivid, and the lightness slider brightens or darkens that color range. These [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] remain non-destructive, allowing editors to revisit and modify them at any point in the [[concepts/photography-workflow|editing workflow]].

## Practical Applications

Targeted color adjustments are particularly useful for correcting [[concepts/color-casts|color casts]] in specific regions, enhancing product colors, or achieving creative [[concepts/photo-tonal-adjustments|color grading]] effects. For example, an editor might desaturate blue skies while maintaining vibrant greens in foliage, or shift orange tones in [[concepts/skin-tones|skin tones]] without affecting background colors. This selective approach preserves the [[concepts/integrity|integrity]] of other colors in the composition while achieving the desired aesthetic outcome.
