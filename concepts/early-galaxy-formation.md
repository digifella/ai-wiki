# Early Galaxy Formation

Early [[concepts/galaxy-formation|galaxy formation]] describes the physical processes responsible for the assembly of galaxies during the universe's first billion years. This epoch is fundamental to understanding the transition from the homogeneous [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]] to the structured cosmos observed today. [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|Theoretical frameworks]], built upon decades of research into [[concepts/gravitational-collapse|gravitational collapse]], [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]] [[concepts/halos|halos]], and baryonic [[concepts/physics|physics]], historically predicted a hierarchical growth model. In this standard view, small structures formed first and gradually merged or accreted gas to build larger systems, implying that the most [[concepts/massive-galaxies|massive galaxies]] should appear relatively late in cosmic history.

Recent observations from the [[concepts/james-webb-space-telescope|James Webb Space Telescope]] (JWST) have identified a population of galaxies at high redshifts that appear unexpectedly mature. These distant objects exhibit high masses and complex structures at times when the universe was significantly younger than previously thought necessary for such development. The presence of these massive, evolved galaxies challenges the timeline of the standard cosmological model, suggesting that star formation and structural assembly proceeded more rapidly in the early universe than current simulations predict.

This discrepancy between theoretical predictions and [[concepts/empirical-evidence|observational data]] has prompted significant re-evaluation of early universe physics. Researchers are investigating potential explanations, including variations in initial conditions, enhanced star formation efficiencies, or modifications to dark [[concepts/chemical-principles|matter properties]]. Resolving these tensions is critical for refining models of cosmic evolution and ensuring that the [[concepts/standard-model-of-particle-physics|standard model]] of [[concepts/cosmology|cosmology]] accurately accounts for the rapid [[concepts/emergent-behavior|emergence]] of large-scale structures in the infant universe.
