# Program Evaluation And Review Technique Pert

Program Evaluation and Review Technique (PERT) is a statistical project management methodology developed in the 1950s by the U.S. Navy for managing the Polaris submarine missile program. It was designed to address the challenges of large-scale, complex projects where task durations and outcomes cannot be predicted with certainty. Unlike traditional project management approaches that rely on single-point time estimates, PERT uses probabilistic estimates to account for inherent uncertainty in novel or research-intensive work.

## Core Methodology

PERT requires project managers to provide three time estimates for each task: the optimistic time (best case), the pessimistic time (worst case), and the most likely time (most probable duration). From these three estimates, PERT calculates an expected duration using a weighted average formula that assigns greater weight to the most likely estimate. This approach produces a probability distribution for project completion rather than a fixed deadline, enabling more realistic scheduling for uncertain work.

## Application in Complex Projects

PERT is particularly suited to research and development projects, software development, and other endeavors with high uncertainty. By explicitly modeling uncertainty, PERT helps project managers identify risks early and allocate resources more effectively. The methodology has influenced modern project management practices and remains relevant in contexts where traditional deterministic scheduling methods prove inadequate, though it is less commonly used in projects with well-understood, repetitive tasks.
