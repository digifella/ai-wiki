# Light

Light is electromagnetic radiation visible to the human eye, occupying wavelengths between approximately 380 and 700 nanometers in the electromagnetic spectrum. It represents a fundamental phenomenon in [[concepts/physics|physics]] and plays a central role in understanding the universe, from the behavior of atoms to the structure of galaxies. Light travels at a constant [[concepts/speed|speed]] of approximately 299,792 kilometers per second in a vacuum, a value denoted as *c* in physics equations.

## Wave-Particle Duality

Light exhibits wave-particle duality, behaving simultaneously as both a wave and a stream of particles called photons. As a wave, it demonstrates properties such as interference, diffraction, and polarization, which are described by Maxwell's equations of electromagnetism. As a particle, light interacts with matter in discrete quanta of energy, a concept central to quantum mechanics and the photoelectric effect. This dual nature is essential for explaining phenomena that classical wave theory alone cannot account for, such as the emission and absorption spectra of atoms.

## Interaction with Matter

When light encounters matter, it can be absorbed, reflected, refracted, or transmitted depending on the material's properties and the light's wavelength. Refraction occurs when light passes from one medium to another, changing speed and direction, a principle utilized in lenses and optical fibers. Absorption involves the transfer of energy from photons to atoms or molecules, often resulting in thermal energy or electronic excitation. These interactions form the basis of optics, spectroscopy, and various technologies ranging from microscopes to solar cells.
