# History Of Science

The history of [[concepts/science|science]] encompasses the development of scientific knowledge, methodologies, and institutional practices across cultures and centuries. Science as a systematic approach to understanding the natural world emerged gradually, building on observations and [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] practices from ancient civilizations. Early contributions came from diverse traditions—including [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] and [[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]] in ancient Mesopotamia and Egypt, [[concepts/physics|natural philosophy]] in Greece, and advances in mathematics and medicine in medieval Islamic and Indian scholarship. The formalization of what we recognize as modern science accelerated during the Renaissance and Scientific [[concepts/revolution|Revolution]] in [[entities/europe|Europe]], roughly from the 16th to 18th centuries, when figures like Galileo, Newton, and others developed the experimental method and mathematical frameworks that became central to scientific practice.

## Evolution of Scientific Disciplines

Scientific knowledge has progressively differentiated into specialized disciplines, each with its own methods and body of knowledge. Physics emerged as a foundational science describing the laws governing matter and energy. Chemistry developed as scientists systematized the study of substances and their reactions. Biology evolved from natural history into a rigorous discipline, particularly after [[concepts/evolutionary-biology|evolutionary theory]] provided an organizing framework. The 20th and 21st centuries have witnessed further [[concepts/specialization|specialization]] and the emergence of interdisciplinary fields, as well as new investigative techniques such as spectroscopy, [[concepts/particle-physics-experiments|particle physics experiments]], and molecular biology that have fundamentally expanded humanity's ability to probe natural phenomena.

## Scientific Method and Progress

Central to the modern history of science is the refinement and application of the [[concepts/scientific-method|scientific method]]—systematic observation, [[concepts/hypothesis-formation|hypothesis formation]], experimentation, and peer review. This iterative approach to building reliable knowledge has enabled successive generations of scientists to test, refine, and sometimes overturn earlier understandings. Major discoveries have often required technological advances, from the telescope and microscope to contemporary tools like particle accelerators and genetic sequencing technology. The history of science is thus inseparable from the history of scientific instruments and the technological capacity to observe natural phenomena [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] ranging from the subatomic to the cosmic.
