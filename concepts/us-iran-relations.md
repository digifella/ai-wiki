# US-Iran Relations

**US-[[entities/iran|Iran]] Relations** refers to the complex, often adversarial diplomatic and strategic interactions between the [[entities/united-states]] and the [[concepts/iran|Islamic Republic of Iran]]. The relationship is characterized by deep mutual distrust, stemming from the 1953 coup, the 1979 Iranian [[concepts/revolution|Revolution]], and the subsequent Iran Hostage [[concepts/crisis|Crisis]]. Key [[concepts/friction|friction]] points include Iran's nuclear program, regional proxy conflicts, and human rights concerns.

## Historical Context
- **1953 Coup**: US-backed overthrow of Mohammad Mosaddegh established long-standing Iranian resentment toward American interference.
- **1979 Revolution**: Establishment of the Islamic Republic led to the severing of diplomatic ties and the hostage crisis.
- **JCPOA (2015)**: The Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action temporarily eased tensions by limiting Iran's nuclear activities in exchange for sanctions relief. The US withdrawal in 2018 under the Trump administration escalated tensions.

## Recent Developments (2026)
Following a period of intense [[concepts/conflict|conflict]] in the Middle East, diplomatic efforts have shifted toward interim agreements to de-escalate immediate threats.

- **[[concepts/memorandum-of-understanding|Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)]]**: A recently signed MOU aims to stabilize relations, though it is viewed by analysts as a "Memorandum of Misunderstanding" due to inherent ambiguities.

- **Ambiguities**: Critics note the lack of enforcement [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] and vague timelines for permanent [[concepts/solution|resolution]], leaving room for future disputes.

## Key Issues
- **Nuclear Program**: Iran's [[entities/pursuitunimelbeduau|pursuit]] of nuclear technology remains the primary [[concepts/security|security]] concern for the US and its allies.
- **Regional Influence**: Iran's support for proxy groups (e.g., Hezbollah, Houthis) conflicts with US interests in [[entities/israel]] and [[entities/saudi-arabia]].
- **Sanctions**: Economic sanctions remain a primary tool of US pressure, impacting Iran's [[concepts/economic-system|economy]] and domestic stability.
