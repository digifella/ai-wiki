# Astrophysics

Astrophysics is the branch of [[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]] that applies the principles of [[concepts/physics|physics]] to understand celestial objects and cosmic phenomena. It combines [[concepts/empirical-evidence|observational data]] from telescopes and satellites with theoretical models grounded in mechanics, thermodynamics, nuclear physics, and [[concepts/electromagnetic-interaction|electromagnetism]]. By analyzing the light, radiation, and particles emitted by [[concepts/stellar-objects|stars]], galaxies, and other structures, astrophysicists determine their physical properties, including temperature, composition, mass, and motion.

## Key Areas of Study

Astrophysics encompasses the formation and evolution of stars across their lifecycles, from their birth in molecular clouds to their death as white dwarfs, neutron stars, or black holes. It also investigates the structure and dynamics of galaxies, the behavior of matter in extreme environments, and the large-scale structure of the universe itself. Additionally, astrophysics addresses phenomena such as supernovae, pulsars, quasars, and gravitational waves, which reveal fundamental physical processes operating at cosmic scales.

## Methods and Tools

Modern astrophysics relies on observations across the electromagnetic spectrum, from radio waves to gamma rays, as well as particle detectors and gravitational wave observatories. Computational modeling plays an increasingly important role, allowing researchers to simulate stellar evolution, galaxy collisions, and the formation of cosmic structures. These combined observational and theoretical approaches enable astrophysicists to test physical laws and construct models of the universe's history and composition.
