# Test Diagnosis

Test diagnosis refers to the systematic evaluation of diagnostic tests and assessment tools used in [[entities/clinical-practice|clinical practice]]. It involves determining whether a test can accurately identify the presence or absence of a disease or condition, and assessing its practical utility in [[concepts/health|healthcare]] settings. The primary purpose is to ensure that diagnostic tools provide reliable, actionable results that support [[concepts/clinical-reasoning|clinical decision-making]].

The performance of diagnostic tests is typically measured through several key metrics. Sensitivity indicates how well a test identifies true positives, while specificity measures the ability to correctly identify true negatives. Other important parameters include positive and negative predictive values, which depend on the prevalence of the condition in the tested population, as well as likelihood ratios that help interpret test results in the context of pre-test [[concepts/probability|probability]].

Validity and [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] are fundamental to test diagnosis. Validity assesses whether the test measures what it intends to measure, often categorized as content, criterion, or construct validity. Reliability evaluates the [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] of the test results over time, across different raters, or under varying conditions. A test must demonstrate both high validity and reliability to be considered clinically useful.

The application of test diagnosis extends beyond statistical accuracy to include considerations of feasibility, cost-effectiveness, and patient impact. Factors such as invasiveness, [[concepts/accessibility|accessibility]], and the potential for false positives or negatives influence the [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] of a diagnostic tool in real-[[entities/earth|world]] settings. [[concepts/continuous-monitoring|Continuous monitoring]] and re-evaluation are necessary to ensure that diagnostic practices remain aligned with evolving medical standards and [[concepts/patient-needs|patient needs]].
