# Modified Gravity

**Modified [[concepts/gravitational-pull|Gravity]]** refers to a class of theories in [[concepts/astrophysics|astrophysics]] and [[concepts/cosmology|cosmology]] that attempt to explain observed [[concepts/gravitational-effects|gravitational phenomena]]—particularly galactic rotation [[concepts/curves|curves]] and large-scale structure formation—without invoking [[concepts/dark-matter]]. These theories propose that the laws of gravity deviate from General Relativity at specific [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] (e.g., low acceleration regimes).

## Core Concepts
- **[[concepts/modified-newtonian-dynamics-mond|MOND]] (Modified Newtonian Dynamics):** A prominent framework suggesting that Newton's second law or the law of gravity changes at very low accelerations, naturally producing flat rotation curves without dark matter [[concepts/halos|halos]].
- **Alternative Theories:** Includes TeVeS (Tensor-Vector-Scalar gravity) and other relativistic extensions of MOND.
- **[[concepts/tensile-stress|Tension]] with Observations:** While modified gravity explains individual galaxy dynamics well, it struggles with the [[concepts/cosmic-microwave-background]] (CMB) power spectrum and Cluster Dynamics (e.g., Bullet Cluster) where dark matter models succeed.

## Recent Developments
- **[[concepts/milky-way|Milky Way]] Rotation Constraints:** New data regarding the Milky Way's rotation curve continues to challenge both standard dark matter halo models and modified gravity predictions.
- **2026 Analysis:** Recent discussions highlight that new [[concepts/empirical-evidence|observational data]] defies easy explanation by either paradigm, suggesting potential gaps in our understanding of baryonic [[concepts/physics|physics]] or gravitational laws.
