# Techno Economics

Techno-economics is the discipline of evaluating whether emerging or novel technologies can be economically viable at scale. Rather than assessing technical feasibility alone, [[concepts/techno-economic-modeling|techno-economic analysis]] combines engineering assessments with [[concepts/financial-modeling|financial modeling]] to determine if a technology can achieve [[concepts/commercial-viability|commercial viability]] within realistic market conditions. This approach is particularly relevant for capital-intensive technologies with long development cycles, such as space-based infrastructure, renewable energy systems, and advanced computing platforms.

## Application to Emerging Technologies

Techno-economic assessment examines multiple factors including capital expenditure requirements, operational costs, market demand, competitive advantage, and timeline to profitability. For space-based [[concepts/software|applications]] such as [[concepts/orbital-computing|orbital data centers]] and AI [[concepts/computing-infrastructure|computing infrastructure]], this involves analyzing launch costs, [[concepts/hardware|hardware]] durability in space environments, power generation and cooling constraints, data transmission bandwidth and latency, and the value proposition relative to terrestrial alternatives. The discipline helps distinguish between technically possible innovations and those with realistic paths to economic sustainability.

## Practical Use in Strategy

Organizations use techno-economic analysis to make investment decisions, determine technology readiness for commercialization, and identify market timing. By combining technical specifications with cost projections and demand modeling, stakeholders can assess whether a technology warrants funding, when market conditions may favor [[concepts/adoption|adoption]], and what technical or cost breakthroughs are necessary for competitive positioning. This framework is essential for evaluating high-risk, high-capital ventures where technical success does not automatically translate to business success.
