# Line of Sight

**Line of sight** refers to the unobstructed path between an observer and an object, allowing light or other signals to travel directly from the source to the receiver. In cosmology, the implications of line of sight are critical for understanding the structure and history of the universe.

## Cosmological Implications

The concept of line of sight is central to resolving [[concepts/olbers-paradox]], which questions why the [[concepts/night-sky|night sky]] is dark if the universe is infinite and filled with stars.

- **The Paradox**: If the universe were infinitely old, infinitely large, and uniformly filled with stars, every line of sight from Earth should eventually terminate on the surface of a star, making the night sky as bright as the surface of the Sun.
- **Resolution**: The night sky is dark because the universe has a finite age and is expanding. This limits the number of stars visible within our observable horizon and redshifts light from distant sources, preventing every line of sight from being blocked by a star.
- **Observable Universe**: We can only see objects whose light has had time to reach us since the Big Bang. This creates a cosmic horizon, effectively limiting the "depth" of our line of sight.
