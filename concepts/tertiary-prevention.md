# Tertiary Prevention

Tertiary prevention is the third tier of disease prevention, applied after a disease or condition has been diagnosed. Rather than preventing disease occurrence or detecting it early, tertiary prevention focuses on managing existing conditions to slow progression, prevent complications, and maintain or improve quality of life. This approach is essential for chronic diseases and conditions where cure may not be possible but outcomes can be significantly improved through proper management.

## Management and Complications Prevention

Tertiary prevention involves ongoing medical treatment, medication management, lifestyle modifications, and regular monitoring to control disease symptoms and prevent further deterioration. For example, a patient with diabetes receives tertiary prevention through blood sugar management, regular check-ups, and preventive care for complications like kidney disease or vision loss. Similarly, someone with heart disease receives tertiary prevention through medications, cardiac rehabilitation, and lifestyle changes to reduce the risk of heart attack or stroke.

## Rehabilitation and Support

An important component of tertiary prevention is rehabilitation and supportive care, which helps patients adapt to their condition and maintain functional abilities. This may include physical therapy for stroke patients, mental health support for those with psychiatric conditions, or occupational therapy to help patients maintain independence. Patient education and self-management programs also play a critical role, enabling individuals to better control their condition and recognize warning signs that require medical attention.
