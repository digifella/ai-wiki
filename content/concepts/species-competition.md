# Species Competition

Species competition in biological contexts refers to the struggle between different species for [[concepts/limited-resources|limited resources]] such as food, habitat, and energy. This fundamental ecological process drives natural selection and shapes the [[concepts/structure|structure]] of ecosystems over time. Competitive interactions can result in resource partitioning, where species evolve to occupy distinct ecological niches, or in competitive exclusion, where one species outcompetes another for essential resources.

## Digital Simulation Applications

[[entities/sakana-ai|Sakana AI]] has developed [[concepts/2d-grid-simulation|digital ecosystems]] that simulate competitive dynamics between [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] [[concepts/agents|agents]], treating them as distinct "species" operating within constrained environments. These simulations model how different AI systems with varying architectures, strategies, and resource requirements interact and coevolve. By studying these artificial competitive [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]], researchers can better understand principles of survival, adaptation, and [[concepts/ecological-balance|ecosystem stability]] that apply both to biological systems and to complex multi-[[entities/agent|agent]] AI environments.

The simulation framework allows researchers to observe emergent behaviors arising from competition—such as [[concepts/specialization|specialization]], cooperation, and extinction—without the constraints of real-world biological experimentation. This computational approach provides insights into how diverse AI systems might coexist in shared digital spaces and how competitive pressures shape the development of more robust and adaptive artificial intelligence.
