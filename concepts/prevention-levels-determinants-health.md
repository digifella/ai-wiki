# Prevention Levels Determinants Health

Prevention in public health is organized into three complementary levels that address disease at different stages of development. These levels work together to reduce disease burden across populations by intervening at points where outcomes can be meaningfully improved.

## Primary Prevention

Primary prevention stops disease before it occurs by addressing underlying risk factors and promoting healthy behaviors. This level targets the general population or at-risk groups before any disease develops. Common primary prevention strategies include vaccination programs, health education campaigns, water sanitation improvements, food safety standards, and workplace safety regulations. Primary prevention is often the most cost-effective approach because preventing disease entirely avoids the need for treatment.

## Secondary Prevention

Secondary prevention aims to detect and treat disease in its earliest stages, before symptoms become severe. This level assumes disease may already be present but has not yet caused significant harm. Screening programs for conditions like cancer, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease are typical secondary prevention activities. Early detection allows for more effective treatment and can prevent progression to advanced disease stages.

## Tertiary Prevention

Tertiary prevention focuses on managing existing disease to prevent complications and reduce disability. This level applies to people who already have a diagnosed condition and aims to improve quality of life and slow disease progression. Rehabilitation programs, medication management, and patient education for chronic disease control exemplify tertiary prevention efforts.

## Health Determinants

The effectiveness of prevention at any level depends on underlying health determinants—the social, economic, environmental, and biological factors that influence population health. These include income and education levels, access to healthcare, environmental quality, housing conditions, and social support networks. Addressing these broader determinants is essential for prevention strategies to succeed, as they shape both disease risk and the ability of individuals and communities to benefit from preventive interventions.
