# Heat Stress

Heat stress is a physiological condition that occurs when the body's core temperature rises to dangerous levels due to prolonged [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] to high environmental temperatures. This happens when the body's cooling mechanisms—primarily sweating and vasodilation—become insufficient to maintain normal temperature [[concepts/regulation|regulation]]. Heat stress can develop gradually or suddenly, depending on environmental conditions, [[concepts/exercise|physical activity]] level, and individual factors such as age, fitness, and acclimatization.

## Physiological Effects

When exposed to excessive heat, the body attempts to dissipate excess thermal energy by increasing blood flow to the skin and producing sweat. However, if these mechanisms cannot keep pace with heat gain, core body temperature rises uncontrollably. This can lead to a cascade of problems including [[concepts/dehydration|dehydration]] (as the body loses water through perspiration), electrolyte imbalances, and reduced physical and cognitive performance. Prolonged or severe heat stress can progress to heat exhaustion or [[concepts/heat-stroke|heat stroke]], both of which are medical emergencies.

## Risk Factors and Prevention

Certain populations face elevated risk, including outdoor workers, athletes, older adults, and those taking certain medications. Environmental factors such as high humidity reduce the effectiveness of evaporative cooling. Prevention focuses on maintaining adequate hydration, allowing rest periods in cooler environments, and gradually acclimating to hot conditions. Monitoring for early warning signs—such as excessive fatigue, dizziness, or cessation of sweating—is important for identifying and managing heat stress before it becomes severe.
