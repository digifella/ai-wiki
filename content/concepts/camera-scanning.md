# Camera Scanning

Camera scanning is a method of digitizing film photographs by photographing them with a digital camera rather than using a traditional [[concepts/flatbed-scanner|flatbed scanner]]. This approach offers a practical alternative for home-based film digitization, allowing photographers to create high-quality digital files without investing in expensive scanning equipment.

## Basic Process

The technique involves positioning film negatives or [[entities/google-slides|slides]] in front of a light source and photographing them with a digital camera mounted on a stand or tripod. The camera captures the film image, which can then be inverted and edited in post-processing [[concepts/software|software]] to create a final digital file. This method works with various film formats and can achieve quality comparable to dedicated film scanners when done carefully.

## Equipment and Setup

Essential components include a digital camera, [[concepts/macro-lens|macro lens]] or extension tubes for close focusing, a light source (such as a lightbox or smartphone screen), and a stable mounting system to keep the film and camera properly aligned. The specific equipment needs vary depending on the film format being scanned and the desired output quality.
