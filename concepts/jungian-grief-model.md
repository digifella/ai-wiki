# Jungian Grief Model

The Jungian Grief Model is a [[concepts/mental-health|mental health]] intervention framework developed at the University of Copenhagen that integrates principles from Jungian [[concepts/psychology|psychology]] with contemporary grief [[concepts/offering-counseling-services|counseling]] practices. The model draws on [[entities/carl-jung|Carl Jung]]'s theoretical concepts regarding the [[concepts/unconscious-mind|unconscious mind]] and psychological integration to help individuals process grief and loss within a structured therapeutic context. Rather than viewing grief as a linear process to be "resolved," the framework emphasizes psychological integration and the meaning-making aspects of loss.

## Core Framework

The model is structured around distinct modules designed to address the complexities of mourning through a Jungian lens. Central to this approach are modules focused on [[concepts/conflict|conflict]] and [[concepts/solution|resolution]], which guide participants in navigating the internal tensions that arise during bereavement. These components encourage individuals to engage with their unconscious material, facilitating a deeper understanding of how loss impacts their psychological landscape.

By prioritizing integration over simple resolution, the framework offers a non-linear path for healing. It allows individuals to explore the symbolic and archetypal dimensions of their grief, fostering a sense of [[concepts/continuity|continuity]] between their past experiences and future identity. This structured yet flexible approach aims to support long-term psychological adjustment and personal growth following significant loss.
