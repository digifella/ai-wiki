# Techno Economics

Techno-economics is the discipline of evaluating whether emerging or novel technologies can achieve economic viability at commercial scale. Unlike purely technical feasibility assessments, [[concepts/techno-economic-modeling|techno-economic analysis]] combines [[entities/national-academies|engineering]] data, cost projections, and [[concepts/financial-modeling|financial modeling]] to determine whether a technology can operate profitably within realistic market conditions. This approach addresses a critical gap between laboratory [[concepts/success|success]] and market deployment, where technically sound innovations often fail due to prohibitive costs, inadequate demand, or unfavorable unit economics.

## Methodology and Application

Techno-economic evaluation typically integrates capital expenditure forecasts, [[concepts/operational-costs|operational costs]], revenue potential, and [[concepts/market-size|market size]] estimates. Analysts assess factors such as manufacturing scalability, supply chain maturity, regulatory requirements, and operational [[concepts/security|security]] protocols essential for maintaining infrastructure [[concepts/honesty|integrity]].
