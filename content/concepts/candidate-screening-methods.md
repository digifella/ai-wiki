# Candidate Screening Methods

Candidate screening methods are systematic approaches used by organizations to evaluate and filter job applicants during recruitment. These techniques serve to reduce large applicant pools to a manageable number of qualified candidates for further assessment, improving efficiency and hiring quality. Screening occurs early in the recruitment funnel and typically relies on standardized criteria aligned with job requirements and organizational needs.

## Common Approaches

Resume and application review remains the most widespread screening method, where recruiters assess qualifications, experience, and fit against job specifications. Many organizations use applicant tracking systems (ATS) to automate initial filtering based on [[concepts/keywords|keywords]], credentials, and other predetermined factors. Phone screening or brief telephone interviews allow recruiters to verify candidate information and assess basic communication [[concepts/skills|skills]] before investing time in in-person interviews. Some employers also use standardized assessments—including technical tests, skills evaluations, and personality inventories—to objectively measure candidate capabilities relevant to the role.

## Effectiveness Considerations

The effectiveness of screening methods varies depending on the role, industry, and organizational context. While resume review is cost-effective at scale, it may miss qualified candidates or introduce unconscious bias. Automated screening through ATS systems increases efficiency but risks filtering out viable candidates due to system limitations or overly rigid criteria. Combining multiple screening methods generally provides more reliable evaluation than relying on a single approach, though this increases resource requirements. Organizations increasingly balance efficiency with fairness, recognizing that screening practices can impact diversity and candidate experience throughout the recruitment process.
