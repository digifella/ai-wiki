# Epidemiology

Epidemiology is the scientific study of how diseases, injuries, and [[concepts/health|health]] conditions are distributed across populations and what factors influence their occurrence. Rather than focusing on individual patients, epidemiologists examine patterns of illness and health outcomes at the population level, investigating the causes, transmission mechanisms, and [[concepts/preventive-care|prevention]] strategies for health problems. This population-based approach distinguishes epidemiology from clinical medicine and forms the foundation of [[concepts/population-health|public health]] practice.

## Core Methods and Applications

Epidemiologists use a range of research designs to investigate health problems, including observational studies that track disease occurrence over time, case-control studies that compare people with and without specific conditions, and randomized controlled trials that test interventions. By analyzing data on disease frequency, [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] across different groups, and associations with potential risk factors, epidemiologists identify preventable causes of illness and inform policy decisions about health interventions, disease surveillance, and resource allocation.

## Historical and Contemporary Role

Epidemiology emerged as a formal discipline in the 19th century with investigations into infectious disease transmission, most notably John Snow's analysis of a cholera outbreak in London. While communicable diseases remain an important focus, modern epidemiology addresses a broad spectrum of health concerns including chronic diseases, injuries, environmental exposures, and behavioral factors. Epidemiological methods continue to support outbreak investigation, disease surveillance systems, vaccine development, and evaluation of public health programs worldwide.
