# Product Launch Hesitation

**Product launch hesitation** refers to the strategic or cultural tendency within technology organizations to delay the release of new products or features due to perfectionism, risk aversion, or [[concepts/viscosity|internal friction]]. This concept is often analyzed through the lens of [[concepts/organizational-culture|organizational culture]], particularly when contrasting companies known for rapid iteration against those with more cautious, research-heavy approaches.

## Key Drivers
*   **Perfectionism vs. [[concepts/velocity|Velocity]]:** The tension between achieving technical excellence and meeting market windows.
*   **Risk Aversion:** Fear of reputational damage or technical failure leading to extended testing phases.
*   **Cultural Inertia:** Legacy processes that slow down decision-making and deployment.

## Case Study: OpenAI vs. DeepMind
Recent analysis highlights a stark [[concepts/contrast|contrast]] in shipping cultures between [[entities/openai|OpenAI]] and [[entities/google-deepmind|DeepMind]], particularly regarding their approaches to [[concepts/large-language-models|large language models]] and chat interfaces.

*   **OpenAI's Shipping Culture:** Characterized by rapid iteration and a "move fast" mentality, prioritizing user feedback and market presence over absolute perfection.
*   **DeepMind's Hesitation:** Historically associated with a more cautious, research-first approach, which can lead to delays in bringing innovations to consumer-facing products.
*   **[[entities/lmchat|LMchat]] Context:** Specific discussions on the development of LMchat illustrate these divergent paths, with OpenAI leveraging its shipping culture to maintain competitive advantage while DeepMind's hesitation is cited as a factor in slower adoption cycles.
