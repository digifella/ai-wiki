# Sharpness

Sharpness in photography refers to the clarity and definition of details within an image. It is determined by several factors including focus accuracy, aperture settings, shutter speed, and lens quality. A sharp image preserves fine details and produces crisp edges, while an unsharp image appears soft or blurred. Achieving sharpness requires careful attention to both camera settings and technique during capture.

## Challenges in Landscape Photography

Landscape photographers face particular difficulties in maintaining sharpness across an entire scene, since foreground and background elements are often at vastly different distances from the camera. Standard focusing techniques may render one plane sharp while leaving others out of focus, limiting the depth of field that can be captured in a single exposure.

## Focus Stacking

Focus stacking is a technique used in landscape photography to enhance image sharpness. This method involves taking multiple photographs of the same scene, each focused on a different distance plane. These images are then combined in post-processing software to create a final composite image where all elements, from the immediate foreground to the distant background, are in sharp focus. This approach effectively overcomes the physical limitations of lens depth of field.
