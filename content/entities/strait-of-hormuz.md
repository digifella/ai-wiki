# Strait Of Hormuz

The Strait of Hormuz is a critical waterway located between Iran and Oman that connects the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman and the Arabian Sea. It is the world's most important oil chokepoint, through which approximately one-third of all globally traded seaborne crude oil passes. The strait is roughly 54 nautical miles wide at its narrowest point, with shipping lanes in both directions separated by only a few kilometers, making it strategically vital to global energy security.

## Geopolitical Significance

The strait's importance stems from the massive oil reserves in the Persian Gulf region, particularly in [[entities/saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], Iraq, [[entities/kuwait|Kuwait]], and the United Arab Emirates. Control or disruption of this waterway has significant leverage in regional [[concepts/geopolitics|geopolitics]], especially given Iran's territorial claims and military presence along the strait's northern coast. Any sustained closure would have severe consequences for global oil supplies and international markets.

## Economic Impact of Closure

A closure of the Strait of Hormuz would create immediate and substantial disruptions to global oil markets. The loss of crude oil exports through the passage would trigger rapid price increases, potentially affecting fuel costs, inflation, and economic growth worldwide. Historical events, such as the 1973 oil embargo and recent regional tensions, have demonstrated how Persian Gulf disruptions can create market volatility. Alternative export routes exist but lack the capacity to fully compensate for a blocked strait in the short term.
