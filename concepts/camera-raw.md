**Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)** is the ratio of the power of a desired signal to the power of background noise. High SNR indicates clear information transmission or capture; low SNR indicates interference or degradation.

## Photography and Imaging

In digital imaging, SNR relates to the quality of data captured by camera sensors.

- **RAW Data**: [[entities/camera-raw|Camera Raw]] files preserve unprocessed sensor data, maximizing available tonal range and SNR before compression artifacts degrade the signal.
- **Noise Management**: Low-[[concepts/light|light]] conditions reduce photon count (signal), lowering SNR and increasing [[concepts/digital-image-noise|digital noise]]. Post-processing involves recovering signal while suppressing noise, leveraging the high bit-depth of raw formats.

## Navigation and Signal Integrity

SNR is critical for navigation systems, particularly when dealing with weak signals or intentional interference.

- **GPS and Jamming**: GPS signals are extremely weak by the time they reach [[entities/earth|Earth]], making them susceptible to [[concepts/jamming|jamming]] and [[concepts/electromagnetic-interference|electromagnetic interference]]. Maintaining high SNR is essential for accurate positioning.
- **[[concepts/historical-context|Historical Context]]**: Early aviation relied on [[lab-notes/2026-08-03-Low-Frequency-Radio-Range-Pioneering-Auditory-Aircraft-N|Low Frequency Radio Range: Pioneering Auditory Aircraft Navigation System]], where pilots interpreted auditory cues from low-frequency radio signals to navigate. This system required pilots to distinguish the desired navigation signal from atmospheric noise, a primitive form of managing SNR in a cockpit environment.
