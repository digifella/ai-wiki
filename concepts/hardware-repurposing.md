# Hardware Repurposing

Hardware repurposing refers to the practice of reusing existing computer equipment or electronics for new applications rather than purchasing new devices. This approach extends the useful life of hardware that might otherwise become obsolete or discarded. Common examples include converting older desktop computers into network servers, using retired laptops for [[entities/storage|storage]] systems, or reprogramming embedded devices for alternative functions.

## Economic and Environmental Benefits

Repurposing hardware reduces personal technology costs by eliminating the need for new purchases. A functional computer, even if outdated for its original purpose, often contains sufficient [[concepts/compute-capacity|processing power]] and storage capacity for secondary applications. This practice also addresses electronic waste by keeping equipment out of landfills, reducing the environmental impact associated with manufacturing new devices and extracting raw materials.

## Practical Applications

One practical example is building a [[concepts/home-server|media server]] from older components using [[concepts/open-source|open-source]] operating systems like [[entities/ubuntu|Ubuntu]]. Such a setup can provide [[concepts/local-storage|local storage]] and [[concepts/streaming-services|streaming services]] for significantly less than commercial subscription alternatives, while using hardware that would otherwise be discarded. Other common applications include converting old devices into backup storage systems, home automation controllers, or file servers for local networks.

## Limitations and Considerations

While repurposing extends hardware life, older equipment may consume more electricity than modern alternatives, affecting long-term operating costs. Additionally, some legacy hardware lacks [[concepts/security|security]] [[concepts/software-updates|updates]] or modern connectivity standards, which can present practical limitations for certain applications.
