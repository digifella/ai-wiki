# Cosmology

Cosmology is the scientific discipline dedicated to understanding the origin, large-scale structure, and evolution of the universe. It integrates principles from [[concepts/theoretical-physics|theoretical physics]] and [[concepts/astronomy|astronomy]] to construct models that describe the cosmos on its grandest [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]]. The field addresses fundamental inquiries regarding the universe's [[concepts/writing|composition]], its historical development over billions of years, and its potential ultimate fate.

Modern cosmology is grounded in rigorous [[concepts/empirical-evidence|observational data]] collected through telescopes and various detection instruments. Key evidence includes the [[concepts/cosmic-background-radiation|cosmic microwave background]] radiation, the distribution of galaxies, and the redshift of distant [[concepts/supernovae|supernovae]]. These observations allow scientists to test [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|theoretical frameworks]], such as the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]] [[concepts/theory|theory]], and refine our understanding of the universe's expansion rate and geometry.

Theoretical cosmology employs general relativity and [[concepts/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]] to explain the [[concepts/fundamental-laws-of-physics|physical laws]] governing the universe. It investigates the nature of [[concepts/the-composition-of-the-universe-extends-beyond-what-is-directly-observable|dark matter and dark energy]], which constitute the majority of the universe's mass-[[concepts/calorie-count|energy content]]. By combining mathematical models with empirical data, cosmologists aim to provide a coherent [[concepts/storytelling|narrative]] of the universe's history from its initial moments to its current state and future trajectory.
