# Sharpness

Sharpness in photography refers to the clarity and definition of details within an image. It is determined by several factors including focus accuracy, aperture settings, shutter speed, and lens quality. A sharp image preserves fine details and produces crisp edges, while an unsharp image appears soft or blurred. Achieving sharpness requires careful attention to both camera settings and technique during capture.

## Challenges in Landscape Photography

Landscape photographers face particular difficulties in maintaining sharpness across an entire scene, since foreground and background elements often exist at vastly different distances from the camera. The depth of field, which is the range of distance that appears acceptably sharp, is limited by the aperture and focal length. When shooting wide-angle landscapes, photographers often stop down the aperture to increase depth of field, but diffraction can eventually reduce overall sharpness if the aperture is too small.

## Focus Stacking

Focus stacking is a technique used in landscape photography to enhance image sharpness. It involves taking multiple photographs of the same scene, each focused on a different plane of distance. These images are then combined in post-processing software to create a single composite image with extended depth of field. This method allows for maximum sharpness from the immediate foreground to the distant horizon, overcoming the optical limitations of a single exposure.
