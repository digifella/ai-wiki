# Oven Method

The oven method is a cooking technique for preparing bacon that uses controlled, uniform heat to achieve consistently crispy results across entire batches. Unlike stovetop pan-frying, which produces uneven browning due to hot spots and requires manual turning, the oven method distributes heat evenly throughout the cooking chamber. This uniform heat application cooks all bacon strips at the same rate, resulting in consistent texture across the batch and eliminating the need for individual attention during cooking.

## Procedure

To use the oven method, bacon strips are arranged in a single layer on a baking sheet, typically lined with foil or parchment paper for easier cleanup. The sheet is then placed in a preheated oven, most commonly at 400°F (200°C), though temperatures between 375°F and 425°F are also used depending on desired crispness and cooking time. Bacon cooks for approximately 15–20 minutes, after which strips are removed and drained on paper towels. The precise cooking time varies based on bacon thickness and individual oven characteristics.

## Advantages

The oven method offers several practical advantages over stovetop cooking. It allows preparation of larger quantities simultaneously, making it efficient for feeding groups or meal preparation. The method produces minimal splashing and smoke compared to pan-frying, improving kitchen safety and comfort. Additionally, the even heat distribution eliminates the need to monitor or flip strips during cooking, freeing the cook to attend to other meal components.
