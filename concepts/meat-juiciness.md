# Meat Juiciness

Meat juiciness refers to the perceived moisture and tenderness of cooked meat, determined by how effectively the meat retains its natural liquids during the cooking process. This quality is a critical component of palatability, as juicy meat is generally perceived as more flavorful and tender than dry meat. The degree of juiciness depends on several factors, including the meat's initial moisture content, the specific cooking temperature and duration, and the methods used in preparation prior to cooking.

## Chemical Modification with Baking Soda

The application of baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) to ground meat alters its pH level, raising it to create an alkaline environment. This chemical modification prevents the proteins in the meat from bonding too tightly during heating, a process known as protein cross-linking. By inhibiting this tight binding, the meat structure remains more open and able to hold onto water, resulting in a softer texture and increased retention of internal juices.

This technique is particularly effective in enhancing browning and texture in dishes where rapid, high-heat cooking is used, such as stir-fries. The alkaline environment also promotes the Maillard reaction, allowing the meat to develop a desirable crust more quickly without overcooking the interior. Consequently, the meat remains moist and tender even after extended cooking times, improving the overall mouthfeel and sensory experience of the dish.
