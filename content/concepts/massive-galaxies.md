# Massive Galaxies

The [[concepts/jwst|James Webb Space Telescope]] ([[entities/jwst|JWST]]) has observed galaxies in the [[concepts/early-universe|early universe]] that challenge fundamental assumptions about how galaxies form and evolve. These observations reveal massive galaxies existing [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] redshifts corresponding to just a few hundred million years after the [[concepts/big-bang|Big Bang]]—far earlier than standard [[concepts/models|models]] predict such large structures could assemble. The sheer mass and apparent maturity of these galaxies suggests either that [[concepts/galaxy-formation|galaxy formation]] occurred much more rapidly than previously thought, or that the models themselves require significant revision.

## Observational Challenge

JWST's infrared sensitivity allows it to detect galaxies at unprecedented distances and early times. Multiple studies have identified galaxies with stellar masses comparable to the [[concepts/milky-way|Milky Way]] existing when the universe was less than one billion years old. Some observations suggest these galaxies may also be older than expected for their epoch, based on stellar population analyses. This creates a tension between what observations show and what current cosmological simulations predict about the timescales required to build such massive structures from smaller components.

## Implications for Galaxy Formation Models

These findings suggest that either the efficiency of star formation in the early universe was higher than modeled, or that the hierarchical assembly of galaxies—where small structures merge to form larger ones—proceeded faster than expected. Alternative [[concepts/explanations|explanations]] include the possibility that some observed objects are more distant than their redshifts indicate, though multiple independent studies have made consistent observations. The discrepancy has prompted ongoing refinement of simulations and [[concepts/theoretical-frameworks|theoretical frameworks]] governing early galaxy assembly.
