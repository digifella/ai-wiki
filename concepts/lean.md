# Lean

Lean is a project management and business methodology focused on maximizing value while minimizing waste. The approach originated from lean manufacturing principles developed at Toyota in the automotive industry during the mid-20th century, where it became foundational to the company's production system. Since then, Lean has been adapted across multiple sectors including software development, healthcare, and general organizational management.

## Core Principles

The methodology centers on continuous improvement and the elimination of inefficiencies in processes. Key concepts include identifying and removing activities that do not add value to the customer, streamlining workflows, and empowering team members to contribute to optimization efforts. Lean emphasizes delivering only what is needed, when it is needed, and in the quantity required—avoiding overproduction and excess inventory.

## Application and Impact

Lean thinking has influenced numerous management frameworks and practices beyond manufacturing. In software development, it shaped agile methodologies by promoting iterative delivery and rapid feedback loops. Organizations across industries have adopted Lean principles to reduce costs, improve quality, and increase responsiveness to customer needs. The framework remains influential in contemporary business strategy, though its effectiveness depends on proper implementation and organizational commitment to continuous refinement.
