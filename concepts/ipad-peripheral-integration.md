# iPad Peripheral Integration

iPad peripheral integration refers to the technical capabilities and standards that enable external devices to connect to iPad models via standardized connection interfaces. Apple's transition to USB-C began with iPad Pro models in 2018, followed by iPad Air in 2022 and base iPad models in 2024. USB-C has become the predominant connection standard across iPad lines, replacing the Lightning connector that previously defined iPad connectivity. This shift aligns iPad peripherals with broader industry standards, enabling compatibility with a wider range of third-party accessories and professional equipment.

## Supported Peripherals and Use Cases

USB-C integration on iPad supports connections to various external devices including external storage drives, displays, audio interfaces, cameras, and input devices such as mice and keyboards. Professional workflows have particularly benefited from enhanced peripheral support, allowing iPad to function as a viable tool for photography, music production, and video editing through connections to specialized hardware. Docking stations and hubs extend single USB-C ports to support multiple simultaneous connections, addressing the limited port availability on most iPad models.

## Technical Considerations

iPad peripheral integration operates within constraints set by iPadOS, which manages power delivery, data transfer rates, and device compatibility. USB-C on iPad typically supports USB 3.1 or Thunderbolt speeds depending on the model generation, though actual bandwidth varies. Power delivery capabilities allow some iPad models to charge via USB-C while simultaneously operating peripherals, though power allocation prioritizes device charging in certain scenarios. Not all USB-C peripherals function identically across iPad models, as support depends on both hardware capabilities and iPadOS updates.
