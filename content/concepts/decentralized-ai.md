# Decentralized AI

[[concepts/decentralized-ai-supercomputer|Decentralized AI]] refers to [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] systems that operate without reliance on a single centralized [[concepts/power|authority]], server, or [[concepts/organization|organization]]. Instead of processing occurring on corporate servers or cloud infrastructure controlled by one entity, decentralized AI distributes computation, data [[entities/storage|storage]], and [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]] across a network of independent [[concepts/nodes|nodes]]. This [[concepts/architecture|architecture]] mirrors decentralized finance and blockchain systems, applying similar principles to [[concepts/computing-architecture|AI infrastructure]] and model [[concepts/deployment|deployment]].

## Technical Architecture

Decentralized AI systems typically leverage distributed computing networks, peer-to-peer protocols, and blockchain technology to coordinate machine [[concepts/learning|learning]] tasks. Participants in the network can contribute [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]], [[concepts/training-data|training data]], or [[concepts/inference|inference]] capacity while maintaining cryptographic [[concepts/verification|verification]] of work performed. This approach aims to reduce single points of failure, improve data [[concepts/privacy|privacy]], and democratize access to [[concepts/capabilities|AI capabilities]] that might otherwise be controlled by well-resourced corporations.

## Applications and Implications

Potential [[concepts/software|applications]] include federated learning across organizations without centralized data aggregation, distributed model [[concepts/training|training]] that no single entity controls, and inference networks where users access AI services without relying on proprietary platforms. Proponents argue decentralized AI could improve privacy, reduce corporate control over AI systems, and enable smaller organizations to participate in [[concepts/ai-development|AI development]]. However, practical challenges remain around coordination, [[concepts/quality-assurance|quality assurance]], and achieving performance comparable to centralized systems.
