## I hacked my camera with AI (and so can you)
**Clip title:** I hacked my camera with AI (and so can you)
**Author / channel:** Tony & Chelsea Northrup
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lbtUnjWh1_c

### Summary
Here is a summary of the video:

**Main Topic:** 
The speaker discusses how artificial intelligence has enabled users to hack into digital cameras—such as Ricoh, Hasselblad, Canon, Sony, Nikon, Sigma, and GoPro—to unlock features that manufacturers previously kept locked, behind paywalls, or omitted entirely. 

**Key Points Discussed:**
* **AI-Assisted Camera Hacks:** The speaker details how developers used AI models like Claude to decode, modify, and rewrite camera firmware. This has allowed users to add features like imperial measurements (feet and inches), continuous autofocus (AF-C), in-camera pixel shift processing (up to 400MP), and even run games like *Doom* or Linux on camera hardware.
* **Open Source Firmware & Manufacturer Responses:** Projects like Magic Lantern and WonderLantern are highlighted as early examples of open-source camera firmware extensions. The speaker notes that some camera manufacturers (like Ricoh and GoPro) have started responding to these trends by releasing their own experimental firmware labs or updating their cameras officially to include requested features.
* **Sony and Nikon Hacks:** The speaker explores how Sony and Nikon users have reverse-engineered camera software to enable features like shutter angle adjustments, custom LUT loading, and live histograms on models that didn't natively support them.
* **Risks of Hacking Firmware:** While firmware hacking unlocks exciting capabilities, the speaker warns that modifying camera code carries the risk of "bricking" the device, voiding warranties, or permanently damaging hardware.

**Conclusions and Takeaways:**
The speaker concludes that AI has dramatically lowered the barrier for non-programmers to tinker with consumer hardware, turning the "wild west" of firmware hacking into an accessible hobby. While camera manufacturers may try to protect their software with security features or digital signatures, the speaker believes this trend is here to stay. Ultimately, the speaker hopes this movement pushes camera manufacturers to embrace open-source collaboration, release better software updates for older cameras, and use AI to improve user interfaces and capabilities.

Hackers added continuous autofocus (AF-C) to the Hasselblad X2D, in-camera 400MP pixel shift to the X2D II, film-camera firmware to the Canon 5D and 5D Mark II, shutter angle to the Sony a7 IV, and pro features to the Nikon Z30 with WonderLantern. I explain how AI reverse engineers camera firmware, the real risk of bricking your camera, what it means for your warranty, and how the EU Cyber Resilience Act could make future cameras much harder to hack.
