# Critical Point

The **Critical Point** is the [[concepts/cli|terminal]] point of a [[concepts/phase|phase]] equilibrium curve in a Phase Diagram, defined by the critical temperature ($T_c$) and critical pressure ($P_c$). Beyond this point, distinct liquid and gas phases do not exist; instead, the substance exists as a Supercritical Fluid.

## Key Characteristics
- **Discontinuity Vanishes**: The boundary between liquid and vapor phases disappears.
- **Critical Opalescence**: Large [[concepts/density|density]] fluctuations cause strong [[concepts/light|light]] scattering near $T_c$.
- **Diverging Correlation Length**: The correlation length of density fluctuations diverges, leading to universal [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]] behavior described by Critical Exponents.
- **Compressibility**: Isothermal compressibility diverges at the critical point.

## Relation to Water Anomalies
While the standard critical point for water is well-defined ($647\text{ K}$, $22.06\text{ MPa}$), recent research suggests complexity in the [[concepts/liquid-state-of-matter|liquid phase]] itself prior to reaching this state.

- **[[concepts/liquid-water|Liquid Water]]'s Dual Identity**: Evidence supports the existence of two distinct liquid states (low-density and high-density) in supercooled water, potentially linked to a hypothesized second critical point.

	- This duality helps explain water's [[concepts/anomalous-properties|anomalous properties]], such as its density maximum at $4^\circ\text{C}$ and high heat capacity.
