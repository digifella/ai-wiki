# Persistent Iteration

Persistent iteration is a continuous optimization methodology within AI-assisted development where code and solutions are systematically refined through structured feedback loops rather than accepted as single-pass outputs. This approach acknowledges that initial AI-generated outputs frequently contain inefficiencies, logical gaps, or suboptimal implementations that benefit from deliberate revision cycles. Each iteration builds on previous results, with developers providing targeted feedback that guides the model toward more accurate and efficient solutions.

## Operation

The process relies on maintaining context across multiple turns of interaction, allowing the AI to adjust its logic based on specific constraints or error reports. Instead of discarding a flawed generation, the developer engages in a dialogue that isolates issues such as API call redundancy or unnecessary computational overhead. This method is particularly relevant in infrastructure and platform domains where cost efficiency and resource management are critical, as seen in specialized tools like the Ralph Wiggum plugin for Claude Code.

By treating the initial output as a draft rather than a final product, teams can achieve higher quality code with lower long-term costs. The iterative nature ensures that optimizations are not accidental but are the result of deliberate, guided refinement. This reduces the risk of deploying inefficient solutions and aligns AI-assisted development with rigorous engineering standards for performance and reliability.
