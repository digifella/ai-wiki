# Microbiome Health

The human microbiome comprises trillions of microorganisms—primarily bacteria, but also fungi, viruses, and other microbes—that inhabit the gut and other body sites. This microbial community plays a fundamental role in digestion, nutrient absorption, and the synthesis of essential compounds including vitamins and short-chain fatty acids. The composition and diversity of the microbiome varies significantly between individuals and changes across the lifespan in response to diet, medication, illness, and environmental factors.

## Health and Disease

A healthy microbiome is associated with proper immune function, intestinal barrier integrity, and metabolic regulation. Conversely, dysbiosis—an imbalance or reduction in microbial diversity—has been linked to various conditions including inflammatory bowel disease, obesity, type 2 diabetes, and certain mental health disorders. The mechanisms by which the microbiome influences these outcomes involve both direct metabolic effects and indirect signaling pathways, such as the gut-brain axis.

## Longevity and Aging

Emerging research suggests that microbiome diversity and composition may influence healthy aging and lifespan. Studies in model organisms and observational research in humans indicate that age-related changes in the microbiome correlate with increased inflammation and reduced metabolic resilience. Maintaining microbial diversity through varied diet, regular physical activity, and reduced antibiotic exposure may support healthier aging, though the causal mechanisms and long-term effects in humans require further investigation.
