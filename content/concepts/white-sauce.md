# White Sauce

White sauce, commonly known as béchamel, is a foundational French mother sauce made from a roux (butter and flour) combined with milk or stock. It serves as a base for numerous dishes across European and international cuisine, providing a creamy binding ingredient for layered and baked dishes.

## Uses in Cooking

Béchamel is frequently used in lasagna, where it creates a creamy layer between pasta sheets and ragout. Beyond lasagna, the sauce appears in moussaka, gratins, croque monsieur sandwiches, and various pasta bakes. Its mild, neutral flavor allows it to complement both meat-based and vegetable dishes without overpowering other ingredients.

## Basic Preparation

The sauce is prepared by making a [[concepts/light|light]] roux with equal parts butter and flour, then gradually whisking in warm milk while [[concepts/stirring-technique|stirring]] constantly to avoid lumps. The mixture is simmered until it reaches the desired [[concepts/logical-consistency|consistency]] and coats the back of a spoon. Seasonings such as salt, white pepper, and nutmeg are typically added to enhance the flavor. Regional and recipe variations may include different liquids or additional ingredients, but the core technique remains consistent.
