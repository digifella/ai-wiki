# Bbq Chicken

BBQ chicken refers to chicken prepared using [[concepts/bbq|barbecue]] [[concepts/cooking-methods|cooking methods]], most commonly grilling or smoking, with the application of barbecue sauce, dry rubs, or other seasonings. The cooking technique imparts characteristic smoky flavors and develops a caramelized exterior while the meat remains moist inside. BBQ chicken is a staple across many regional barbecue traditions, particularly in North American cuisine, though variations exist globally in terms of sauce styles, wood choices, and cooking temperatures.

## Preparation and Cooking

BBQ chicken is typically prepared by [[concepts/marinating|marinating]] the meat prior to cooking to enhance flavor and tenderness. The chicken is then cooked over indirect heat, which allows for slower, more even cooking compared to direct high-heat grilling. This method prevents the sugars in barbecue sauces from [[concepts/burning|burning]] while ensuring the internal temperature reaches safe levels. Common cuts include whole chickens, quarters, wings, and breasts, each requiring adjusted cooking times and temperatures to achieve optimal results.

## Regional Variations

Regional styles significantly influence the [[concepts/preparation|preparation]] and flavor profile of BBQ chicken. In the Southern [[entities/united-states|United States]], vinegar-based or mustard-based sauces are often used, particularly in South Carolina and North Carolina, where the sauce is applied during or after cooking rather than just as a [[concepts/glazing|glaze]]. In [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], Kansas City-[[concepts/style|style]] BBQ chicken typically features a thicker, sweeter tomato-based sauce applied generously during the final stages of cooking. International variations may incorporate local spices, such as jerk seasoning in the Caribbean, or use different smoking woods like hickory, mesquite, or applewood to define the regional taste.
