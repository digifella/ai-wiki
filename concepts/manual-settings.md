# Manual Settings

Manual settings are essential controls available on cameras and scanning equipment that allow users to override automatic functions and maintain direct command over exposure, focus, and other technical parameters. Rather than relying on a device's built-in automation, manual settings give operators precise control over how an image is captured or digitized. This level of control is particularly valuable when working with varied or challenging source materials where automatic systems may produce inconsistent results.

## Film Scanning and Manual Control

When scanning film at home, manual adjustments are critical for preserving the dynamic range and color fidelity of the original negative. Automated scanners often struggle with high-contrast scenes or specific film stocks, leading to clipped highlights or crushed shadows. By manually setting the exposure, white balance, and gamma, users can ensure that the digital file accurately reflects the tonal information present in the physical medium.

Guides such as the tutorial by Lotta Milde from B&H Photo emphasize the importance of understanding how light interacts with film emulsion during the digitization process. Proper manual configuration allows for the correction of color casts and the optimization of contrast levels before the image is finalized. This approach ensures that the resulting digital archive remains a faithful representation of the source material, providing greater flexibility for subsequent editing and restoration work.
