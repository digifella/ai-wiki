# Lasagna Preparation

Lasagna is a multi-layered cooking technique that combines three essential components: ragout, pasta sheets, and béchamel. The ragout serves as the foundational flavor layer, developed through slow cooking to ensure depth and consistency. This meat-based sauce is prepared separately to allow for the reduction of liquids and the melding of spices before assembly.

The structural integrity of the dish relies on the precise coordination of the pasta sheets and the béchamel, a creamy white sauce. The béchamel provides moisture and binds the layers, preventing the pasta from drying out during the baking process. This method ensures that the final product maintains its shape while delivering a uniform texture across all strata.

## Assembly and Baking

The construction of the dish involves alternating layers of pasta, ragout, and béchamel. This layering process is critical for distributing heat evenly and allowing the flavors to integrate. The final layer typically consists of béchamel or cheese to create a cohesive top crust.

## Variations and Context

While the core components remain consistent, variations exist based on regional preferences and ingredient availability. Some iterations may substitute traditional ragout with vegetable-based alternatives or incorporate different types of cheese. The technique is widely recognized in culinary arts for its complexity and the balance required between the sauce components and the pasta structure.
