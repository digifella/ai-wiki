# Strategic Release

Strategic [[concepts/deployment|release]] refers to the planned, deliberate approach organizations use when launching new product versions or capabilities. Rather than releasing products as soon as they are technically feasible, organizations practicing strategic release carefully time and structure launches based on multiple factors including readiness of deployment infrastructure, adequacy of safety testing, market conditions, and stakeholder preparedness. This approach is particularly relevant in [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] development, where performance improvements must be balanced against safety considerations and broader societal impacts.

## Key Considerations

Organizations implementing strategic release typically evaluate several dimensions before launch. Technical readiness involves confirming that performance improvements have been validated and that deployment infrastructure can support the new version. Safety assessment focuses on identifying potential risks or failure modes through testing and [[concepts/red-teaming|red-teaming]] exercises. Market readiness examines whether customers, regulators, and the public are prepared for the new capabilities. Communication planning ensures stakeholders understand what is being released and why, including any limitations or intended constraints on use.

## Balance of Performance and Safety

A central tension in strategic release is managing the trade-off between advancing capabilities and maintaining appropriate safety boundaries. Releasing powerful new capabilities too quickly risks inadequate testing or unforeseen consequences, while excessive delays may forfeit competitive advantages or delay beneficial applications. This balance requires clear [[concepts/decision-making-frameworks|decision-making frameworks]] that weigh technical [[concepts/performance-gains|performance gains]] against demonstrated safety measures and the organization's confidence in responsible deployment.
