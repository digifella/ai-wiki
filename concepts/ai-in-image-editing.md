# AI in Image Editing

[[concepts/ai-technologies|Artificial intelligence]] has increasingly been integrated into [[concepts/image-processing-software|image editing software]] to automate and enhance common workflow tasks. These applications leverage [[concepts/artificial-intelligence-models|machine learning models]] trained on [[entities/big-data|large datasets]] to recognize visual patterns, understand image content, and apply sophisticated [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] with minimal user input. Rather than replacing manual editing entirely, AI features typically serve to accelerate repetitive processes and provide intelligent suggestions that editors can refine according to their needs.

## Automated Masking and Selection

One of the most widely adopted [[concepts/ai-powered-applications|AI applications]] in [[concepts/image-editing|image editing]] is [[concepts/ai-masking|automated masking]], which uses [[concepts/ai-models|neural networks]] to intelligently isolate specific elements within an image. These systems can distinguish between subjects and backgrounds, identify individual objects, or separate layers based on semantic content. This capability reduces the time spent on manual selection work, which has historically been tedious and required significant [[concepts/skill|skill]] to execute cleanly, particularly around complex edges like hair or foliage.

## Color and Tone Adjustment

AI-powered [[concepts/tint-adjustment|color adjustment]] tools analyze image content to suggest or automatically apply modifications to [[concepts/exposure|exposure]], saturation, white balance, and other tonal properties. These systems can adapt adjustments based on detected scene type—applying different [[concepts/open-source-philosophy|logic]] to landscapes, portraits, or indoor photography—and learn from user corrections to improve future suggestions. Such features help establish consistent results across image batches while remaining adjustable by the editor.

## Current Limitations and Integration

While [[concepts/ai-tools|AI tools]] have demonstrated clear utility in image editing workflows, they operate most effectively as assistants within broader creative processes rather than as fully autonomous solutions. The quality of AI-generated results depends heavily on [[concepts/custom-dataset|training data]] and the specificity of the editing task, and edge cases or unusual image content often require human oversight and correction.
