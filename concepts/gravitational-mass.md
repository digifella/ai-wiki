# Gravitational Mass

Gravitational mass is the intrinsic property of matter that determines the strength of the gravitational attraction it exerts on other objects. In classical [[concepts/physics|physics]], this quantity is equivalent to [[concepts/inertia|inertial mass]], which measures an object's resistance to acceleration. This equivalence is formalized in Einstein's equivalence principle and serves as a foundational pillar of general relativity. [[concepts/experimental-verification|Experimental verification]] has confirmed this relationship to extraordinary [[concepts/accuracy|precision]], demonstrating that the same physical property governs both how objects fall in a gravitational field and how they resist changes in their state of motion.

In the context of [[concepts/cosmology|cosmology]], the concept of gravitational mass is critical for understanding the dynamics of large-scale structures such as [[concepts/galaxy-clusters|galaxy clusters]]. Observational challenges arise when comparing the visible [[concepts/baryonic-matter|baryonic matter]] with the total mass required to explain the observed [[concepts/gravitational-effects|gravitational effects]]. The discrepancy between the mass inferred from gravitational interactions and the mass accounted for by luminous baryonic matter provides key evidence for the existence of [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]]. This distinction highlights the necessity of distinguishing between the mass detectable through [[concepts/light|electromagnetic radiation]] and the total gravitational mass governing cosmic structure formation.
