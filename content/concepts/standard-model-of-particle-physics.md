# Standard Model Of Particle Physics

The Standard Model of Particle Physics is the [[concepts/theory|theoretical framework]] that describes the [[concepts/elementary-particles|fundamental particles]] and forces of [[entities/nature|nature]], except [[concepts/gravitational-pull|gravity]]. It classifies elementary particles into quarks and leptons, which interact through the exchange of force-carrying particles called bosons. The model unifies three of the four fundamental forces: the strong nuclear force, the weak nuclear force, and [[concepts/electromagnetic-interaction|electromagnetism]]. It has been extensively validated through experimental tests over several decades and remains the most successful theory in [[concepts/physics|physics]] for explaining particle interactions at high energies.

## Particle Content and Interactions

The Standard Model contains six types of quarks (up, down, charm, strange, top, and bottom) and six leptons (electron, muon, tau, and their associated neutrinos). These particles interact through the exchange of bosons: gluons mediate the strong force, W and Z bosons mediate the weak force, [[concepts/light|photons]] mediate electromagnetism, and the Higgs boson is associated with mass generation. The mathematical framework combines quantum field theory with gauge symmetry principles to predict particle behaviors and interaction probabilities.

## Experimental Validation and Open Questions

Major experiments such as those conducted at CERN's [[entities/lhc|Large Hadron Collider]] and Fermilab's facilities have confirmed many Standard Model predictions, including the discovery of the Higgs boson in 2012. However, the model does not account for [[concepts/dark-matter|dark matter]], dark energy, or neutrino masses, and it remains incompatible with general relativity. Precision measurements in experiments like the [[concepts/muon-g-2-experiment|Muon g-2]] test continue to search for deviations from Standard Model predictions that might indicate new physics beyond the current framework.
