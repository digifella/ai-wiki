# Herculaneum Papyri

The **Herculaneum Papyri** are a collection of ancient scrolls discovered in the Villa of the Papyri in Herculaneum, buried by the eruption of Vesuvius in 79 CE. Charred by volcanic heat, the scrolls are fragile and largely illegible to the naked eye, representing one of the most significant challenges in paleography and archaeology.

## Overview
- **Origin**: Library of the Villa of the Papyri, likely belonging to Lucius Calpurnius Piso Caesoninus, father-in-law of Julius Caesar.
- **Content**: Primarily Epicurean [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]], including works by Philodemus, though other genres (poetry, rhetoric) are present.
- **Condition**: Carbonized rolls; unrolling physically destroys the material.
- **Significance**: Largest surviving collection of [[concepts/ancient-greek|ancient Greek]] literature from antiquity; crucial for understanding Epicureanism.

## Deciphering Methods
### Historical Attempts
- **18th Century**: Early attempts involved unrolling scrolls with water and glue, resulting in significant damage.
- **19th Century**: Giuseppe Valadier and others attempted mechanical unrolling with limited [[concepts/success|success]].

### Modern Technology
Recent advancements in [[concepts/non-invasive-imaging|non-invasive imaging]] have revolutionized access to the texts:
- **X-ray Phase-[[concepts/contrast|Contrast]] Tomography**: Allows visualization of ink (containing iron) against carbonized papyrus without unrolling.
- **Virtual Unrolling**: [[concepts/algorithms|Algorithms]] reconstruct the scroll surface digitally to enhance contrast between ink and substrate.
- **[[concepts/machine-learning|Machine Learning]]**: [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] trained on known fragments assist in predicting text structure and [[concepts/optical-character-recognition|character recognition]].

## Recent Developments (2026)
- **Euripides Discovery**: Recent analysis suggests the presence of previously unknown works by Euripides among the scrolls, challenging the assumption that the library was exclusively Epicurean.
- **Cross-Disciplinary Tech**: Techniques developed for Herculaneum are being applied to other ancient media, including Cuneiform tablets, demonstrating broader applicability of digital deciphering tools.
