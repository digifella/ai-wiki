# Grammar

Grammar is the system of rules that governs the [[concepts/structure|structure]] of language, determining how words combine to form meaningful sentences and utterances. It encompasses syntax (word order and sentence structure), morphology (word formation and inflection), and phonology (sound patterns). Grammar provides the framework through which speakers and writers convey meaning, distinguish between different [[concepts/ideas|ideas]], and communicate with clarity and precision.

## Function and Scope

The primary function of grammar is to establish patterns that allow language users to generate and interpret an infinite variety of expressions from a finite set of rules and vocabulary. Grammar operates [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] multiple levels: phonological rules govern acceptable sound combinations; morphological rules determine how roots and affixes combine; syntactic rules organize words into phrases and clauses. Different languages employ dramatically different grammatical systems, reflecting varied ways of [[concepts/encoding|encoding]] meaning through [[concepts/language-grammar|language structure]].

## Variation and Prescription

Grammar exists both as a descriptive system—how language is actually used by speakers and writers—and as a prescriptive system—how language "should" be used according to established conventions. Descriptive grammarians document patterns observed in natural language use, while prescriptive grammar enforces rules taught in schools and formal contexts. These perspectives often diverge; spoken language frequently violates prescriptive rules while remaining fully comprehensible and grammatical within the [[entities/speaker|speaker]]'s dialect or community.
