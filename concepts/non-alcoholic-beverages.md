# Non-Alcoholic Beverages

**Non-alcoholic beverages** are drinks that contain little to no [[concepts/alcohol|Alcohol]]. This category encompasses a wide range of liquids, from water and juices to complex processed drinks like [[concepts/coffee|Coffee]] and [[concepts/beer|Beer]] derivatives. The production of these beverages often involves specific extraction or removal processes to eliminate [[concepts/psychoactive-compounds|psychoactive compounds]] while preserving flavor profiles.

## Processing and Production Methods

The creation of non-alcoholic variants of traditionally alcoholic or caffeinated drinks relies on advanced chemical and physical separation techniques. Key processes include:

- **[[concepts/caffeine-removal|Decaffeination]]**: The removal of [[concepts/caffeine|Caffeine]] from coffee beans or tea leaves. Methods vary from direct [[concepts/solvent-extraction|solvent extraction]] to water-based processes (Swiss [[concepts/water-process|Water Process]]) and supercritical [[concepts/carbon-dioxide|Carbon Dioxide]] extraction.
- **[[concepts/ethanol-removal|De-alcoholization]]**: The removal of [[concepts/ethanol|Ethanol]] from fermented beverages such as wine or beer. Techniques often involve [[concepts/vacuum-distillation|vacuum distillation]] at low temperatures to preserve volatile aroma compounds or [[concepts/desalination-technology|reverse osmosis]].
- **Historical and Scientific Context**: Detailed histories of these methods, including the evolution from early solvent-based techniques to modern preservation-focused processes, are documented in [[lab-notes/2026-06-24-Decaffeination-and-De-alcoholization-Processes-History-a|Decaffeination and De-alcoholization Processes: History and Scientific Methods]].

## Market Trends

Growing consumer demand for health-conscious alternatives has driven [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] in flavor [[concepts/storing|retention]] technologies, ensuring that non-alcoholic versions closely mimic the sensory [[concepts/experience|experience]] of their traditional counterparts.
