# Air Defence

**Air defence** (AD) encompasses military measures designed to protect a nation's airspace and critical infrastructure from aerial attack. It involves a layered system of detection, command and control, and engagement assets ranging from short-range mobile units to long-range surface-to-air missile systems.

## Core Components
- **Detection & Tracking:** Radar networks, acoustic sensors, and electro-optical systems for early warning.
- **Command & Control (C2):** Integration layers that coordinate interceptors with threat data.
- **Interceptors:** Surface-to-air missiles (SAMs), anti-aircraft artillery (AAA), and fighter aircraft.

## Strategic Role in Modern Conflict
Air defence is critical for securing logistics, enabling freedom of [[concepts/exercise|movement]] for friendly aviation, and degrading enemy reconnaissance capabilities. In asymmetric conflicts, it serves as a force multiplier against numerically superior adversaries.

- **Counter-AD Tactics:** Offensive operations increasingly target static air defence [[concepts/nodes|nodes]] to create corridors for mid-range strikes, reducing the efficacy of integrated air defence systems.
- **Supply Line [[concepts/vulnerability|Vulnerability]]:** The destruction of logistics hubs reduces the sustainability of air defence networks by cutting off ammunition and fuel resupply routes.
