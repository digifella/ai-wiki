# Energy Capture

Energy capture refers to the process of collecting and storing energy from various sources for later use or redistribution. This encompasses both the technological systems that extract energy and the methods by which that energy is preserved in usable forms. Energy capture serves as a foundational step in most energy systems, preceding conversion, [[entities/storage|storage]], and consumption stages.

## Common Sources and Methods

Energy is captured from diverse sources including solar radiation, wind, flowing water, geothermal heat, and fossil fuels. Each source requires specialized capture technology—solar panels convert [[concepts/light|photons]] to electrical current, wind turbines extract kinetic energy from air [[concepts/exercise|movement]], and hydroelectric dams harness gravitational potential energy. Capture efficiency varies significantly by technology and environmental conditions, influencing the economic viability of different systems.

## Storage and Redistribution

Once captured, energy must often be stored for use during periods when the source is unavailable or demand differs from supply. Storage methods range from batteries and pumped hydro systems to thermal storage and hydrogen production. The choice of storage technology depends on the energy form, required duration, scale, and cost constraints. Effective capture and storage systems are essential for managing variable renewable energy sources and maintaining grid [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]].
