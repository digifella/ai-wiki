# Déjà Vu

Déjà vu is a psychological phenomenon in which a person experiences a strong sense of familiarity when encountering a situation, place, or conversation for the first time. The term derives from French, literally meaning "already seen." The experience is typically brief and fleeting, lasting only seconds to minutes, yet often leaves a striking impression on the person experiencing it.

## Prevalence and Experience

Research suggests that déjà vu is a common experience across human populations. Studies indicate that between 60 and 80 percent of people report having experienced déjà vu at least once in their lives. The phenomenon occurs more frequently in people aged 15 to 25 and tends to decline with age. Episodes are typically triggered by environmental cues such as similar settings, conversations, or sensory patterns that unconsciously resemble past experiences.

## Proposed Explanations

Several theories attempt to explain the mechanism behind déjà vu. The hologram theory suggests that memories are stored as interconnected patterns, and partial matches to current experiences can create false familiarity. The divided attention theory proposes that déjà vu occurs when attention shifts between different cognitive processes, causing information to seem both new and familiar simultaneously. Other researchers attribute the phenomenon to brief glitches in memory retrieval systems or to the brain's pattern-recognition processes creating false matches between current and past experiences.
