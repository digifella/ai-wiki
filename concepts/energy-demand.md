# Energy Demand

Energy demand refers to the quantity of energy required to operate industrial and infrastructural systems. In the context of [[concepts/desalination|desalination]], it represents a substantial operational cost and environmental consideration, as removing salt from seawater is an inherently energy-intensive process. The specific energy requirements vary significantly depending on the [[concepts/desalination-technology|desalination technology]] employed and local operational conditions.

## Thermal and Electrical Requirements

Desalination processes require both thermal and electrical energy in varying proportions. [[concepts/msf-technology|Multi-stage flash]] (MSF) technology, widely used in [[concepts/gulf-cooperation-council|Gulf Cooperation Council]] (GCC) countries, relies heavily on thermal energy for the evaporation stages, while also requiring electricity for pumps and auxiliary systems. This dual requirement distinguishes thermal desalination from membrane-based methods, which primarily depend on electrical energy for high-[[concepts/pressure|pressure]] filtration. The balance between these energy forms impacts the overall efficiency and carbon footprint of the facility.
