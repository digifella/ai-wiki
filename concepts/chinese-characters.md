# Chinese characters

Logographic [[concepts/writing|writing]] system used for Chinese language, where each character encodes a Morpheme rather than a direct phonetic value. Characters function as stable visual units representing meaning, independent of pronunciation shifts across dialects or time.

## Core Properties
- **Morphemic [[concepts/integrity|integrity]]:** Each character corresponds to a meaningful linguistic unit; pronunciation is auxiliary, requiring Pinyin or Zhuyin for phonetic mapping.
- **Structural composition:** Composed of Stroke organized into Radical and Phonetic component; classified by Liushu including Pictograph, Ideogram, Ideogram-phonogram, Compound ideograph, [[concepts/text-transcript|Transcript]], and Reversed compound.
- **Variants and usage:** Simplified Chinese vs. Traditional Chinese orthographies; [[concepts/kanji|Kanji]] in Japanese and Hanja in Korean reflect cross-cultural [[concepts/adoption|adoption]] with localized modifications.

## Historical Evolution
- **Origins:** Traces back to [[entities/oracle|Oracle]] bone script (~1200 BCE); continuous development through Bronze script, Small Seal script, Clerical script, Regular script, and cursive forms.
- **[[concepts/continuity|Continuity]]:** Morphemic stability preserves intelligibility of ancient texts for trained modern readers, contrasting with phonetic drift in alphabetic traditions.
