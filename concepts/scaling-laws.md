# Scaling laws

Empirical [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] between [[concepts/model-size|model size]], data volume, and [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]] in AI, typically showing [[concepts/performance-gains|performance gains]] with increased scale (e.g., loss decreasing as $ \text{size}^{-\[[concepts/fine-structure-constant|alpha]]} $). Early work (e.g., Kaplan et al., 2020) established foundational [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]] behavior.

**Key principles:**
- Performance [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]] predictably with [[concepts/code-size|model size]] and data
- Data efficiency and [[concepts/compute|compute]] efficiency are critical factors

**Recent debates:**
- The 2025-2026 surge in large [[concepts/model-releases|model releases]] (e.g., [[entities/granite|Granite]] models) has challenged traditional [[concepts/scaling-law|scaling law]] assumptions
