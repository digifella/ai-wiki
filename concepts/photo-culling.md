# Photo Culling

Photo culling is the process of reviewing and selecting photographs from a larger set to identify the best or most usable images for a project. It is a standard workflow step in photography that occurs after a shoot and before editing or final delivery. During culling, photographers evaluate images based on technical criteria such as sharpness and exposure, as well as subjective factors including composition, subject matter, and alignment with artistic intent.

## Manual and Assisted Culling

Traditionally, culling has been performed manually, with photographers reviewing each image individually to flag favorites or discard unusable shots. This method requires significant time and visual attention, particularly for high-volume shoots. To address this, modern software solutions have integrated artificial intelligence to accelerate the process. Tools like Adobe Lightroom use AI to analyze metadata and visual data, automatically suggesting images based on technical quality or grouping similar shots to streamline the selection phase.

## Workflow Integration

Culling serves as a critical filter that reduces the workload for subsequent post-production stages. By narrowing down the pool of images early, photographers can focus their editing efforts on a manageable number of high-potential files. The efficiency gains from assisted culling allow for faster turnaround times and more consistent selection criteria, although the final artistic judgment remains a human decision.
