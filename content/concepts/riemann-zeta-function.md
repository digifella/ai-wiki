# Riemann Zeta Function

The Riemann zeta function is a complex analytic function defined for complex numbers with real part greater than 1, expressed as the infinite series ζ(s) = 1 + 1/2^s + 1/3^s + 1/4^s + ... It can be analytically continued to nearly all complex numbers through functional equations, making it one of the most studied objects in [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]]. The function encodes deep information about the [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] and frequency of [[concepts/prime-numbers|prime numbers]].

## Connection to Prime Numbers

The zeta function's connection to primes arises through the Euler product formula, which expresses ζ(s) as a product over all prime numbers. This relationship allows researchers to study the spacing and asymptotic behaviour of primes by examining properties of the zeta function. Understanding where the function equals zero—particularly the location of its non-trivial zeros—directly illuminates how primes are distributed along the [[concepts/number-line|number line]].

## The Riemann Hypothesis

The Riemann Hypothesis, formulated in 1859, conjectures that all non-trivial zeros of the zeta function lie on a single vertical line in the complex plane (the critical line where the real part equals 1/2). Despite extensive computational verification and theoretical investigation, this conjecture remains unproven. A proof of the Riemann Hypothesis would have profound implications for understanding prime distribution and would resolve numerous related problems in analytic [[concepts/number-theory|number theory]].
