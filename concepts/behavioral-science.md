# Behavioral Science

Behavioral science is an interdisciplinary field that studies human and animal behavior through systematic observation, experimentation, and analysis. It integrates insights from psychology, neuroscience, economics, and sociology to understand why people and animals act as they do and how behavior patterns develop and persist. Rather than focusing solely on internal mental states or abstract theory, behavioral science emphasizes observable actions and the environmental factors that influence them.

## Core Principles

The field prioritizes empirical evidence and measurable outcomes. Researchers use controlled experiments, longitudinal studies, and real-world observations to test hypotheses about behavior. A central tenet is that behavior is shaped by both internal factors—such as cognitive processes and biological drives—and external factors, including social context, incentive structures, and situational constraints. This perspective has proven valuable for understanding everything from consumer decisions to organizational dynamics to public health outcomes.

## Practical Applications

Behavioral science findings have been applied across numerous domains. In public health, research on behavior change informs interventions around exercise, nutrition, and sleep. In economics and policy, behavioral insights help explain why people deviate from purely rational decision-making and have led to the development of "nudges"—subtle environmental or structural changes that encourage better choices. Organizations use behavioral science to improve workplace productivity, safety, and employee well-being by designing systems and cultures that support desired behaviors.
