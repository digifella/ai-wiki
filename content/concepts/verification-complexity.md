# Verification Complexity

[[concepts/verification|Verification]] complexity is a core concept in computer [[concepts/science|science]] and [[concepts/cryptography|cryptography]], referring to the ease or difficulty of verifying the correctness of solutions to problems. This concept lies [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] the heart of understanding [[concepts/algorithm-efficiency|computational efficiency]] and [[concepts/problem-solving|problem-solving]] [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]].

- **[[concepts/p-vs-np-problem|P vs NP Problem]]:** A central question in [[concepts/theoretical-computer-science|theoretical computer science]] asks whether every problem whose [[concepts/solution|solution]] can be quickly verified by a computer (NP) can also be solved quickly (P). The distinction between these two classes is pivotal for understanding which problems are computationally feasible.

- *If a problem's [[concepts/solution|solution]] is easy to verify, does it necessarily mean that finding the solution is equally easy?* ([P vs NP Problem Computational Complexity and Implications Summary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHp4FPyajKQ))

- **Implications:** Solving or proving the [[concepts/disconnection|separation]] of P from NP would have profound implications for fields such as [[concepts/cryptography|cryptography]], algorithm [[concepts/design|design]], and optimization problems.
