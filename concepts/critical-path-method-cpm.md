# Critical Path Method CPM

The Critical Path Method (CPM) is a project management technique used to identify the longest sequence of dependent tasks required to complete a project. By systematically mapping all project activities, their durations, and interdependencies, CPM determines which tasks are critical to the overall timeline and which have scheduling flexibility. This distinction enables project managers to understand the minimum duration needed to deliver a project and to allocate resources more effectively where they have the greatest impact.

## Key Components and Process

CPM requires project managers to define all individual tasks, estimate their duration, and map their dependencies and relationships. Once this network diagram is constructed, the method calculates the critical path—the sequence of activities that directly determines the project's completion date. Tasks on the critical path have zero slack or float, meaning any delay in these activities extends the overall project timeline. Non-critical tasks have flexibility in their scheduling and can be delayed without affecting the final delivery date, provided they do not exceed their available slack.

## Practical Application

CPM is widely used in construction, engineering, software development, and other industries where projects involve multiple interdependent activities. The method supports informed decision-making about resource allocation, helps identify scheduling bottlenecks, and enables project managers to focus attention and mitigation efforts on the activities most likely to impact delivery. By providing a clear visualization of project sequencing and constraints, CPM facilitates communication among stakeholders and supports more realistic project planning and execution.
