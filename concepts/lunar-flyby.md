# Lunar Flyby

A lunar flyby is a crewed spaceflight trajectory that brings a spacecraft and its crew near the Moon without entering lunar orbit or landing on the surface. This mission profile allows astronauts to travel beyond Earth orbit, conduct scientific observations, and test spacecraft systems in the deep space environment while returning to Earth on a predetermined trajectory. Lunar flybys serve as intermediate stepping stones in human spaceflight programs, providing critical data on human endurance and vehicle performance in deep space conditions.

## Artemis II Mission

Artemis II represents the first crewed flight of NASA's Artemis program and the first time humans have traveled beyond low Earth orbit since the Apollo 17 mission in 1972. The mission utilizes the Orion spacecraft and the Space Launch System rocket to carry four astronauts on a approximately ten-day journey around the Moon. The primary objectives include testing the Orion life support systems, evaluating crew health and performance in deep space, and validating operational procedures for future lunar landings.

The trajectory involves a free-return path that loops around the Moon, allowing the crew to experience the lunar environment without the need for complex orbital insertion maneuvers. This approach minimizes risk while providing valuable data on radiation exposure and microgravity effects on the human body. The mission serves as a critical precursor to Artemis III, which aims to land the first woman and the next man on the lunar surface.
