# Classical computing

A computing paradigm based on the manipulation of discrete states (0 and 1) via deterministic logic and Boolean operations.

## Fundamentals
- **Information Unit:** Utilizes Binary [[entities/digit|digit]] to represent data.
- **Logic:** Operates through Boolean algebra and Logic gates (e.g., AND, OR, NOT).
- **[[concepts/architecture|Architecture]]:** Traditionally follows the Von Neumann [[concepts/architecture|architecture]], characterized by the separation of the [[concepts/cpu|CPU]] and [[concepts/memory|memory]].
- **[[entities/nature|Nature]]:** Deterministic; the same input and initial state always produce the same output.

## Relation to Quantum Computing
- **[[concepts/contrast|Contrast]]:** Unlike [[concepts/classical-bits|classical bits]], [[entities/quantum-computing]] utilizes [[concepts/qubits]] to leverage superposition and entanglement.
- **Competitive Landscape:**
    - The perceived gap in computational utility between classical and quantum systems is being re-evaluated.
    - Critical perspectives suggest that the promised advantages of quantum systems are diminishing as research matures.
    - Reference: Quantum Computing's Diminishing Advantage: Hype Versus Reality.

## Limitations
- **[[concepts/computational-scaling|Scaling]]:** Progress is increasingly constrained by the physical limits of Moore's Law and transistor miniaturization.
- **Complexity:** Inherently limited by [[concepts/computational-complexity]] classes (e.g., inability to efficiently solve [[concepts/np-hard|NP-hard]] problems).
