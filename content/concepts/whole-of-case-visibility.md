# Whole Of Case Visibility

Whole of Case Visibility refers to the requirement for comprehensive, [[concepts/segmented-responsibility|forensic-level transparency in AI]] projects, a principle derived from lessons learned in the [[entities/farah-jama|Farah Jama]] case. The concept emphasizes that AI systems used in consequential [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]]—particularly in criminal justice, legal proceedings, and similar high-stakes domains—must be subject to the same level of scrutiny and documentation as would be applied in a forensic investigation. This means that every stage of an AI system's development, [[concepts/deployment|deployment]], and decision-making process should be fully auditable and explainable.

## Forensic-Level Transparency

The core requirement of whole of case visibility is [[concepts/forensic-level-transparency|forensic-level transparency]], which demands that all relevant information about an AI system's operation be available for examination. This includes [[concepts/training-data|training data]] sources, [[concepts/architecturetechnique|model architecture]] decisions, algorithmic [[concepts/parameters|parameters]], validation methodologies, and real-world [[concepts/performance-data-gathering|performance metrics]]. Such transparency allows independent parties—whether auditors, legal representatives, or domain experts—to reconstruct and understand exactly how specific decisions were made.

## Implications for AI Projects

The principle has direct implications for how organizations [[concepts/design|design]] and implement AI systems. Rather than treating transparency as an optional feature or post-hoc justification, projects operating under whole of case visibility must build documentation and explainability into their core processes from inception. This approach reduces the risk of deploying AI systems that [[entities/make|make]] consequential decisions without adequate oversight or the ability to demonstrate due diligence when outcomes are contested or questioned.
