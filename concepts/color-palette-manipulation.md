# Color Palette Manipulation

The [[concepts/color-mixer|Color Mixer]] in [[entities/lightroom-classic|Lightroom Classic]] enables photographers to adjust specific [[concepts/color-ranges|color ranges]] within an image using HSL (Hue, Saturation, Luminance) controls. Rather than applying broad [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] across an entire photograph, this tool isolates individual color channels—such as reds, oranges, yellows, greens, cyans, blues, and magentas—allowing independent modifications to each. This selective approach proves particularly useful when one color in an image requires correction without affecting surrounding tones.

## How HSL Adjustments Work

The three components of HSL allow for precise control over color [[concepts/presence|appearance]]. Hue adjustments shift a color toward its neighbors on the color wheel, such as moving a red toward orange or purple. Saturation controls the intensity or purity of the color, ranging from fully desaturated (gray) to fully vibrant. Luminance adjusts the brightness of the specific color channel, enabling photographers to lighten or darken particular tones without altering the overall [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] of the image.

By manipulating these parameters independently, users can correct [[concepts/color-casts|color casts]], enhance [[concepts/skin-tones|skin tones]], or create specific moods. For instance, reducing the luminance of blues can darken a sky, while increasing the saturation of oranges can make foliage appear more vivid. This targeted method ensures that global adjustments do not inadvertently distort unrelated colors, providing greater [[concepts/accuracy|precision]] in post-processing workflows.
