# 2nm Chip Manufacturing

2nm chip manufacturing refers to semiconductor production at the 2-nanometer process [[entities/nodejs|node]], representing one of the most advanced tiers of integrated circuit fabrication. The "2nm" designation is a marketing convention established by the semiconductor industry rather than a precise physical measurement. It does not correspond directly to the actual dimensions of transistors or other physical features on the chip. Instead, process node names reflect historical naming practices that have persisted as transistor [[concepts/computational-scaling|scaling]] has slowed in recent years.

## Technical Characteristics

As transistor scaling approaches physical limits, the 2nm node relies on advanced lithography techniques, such as high-numerical-[[concepts/aperture|aperture]] extreme ultraviolet (HNA EUV) lithography, to define features smaller than previous generations. This process typically employs gate-all-around (GAA) transistor architectures, specifically nanosheet or nanowire designs, to improve electrostatic control and reduce leakage current. These structural changes are critical for maintaining [[concepts/performance-gains|performance gains]] and [[concepts/energy-efficiency|power efficiency]] improvements that traditional finFET designs can no longer provide at this scale.

## Industrial Context and Vertical Integration

In the context of specific corporate strategies, manufacturing at this node involves significant capital investment and supply chain complexity. [[entities/tesla|Tesla]] and [[entities/spacex|SpaceX]] have announced plans to utilize their [[concepts/terafab|Terafab]] facility for the production of 2nm [[concepts/ai-chips|AI chips]]. This initiative emphasizes [[concepts/vertical-integration|vertical integration]], aiming to control the entire production pipeline from design to fabrication. The goal is to produce [[concepts/custom-ai-hardware|custom silicon]] optimized for [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] workloads, reducing reliance on external foundries and potentially accelerating the [[concepts/iteration|iteration]] cycle for proprietary hardware.
