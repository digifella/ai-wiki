# Predictive Modeling

Predictive modeling, as articulated by physicist [[concepts/feynman|Richard Feynman]], describes a systematic approach to [[concepts/scientific-method|scientific inquiry]] that emphasizes the relationship between [[concepts/theory|theory]] and observation. Rather than viewing [[concepts/mathematics|mathematics]] as an end in itself, Feynman presented it as a practical tool for formulating testable predictions about natural phenomena. This perspective challenges purely abstract or descriptive approaches to mathematics, instead grounding mathematical inquiry in its capacity to [[entities/make|make]] accurate forecasts about the physical world.

## The Three-Step Method

Feynman's framework consists of three essential steps: first, formulating a guess or hypothesis about how a system behaves; second, computing the logical and mathematical consequences of that guess; and third, comparing those computed predictions directly against empirical observations and experimental data. This cycle—guess, [[concepts/compute|compute]], [[concepts/feynmans-three-step-scientific-method|compare]]—establishes a [[concepts/feedback|feedback]] [[concepts/loop|loop]] where theory must ultimately answer to [[entities/nature|nature]]. If predictions fail to match observations, the initial guess must be revised or abandoned, regardless of how mathematically elegant it may be.

## Practical Application

The method prioritizes predictive [[concepts/accuracy|accuracy]] over explanatory narrative. A theory that produces correct predictions is retained even if our intuitive understanding of the underlying mechanism remains incomplete. Conversely, an explanation that feels satisfying but generates inaccurate predictions has failed the fundamental test of [[concepts/science|science]]. This distinction between mathematical description and genuine understanding underscores Feynman's emphasis on validation through [[concepts/comparing-predictions-to-observations|empirical comparison]] as the decisive criterion for scientific [[concepts/models|models]].
