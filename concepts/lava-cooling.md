# Lava Cooling

**Lava cooling** is the thermodynamic process where molten rock (magma/lava) loses heat to its surroundings, transitioning from a liquid to a solid state. This process drives igneous [[concepts/geological-formation|rock formation]] and influences structural [[concepts/geology|geology]] through [[concepts/thermal-contraction|thermal contraction]].

## Key Mechanisms

- **Thermal Contraction**: As lava cools, it shrinks in volume. This contraction generates tensile [[concepts/stress|stress]] within the cooling mass.
- **Crack Propagation**: When [[concepts/tensile-stress|tensile stress]] exceeds the rock's [[concepts/tensile-strength|tensile strength]], fractures initiate. These cracks propagate perpendicular to the direction of heat loss (typically downward or outward from the cooling surface).
- **Polygonal Column Formation**: The intersection of these contraction cracks often results in regular polygonal shapes, most commonly hexagons, due to the efficient packing of cracks in a cooling sheet.

## Basalt Column Formation

Basalt columns are a primary example of lava cooling effects. They form when basaltic [[concepts/lava-flows|lava flows]] cool relatively uniformly.

- **Process**:
  - Hot basaltic lava flows over a surface.
  - The upper and lower surfaces cool first, creating a crust.
  - Continued cooling [[concepts/causes|causes]] the entire mass to contract.
  - A network of vertical joints forms to accommodate the shrinkage.
  - These joints intersect to form hexagonal or polygonal columns.
- **Characteristics**:
  - Regular, often hexagonal cross-sections.
  - Vertical orientation relative to the cooling surface.
  - Common in Iceland and [[entities/united-kingdom|Northern Ireland]].
