Google Search is a web search engine developed and operated by Google that indexes and retrieves information from billions of web pages, images, videos, news articles, and other digital content across the internet. Launched in 1998, it has become the dominant search engine globally, handling the majority of internet search queries worldwide. The service processes user queries and returns ranked results intended to match what users are seeking.

## How It Works

Google Search uses algorithmic ranking to determine the order of results presented to users. The system crawls and indexes web content continuously, analyzing factors such as page relevance, authority, and user signals to rank pages. When a user enters a search query, Google's algorithms identify matching pages and order them based on these ranking factors. The exact mechanisms of Google's ranking algorithm are proprietary and periodically updated, though the company publicly documents some key principles through its Search Central resources.

## Role in AI and Information Access

As a foundational information retrieval system, Google Search serves as a critical infrastructure component in the broader AI ecosystem. Many AI applications and agents integrate search capabilities to access current information, verify facts, or retrieve context beyond their training data. The search index itself represents one of the largest organized knowledge repositories available, making it a common resource for AI systems that need real-time or comprehensive information access.
