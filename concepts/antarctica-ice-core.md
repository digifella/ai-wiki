# Antarctica Ice Core

Antarctica ice core records preserve a continuous, high-[[concepts/solution|resolution]] archive of [[entities/earth|Earth]]'s climate history, atmospheric [[concepts/writing|composition]], and extraterrestrial material deposition over millions of years. Drilled primarily from the East Antarctic Plateau and West Antarctica, these cores provide critical data on [[concepts/greenhouse-gases]], temperature proxies, and cosmic events.

## Composition & Methodology
- **Stratigraphy:** Annual layers identified via seasonal variations in dust concentration, isotopic ratios (δ¹⁸O, δD), and electroconductivity.
- **Trapped Gases:** Air bubbles trapped in firn allow direct measurement of past CO₂, CH₄, and N₂O concentrations.
- **Chronology:** Dated via ice-core-dating methods including counting annual layers, [[concepts/volcanic-ash|volcanic ash]] markers (tephra), and orbital tuning.

## Key Scientific Insights
- **Paleoclimate:** Correlation between ice-age cycles, Milankovitch cycles, and greenhouse gas levels.
- **Volcanism:** Global [[concepts/volcanic-eruptions|volcanic eruptions]] identified by sulfate spikes, aiding in dating and climate impact assessment.
- **Cosmic Signals:** Detection of extraterrestrial isotopes such as ⁶⁰Fe and ²⁶Al indicates nearby [[concepts/supernova]] events or interstellar cloud interactions.

## Recent Developments
- **[[concepts/iron-60|Iron-60]] [[concepts/anomaly|Anomaly]]:** Elevated concentrations of ⁶⁰Fe in specific Antarctic layers suggest the Solar System's entry into the Local Interstellar Cloud and potential interaction with recent [[concepts/supernova]] remnants. - **Resolution Improvements:** New drilling technologies allow for higher-resolution sampling of rapid climate transitions during the Pleistocene.
