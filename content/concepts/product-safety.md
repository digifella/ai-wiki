# Product Safety

Product Safety is a four-year experimental documentary series by [[entities/destin-sandlin|Destin Sandlin]] that investigates manufacturing practices and safety standards across American industries. The project examines how products are made, tested, and regulated, with particular [[concepts/attention-mechanisms|attention]] to the gaps between consumer expectations and actual safety protocols. Through direct observation and engagement with manufacturers, engineers, and safety officials, the series documents both the intentional safeguards built into production and the cost-benefit decisions that shape final products.

The work explores systemic questions about responsibility in manufacturing: who decides what constitutes acceptable risk, how safety standards are enforced, and what economic pressures influence these decisions. Rather than focusing on individual product failures, the series contextualizes safety within broader [[concepts/supply-chains|supply chains]], regulatory frameworks, and market incentives. Sandlin's approach emphasizes understanding rather than assigning blame, treating manufacturers as complex systems with competing priorities.

The project contributes to public understanding of how everyday products reach consumers and the often-invisible engineering and regulatory work involved. By documenting actual manufacturing environments and decision-making processes, it provides concrete examples of how abstract safety concepts are implemented in practice across different industries and product categories.
