# Low Cost Computing

Low Cost Computing refers to the practice of constructing functional computer systems with minimal financial investment by leveraging open-source software and commodity hardware components. This approach aims to make digital infrastructure accessible to individuals and organizations with limited budgets, thereby reducing reliance on expensive proprietary systems and recurring subscription services. Rather than purchasing pre-built solutions from major vendors, users assemble capable computing systems by combining inexpensive off-the-shelf components with freely available software.

## Practical Applications

A primary application of this concept is the creation of personal media servers to replace paid streaming subscriptions. Projects demonstrating this strategy often utilize Ubuntu Linux to build a media server for under $100. By repurposing low-cost hardware and utilizing free media management software, users can host their own video and audio libraries. This method allows for the consolidation of digital media consumption into a single, self-hosted environment, eliminating monthly fees associated with commercial platforms while maintaining control over data privacy and content organization.
