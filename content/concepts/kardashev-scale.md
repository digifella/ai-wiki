# Kardashev Scale

The Kardashev Scale is a [[concepts/theory|theoretical framework]] proposed by Soviet astrophysicist Nikolai Kardashev in 1964 for classifying civilizations based on their capacity to [[concepts/harness|harness]] and utilize energy. A Type I civilization controls energy [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] the planetary scale (approximately 10^16 watts), a Type II civilization harnesses the energy [[concepts/output|output]] of an entire star (10^26 watts), and a Type III civilization [[concepts/commands|commands]] the energy of a galaxy (10^36 watts). The scale provides a speculative but systematic way to think about technological advancement in terms of energy consumption and [[concepts/infrastructure-development|infrastructure development]].

## Application to Space-Based Infrastructure

The Kardashev Scale has become relevant to contemporary discussions of large-scale technological projects, particularly regarding [[concepts/space-based-ai-infrastructure|space-based data centers]]. As [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]] and computing demands increase substantially, researchers and engineers have examined whether orbital or space-based infrastructure could represent a necessary step toward managing planetary energy constraints. The scale offers a conceptual lens for evaluating whether such projects represent progress along a measurable trajectory of technological capability, though current human civilization remains far below Type I status at approximately 10^12 watts of energy consumption.

The framework is primarily useful as a thought experiment and analytical tool rather than as a predictive model. It [[concepts/highlights|highlights]] the relationship between energy availability and technological capacity, which remains relevant to feasibility studies of [[concepts/megastructures|large-scale engineering projects]]. However, the scale does not account for efficiency improvements, alternative energy sources, or non-energy-based technological advancement, limiting its applicability to real-world technology assessment.
