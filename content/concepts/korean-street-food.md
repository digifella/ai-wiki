# Korean Street Food

Korean street food represents a significant culinary tradition reflecting the country's food culture and daily life. These foods have evolved from practical, affordable meal solutions for working people and commuters into iconic dishes that define Korean gastronomy. Street food vendors remain common fixtures in Korean neighborhoods, markets, and transportation hubs, serving both traditional recipes and contemporary variations.

## Korean Street Toast

[[concepts/breakfast-sandwich|Korean street toast]], known locally as a breakfast sandwich, exemplifies the fusion of Western and Korean culinary influences. The sandwich typically combines simple bread with fillings such as eggs, ham, cheese, and vegetables, often finished with a distinctive sweet and savory sauce. The dish became popular as a quick, portable breakfast option for commuters and students, developing into a standardized street food with regional variations.

[[concepts/preparation|Preparation]] of Korean street toast involves specific assembly techniques that balance [[concepts/texture|texture]] and flavor. The bread is typically buttered and griddled to achieve a crispy exterior while maintaining softness inside. Fillings are layered methodically, with eggs cooked to order and other components arranged to prevent sogginess. The finishing sauce application is crucial to the final product's taste profile, applied with precision to avoid overwhelming the other flavors.
