# Mammalian Cloning

[[concepts/de-extinction|Mammalian cloning]] is the process of creating a genetically identical copy of an existing mammal. The most widely used technique is [[concepts/cloning|somatic cell nuclear transfer]] (SCNT), in which the nucleus of an adult somatic cell is removed and inserted into an egg cell that has had its nucleus extracted. The reconstructed embryo is then stimulated to begin development and typically implanted into a surrogate mother. The resulting offspring carries the genetic material of the nuclear donor rather than the egg provider or surrogate mother.

## Key Techniques and Mechanisms

SCNT remains the primary cloning method because it bypasses the need for sperm fertilization and allows precise genetic control. The process requires careful synchronization of the cell cycle and reprogramming of the adult nucleus to an embryonic state. Alternative approaches, such as embryo splitting, have also been used to create genetically identical organisms, though with different [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]] and applications.

## Challenges and Genetic Effects

Cloned [[entities/mammals|mammals]] often exhibit reduced viability and developmental abnormalities, attributed to incomplete epigenetic reprogramming during the cloning process. Research has indicated that repeated [[concepts/serial-cloning|serial cloning]]—creating clones from clones—may accumulate genetic errors and lead to progressive deterioration of genetic quality across generations. This phenomenon raises concerns about the long-term sustainability of cloning as a reproductive strategy. Additionally, cloned animals frequently show premature aging, compromised [[concepts/immune-system-function|immune function]], and shortened lifespans compared to naturally conceived organisms.

## Applications and Implications

Mammalian cloning has applications in [[concepts/agriculture|agriculture]], medicine, and [[concepts/preservation|conservation]] efforts, including the potential preservation of endangered species. However, the technique remains inefficient, with [[concepts/success-rates|success rates]] typically ranging from 1-4% of attempted procedures. Ethical and regulatory frameworks vary globally, with many countries restricting reproductive cloning while permitting therapeutic cloning for research purposes.
