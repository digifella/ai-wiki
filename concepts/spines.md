# Spines

Spines were rigid, spike-like structures that protruded from the bodies of various shark species, particularly during the Paleozoic era. Composed of bone or calcified cartilage, these structures represented significant evolutionary developments in shark morphology. The prominence of spines in the fossil record indicates they were widespread features across multiple shark lineages during the Golden Age of Sharks, suggesting they provided functional advantages in competitive marine environments.

## Structure and Diversity

Shark spines varied considerably in size, shape, and placement across different species. Some spines were short and robust, while others were elongated and slender, often positioned along the dorsal or lateral aspects of the body. This morphological diversity suggests that spines served multiple roles, potentially including defense against predators, stabilization during swimming, or intraspecific combat. The specific configuration of these structures often correlated with the ecological niche and predatory habits of the individual shark lineage.
