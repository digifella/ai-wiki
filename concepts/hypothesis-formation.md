# Hypothesis Formation

Hypothesis formation is a fundamental stage in the [[concepts/scientific-method|scientific method]] in which researchers propose testable explanations for natural phenomena. A hypothesis serves as a provisional [[concepts/solution|answer]] to a research question, crafted before empirical investigation, and must be falsifiable—capable of being proven wrong through observation or [[concepts/scientific-experiment|experiment]]. Effective hypotheses are grounded in existing knowledge while remaining specific enough to guide [[concepts/experimental-design|experimental design]].

In the context of [[concepts/physics|physics]] research, this process is often characterized by a rigorous cycle of intuition and [[concepts/verification|verification]]. [[concepts/feynman|Richard Feynman]] described this methodology as involving four key steps: guessing the law, [[concepts/computation|computing]] the consequences of that guess, and comparing the results to nature. This approach emphasizes that a hypothesis is not merely a speculative idea but a precise mathematical or logical structure that yields specific, measurable predictions.

The validity of a hypothesis depends entirely on its ability to withstand comparison with empirical data. If the computed consequences of the guess align with experimental observations, the hypothesis gains credibility, though it remains provisional. Conversely, if the predictions fail to match nature, the hypothesis is rejected or modified. This [[concepts/iterative-refinement|iterative process]] ensures that [[concepts/scientific-theories|scientific theories]] remain anchored in observable reality rather than abstract speculation.
