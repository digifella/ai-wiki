## Lightroom Classic: Texture, Clarity, and Dehaze Sliders Explained
**Clip title:** Enhancing Texture, Clarity and Dehaze in Lightroom Classic
**Author / channel:** [[entities/julieanne-kost|Julieanne Kost]]
**URL:** https://youtu.be/vMqQBPffFbQ

### Summary
This video by [[entities/julianne-kost|Julianne Kost]] provides a detailed explanation of three key "Presence" sliders in [[entities/adobe-lightroom|Adobe Lightroom]] Classic: [[concepts/texture|Texture]], Clarity, and Dehaze. The main topic is to illustrate how each slider subtly yet distinctly enhances image [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], texture, and atmospheric quality, making them powerful tools for refined [[concepts/photo-editing|photo editing]]. Although all three affect [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], they do so in fundamentally different ways.

The [[concepts/texture-slider|Texture slider]] is designed to affect fine details by adjusting the contrast of edges within a very small pixel radius. Moving the slider to the left reduces this edge contrast, making surfaces appear smoother, which is useful for minimizing skin texture or smoothing backgrounds. Conversely, moving it to the right increases edge contrast, intensifying fine details like fur, hair, or foliage. Kost emphasizes that Texture's impact on the [histogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histogram) is subtle, changing many columns slightly rather than dramatically shifting values. In contrast, the [[concepts/clarity-slider|Clarity slider]] also targets edges but operates on a wider [[concepts/range|range]] of pixels, producing a broader, softer transitional contrast effect. It is intentionally biased towards midtones, having less impact on highlights and [[concepts/shadows|shadows]], and it also affects [luminance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminance) and saturation more than the Texture slider. Adjusting Clarity creates a more significant shift in the histogram values compared to Texture, impacting the overall punch of the image.

The [[concepts/dehaze-slider|Dehaze slider]] stands apart as it's based on a physical model of how [[concepts/light|light]] is transmitted through the atmosphere, estimating and correcting [[concepts/light|light]] lost due to absorption and scattering. Moving the Dehaze slider to the left increases atmospheric haze, making the photo appear softer, less saturated, and less detailed, as if viewed through fog. Moving it to the right decreases haze, effectively cutting through [atmospheric perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_perspective), adding saturation, and enhancing detail. The histogram reveals that Dehaze primarily affects the [[concepts/shadows|shadows]] and lower portions of the image, with minimal change in highlights.

As key takeaways, Kost provides practical tips for using these tools. When adjusting Dehaze, holding down the Option ([[entities/mac|Mac]]) or Alt ([[entities/windows|Windows]]) key allows users to visualize clipped shadows, ensuring detail is preserved. Dehaze can also be effectively used to set the [[concepts/black-point-adjustment|black point]] of an image while simultaneously boosting saturation. Importantly, while demonstrated with global [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]], all three sliders—Texture, Clarity, and Dehaze—can be precisely applied to specific areas of an image using [[concepts/lightroom|Lightroom]]'s [[concepts/masking|masking]] features, offering highly controlled and localized enhancements.
