# Scientific Experiment

A **scientific experiment** is an empirical procedure conducted under controlled conditions to test, validate, or falsify a hypothesis. It serves as the cornerstone of the [[concepts/scientific-method]], distinguishing [[concepts/purpose|objective]] inquiry from speculation through reproducibility and rigorous data collection.

## Core Components
- **Hypothesis**: A predictive statement proposing a relationship between variables.
- **Variables**:
  - Independent (manipulated)
  - Dependent (measured)
  - Control (held constant to isolate effects)
- **Control Group**: A baseline subject group not exposed to the experimental variable, used for comparison.
- **Procedure**: Step-by-step [[concepts/instructions|instructions]] ensuring Reproducibility.

## Methodological Rigor
- **Double-Blind Design**: Prevents Observer Effect and bias by ensuring neither participants nor researchers know who receives the treatment during data collection.
- **[[concepts/statistical-significance|Statistical Significance]]**: Results must meet a predetermined threshold (e.g., p < 0.05) to rule out [[concepts/probability|chance]] variations.
- **[[concepts/document-review|Peer Review]]**: External validation of methodology and findings before publication in Scientific Literature.
