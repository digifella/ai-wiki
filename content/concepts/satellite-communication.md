# Satellite Communication

Satellite communication refers to the transmission of signals between ground stations and orbiting satellites, enabling [[concepts/global-connectivity|global connectivity]] independent of terrestrial infrastructure. [[entities/spacex|SpaceX]]'s Starlink represents a modern implementation of this technology, designed to provide broadband internet coverage across remote and underserved regions. The system operates through a constellation of satellites in low Earth orbit, which relay signals between users and ground stations.

## Technical Evolution

Starlink's satellite design has undergone several iterations, progressing from V1.5 through later generations including V3. These versions reflect refinements in antenna design, power systems, and [[concepts/signal-processing|signal processing]] capabilities. The evolution addresses practical challenges including improved inter-satellite communication, enhanced signal quality, and [[concepts/cost|operational efficiency]]. Each generation maintains compatibility with the broader constellation [[concepts/architecture|architecture]] while incorporating lessons learned from operational [[concepts/deployment|deployment]].

## Orbital Architecture and Signal Physics

The Starlink constellation operates at relatively low altitudes compared to traditional geostationary satellites, resulting in lower latency and reduced signal delay. This orbital configuration requires a large number of satellites—currently numbering in the thousands—to maintain continuous global coverage. Signal transmission involves complex coordination between satellites, ground stations, and user terminals, with handoff procedures managing connections as satellites orbit overhead. The physics of radio propagation, atmospheric interference, and orbital mechanics all influence system performance and [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]].
