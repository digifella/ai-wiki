# Reasoning Steps

Reasoning steps are the intermediate computational processes that [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] use to work through complex problems, particularly in research and analysis tasks. Rather than generating answers directly, [[concepts/agents|agents]] that employ reasoning steps break down problems into smaller components, evaluating evidence and logical connections before arriving [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] conclusions. This approach is particularly relevant for research-oriented AI systems that need to synthesize information from multiple sources or verify findings against existing knowledge.

## Comparative Performance in AI Research Agents

Several modern AI systems have implemented [[concepts/reasoning|reasoning]] step [[concepts/capabilities|capabilities]] with varying approaches. [[entities/moonshot-ai|Moonshot AI]]'s [[concepts/kimi-k2|Kimi K2]] model demonstrates research capabilities that include systematic reasoning processes. Other notable implementations include [[entities/openai|OpenAI]]'s [[entities/o3|o3]] model, [[concepts/google-search|Google]]'s [[concepts/gemini|Gemini]], [[entities/grok-deepsearch|Grok DeepSearch]], and [[entities/manus|Manus]]. These systems are typically evaluated on standardized benchmarks to assess the quality and [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] of their reasoning processes when tackling research tasks.

The distinction between different reasoning approaches lies in how agents allocate [[concepts/computational-resources|computational resources]] across reasoning steps and how transparently they present their intermediate work. Some systems prioritize showing detailed [[concepts/multi-step-reasoning|step-by-step reasoning]], while others optimize for efficiency by condensing multiple logical operations into fewer visible steps. The effectiveness of each approach depends on the specific research domain and task requirements.
