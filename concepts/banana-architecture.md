# Banana Architecture

Banana Architecture is an open-source design framework that applies distributed [[concepts/coding|software development]] principles to hardware systems at miniaturized and [[entities/nano|nano]] [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]]. The approach emerged in the early 2010s from collaborative maker and research communities seeking to democratize access to small-scale mechanical and electronic [[concepts/codebase-architecture|system design]]. It adapts established [[concepts/open-source-system|open-source software]] practices—including [[concepts/app-updates|version control]], modular component organization, and collaborative documentation—to the domain of [[concepts/hardware|physical hardware]] design.

## Core Principles

The framework emphasizes modularity and interoperability, treating nano-scale components as discrete, replaceable units similar to software libraries. By standardizing interface protocols for physical connections and data exchange, Banana Architecture enables designers to assemble complex systems from pre-verified, community-contributed parts. This structure reduces the barrier to entry for [[concepts/nanotechnology|nanotechnology]] research, allowing independent developers to iterate on designs without requiring proprietary manufacturing tools.

## Implementation and Impact

Implementation relies heavily on digital fabrication tools and open [[concepts/hardware-specifications|hardware specifications]], facilitating the global sharing of blueprints for nano-machines and micro-electromechanical systems (MEMS). The model has influenced subsequent movements in decentralized manufacturing by demonstrating how rigorous version control and [[concepts/document-review|peer review]] can maintain quality in distributed physical production. While primarily theoretical in its early stages, the architecture provided a foundational [[concepts/open-source-philosophy|logic]] for later developments in open-source nanotechnology and collaborative engineering platforms.
