# Accidental Setting Prevention

Accidental Setting Prevention is a feature found on [[entities/fujifilm|Fujifilm]] [[concepts/digital-cameras|digital cameras]] designed to protect camera settings from unintended changes during operation. The feature allows photographers to lock specific controls and [[concepts/parameters|parameters]], preventing accidental [[concepts/adjustments|adjustments]] that could compromise image capture or disrupt workflow. This is particularly useful in professional or fast-paced shooting environments where camera handling is frequent and settings must remain stable.

## Implementation

Fujifilm implements this protection across numerous camera controls—reportedly covering over 50 different settings and functions depending on the camera model. Users can selectively lock individual controls such as [[concepts/exposure|exposure]] compensation, ISO, white balance, focus modes, and physical [[concepts/buttons|buttons]], while leaving other settings accessible. This granular approach allows photographers to protect critical settings while maintaining flexibility for adjustments they may need to make during a shoot.

## Practical Use

The feature is activated through the camera's menu system and typically involves assigning locks to specific dials, buttons, or menu parameters. Once locked, these controls require deliberate unlocking before they can be changed, adding a safeguard against accidental knob rotation, button presses, or menu navigation errors. This is especially valuable during extended shooting sessions or when the camera is being handled by multiple users.
