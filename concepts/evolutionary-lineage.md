# Evolutionary Lineage

**Evolutionary Lineage** refers to the sequence of species, each of which is considered to be a direct descendant of its predecessor. It represents the historical [[concepts/continuity|continuity]] of genetic material and morphological traits through time, forming the branches of the Tree of Life.

## Core Principles

- **Descent with Modification**: The process by which a species changes over time, producing new species.
- **Common Ancestry**: All life forms share a common origin, traceable back to the Last Universal Ancestor (LUCA).
- **Phylogenetics**: The study of evolutionary [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] among biological [[concepts/nodes|entities]], often visualized via Phylogenetic Trees.

## Key Mechanisms

- **Natural Selection**: Differential survival and reproduction of individuals due to differences in phenotype.
- **Genetic Drift**: Random fluctuations in allele frequencies within a population.
- **Speciation**: The evolutionary process by which populations evolve to become distinct species.

## Case Studies in Deep Time

Understanding lineage requires examining ecosystems that have persisted through major geological shifts, preserving ancient genetic lineages.

- **Daintree Rainforest Continuity**:
	- The [[concepts/daintree-rainforest]] in Queensland, Australia, represents one of the oldest living tropical ecosystems on [[entities/earth|Earth]].
	- Its [[concepts/rock-stability|geological stability]] has allowed for the [[concepts/preservation|preservation]] of [[concepts/ancient-biodiversity|ancient biodiversity]] and unique evolutionary lineages that predate many modern tropical forests.
	- Recent analyses highlight its role in understanding [[concepts/geological-survival|geological survival]] and the [[concepts/data-persistence|persistence]] of prehistoric flora and fauna.
