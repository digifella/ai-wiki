# Big Bang formation timescale

## Definition
The chronological framework describing the evolution of the universe from the initial [[concepts/intelligence-explosion|singularity]] of the [[concepts/big-bang]] to the present epoch, characterized by distinct phases of particle formation, [[concepts/element-synthesis|nucleosynthesis]], recombination, and structure formation.

## Key Epochs
- **[[entities/planck|Planck]] Epoch ($t < 10^{-43}$ s):** [[concepts/quantum-gravity|Quantum gravity]] dominates; current [[concepts/fundamental-laws-of-physics|physical laws]] break down.
- **Inflationary Epoch:** Rapid exponential expansion smoothing [[concepts/density|density]] fluctuations.
- **Quark-Gluon Plasma & Hadronization:** Formation of [[concepts/protons|protons]] and neutrons.
- **Big Bang Nucleosynthesis ($3–20$ min):** Formation of [[concepts/light|light]] elements (H, He, Li).
- **Recombination/Photon Decoupling ($\sim 380,000$ years):** Electrons bind to nuclei; universe becomes transparent, releasing the [[concepts/cosmic-microwave-background]].
- **Dark Ages & Cosmic Dawn:** [[concepts/first-generation-of-stars|First stars]] and galaxies form, ending the dark ages via reionization.

## Observational Constraints & Recent Developments
- Standard $\Lambda$CDM models predict [[concepts/galaxy-formation|galaxy formation]] proceeds hierarchically, with massive structures assembling over billions of years.
- Early [[concepts/jwst|James Webb Space Telescope]] ([[entities/jwst|JWST]]) observations suggested potential "impossibly mature" galaxies at high redshifts ($z > 10$), challenging standard formation timescales.
- Recent analysis indicates some of these anomalies may be misidentified foreground objects rather than high-redshift galaxies, refining constraints on early structure formation.
