# High Energy Particles

High energy particles are subatomic particles possessing extremely high kinetic energy, typically measured in gigaelectronvolts (GeV) or higher. These particles are primarily investigated through cosmic ray observations and particle accelerator experiments. Their detection and analysis provide critical insights into fundamental physics, astrophysics, and the mechanisms driving the most energetic processes in the universe.

The origins of these particles are linked to violent cosmic phenomena. Key sources include supernovae, active galactic nuclei, pulsars, and black hole accretion disks. Recent observations by the Large High Altitude Air Shower Observatory (LHAASO) have confirmed the existence of extremely powerful cosmic ray sources in the galaxy, helping to resolve long-standing questions regarding the acceleration mechanisms of cosmic rays.

Research into high energy particles bridges the gap between terrestrial particle physics and astrophysics. By studying the composition and energy spectrum of these particles, scientists can probe the physical conditions in extreme environments that cannot be replicated in laboratories. This field continues to evolve with advancements in detector technology and data analysis methods, allowing for more precise mapping of high-energy sky regions.
