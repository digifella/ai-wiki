# Salting Eggs

Salting eggs is a fundamental cooking technique that significantly influences the texture and tenderness of scrambled eggs and other egg-based dishes. When salt is added to raw eggs, the sodium chloride dissolves and initiates the denaturation of proteins within both the egg white and yolk. This chemical process alters the way proteins coagulate during the heating phase, directly determining the final structural integrity of the dish.

The timing of salt addition is a critical variable in this process. Adding salt immediately before or during cooking allows the ions to interact with the protein structures, weakening the bonds that hold them together. This results in a more tender and moist final product, as the proteins do not tighten as aggressively when heated. Conversely, salting eggs well in advance can lead to excessive breakdown of the protein network, potentially resulting in a watery or rubbery texture depending on the specific application.

Research by culinary organizations such as America's Test Kitchen has demonstrated that salting eggs 15 minutes prior to cooking can enhance flavor distribution and improve texture consistency. This method allows the salt to fully penetrate the egg matrix without causing the negative structural effects associated with immediate high-heat exposure. For optimal results, chefs often recommend salting eggs shortly before cooking to balance flavor enhancement with the preservation of desired structural properties.
