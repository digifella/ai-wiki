# Algorithmic decision-making

The use of Automated Decision Systems (ADS) and mathematical models to automate conclusions, [[concepts/recommendations|recommendations]], or actions that affect human lives, often reducing the role of human oversight.

## Critical Dimensions
- **Algorithmic Bias**: Systematic and unfair discrimination resulting from biased [[concepts/training-data|training data]] or model design.
- **[[concepts/opacity|Transparency]] and Explainability**: The "Black Box" problem, where the [[concepts/open-source-philosophy|logic]] behind a decision is inaccessible to users or regulators.
- **Algorithmic [[concepts/accountability|Accountability]]**: The difficulty in assigning legal or ethical responsibility for errors or harms caused by automated processes.
- **Automation Bias**: The human tendency to over-rely on or [[concepts/trust|trust]] automated suggestions, even when they are erroneous. This cognitive bias is critical in high-stakes environments like aviation, where human operators may defer to [[concepts/ai-models|AI systems]] despite contradictory evidence.

## Case Studies & Failures
- **[[concepts/robodebt-scheme|Robodebt Scheme]]**: Australia's unlawful [[concepts/automated-debt-recovery|automated debt recovery]] program.
- **[[lab-notes/2026-07-01-Air-India-Crash-Human-Responsibility-and-Evolving-AI-Cog|Air India Crash: Human Responsibility and Evolving AI Cognition in Aviation]]**: Analysis of the Air [[entities/india|India]] crash highlights the tension between [[concepts/human-responsibility|human responsibility]] and evolving [[concepts/ai-cognition|AI cognition]]. The incident underscores how automation bias can lead to catastrophic failures when human oversight is compromised by over-trust in [[concepts/automations|automated systems]].
