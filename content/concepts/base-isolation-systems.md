# Base Isolation Systems

[[concepts/earthquake-bearings|Base isolation systems]] are a critical component in earthquake-resistant [[concepts/design|design]] that aims to protect buildings and other structures from the damaging effects of seismic activity. These innovative engineering solutions help mitigate structural damage by decoupling a [[concepts/structure|structure]] from its foundation, allowing it to move independently during an earthquake.

### Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/earthquake-base-isolation|Seismic Isolation]]**: A method used to reduce building movements caused by [[concepts/earthquakes|earthquakes]].
- **Bearings**: Critical components in base isolation systems that allow relative [[concepts/exercise|movement]] between the superstructure and substructure of buildings.
- **Critical Infrastructure [[concepts/resilience|Resilience]]**: The ability of essential services to maintain operations during and after a disaster.

### Detailed Points
- Base isolation systems can significantly reduce structural damage during [[concepts/earthquakes|earthquakes]].
- The effectiveness of these systems is demonstrated through various case studies where buildings with base isolation sustained minimal damage compared to those without such technology.
- Key components include bearings designed to absorb and dissipate seismic energy, thereby protecting the [[concepts/integrity|integrity]] of the building's structure.
