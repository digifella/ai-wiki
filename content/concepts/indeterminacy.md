# Indeterminacy

Indeterminacy in [[concepts/physics|physics]] refers to the inherent unpredictability of certain physical phenomena [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] the microscopic level, particularly within the framework of [[concepts/quantum-mechanics|quantum mechanics]]. This concept challenges classical notions of determinism and [[concepts/highlights|highlights]] the probabilistic [[entities/nature|nature]] of [[concepts/subatomic-particles|subatomic particles]].

### Key Concepts
- **Quantum Mechanics:** The branch of physics that deals with phenomena on a very small scale, such as individual atoms and subatomic particles.
- **[[concepts/superdeterminism|Superdeterminism]]:** A hypothesis in quantum mechanics where all events are pre-determined by initial conditions, making free [[entities/will|will]] an illusion. It proposes that the universe is set up in such a way that outcomes align perfectly without any randomness.

### Implications
- Challenges classical notions of [[concepts/local-realism|local realism]] (the idea that objects have definite properties independent of observation).
- Questions the traditional understanding of causality and predictability.
- Suggests that physical systems do not possess well-defined states until they are measured, reflecting an indeterminate nature.
