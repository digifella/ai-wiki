## World Models: Bridging Human-AI Understanding of Physical Reality

### Summary
The video explores the fundamental difference between how humans and [[concepts/large-language-model-llm|Large Language Models]] (LLMs) perceive and understand the physical world, introducing "[[concepts/world-models|World Models]]" as a promising approach to bridge this gap. Humans develop an innate understanding of [[concepts/physics|physics]] and [[concepts/cause-and-effect|cause-and-effect]] through continuous, [[concepts/multi-modal-observation|multi-modal observation]] and interaction with their environment. In [[concepts/contrast|contrast]], traditional LLMs are primarily trained on trillions of [[concepts/text|text]] [[concepts/tokens|tokens]], which are a high-level [[concepts/abstraction|abstraction]] of the world, meaning they lack direct "embodied experience" of physical laws. This raises concerns about their inherent ability to truly grasp [[concepts/physical-reality|physical reality]] beyond linguistic patterns.

The core idea of World Models is to enable AI to learn a simulated, internal representation of the physical world. The video [[concepts/highlights|highlights]] a foundational 2018 paper by [[entities/david|David]] Ha, which outlines three key components: a [[concepts/computer-vision|Vision]] Model (VAE) to compress environmental observations into a lower-dimensional latent space, an MDN-RNN (Mixture Density Network - Recurrent [[concepts/neural-network|Neural Network]]) to process this compressed information and predict future states based on past hidden states, and a Controller to generate actions. This system allows an [[concepts/ai-agent|AI agent]] to train entirely within its own simulated "world model," [[concepts/learning|learning]] [[concepts/cause-and-effect|cause-and-effect]] and the laws of physics by interacting with this internal representation, even if it's disconnected from the real environment. This approach demonstrated remarkable efficiency with a low [[concepts/parameter-count|parameter count]] in a car racing [[concepts/simulation|simulation]].

Since their resurgence around 2018, World Models have seen significant iterations and different "flavors." The lines between LLMs and World Models began blurring around 2023 with the advent of multi-modal LLMs like [[concepts/gpt-4|GPT-4]] and Gemini, which incorporate visual language models (VLMs) that can process [[concepts/images|images]] using [[concepts/cross-attention|cross-attention]], allowing them to "perceive." Further developments include Visual Language Actions (VLA) in [[concepts/humanoid-robots|humanoid robots]], combining vision [[concepts/transformers|transformers]] with LLMs to generate action tokens for physical interaction. Companies like Feifei Li's World [[entities/labs|Labs]] (Marble) and [[concepts/google-search|Google]] (SIMA, Genie 3) are actively developing interactive, hyper-realistic simulated worlds and [[concepts/agents|agents]] that can operate within them, demonstrating spatial intelligence and cause-and-effect understanding.

While LLMs have scaled beautifully as "[[concepts/foundation-model|foundation models]]" capable of performing diverse downstream tasks, early World Models were often domain-specific. The ongoing debate questions whether LLMs truly "understand" the physical world or merely predict tokens based on text, as argued by proponents like [[entities/yann-lecun|Yann LeCun]]. World Models, by contrast, focus on building an internal, physics-grounded representation that enables interaction and planning in complex environments. The advancements in multi-[[concepts/modality|modality]] and simulation platforms like [[entities/nvidia|NVIDIA]] Cosmos suggest a convergence, where World Models provide the physical grounding and understanding, potentially getting us much closer to [[concepts/agi|Artificial General Intelligence]] by mirroring how humans learn through embodied experience. The ultimate philosophical question remains: Do these models truly think like humans, and does it even matter, or do LLMs and World Models simply solve different, complementary problems in the quest for [[concepts/ai-technologies|artificial intelligence]]?
