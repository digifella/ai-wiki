# Love

Love is a complex emotional and psychological state characterized by deep affection, [[concepts/attachment-theory|attachment]], and care toward other people, oneself, or abstract concepts. It operates across multiple dimensions—romantic, familial, platonic, and altruistic—each involving distinct patterns of attachment, commitment, and neurochemical activity. Psychologically, love engages reward pathways in the brain, particularly involving oxytocin and dopamine, alongside [[concepts/human-cognition|cognitive processes]] that shape perception, [[concepts/decision-making|decision-making]], and behavior toward its object.

## Ancient Perspectives

[[concepts/ancient-greek|Ancient Greek]] [[concepts/philosophy|philosophy]] identified several forms of love through distinct terms: *eros* (passionate, romantic love), *philia* (friendship and mutual affection), *storge* (familial bonds), and *agape* (universal [[concepts/compassion|compassion]]). These categorizations influenced [[concepts/western-thought|Western thought]] for millennia and remain useful for understanding love's varied expressions across human [[concepts/experience|experience]].

## Contemporary Relevance

Modern [[concepts/psychology|psychology]] and [[concepts/neuroscience|neuroscience]] have revealed love's significant impact on physical and mental [[concepts/health|wellbeing]]. [[concepts/secure|Secure]] attachment [[concepts/relationships|relationships]] correlate with better [[concepts/immune-system-function|immune function]], lower [[concepts/stress|stress]] responses, and improved [[concepts/mental-health|mental health]] outcomes. Conversely, social [[concepts/disconnection|isolation]] and lack of meaningful [[concepts/connection|connection]] are associated with increased risk of [[concepts/depression|depression]], [[concepts/anxiety|anxiety]], and various health conditions. Love also functions as a motivational force driving cooperation, commitment, and prosocial behavior within societies.

The experience and expression of love vary considerably across cultures, historical periods, and individuals, shaped by social norms, personal history, and neurobiological differences. Understanding love as both a neurobiological phenomenon and a culturally-mediated experience remains central to health and [[concepts/wellbeing|wellbeing]] research.
