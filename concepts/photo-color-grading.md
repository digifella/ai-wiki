# Photo Color Grading

Photo color grading is the creative process of adjusting colors, tones, and color casts in photographs during post-processing to establish a specific visual mood or aesthetic. Unlike basic color correction, which aims to restore accurate colors, color grading is an intentional artistic choice that shapes how viewers perceive and feel about an image. This technique is fundamental to contemporary photography, allowing creators to unify the appearance of multiple images or establish a cohesive narrative across a body of work.

In the context of Adobe Lightroom, this process often involves manipulating the tone curve, split toning, and HSL (Hue, Saturation, Luminance) panels to achieve desired effects. For dark and moody aesthetics, practitioners typically lower the exposure and contrast while selectively desaturating certain hues or shifting color temperatures toward cooler tones. These adjustments enhance dramatic visual impact by emphasizing shadows and creating a distinct atmospheric quality.

The practice extends beyond individual images to encompass the broader workflow of digital photography. By applying consistent grading parameters across a series, photographers can ensure visual harmony in portfolios, social media feeds, or published collections. This systematic approach transforms raw data into a stylized representation, prioritizing emotional resonance and artistic expression over strict realism.
