# Regulatory Fines

**Regulatory fines** are monetary penalties imposed by government agencies or regulatory bodies on organizations for non-[[concepts/compliance|compliance]] with laws, regulations, or industry standards. These fines serve as deterrents against negligence, ensuring adherence to safety, [[concepts/internet-security|data protection]], and service [[concepts/software-reliability|reliability]] mandates.

## Key Characteristics
- **Enforcement Authority**: Imposed by [[concepts/nodes|entities]] such as the ACMA (Australian Communications and Media Authority), FTC, or [[concepts/gdpr]] supervisory authorities.
- **Trigger Events**: Typically result from data breaches, service outages, environmental violations, or financial misconduct.
- **Calculation Factors**: Based on severity of harm, duration of non-compliance, intent (negligence vs. willful), and organizational revenue.

## Case Study: Telstra Network Outage (2026)
A prominent example of regulatory fines arising from infrastructure failure is the 2026 Telstra outage.

- **Incident Overview**: A nationwide disruption in Australia caused by an obsolete GPS [[concepts/time-server|Time Server]] failure.
- **Regulatory Impact**: The incident triggered potential fines up to **$30 million** due to failure to maintain critical infrastructure standards.
