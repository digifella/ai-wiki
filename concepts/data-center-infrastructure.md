# Data Center Infrastructure

Data center infrastructure comprises the physical facilities, [[concepts/hardware|mechanical systems]], and operational frameworks that enable [[concepts/large-scale-computing|large-scale computing]] operations. This includes the building structure itself, power distribution networks, [[concepts/cooling-systems|cooling systems]], network connectivity, and [[concepts/security|security]] [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]]. These components work together to maintain stable operating conditions for servers and computing equipment while protecting against [[concepts/environmental-risks|environmental hazards]] and [[concepts/security-exposure|unauthorized access]].

## Power and Cooling

Power delivery and thermal management are critical infrastructure functions in [[concepts/techno-economics|data centers]]. Uninterruptible power supplies (UPS) and backup generators protect against grid failures, while distribution systems route electricity to thousands of devices simultaneously. Cooling infrastructure—whether through air conditioning, liquid cooling, or more specialized approaches—removes the substantial heat generated by densely packed computing hardware. As [[concepts/ai-models|AI models]] scale, the [[concepts/density|density]] of [[concepts/compute|compute]] units increases thermal output per square foot, necessitating advanced cooling strategies to prevent thermal throttling and hardware failure.

## Hardware Design and Compute Scaling

Infrastructure evolution is driven by the demands of [[concepts/machine-learning|machine learning]] workloads, particularly regarding the shift from training to [[concepts/inference|inference]] efficiency. Key insights from [[lab-notes/2026-06-02-Jeff-Dean-on-AIs-Future-Data-Inference-and-Hardware-Desi|Jeff Dean on AI's Future: Data, Inference, and Hardware Design]] highlight:

- **[[concepts/feynmans-three-step-scientific-method|Compute]] Leap Impact:** A potential 1,000,000x increase in AI [[concepts/compute-capacity|compute capacity]] [[entities/will|will]] fundamentally alter data center energy budgets and hardware density requirements.
- **Inference-Centric Design:** Future infrastructure must prioritize low-latency inference capabilities over raw training throughput, influencing chip architecture and [[concepts/memory|memory]] [[concepts/hierarchy|hierarchy]] designs.
- **Hardware-Software [[concepts/participatory-research|Co-design]]:** Optimizing hardware for specific [[concepts/neural-networks|neural network]] patterns reduces power consumption and increases effective throughput.
- **Data [[concepts/exercise|Movement]] Bottlenecks:** As [[concepts/computational-resources|compute]] [[concepts/musical-scales|scales]], the cost of moving data between [[concepts/storage|storage]] layers and processing units becomes a dominant constraint, requiring tighter integration of memory and compute resources.
- **[[concepts/energy-efficiency|Energy Efficiency]]:** Sustainable operations depend on improving performance-per-watt metrics, driving [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] in both power delivery and [[concepts/cooling-systems|cooling]] technologies.
