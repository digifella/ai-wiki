# High Voltage Direct Current (HVDC)

High Voltage Direct Current (HVDC) is a method of transmitting electrical power over long distances, typically using long-distance power lines or cables. Unlike [[concepts/alternating-current-ac|Alternating Current (AC)]], which requires power conversion for long-distance transmission, DC power is inherently suited for this [[concepts/motivation|purpose]], especially in high-power, long-distance [[concepts/software|applications]].

## Core Principles of HVDC

HVDC systems operate by converting electrical energy to DC before transmission and converting it back to AC [[concepts/assistive-technology|at]] the receiving end (using converters).

*   **Efficiency:** HVDC systems can be more efficient for very long-distance transmission compared to AC systems, minimizing energy losses over extreme distances.
*   **Voltage [[concepts/power|Control]]:** DC allows for precise control over voltage and current [[concepts/flow|flow]], which is critical for managing power [[concepts/grids|grids]].
*   **Reduced Infrastructure:** For very long submarine or overland transmission, HVDC reduces the need for numerous intermediate substations compared to traditional AC transmission.
*   **Application Focus:** HVDC is primarily used in bulk power transmission rather than [[concepts/distribution|distribution]] networks.

## HVDC Applications

HVDC technology is essential for modern, interconnected power grids and specialized industrial uses.

*   **Intercontinental Transmission:** Linking power grids between countries or continents, often involving submarine cables.
*   **Submarine Cables:** Essential for connecting offshore renewable energy generation (like offshore wind farms) to onshore grids.
*   **Long-Distance Bulk Power:** Efficient transmission of massive amounts of power across vast geographical distances.
*   **Renewable Energy [[concepts/integration|Integration]]:** Facilitating the transmission of power generated remotely, such as from remote hydroelectric plants or large wind farms.

## Historical Context and Resurgence

While AC systems dominated early power transmission, DC systems played a crucial role in specialized high-power applications. Recently, there has been a renewed focus on DC technology due to its advantages in specific transmission [[concepts/scenarios|scenarios]].

*   The historical rivalry between AC and DC marked the evolution of electrical engineering, with AC ultimately prevailing for general distribution.
*   DC's resurgence in specialized applications is driven by the need for high-efficiency power transfer in emerging grid designs.
*   Recent [[concepts/innovation|innovation]] efforts highlight the modern relevance of these transmission methods, as seen in developments like Sweden's HVDC Innovation: Resurgence of Direct Current Transmission.
