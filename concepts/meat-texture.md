# Meat Texture

Ground meat texture during cooking is influenced by protein structure and moisture content. When meat is heated, proteins denature and contract, which can squeeze out moisture and result in a dense, dry final product. The surface texture also affects browning quality, as moisture on the meat's surface can inhibit the Maillard reaction—the chemical process responsible for developing flavor and color.

## Baking Soda's Effect

Baking soda improves ground meat texture through a process called alkaline hydrolysis. When baking soda is mixed with ground meat before cooking, it raises the pH level, which allows proteins to denature more slowly and retain more moisture during heating. This gentler protein breakdown results in a juicier final product compared to untreated meat. Additionally, the drier surface created by baking soda's chemical action promotes better browning, as less surface moisture inhibits the Maillard reaction.

The typical approach involves coating ground meat with a small amount of baking soda (roughly 1/4 teaspoon per pound) several minutes before cooking, which allows the chemical reaction to take effect. This technique is particularly useful for applications where texture matters, such as burgers, meatballs, and ground meat dishes where moisture retention directly impacts palatability.
