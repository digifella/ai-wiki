# Physical Environment Modeling

Physical environment modeling in robotics involves the computational simulation of real-world spaces and dynamics to train and evaluate robotic systems. These models generate digital representations of physical environments, enabling researchers to test robot behaviors, algorithms, and control strategies within a virtual setting before deploying them in actual physical contexts. Accurate modeling is a prerequisite for developing robots capable of executing complex manipulation and navigation tasks with reliability.

A central challenge in this domain is the simulation-to-reality transfer problem, which addresses the discrepancy between simulated data and real-world conditions. NVIDIA's DreamDojo AI is a notable approach designed to mitigate this gap by improving the fidelity of simulations, thereby facilitating more effective training for complex task execution. By reducing the reality gap, such systems allow robotic agents to generalize learned behaviors more successfully when transitioning from digital simulations to physical hardware.
