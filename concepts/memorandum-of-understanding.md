# Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)

A **Memorandum of Understanding** (MOU), also known as a memorandum of base or memorandum of agreement, is a document describing the terms and details of an agreement reached between two or more parties. It expresses a convergence of [[entities/will|will]] between the parties, showing that a basic understanding has been reached, even if it lacks the legal force of a formal treaty or contract.

## Characteristics
- **Non-binding Nature**: Generally, MOUs are not legally binding in the same way as treaties, though they may carry political weight.
- **Flexibility**: Used to outline preliminary agreements, frameworks for cooperation, or steps toward a final treaty.
- **[[concepts/ambiguity|Ambiguity]]**: Often contains vague language to allow parties to agree on general principles while deferring specific details.

## Recent Application: US-Iran Relations
In the context of post-2025 Middle East conflicts, the US and [[concepts/iran|Iran]] utilized an MOU framework to de-escalate tensions.

- **Context**: Signed following a period of intense regional [[concepts/conflict|conflict]], aiming to establish a baseline for diplomatic engagement.
- **Key Provisions**:
  - Outlines preliminary steps for nuclear monitoring and sanctions relief.
  - Establishes communication channels for [[concepts/crisis|crisis]] management.
- **Ambiguities**:
  - Critics argue the document lacks enforcement [[concepts/causes|mechanisms]], leading to potential "misunderstandings" regarding [[concepts/compliance|compliance]].
  - Specific timelines for [[concepts/verification|verification]] remain undefined.
