# Rice Treatment Methods

Rice treatment is a fundamental step in preparing fried rice, as the texture and moisture content of the rice directly affect the final dish. The primary goal of rice treatment is to achieve separate, non-clumpy grains that can absorb flavors and ingredients evenly. Raw rice contains surface starches that cause grains to stick together, so proper preparation begins with rinsing the rice to remove excess starch before cooking.

## Cooking and Cooling

After rinsing, rice should be cooked using a standard water-to-rice ratio, typically 1.5 to 2 parts water per part rice, depending on the variety. Once cooked, the rice must be cooled completely before frying, as hot or warm rice tends to clump and become mushy when exposed to heat in the wok or pan. The most effective method is to spread cooked rice on a sheet pan and refrigerate it for several hours or overnight, allowing moisture to evaporate and grains to firm up.

## Mix-In Ingredients

Common additions to fried rice include vegetables such as peas, carrots, and onions, along with proteins like eggs, chicken, shrimp, or tofu. Seasonings typically consist of soy sauce, sesame oil, and white pepper, added during cooking to coat the grains evenly. Aromatics such as garlic and ginger are often fried first in oil to build flavor before the rice is added. The timing and order of ingredient addition affects both flavor distribution and the final texture of the dish.
