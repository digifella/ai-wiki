# Zipper Mechanics

The zipper evolved from the clasp locker, a fastening device patented in the late 19th century that used hooks and eyes to join fabric edges. Early clasp lockers were mechanically unreliable, prone to jamming, and saw limited adoption beyond novelty use. The fundamental problem lay in the sequential engagement of individual hooks and eyes, which created friction and misalignment when fastened or unfastened.

## Development and Innovation

Gideon Sundback, a Swedish engineer working for the Universal Fastener Company, addressed these mechanical failures between 1906 and 1913 by redesigning the hook-and-eye system into interlocking teeth. His innovation, initially called the "Hookless Fastener," featured a slider mechanism that could smoothly engage multiple teeth simultaneously, dramatically improving reliability and ease of use. The modernized design was eventually marketed under the name "zipper" in the 1920s, referencing the distinctive sound produced during operation.

## Manufacturing and Modern Standards

The development of efficient zipper manufacturing became central to the technology's widespread adoption in clothing, luggage, and other applications. YKK (Yoshida Kogyo Kabushikikaisha), a Japanese company founded in 1934, became the dominant global manufacturer by perfecting the precision engineering required for consistent zipper production. The company's standardization of materials, tooth profiles, and slider mechanics established quality benchmarks that remain industry standards. Modern zippers rely on carefully calibrated tolerances between metal or plastic teeth and the slider mechanism to ensure smooth operation while preventing slippage or misalignment.
